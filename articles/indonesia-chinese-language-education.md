# 从一年级学中文：普拉博沃"外语从娃娃抓起"背后的华文复兴


## 一份国情咨文中的"语言清单"

据中新社报道，普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）在人民协商会议年度会议暨国会联席会议上发表国情咨文，明确提出印尼（Indonesia）儿童应从小学一年级开始学习外语，包括英语、中文和阿拉伯语等。他表示，儿童从小学习外语更容易掌握，具备外语能力将帮助年轻人未来进入国际就业市场。

他开出的"语言清单"相当长：除英语、中文、阿拉伯语，还点名日语、韩语、西班牙语、法语和俄语，并特别提到俄罗斯在科技领域的实力。普拉博沃把学习中文与中国经济活力直接挂钩——他说，中国拥有富有活力的经济，印尼年轻人应通过掌握外语提升自身能力。对一个长期将华文教育视为敏感议题的国家而言，总统在年度政治舞台上公开把中文置于"必须从小学起掌握"的清单，本身就是风向的变化。

## 断层与复苏：被中断30余年的华文教育

理解这句话的分量，需要一点历史纵深。受历史与政治环境影响，印尼的华文教育曾中断长达30余年。从苏哈托（Suharto）时期对华文学校、社团与刊物的系统性压制，到世纪之交逐步松绑，印尼华人社会用了两代人的时间，才重新把"学习自己的语言"变成一件不必讳言的事。

近年的回暖有数据支撑。随着中印尼在经贸、人文、科技等领域合作持续深化，双边留学、旅游与商务往来日益频繁，中文学习热度不断回升。据统计，2025年印尼参加汉语水平考试（HSK）的考生已超过3万人，位居全球第五；目前有超过1.7万名印尼学生在华留学。尽管中文尚未纳入印尼全国小学必修课程，但全国约有30所高等院校已开设中文专业。

## 为什么是现在：劳动力、侨胞与区域格局

普拉博沃的 timing 并非偶然。他提到，已有多个外国领导人向印尼招募旅游、医疗护理、照护等行业的劳工，而这些岗位普遍要求外语能力。把外语能力"前置"到小学一年级，既是为了让年轻人在国际劳动力市场更具竞争力，也暗含借侨力、借区域链条拉动国内增长的算计。

华人族群是这条链条上特殊的一环。印尼是海外华侨华人数量最多的国家之一，华社长期是中文教育与中印尼民间往来的承载者。雅加达（Jakarta）华文教育协调机构执行主席蔡昌杰在接受中新社采访时表示，随着两国各领域交流合作深化，中文的实用价值和社会需求不断增长；普拉博沃的公开表态，有助于提升社会对中文教育的重视。从"历史断层"到"越来越多年轻人主动学中文"，华文教育正在印尼焕发新的生命力。

## 警惕浪漫化：语言政策从来不是孤立的

当然，把一句国情咨文读作"华文教育的春天"需要克制。印尼的语言政策始终在国家认同、族群关系与对外经济需求之间摇摆。中文被纳入"应从小学起掌握"的清单，更多反映的是实用主义——中国作为印尼第一大贸易伙伴与重要投资来源，其语言的市场价值水涨船高，而非对华人文化权利的系统性补课。

印尼智民学院院长陈玉兰的观察更为平实：从历史断层到如今年轻人主动学习，华文教育连接的不只是语言与文化，也连接着教育、就业与日益紧密的中印尼交流。换言之，这门语言的复苏，是经济引力先于政治姿态的结果；它能否沉淀为稳定的制度，仍取决于两国关系的韧性与印尼国内的族群政治走向。

## 结语：一门语言，半部关系史

普拉博沃要让一年级小学生开口说外语，这件事的涟漪，远不止课堂。对印尼华人而言，它是被压抑的母语记忆重新浮出水面；对区域而言，它是中印尼关系从高层互动下沉到民间与代际的缩影。

一门语言能否从"可学的选项"变成"国民素养的一部分"，最终要看教材、师资与制度能否跟上口号。但至少在这一天，当总统在议会大厅把"中文"与"一年级"并置，那个曾被长期噤声的音节，有了被重新听见的可能。

## 延伸阅读

**华文教育（Chinese-language education）**：面向华侨华人及非华裔群体的汉语教学；在印尼曾因历史与政治原因中断数十年，近年随中印尼关系改善而逐步恢复。

**汉语水平考试（HSK, Hanyu Shuiping Kaoshi）**：面向非母语者的国际中文能力标准化考试，由中外语言合作交流中心组织，是全球衡量中文水平的主要标尺之一。

**人民协商会议（MPR, Majelis Permusyawaratan Rakyat）**：印尼最高立法机构，由国会（DPR）与地方代表理事会（DPD）组成，总统每年在此发表国情咨文。

# Learning Chinese from Grade One: What Prabowo's "Languages from Childhood" Reveals About Indonesia's Chinese Revival


## A "language list" in the state address

According to China News Service, in his annual state address to the MPR annual session and joint congressional meeting, Prabowo proposed that Indonesian children start learning foreign languages — including English, Mandarin and Arabic — from the first grade of elementary school. He argued that young children acquire languages more easily, and that foreign-language ability will help the young enter the international job market.

His "language list" is long: beyond English, Mandarin and Arabic, he named Japanese, Korean, Spanish, French and Russian, noting Russia's strength in science and technology. Prabowo tied Mandarin directly to China's economic vitality — China, he said, has a dynamic economy, and Indonesian youth should build their capacities by mastering foreign languages. For a country that long treated Chinese-language education as sensitive, a president placing Mandarin on the "must-learn-from-primary-school" list on the annual political stage is itself a shift in the wind.

## Rupture and revival: Chinese education suspended for three decades

To weigh that sentence, some historical depth helps. Shaped by history and politics, Indonesia's Chinese-language education was suspended for over thirty years. From the Suharto-era systemic suppression of Chinese schools, associations and publications to the gradual loosening around the turn of the century, Indonesian society took two generations to make "learning one's own language" something that could be spoken without evasion.

The recent thaw has data behind it. As China–Indonesia cooperation deepens across trade, culture and technology, two-way study, tourism and business travel have grown busy, and interest in Chinese has kept rising. In 2025, more than 30,000 Indonesians sat the HSK Chinese proficiency test, ranking fifth globally; over 17,000 Indonesian students are now studying in China. Mandarin is not yet a national compulsory primary subject, but about 30 Indonesian universities already offer Chinese majors.

## Why now: labour, diaspora and regional geometry

Prabowo's timing is no accident. He noted that several foreign leaders have approached Indonesia to recruit workers in tourism, healthcare, nursing and caregiving — roles that generally require foreign-language skill. Pushing language ability down to grade one serves both to make youth competitive in the international labour market and, quietly, to leverage the diaspora and regional chains to pull domestic growth.

The ethnic Chinese community is a special link in that chain. Indonesia hosts one of the world's largest overseas Chinese populations, and its community has long carried Chinese-language education and people-to-people ties with China. Cai Changjie, executive chair of Jakarta's Chinese Education Coordination Agency, told China News Service that as bilateral cooperation deepens, the practical value and social demand for Chinese keep growing; Prabowo's public stance helps raise society's regard for Chinese education. From "historical rupture" to "more young people learning voluntarily," Chinese teaching is finding new life in Indonesia.

## Resist the romance: language policy is never isolated

Of course, reading one state address as "the spring of Chinese education" calls for restraint. Indonesia's language policy has always oscillated among national identity, ethnic relations and external economic need. Mandarin's place on the "learn-from-grade-one" list reflects pragmatism more than a systemic reckoning with ethnic Chinese cultural rights — China is Indonesia's largest trading partner and a major source of investment, and the market value of its language has risen accordingly.

Chen Yulan, head of Jakarta's Cerdas Budaya institute, put it more plainly: from historical rupture to today's voluntary learners, Chinese education connects not only language and culture but also education, employment and tightening China–Indonesia exchange. In other words, the language's revival is the result of economic gravity preceding political gesture; whether it settles into stable institution depends on the resilience of bilateral ties and the trajectory of Indonesia's domestic ethnic politics.

## Closing: one language, half a history of relations

Prabowo's wish to have first-graders speak a foreign language ripples far beyond the classroom. For Indonesian Chinese, it is a suppressed mother-tongue memory resurfacing; for the region, it is a miniature of China–Indonesia relations sinking from high-level interaction into the civil and generational layer.

Whether a language moves from "an option one may study" to "part of national literacy" ultimately hinges on textbooks, teachers and institutions keeping pace with slogans. But at least on this day, when the president placed "Chinese" and "grade one" side by side in the parliamentary hall, a syllable long silenced gained a chance to be heard again.

# 印尼要给镍冶炼厂“踩刹车”：75%靠中资的产业链，谁先慌

10月7日，雅加达一场投资者峰会上，印尼投资与下游产业统筹部长、同时兼任能源与矿产资源部长的巴赫利尔·拉哈达利亚（Bahlil Lahadalia）抛出一个信号：印尼正在考虑对新建“半成品”镍冶炼厂实施限制甚至冻结。一句话，给过去十年狂飙突进的镍冶炼产能踩一脚刹车。表面理由是全球镍供过于求、价格被打崩；里子则是一场关于“镍矿红利到底该留在谁手里”的重新谈判。而这场谈判的另一头，站着握有印尼镍冶炼业约75%投资的中国资本。

## 现场：一场峰会上的一句“冻结”

10月7日的投资者峰会（Investor Daily Summit）上，巴赫利尔称，当前全球镍的库存与产能造成约250万吨过剩，“价格才会下跌”；他提出可能对半成品镍产业“设限甚至暂停”，转而优先发展能产出成品下游产品的产业。所谓半成品，包括镍铁、镍生铁、镍锍与氢氧化镍钴（MHP），约占印尼全国镍产量的50%—60%。他的团队正在挑出“该继续”与“该冻结”的冶炼厂，再向总统普拉博沃（Prabowo）提交建议。

## 数字：从“禁原矿出口”到“过剩250万吨”

六年前的2020年，印尼全面禁止镍原矿出口，逼着矿留在国内冶炼，引来中资投入的累计超千亿美元（约7200亿元人民币），在苏拉威西与马鲁古建起莫罗瓦利（Morowali）等巨型园区。这套打法成功了，也反噬了自身：自2023年起全球镍供应持续过剩、价格承压。印尼2023年占全球镍市场逾半，2025年精炼镍供应约61%，预计2028年升至74%。一座由中资撑起来的“镍的欧佩克”，开始用自己的产能反噬定价。

## 谁在裸奔：中资占冶炼业约75%

据美国国防分析研究中心（C4ADS）估算，中资约占印尼镍冶炼业的75%。从青山（Tsingshan，韦达湾矿51.3%股权）到华友钴业、宁德时代旗下的邦普（Brunp），再到德龙系的印尼镍业（Gunbuster/GNI），中资是这条链的绝对主角。冻结一旦落地，最先感到寒意的不是雅加达的规划者，而是那些已经把数百亿美元（约数千亿元人民币）砸进苏拉威西与北马鲁古的中国企业。

## 已经发生的“预演”：配额砍、产能停

冻结尚未正式落地，压力早已释放。2026年印尼镍矿开采配额（RKAB）从2025年的3.79亿吨砍到2.6亿—2.7亿吨，降幅约三分之一；全球最大镍矿韦达湾（Weda Bay）配额被砍逾70%，5月底停产。华友暂停约一半产能，邦普原料中断，印尼镍业（GNI）进入债务重整，华迪（Huadi）停产，万向（Wanxiang）减产。这不是将来时，是进行时。

## 中资反弹：一封写给总统的“诉苦信”

中国印尼商会（CCCI）向普拉博沃总统递交公开信，列举配额缩减、税费提高乃至官员索贿等抱怨，并抄送中国使馆——这是中资一次集体喊话。信号耐人寻味：当“欢迎投资”变成“收紧绞索”，资本在“用脚投票”之前，先递上一封最后的规劝。

## 博弈：资源民族主义的“聪明”与“危险”

巴赫利尔的逻辑是下游化（hilirisasi）：宁可压减半成品，也要逼出电池级硫酸镍与正极材料。放在观察者眼里，这步棋既聪明又冒险。聪明在把镍的附加值留在国内、逼外资向上游升级；危险在政策频翻烧饼——2024年禁矿、2025年招商、2026年冻结——监管的不确定性本身，正如新加坡尤索夫伊萨研究所（ISEAS）的施瓦奇（Siwage Dharma Negara）所言，比冻结更伤信心。中国人民大学赵锡军则点出更冷的现实：若印尼自己接不住冶炼，中资大可转投他国，留下未开发的矿。

## 区域镜鉴：当“镍的欧佩克”想学欧佩克

英国《金融时报》（FT）引述业界看法：印尼想当“镍的欧佩克”，但欧佩克之能压价，前提是自己愿减产。印尼一方面要主导定价，一方面又靠中资产能撑起份额——既要筹码、又要抽身，难度不小。对菲律宾、越南等东南亚其余资源国，这是一面镜子：用外资建链易，把链攥在自己手里难。

## 冷思考：刹车踩下去，谁来接盘

印尼想用冻结换“更有价值的镍”，但产业升级不是一声令下。中资若转向非洲、菲律宾，留下的将是未消化的矿与半截园区。欧盟已批评印尼“过度保护主义”，并指其镍“不干净”——煤电与毁林。这场重新谈判，考验的不只是普拉博沃的定力，还有中资能否从“铺产能”转向“做技术、守环保、吃高端”。否则，刹车之后，摔的未必只是印尼。

## 延伸阅读

- **下游化（hilirisasi）**：印尼自2020年全面禁止镍原矿出口后力推的产业政策，目标是把矿物留在国内加工、向高附加值成品升级。
- **韦达湾（Weda Bay）**：全球最大镍矿，青山持股51.3%、法国埃赫曼（Eramet）37.8%、印尼国企安塔姆（Antam）10%，2026年配额被砍逾70%。
- **中国印尼商会（CCCI）**：在印尼中资企业的联合商会，本次就镍政策向普拉博沃总统递交公开信表达关切。

# Indonesia's Nickel Freeze: Who Blinks First in a Supply Chain 75% Built by Chinese Capital

On 7 October, at an investor summit in Jakarta, Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Downstreaming and Energy Transition — who also serves as the energy and mineral resources minister — Bahlil Lahadalia floated a signal: Indonesia is weighing restrictions, or even a moratorium, on new "semi-finished" nickel smelters. In plain terms, a foot on the brake of a nickel-smelting capacity that has raced ahead for a decade. The stated reason is a global nickel glut that has crushed prices. The subtext is a renegotiation of who keeps the spoils of the nickel boom. And on the other side of that table sits Chinese capital, which accounts for roughly 75 per cent of investment in Indonesia's nickel refining industry.

## The scene: one word — "freeze" — at a summit

At the Investor Daily Summit on 7 October, Bahlil said global nickel reserves and capacity had produced an oversupply of about 2.5 million tonnes, "which is why prices are falling." He raised the prospect of limiting — or even imposing a moratorium on — the semi-finished nickel goods industry, while prioritising sectors that produce finished downstream products. Semi-finished products — ferronickel, nickel pig iron, nickel matte and mixed hydroxide precipitate (MHP) — make up roughly 50 to 60 per cent of national output. His team, he said, is now sorting which smelters should be allowed to continue and which should be frozen, before reporting to President Prabowo Subianto.

## The numbers: from "no raw ore exports" to "2.5 million tonnes surplus"

Six years ago, in 2020, Indonesia imposed a full ban on exporting unprocessed nickel ore, forcing the metal to be smelted at home and drawing more than US$100 billion in Chinese investment — about 720 billion yuan — into parks such as Morowali in Sulawesi and Maluku. The strategy worked, and then boomeranged. Since 2023 the global nickel market has been oversupplied and prices have been under pressure. Indonesia held more than half the global nickel market in 2023, accounted for about 61 per cent of refined supply in 2025, and is projected to reach 74 per cent by 2028. A "nickel Opec" built largely on Chinese capital is now using its own capacity to undercut its own pricing.

## Who is exposed: Chinese capital holds about 75 per cent

According to an estimate by the US-based Centre for Advanced Defense Studies (C4ADS), Chinese investment accounts for roughly 75 per cent of Indonesia's nickel refining industry. From Tsingshan — which holds 51.3 per cent of the Weda Bay mine — to Huayou Cobalt, CATL's recycling arm Brunp, and the Delong-linked Gunbuster Nickel Industry, Chinese firms are the undisputed protagonists of this chain. If a moratorium lands, the first to feel the chill will not be Jakarta's planners but the Chinese companies that have already sunk tens of billions of dollars into Sulawesi and North Maluku.

## The rehearsal already underway: quotas cut, capacity halted

The freeze has not formally arrived, but the pressure is already released. In 2026 Indonesia's annual nickel ore quota (RKAB) was cut from 379 million wet metric tonnes in 2025 to 260–270 million — roughly a third. Weda Bay, the world's largest nickel mine, saw its quota slashed by more than 70 per cent, and mining there halted at the end of May. Huayou paused about half its production capacity, Brunp faced feedstock disruptions, Gunbuster was placed under debt restructuring, Huadi ceased operations and Wanxiang ran at reduced capacity. This is not future tense; it is present continuous.

## Beijing's pushback: a "letter of grievance" to the president

The China Chamber of Commerce in Indonesia (CCCI) has submitted an open letter to President Prabowo detailing its complaints — reduced quotas, higher taxes and levies, and even alleged extortion by officials — copied to the Chinese embassy. It is a collective shout from Chinese capital, and the signal is telling: when "welcome investment" turns into "tightening the noose," money's last remonstrance comes before voting with its feet.

## The gamble: the smart and the dangerous of resource nationalism

Bahlil's logic is hilirisasi — downstreaming: cut the semi-finished, force out battery-grade nickel sulfate and cathode material. Seen from the observer's seat, the move is both clever and risky. Clever, because it keeps nickel's value added at home and forces foreign investors up the value chain. Risky, because policy has spun through cycles — a 2024 ore ban, a 2025 investment drive, a 2026 freeze — and the regulatory uncertainty itself, as the ISEAS – Yusof Ishak Institute's Siwage Dharma Negara argues, hurts confidence more than the freeze. Renmin University finance professor Zhao Xijun points to a colder reality: if Indonesia cannot run the smelters itself, Chinese firms can simply redeploy elsewhere, leaving its mines undeveloped.

## A regional mirror: when "nickel Opec" tries to act like Opec

As the Financial Times notes, Indonesia wants to be the Opec of nickel — but Opec's power to lift prices rests on a willingness to cut its own output. Jakarta wants both to dominate pricing and to rely on Chinese capacity to hold its share; to keep the chips while walking away from the table is a hard act. For the rest of Southeast Asia's resource economies — the Philippines, Vietnam — the lesson is a mirror: building a chain with foreign capital is easy; keeping the chain in your own hands is hard.

## A cooler read: when the brake is applied, who catches the fall

Indonesia wants a freeze to buy a "more valuable nickel," but industrial upgrading is not a decree. If Chinese capital turns to Africa or the Philippines, what is left is an undigested mine and a half-built park. The European Union has already criticised Indonesia's "excessive protectionism" and its "dirty nickel" — coal-fired plants and deforestation. This renegotiation tests not only Prabowo's nerve but also whether Chinese investors can pivot from "pouring capacity" to "doing technology, keeping environmental standards, eating the high end." Otherwise, after the brake, it may not be only Indonesia that stumbles.

## Further reading

- **Downstreaming (hilirisasi)**: Indonesia's industrial policy, intensified since the 2020 nickel ore export ban, aimed at processing minerals at home and climbing to higher-value finished products.
- **Weda Bay**: The world's largest nickel mine; Tsingshan holds 51.3 per cent, France's Eramet 37.8 per cent and state miner Antam 10 per cent. Its 2026 quota was cut by more than 70 per cent.
- **China Chamber of Commerce in Indonesia (CCCI)**: The umbrella body for Chinese-invested firms in Indonesia, which this time sent an open letter to President Prabowo over nickel policy.

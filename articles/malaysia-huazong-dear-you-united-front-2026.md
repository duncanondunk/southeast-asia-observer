# 一部讲华侨的电影，为何在马来西亚引出"统战"之争？

## 一部"下南洋"电影，意外点燃身份政治

2026年6月27日，马来西亚中华大会堂总会（华总）与属下的马来西亚华人博物馆，在雪兰莪州沙登的华总大厦举办《给阿嬷的情书》电影主题分享会。这部讲述华侨南迁与"侨批"文化的影片，在新加坡上映后好评如潮，却也意外引发"是否带有统战色彩"的争论。华总作为马来西亚最具代表性的华团联合组织，选择以一场公开分享会正面回应争议，把一场文化讨论推到了身份政治的前台。

## 华总总会长林家全：电影不是统战工具

华总总会长兼马来西亚华人博物馆主席、拿督林家全在致辞中立场鲜明：华总"全面排斥"将"阿嬷"与"统战"扯上任何关系。他指出，把观赏一部电影延伸到"被统战"，既无事实依据，也是对华侨历史的扭曲。林家全强调，这种说法毫无理据且不必要，华总坚决反对。这番表态并非孤立事件，而是大马华社面对外部标签时一次清晰的自我界定。

## "祖国"与"祖籍国"：大马华人的双层认同

林家全借机梳理了马来西亚华人的身份逻辑：先辈早年"下南洋"，有人衣锦还乡、落叶归根，也有人落地生根、繁衍后代。对在这里土生土长的华人而言，马来西亚就是祖国，中国则是祖籍国——这一身份认同与定位，是坚定而忠诚的。他提醒，尽管血缘与文化令华社关心祖籍国发展，但不能据此简单归结为"统战"。双层认同，正是离散族群最真实的政治自觉。

## "侨批"为何成为文化纽带

影片的关键信物"侨批"，是早期华侨寄回家乡的书信与汇款凭证。马来西亚华人博物馆数年前已设侨批与家书展区，此番配合电影热潮重组并推出"侨批特展与侨批文化体验"，还推出"华博银信局"活动，鼓励民众亲手写信。华总更以"看电影·逛华博·寻侨批"联动，凭票根30天内可免费兑换入馆门票。中国驻马来西亚大使欧阳玉靖亦到场支持，凸显这股文化热流的双向温度。

## 票房与共鸣：一部电影为何能打动大马华人

《给阿嬷的情书》在马来西亚上映短短5天，票房即突破580万令吉（约968万元人民币）。林家全点出影片与马来西亚的深厚渊源：导演兼编剧蓝鸿春三年前筹备时，曾专程到槟城大山脚实地田野调查；女主角谢南枝所居老厝，正是马来西亚华社领袖丹斯里陈国平在中国的祖居。正因嵌入了本地记忆，影片才在大马华人社会引发广泛共鸣，而非被当作外来叙事。

## 从争议看离散族群的处境

这场争论折射的，是东南亚华人长期面对的结构性张力：既要安身于所在国，又与文化母国保持情感与血缘联结。将一部怀旧电影政治化，既低估了华侨历史的厚度，也无助于理解离散族群的真实处境。对华总而言，澄清"不是统战电影"，守护的正是海外华人自主界定自身故事的权利——这比电影本身，更值得被认真对待。

## 延伸阅读

**侨批（Qiaopi）**：早期华侨寄回家乡的书信与汇款凭证，是离散群体与祖籍国之间的物质与情感纽带，2013年入选联合国教科文组织记忆遗产。

**下南洋（Migration to Nanyang）**：19至20世纪中国东南沿海民众赴东南亚谋生的历史浪潮，塑造了今日东南亚华人社会的基本格局。

**华文教育（Chinese-Medium Education）**：东南亚华人保存语言与文化的核心机制，也是身份认同代际传递的关键场域。

# A diaspora film, a 'united front' debate: Malaysian Chinese navigate identity politics

## A 'Nanyang' film unexpectedly ignites identity politics

On 27 June 2026, the Malaysian Chinese Assembly Hall (Huazong) and its affiliated Malaysian Chinese Museum held a screening and discussion of the film *Dear You* at the Huazong building in Serdang, Selangor. The film, which tells the story of early Chinese migration and the *qiaopi* remittance culture, drew acclaim after its Singapore release but also sparked a debate over whether it carried "united-front" overtones. As the country's flagship federation of Chinese associations, Huazong chose to answer the controversy head-on with a public forum, pushing a cultural conversation to the front line of identity politics.

## Huazong chief Lim Jia Chuan: the film is not a united-front tool

In his address, Huazong president and Malaysian Chinese Museum chairman Datuk Lim Jia Chuan was unequivocal: the organisation "fully rejects" linking *Dear You* with "united-front" work. He argued that extending the act of watching a film into a charge of being "united-fronted" has no factual basis and distorts overseas Chinese history. The claim, he said, is groundless and unnecessary, and Huazong opposes it outright. The statement was not an isolated incident but a clear act of self-definition by the Malaysian Chinese community in the face of external labels.

## 'Motherland' and 'ancestral country': the dual identity of Malaysian Chinese

Lim used the occasion to lay out the logic of Malaysian Chinese identity. Earlier generations "went down to Nanyang"; some returned home in glory, others put down roots abroad. For those born and raised in Malaysia, Malaysia is the motherland and China the ancestral country — an identity that is firm and loyal. He cautioned that, while ties of blood and culture lead the community to care about the development of the ancestral country, that cannot simply be reduced to "united-front" work. This dual identity is the most authentic political consciousness of the diaspora.

## Why 'qiaopi' became a cultural bond

The film's talisman, the *qiaopi*, were letters and money orders sent home by early migrants. The Malaysian Chinese Museum had already displayed such letters years ago; this time, riding the film's popularity, it relaunched a *qiaopi* special exhibition and a hands-on "silver-letter office" experience encouraging visitors to write their own letters. Huazong bundled the campaign as "watch the film, visit the museum, find the *qiaopi*," offering free entry within 30 days with a ticket stub. China's ambassador to Malaysia, Ouyang Yujing, attended in support, underscoring the two-way warmth of this cultural current.

## Box office and resonance: why the film moves Malaysian Chinese

Within just five days of its Malaysian release, *Dear You* grossed more than RM5.8 million (about US$1.4 million). Lim highlighted the film's deep Malaysian roots: director-screenwriter Lan Hongchun travelled to Bukit Mertajam in Penang three years ago for field research, and the old house of protagonist Xie Nanzhi is in fact the ancestral home in China of Malaysian Chinese leader Tan Kok Ping. Precisely because it embeds local memory, the film resonated widely among Malaysian Chinese rather than being received as an imported narrative.

## What the controversy reveals about the diaspora

The dispute exposes a structural tension long faced by Southeast Asia's ethnic Chinese: rooted in their country of residence while emotionally and culturally tied to the ancestral homeland. Politicising a nostalgic film underestimates the depth of overseas Chinese history and obscures the real lived experience of the diaspora. For Huazong, clarifying that it is "not a united-front film" is ultimately about defending the community's right to define its own story — a cause that deserves to be taken more seriously than the film itself.

## Further reading

**Qiaopi (Overseas Chinese Remittance Letters)**: Letters and money orders sent home by early Chinese migrants, a material and emotional link between the diaspora and their ancestral homeland; inscribed on UNESCO's Memory of the World register in 2013.

**Migration to Nanyang**: The wave of migration from coastal southeast China to Southeast Asia in the 19th–20th centuries that shaped today's ethnic-Chinese communities across the region.

**Chinese-Medium Education**: The core mechanism by which Southeast Asian Chinese preserve language and culture, and a key site for intergenerational transmission of identity.

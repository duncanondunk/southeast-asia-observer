# 印尼角逐世卫组织总干事：普拉博沃的"全球南方"棋局

2026年8月31日，印度尼西亚卫生部长布迪·古纳迪·萨迪金证实了一件传了数月的事：他已正式报名角逐下一任世界卫生组织总干事。值得玩味的不是参选本身，而是它的出处。布迪说，这一决定是总统普拉博沃亲自授意的结果——在总统看来，这是印尼首次真正有机会领导一个联合国体系下的重要机构。对于一个长期在多边机构中影响力与体量不匹配的国家而言，这是一次精心计算、直扑全球舞台中央的跃升，也让我们看清"全球南方"究竟是如何学会玩转治理游戏规则的。

## 总统亲自授意的使命

布迪对背后推手说得很直白。他在雅加达对记者表示："既然这是总统赋予我的使命，我就必须全力以赴。"他解释，普拉博沃把这次竞选看作历史性的机会：印尼这个拥有近2.9亿人口的国家，从未在联合国机构中占据过最高领导职位。据称，总统还指示他，一旦当选，必须利用这一职位为发展中国家在国际秩序中争取更多关注与资源。

这一表述值得细品。这并非技术官僚的私下职业台阶，而是披着卫生外衣、由元首亲自投射的外交政策。普拉博沃上台后一直把印尼定位为"桥梁强国"，同时受到华盛顿、北京与莫斯科的拉拢；而竞逐世卫，正是一种成本低、能见度高的方式，把这种左右逢源转化为制度性权力。对国内而言，这也递给总统一个叙事：作为东南亚经济与人口第一大国，印尼终于坐上了牌桌。

## 数字：四强混战

选情算术相当严峻。提名截止日为2026年9月24日。世卫组织执行委员会预计在2027年1月的第160届会议上把候选人缩减为三人短名单，世界卫生大会将于2027年5月在日内瓦选出新任总干事。当选者2027年8月16日就职，接替埃塞俄比亚人谭德塞——世卫首位来自非洲的总干事，正完成其第二也是最后一个任期。

布迪是四名公开参选者之一，且均为政府提名。对手皆是重量级内部人：沙特阿拉伯的巴尔基，现任世卫东地中海区域主任；卡塔尔前卫生部长库瓦里；以及比利时的克鲁格，即将卸任的世卫欧洲区域主任。四人中有三位是现任或前任世卫区域主管，克鲁格更为了参选专门辞去区域职务。布迪是那个异类：一位没有任何世卫资历的政治家部长。

## "全球南方"的话语权逻辑

布迪的核心论点直接取自"全球南方"的话术。他主张，发展中国家人口更庞大、贫困与卫生挑战更严峻，却在国际机构领导层中代表不足。"必须要有来自全球南方的代表来领导世卫这样的全球机构，"他说。按布迪的说法，普拉博沃的指令，就是要在全球秩序的核心为这些声音扩音。

这一论证在战术上相当精明。随着谭德塞离任，这场继任战是世卫历史上首次"全球多数"既握票数、又握有可统一诉苦的继任争夺。海湾的金钱与亚洲的人口，让2027年的角逐看起来不再是一场西方护盘，而更像是全球南方内部阵营的对决——利雅得与多哈对阵雅加达，布鲁塞尔则是经合组织中唯一的旗手。印尼的优势在于，它可以自居为非君主制、真正拥有庞大人口的代表，而非靠石油美元买影响力的海湾国家。

## 从银行家到卫生部长

布迪的个人履历本身就是卖点的一部分。他本是核物理学家、银行家出身，曾执掌东南亚资产规模最大的曼迪里银行，后转任国有矿企印尼因达卢姆矿业控股，2020年12月出任卫生部长。他主导了印尼的冠病疫苗接种与卫生体系改革，并被誉为世界银行旗下"大流行病基金"的架构者之一。他表示，若当选将辞去卫生部长职务。

这种商界与金融背景是一把双刃剑。支持者认为，这让他罕见地精通卫生筹资与体系治理——正是世卫在后疫情时代最纠结的优先事项。批评者则指出，他并非医生或公共卫生学者，与多数前任不同，而印尼自身的疫情记录也良莠不齐。与浸淫世卫机器多年的克鲁格、巴尔基这两名职业临床医生相比，反差鲜明。选民究竟看重管理魄力还是技术血统，将决定大半走向。

## 拦路虎：缺席的执行董事会席位

真正的硬仗在这里。印尼全球卫生专家迪基·布迪曼警告，在2027年那场将敲定短名单的提名会议期间，印尼在执行董事会中并无席位。真正做裁剪的是董事会而非全体大会，而印尼届时将在没有同国人坐镇的情况下陈述自己的理由。国内公信力是另一处软肋：布迪的外交成色，将由印尼如何解决自身的卫生争议来检验，以免这一提名在世界卫生大会上反噬自身。

钱能帮衬，却买不来胜局。雅加达今年7月向世卫组织自愿捐款约3000万美元（约2.1亿元人民币）以填补2026—2027年基本预算缺口——这是认真的信号，但海湾对手轻易就能用更高的数额反超。正如布迪自己所承认的，参选是一场持久战。

## 对东南亚意味着什么

把人物放到一边，这场提名是东南亚外交悄然成熟的一个标记。印尼历史上偏好规范性领导——主持东盟、宣讲不结盟——而非占据全球机构的角室。竞逐世卫，逆转了这一本能。即便雅加达只是挤进最后三人，也足以重塑本地区投射软权力的方式：不再仅仅是大国之间的一枚平衡棋子，而是规则本身的制定者。

对更宏大的"全球南方"工程而言，印尼的参选是一次压力测试。它要回答：一个以人口体量加持、手握务实银行家外交官的中等收入亚洲民主国家，能否打破高收入国家对全球最高卫生职位的垄断；还是说，当董事会2027年1月开会时，那套旧的权力地理将再度自我重申。答案比任何一场日内瓦的演讲，都更能预示多边主义的未来。

## 延伸阅读

**世界卫生组织执行董事会（WHO Executive Board）**：由34个成员国组成，负责为世界卫生大会拟定议程并筛选总干事候选人，是继任战真正决定短名单的机构。

**全球南方（Global South）**：泛指亚洲、非洲、拉美等发展中国家集合，近年来在多边机构领导层代表性议题上形成统一话语权诉求。

**大流行病基金（Pandemic Fund）**：世界银行托管的多边基金，旨在为低收入国家卫生体系筹资，布迪被视为其架构者之一。

# Indonesia's Bid for the WHO Helm: Prabowo's Global South Gambit

On 31 August 2026, Indonesian Health Minister Budi Gunadi Sadikin confirmed what had been rumbling for months: he is formally running to become the next Director-General of the World Health Organization. The twist is not the candidacy itself but its provenance. Budi says the bid is a direct mandate from President Prabowo Subianto, who saw in it Indonesia's first real shot at leading a major UN agency. For a country that has long punched below its weight in multilateral institutions, this is a calculated leap onto the global stage — and a revealing window into how the Global South is learning to play the governance game.

## A mandate from the palace

Budi has been unambiguous about who is really behind the run. "Since this is the mission the president has given me, I must give it my all," he told reporters in Jakarta. Prabowo, he explained, regards the campaign as a historic opportunity: Indonesia, a nation of nearly 290 million, has never held a top leadership post in a UN body. The president reportedly instructed Budi that, should he win, he must use the office to secure more attention and resources for developing countries within the international order.

That framing matters. This is not a technocrat's quiet career move; it is a head-of-state foreign-policy projection wrapped in a health portfolio. Prabowo has spent his presidency asserting Indonesia as a bridge power — courted by Washington, Beijing and Moscow alike — and a WHO bid is a cheap, high-visibility way to convert that equidistance into institutional clout. It also hands the president a domestic narrative: Indonesia, the largest economy and population in Southeast Asia, finally claiming its seat at the table.

## The numbers: a crowded four-way field

The arithmetic is daunting. Nominations close on 24 September 2026. The WHO Executive Board is expected to narrow the field to three finalists at its 160th session in January 2027, and the World Health Assembly will elect the winner in Geneva in May 2027. The new Director-General takes office on 16 August 2027, succeeding Ethiopia's Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, the first African to lead the agency, who is completing his second and final term.

Budi is one of four publicly declared candidates, each government-nominated. His rivals are heavyweight insiders: Hanan Balkhy of Saudi Arabia, the WHO Regional Director for the Eastern Mediterranean; Hanan Mohammed Al-Kuwari of Qatar, the Gulf state's former minister of public health; and Hans Henri P. Kluge of Belgium, the outgoing WHO Regional Director for Europe. Three of the four are current or former WHO regional chiefs — and one, Kluge, stepped down from his regional post precisely to run. Budi is the outlier: a politician-minister with no prior WHO rank.

## The Global South argument

Budi's central pitch is borrowed straight from the Global South playbook. Developing countries, he argues, have larger populations and graver poverty and health challenges, yet remain underrepresented in the leadership of international institutions. "There must be a representative from the Global South leading global institutions, such as the WHO," he said. Prabowo's instruction, in Budi's telling, is to amplify those voices at the heart of the global order.

The argument is tactically shrewd. With Tedros's departure, the succession is the first DG race in the agency's history in which the "global majority" has both the numbers and a plausible unifying grievance. Gulf money and Asian demography make the 2027 contest look less like a Western safeguard and more like a contest among the Global South's own blocs — Riyadh and Doha versus Jakarta, with Brussels as the lone OECD standard-bearer. Indonesia's edge is that it can pose as the non-monarchical, genuinely large-population advocate, rather than a petro-state buying influence.

## The banker-turned-minister

Budi's own profile is part of the sales pitch. A nuclear physicist and banker by training, he ran Bank Mandiri — Southeast Asia's largest bank by assets — and later the state mining holding Inalum before becoming health minister in December 2020. He steered Indonesia's COVID-19 vaccination drive and health-system reform, and is credited as one of the architects of the World Bank-hosted Pandemic Fund. If elected, he says, he would resign the health portfolio.

That business-and-finance background is a double-edged credential. Supporters note it gives him rare fluency in health financing and system governance — exactly the post-pandemic priorities WHO wrestles with. Critics note he is not a physician or public-health scholar, unlike most predecessors, and Indonesia's own pandemic record was uneven. The contrast with Kluge and Balkhy, career clinicians steeped in WHO machinery, is stark. Whether voters prize managerial heft or technical pedigree will decide much.

## The obstacles: a board seat Indonesia does not hold

Here lies the hard part. Indonesian global-health expert Dicky Budiman has warned that Indonesia holds no seat on the WHO Executive Board during the very nomination session that will shortlist candidates in 2027. The Board, not the floor, does the winnowing — and Indonesia will be arguing its case without a compatriot in the room. Domestic credibility is the other vulnerability: Budi's diplomacy will be judged by how Indonesia resolves its own health controversies, lest the bid boomerang at the World Health Assembly.

Money helps, but does not guarantee. Jakarta contributed about US$30 million in voluntary funding to WHO in July 2026 to help close a gap in the 2026–2027 base budget — a signal of seriousness that Gulf rivals can easily outspend. Candidacy, as Budi himself acknowledged, is a long game.

## What it means for Southeast Asia

Step back from the personalities, and the bid is a marker of Southeast Asia's quiet diplomatic maturation. Indonesia has historically preferred normative leadership — chairing ASEAN, preaching non-alignment — over occupying the corner offices of global agencies. A WHO run reverses that instinct. If Jakarta can even reach the final three, it reshapes how the region projects soft power: not merely as a balancing pawn between great powers, but as a rule-setter in its own right.

For the broader Global South project, Indonesia's candidacy is a test case. It asks whether a middle-income Asian democracy, armed with demography and a pragmatic banker-diplomat, can break the high-income monopoly on the world's top health job — or whether, when the Board meets in January, the old geography of power simply reasserts itself. The answer will tell us more about the future of multilateralism than any speech in Geneva.

## Further reading

**WHO Executive Board**: A 34-member body that sets the World Health Assembly's agenda and shortlists DG candidates — the institution that actually decides the finalists in the succession race.

**Global South**: A loose coalition of developing states across Asia, Africa and Latin America that has coalesced around demands for greater representation in the leadership of multilateral institutions.

**Pandemic Fund**: A World Bank-hosted multilateral facility financing health systems in low-income countries; Budi is credited as one of its architects.

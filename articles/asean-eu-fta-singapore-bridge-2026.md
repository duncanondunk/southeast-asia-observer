# 新加坡搭台，东盟欧盟重启自贸谈判：缅甸这道坎，谁来迈？

黄循财借与德国总理会晤抛出"桥梁"说，但十九年的拉锯与缅甸制裁，让这份跨区域协定依旧悬而未决。

## 新加坡为何甘当"桥"

东盟（ASEAN）与欧盟的自贸协定（FTA）谈判，最早可追溯到2007年，2009年搁浅，2017年重启，至今仍是"只听楼梯响"。据《南华早报（South China Morning Post）》报道，新加坡总理黄循财在10月27日与到访的德国总理默茨（Friedrich Merz）联合记者会上明确表示，新加坡将在2027年出任东盟轮值主席国期间，推动这份"期待已久"的协定。表面看，这是一次常规的外交表态；细想却不难发现，新加坡是在主动争当一个"桥梁"角色。

它为何有底气？一来，新加坡是东盟里唯一已与欧盟签署自贸协定的国家（2019年生效），对布鲁塞尔的谈判逻辑最熟悉；二来，作为区域金融与贸易枢纽，它天然具备"转译"双方诉求的中介身份；三来，2027年轮值主席的身份，赋予了它设定议程的实质权力。把桥搭起来，新加坡自己也是受益者——区域贸易架构一旦统一，它的枢纽地位只会更稳。

## 十九年拉锯：从2007到2026

这份协定谈得有多难？时间线本身就是答案。2007年启动，2009年因分歧暂停，2017年才重新拾起。近二十年里，世界几度变天，协定却始终卡在"谈判中"。更讽刺的是，欧盟并没有干等：它绕过东盟这个整体，先后与新加坡（2019）、越南（Vietnam）（2020）签下双边自贸协定，眼下还在与印度尼西亚（Indonesia）、马来西亚（Malaysia）、泰国（Thailand）分别谈。换句话说，欧盟嘴上要"区域协定"，脚下却走的是"国别路线"。

这种碎片化并非偶然。东盟内部发展悬殊、规则不一，要十国拧成一股绳对外谈成一个统一关税与原产地规则框架，难度极高；而欧盟出于务实，自然先挑"好谈的"下手。结果是：区域层面空转，双边层面铺开。新加坡想做的，恰恰是把这些零散的双边成果重新拼回一张区域蓝图——但这张蓝图，恰恰卡在东盟自己最薄弱的环节上。

## 缅甸：横在门前的硬石头

真正的拦路虎是缅甸。据《南华早报》引述分析人士，欧盟至今不承认2025年12月至2026年1月那场选举产生的缅甸政府为合法政府，对相关当局的接触受到制裁严格限制；欧盟对缅制裁已延长至至少2027年5月。一个连谈判对手都不被承认的成员国，如何被塞进一份"东盟—欧盟"的区域协定？这道算术题，目前无解。

新加坡作为2027年轮值主席，势必要在"东盟不干涉内政"的传统原则与欧盟"价值观挂钩"的贸易条件之间走钢丝。桥要两头都通，可一头是坚持非干预的东盟，另一头是拿制裁当门票的欧盟。新加坡能做的，多半是技术性协调——比如把数字经济、投资保护等低敏感议题先谈起来，把缅甸问题暂时"挂起"。但这恰恰说明，桥修得再漂亮，最关键的那个桥墩，暂时还是空的。

## 欧盟的"去风险"算盘

把视角拉到欧盟一侧，这份协定从来不只是生意。在"去风险（de-risking）"与"战略自主（strategic autonomy）"的旗号下，布鲁塞尔正急切地拉近与"全球南方"的距离，降低对华供应链依赖。东南亚（Southeast Asia）坐拥约6.8亿人口的大市场，又是芯片、矿产与制造业的关键节点，自然成了欧盟对冲风险的理想落点。德国总理默茨此次访新，更把合作范畴从贸易扩到人工智能、关键海底基础设施防护倡议（GUIDE）乃至东盟电网（ASEAN Power Grid），用意不言自明。

但要清醒：欧盟的"走近"带着明显的工具性。它要的是可信任的供应链、可约束的规则、可对接的数字标准，而非无条件拥抱东盟。自贸协定若成，更多是欧盟"友岸外包（friend-shoring）"的一块拼图，而非对东盟的整体让利。

## 东盟中心性的一次压力测试

对东盟而言，这何尝不是一次"中心性（ASEAN Centrality）"的压力测试。一边是中国的东盟—中国自贸区3.0，一边是欧盟的区域协定诉求，东盟被夹在两大经济体之间"被争取"。新加坡主动搭桥，某种程度上也是在替东盟抢回议程设置权——与其被动等待被双边协定"瓜分"，不如自己把区域框架立起来。

可隐患也在：若缅甸问题长期无解，这份协定要么把缅甸架空、损及东盟一体性的门面，要么整体搁浅、让十九年的努力再次泡汤。无论哪种，受伤的都是东盟自身的凝聚力叙事。

## 别把这座桥想得太宽

所以，对黄循财"有挑战、很复杂，但并非没有理由乐观"的表态，不妨换个读法。那不是胜利预告，而是外交辞令里的谨慎留白。一份谈了十九年、卡在制裁与内部分歧上的协定，短期内落地的概率并不高。新加坡的"桥梁"角色真实存在，但它的承载力有限：它能促成对话、设定议程、推进低敏感议题，却搬不动缅甸这块石头，也填不满欧盟与东盟之间的规则鸿沟。

真正值得关注的，或许不是这份协定何时签署，而是新加坡借2027年主席国身份，能否把东盟从一个"被各方争取的对象"，重新推回"自己定规则的一方"。桥的意义，不在宽窄，而在谁握着图纸。

## 延伸阅读

**自贸协定（Free Trade Agreement, FTA）**：两个或多个经济体之间取消或削减关税、统一原产地与贸易规则的条约；本文指东盟十国与欧盟二十七国之间的区域集团对区域集团谈判。

**数字经济框架协议（Digital Economy Framework Agreement, DEFA）**：东盟首份区域性数字经济治理协定，涵盖数字贸易、跨境电商、网络安全、数字身份与数据流动，原计划于2026年11月马尼拉峰会签署。

**友岸外包（Friend-shoring）**：将供应链从"成本优先"转向"政治与安全风险可控的盟友圈"的产业政策，是近年欧美重构与东南亚经贸关系的核心理念之一。

# Singapore's bridge gambit: can it unblock an EU–ASEAN trade pact stuck for 19 years?

Prime Minister Lawrence Wong offered to broker a bloc-to-bloc free trade deal as ASEAN chair in 2027 — but Myanmar's isolation and Europe's own patchwork of bilateral pacts keep the prize distant.

## Why Singapore is willing to be the bridge

The idea of a free trade agreement (FTA) between ASEAN and the European Union is old news that never became news. Talks first opened in 2007, stalled in 2009, and were revived in 2017 — and the deal is still, in the frank phrasing of one observer, "much talked about, never landed." According to the South China Morning Post, Prime Minister Lawrence Wong told a joint press conference with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on 27 October that Singapore would push the "long-awaited" pact forward during its 2027 ASEAN chairmanship. On the surface that is routine diplomatic signalling. Look closer, and Singapore is clearly volunteering for the role of broker.

Why does it have the standing to do so? First, Singapore is the only ASEAN member that already has an EU FTA in force (since 2019), so it knows Brussels' negotiating logic better than anyone in the room. Second, as the region's financial and trading hub, it is a natural translator of each side's demands. Third, the 2027 chair gives it real agenda-setting power. A unified regional trade architecture would only cement Singapore's own hub status — so the bridge serves the builder, too.

## Nineteen years of tug-of-war: 2007 to 2026

How hard is this deal? The timeline is the answer. Launched in 2007, suspended in 2009, resumed in 2017. Nearly two decades of a changing world, and the agreement is still "under negotiation." The irony is sharper: the EU did not simply wait. It bypassed ASEAN as a bloc and signed bilateral FTAs with Singapore (2019) and Vietnam (2020), and is now negotiating separately with Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand. In other words, Brussels asks for a regional pact while walking the bilateral path.

This fragmentation is no accident. ASEAN's members differ wildly in development and rule-making capacity; aligning ten economies behind one tariff and rules-of-origin schedule is brutally difficult. The EU, being pragmatic, picked the easy partners first. The result: the bloc track spins in place while the bilateral track lays track. Singapore's ambition is to reassemble those scattered bilateral wins into a regional blueprint — except the blueprint snaps at ASEAN's weakest joint seam.

## Myanmar: the roadblock at the door

The real obstacle is Myanmar. As the SCMP notes, citing analysts, the EU does not recognise the government that emerged from the December 2025–January 2026 election as legitimate, and sanctions tightly restrict engagement with its authorities; EU measures on Myanmar have been extended to at least May 2027. How do you fold a member whose interlocutors are not even recognised into a regional "ASEAN–EU" agreement? There is, for now, no clean arithmetic.

As 2027 chair, Singapore will have to walk a tightrope between ASEAN's tradition of non-interference and the EU's values-linked trade conditionality. For the bridge to connect both ends, one side insists on non-intervention and the other treats sanctions as the entry ticket. What Singapore can realistically do is technical coordination — advancing low-sensitivity items like digital trade and investment protection while "parking" Myanmar. That only proves the point: however elegant the span, its most critical pier is, for now, empty.

## Europe's 'de-risking' calculus

From the European side, this was never only about commerce. Under the banners of "de-risking" and "strategic autonomy," Brussels is urgently courting the Global South to reduce dependence on Chinese supply chains. Southeast Asia — a market of some 680 million people and a critical node for semiconductors, minerals and manufacturing — is the obvious landing spot. Chancellor Merz's visit widened the agenda from trade to artificial intelligence, protection of critical undersea infrastructure (the GUIDE initiative) and even the ASEAN Power Grid. The intent needs no decoding.

But be clear-eyed: Europe's outreach is instrumental. It wants trusted supply chains, enforceable rules and interoperable digital standards, not an unconditional embrace of ASEAN. If the FTA lands, it will be one tile in the EU's friend-shoring mosaic, not a sweeping concession to the region.

## A stress test for ASEAN centrality

For ASEAN, this is a stress test of its much-invoked "centrality." On one side sits China's ASEAN–China FTA 3.0; on the other, the EU's regional ambitions. ASEAN is being competed for by two large economies at once. Singapore's brokering is, in part, an attempt to seize the agenda back — rather than be carved up by bilateral deals, ASEAN should erect its own regional framework.

The hazard is real, though. If Myanmar remains unresolved, the pact either marginalises Myanmar — denting the façade of ASEAN unity — or stalls entirely, wasting another nineteen years. Either way, it is ASEAN's cohesion narrative that takes the bruising.

## Don't imagine the bridge too wide

So read Wong's line — "challenging," "complex," but "reason for some optimism" — differently. It is not a victory forecast; it is the careful whitespace of diplomatic hedging. A deal that has dragged nineteen years and is jammed by sanctions and internal divisions is unlikely to land soon. Singapore's bridge is real, but its load-bearing capacity is limited: it can convene dialogue, set the agenda and advance low-sensitivity items, yet it cannot move the Myanmar rock or fill the rule gap between Europe and ASEAN.

What may matter more than the signing date is whether Singapore, via its 2027 chairmanship, can push ASEAN back from "an object competed for" to "a party that sets the rules." The point of a bridge is not its width. It is who holds the blueprint.

## Further reading

**Free Trade Agreement (FTA):** A treaty among two or more economies to cut or remove tariffs and harmonise rules of origin and trade; here, the bloc-to-bloc negotiation between the ten ASEAN states and the twenty-seven EU members.

**Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA):** ASEAN's first region-wide governance pact on the digital economy, covering digital trade, cross-border e-commerce, cybersecurity, digital identity and data flows, originally slated for signing at the November 2026 Manila summit.

**Friend-shoring:** An industrial policy that shifts supply chains from "cost-first" to "politically and security-trusted allies," now a core lens through which the US and Europe rewire trade with Southeast Asia.

# 从"守望相助"到"走进主流"：第十一届世界华侨华人社团联谊大会的变与常

第十一届世界华侨华人社团联谊大会7月14日在北京（Beijing）闭幕，汇聚全球140个国家和地区、400余位华侨华人社团负责人。大会以"凝侨心、聚侨力、谋发展"为主题，但真正值得咀嚼的，是海外侨社正在发生的两重转变——功能的升级与代际的交接。

## 从"守望相助"到"走进主流"

传统侨团的首要功能是互助。初到异乡的侨胞需要接济、翻译，红白事需要照应，这种"守望相助"的逻辑，源于早期移民单打独斗的脆弱。但本届大会勾勒的新方向，是让侨团从"侨胞身边"走向"主流之间"。

据中国新闻网报道，坦桑尼亚江苏商会会长张路在主题论坛上谈到，侨团应鼓励青年侨胞融入主流社会，主动创造交流机会，通过邀请当地各界人士赴华参访、参与文化活动来增进彼此理解。这已不是简单的乡情联谊，而是把侨团变成了中外民间的"接口"。

柬埔寨柬华理事总会秘书长钟耀辉的介绍更具体：他们牵线当地的华文学校与中国院校合作，把中文课程与电子商务、旅游服务等职业教育结合，让青年侨胞在学中文的同时掌握职业技能。侨团的服务半径，正在从"同乡互助"扩展到"能力共建"。

## 老侨与新侨：一场静默的交接

代际传承是本届大会被反复提及的词。资深侨领代表着"创会初心"——守望相助、情系桑梓；而新生代侨青被描述为"中坚力量"：思维活跃、视野国际化、专业能力突出。

据中国新闻网报道，汤加华侨华人联合会会长郭忠兴是该国首位"90后"会长，他把中国熊猫花车引入汤加"海拉拉节"，用新媒体助推两国文旅交流；马来西亚（Malaysia）留华同学会会长林国元组织华裔青少年参加"中国寻根之旅"冬夏令营；缅甸中华总商会青年在2025年3月缅甸7.9级地震中，为中国救援队担任翻译和向导。新老之间的交接，是具体的接班，而非空泛的口号。

但也需冷静：代际传承能否真正落地，取决于青年是否拥有对等的话语权与资源，而非只是被请上台、合影完毕便退场的"新面孔"。

## "根、魂、梦"：国家叙事下的侨社

大会开幕时，中央统战部部长李干杰强调海外侨胞要牢记"根、魂、梦"。这套叙事把侨社定位为"连接海内外的关键桥梁"与"中国式现代化的重要参与者"。

从国家视角看，6000余万海外侨胞分布在近200个国家和地区，是全球数万家侨社的"宝贵资源"。但叙事与在地现实之间总有张力：侨胞既是中华文化的传承者，也是住在国的公民；既要"融通中外"，也要避免被单一期待为"治外忠诚"的符号。

本届大会表彰的十家"华社之光"涵盖亚、非、欧、美——马来西亚宗乡青联合总会、印尼（Indonesia）中华总商会、泰国（Thailand）潮州会馆等——恰好说明侨社的活力来自在地深耕，而非单向的乡愁。

## 结语

第十一届大会落幕，代表们分赴浙江、福建、河南、新疆考察。一次大会不会改变什么，但它像一面镜子：照见海外侨社正从互助型同乡网络，转向专业化的民间外交平台；也照见新老两代如何在传承中试探着交接。

对东南亚（Southeast Asia）研究而言，值得追问的是——当"走进主流"成为共识，那些长期在族裔边界内运转的侨团，能否真正跨越边界？而代际交接之后，侨社与祖（籍）国之间的纽带，是会收紧，还是会松解成更松散、更平等的伙伴关系。

## 延伸阅读

- **世界华侨华人社团联谊大会（World Conference for Friendship of Overseas Chinese Associations）**：自2001年首次召开、每两三年一届的全球性侨团盛会，是全球华侨华人主要社团及负责人联谊交流的最高层级平台。
- **华社之光（Glory of the Chinese Community）**：自2014年第七届大会设立的巡礼活动，公开表彰在为侨服务、维护权益、传承文化、促进友好方面表现卓越的海外侨团，本届表彰十家。
- **中国寻根之旅（Root-Seeking Journey）**：由侨联等机构组织的华裔青少年夏冬令营，通过文化体验与探亲式参访，帮助海外出生的华裔新生代建立与中华文化的情感联结。

# From Mutual Aid to the Mainstream: What the 11th World Congress of Overseas Chinese Associations Reveals About a Shifting Diaspora

The 11th World Congress of Overseas Chinese Associations closed in Beijing on 14 July 2026, gathering more than 400 community leaders from 140 countries and regions. Its theme, "Unite hearts, gather strength, pursue development," is familiar. What is worth chewing on is the two transitions underway inside the global diaspora — a functional upgrade and a generational handover.

## From Mutual Aid to the Mainstream

The primary function of traditional diaspora associations is mutual aid. New arrivals need material help, translation, and support through weddings and funerals; the logic of "watching over and helping one another" grew out of the vulnerability of early migrants labouring alone. But the direction sketched at this congress is for associations to move from "beside the compatriots" to "between the mainstream."

According to China News Service, Zhang Lu, head of the Tanzania Jiangsu Chamber of Commerce, told the forum that associations should encourage young overseas Chinese to integrate into mainstream society, proactively create exchange opportunities, and deepen mutual understanding by inviting local figures to visit China and join cultural activities. This is no longer simple hometown fellowship; it turns the association into an interface between Chinese and foreign civil societies.

Cambodia's Secretary-General Zhong Yaohui was more concrete: his federation links local Chinese schools with Chinese institutions, combining Chinese-language courses with vocational training in e-commerce and tourism so that young compatriots acquire job skills while learning the language. The service radius of these associations is expanding from "helping fellow townsmen" to "co-building capacity."

## Old Guard, New Force: A Quiet Handover

Generational succession was a word repeated throughout the congress. Veteran leaders embody the "founding intent" — mutual aid and loyalty to the homeland — while the new generation of youth leaders are described as the "backbone": quick-thinking, internationally minded, and professionally strong.

According to China News Service, Guo Zhongxing, head of the Tonga Overseas Chinese Federation, is the country's first post-90s president; he brought a Chinese panda float to Tonga's Heilala Festival and uses new media to boost cultural-tourism exchange between the two countries. Lim Kok Yuan of the Malaysian Alumni Association organises "Root-Seeking Journey" camps for Chinese-heritage youth, while young members of Myanmar's Chinese chamber served as interpreters and guides for Chinese rescue teams after the March 2025 earthquake. The handover is concrete succession, not empty sloganeering.

Yet a cool head is needed: whether generational succession truly takes root depends on whether youth gain commensurate voice and resources, rather than being merely "new faces" invited on stage for a photo.

## "Roots, Soul, Dream": The State Narrative

At the opening, Li Ganjie, head of the United Front Work Department, urged overseas Chinese to remember their "roots, soul, and dream." This framing positions diaspora associations as "a key bridge connecting home and abroad" and "important participants in Chinese modernisation."

From the state's perspective, more than 60 million overseas Chinese live across nearly 200 countries and regions, a "precious resource" behind tens of thousands of associations worldwide. But tension always sits between the narrative and on-the-ground reality: compatriots are both inheritors of Chinese culture and citizens of their countries of residence; they must "bridge China and the world" without being reduced to a symbol of extraterritorial loyalty.

The ten "Glory of the Chinese Community" honourees — spanning Asia, Africa, Europe and the Americas, including Malaysia's Federation of CLC Youth, Indonesia's Chinese Chamber of Commerce, and Thailand's Teochew Association — show precisely that diaspora vitality comes from local rootedness, not one-way nostalgia.

## Conclusion

When the congress closed, delegates fanned out to Zhejiang, Fujian, Henan and Xinjiang. A single congress changes little, but it is a mirror: it reflects a diaspora shifting from mutual-aid hometown networks toward professionalised people-to-people diplomacy, and an older and younger generation feeling their way through a handover.

For Southeast Asian studies, the question worth asking is this — now that "entering the mainstream" has become consensus, can associations long confined to ethnic boundaries truly cross them? And after the generational handover, will the tie between diaspora associations and the ancestral country tighten, or loosen into a more diluted, more equal partnership?

## Further reading

- **World Conference for Friendship of Overseas Chinese Associations**: A global gathering of overseas Chinese community organisations first convened in 2001 and held roughly every two to three years; it is the highest-level platform for networking among leading diaspora associations and their leaders.
- **Glory of the Chinese Community (Huashe zhi Guang)**: An honour roll launched at the 7th congress in 2014, publicly recognising overseas associations that excel in serving compatriots, protecting rights, preserving culture, and promoting friendship; ten were honoured this year.
- **Root-Seeking Journey (Xungen zhi Lü)**: Summer and winter camps organised by the All-China Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese and others, using cultural immersion and homeland visits to help overseas-born youth build an emotional bond with Chinese culture.

# 一颗榴莲的双重棋局：越南对华出口破12亿新元背后的产业链逻辑

越南海关总署最新数据显示，2026年上半年，越南对中国的榴莲出口额达9.88亿美元（约合70.9亿元人民币、12.65亿新元），同比增长43.4%，占越南对华果蔬出口总额的48.45%，超过火龙果、香蕉、菠萝蜜、椰子和芒果的总和。一颗带刺的热带水果，正成为观察中越产业链与区域农业格局的一扇窗口。

## 数字背后的结构变化

今年上半年，越南对中国的水果与蔬菜出口总额达20.4亿美元（约合146亿元人民币），同比增长近25%。其中榴莲一项便占去48.45%，超越了其余主要水果的合计。这并非简单的量价齐升，而是一次结构性位移：榴莲已跃居越南对华农产品出口中单一占比最大的品类，中国消费者的果盘正在重塑邻国的种植版图。

## 西原主季与下半年的想象空间

越南果蔬协会秘书长邓福源（Dang Phuoc Nguyen）指出，目前越南最大的榴莲种植区西原地区（Central Highlands）开始进入主季，预计能为下半年出口提供更多货源。他表示，若能保持当前增速，全年榴莲出口量有望超过去年。这意味着上半年的高增长，或许只是全年曲线的一段序曲。

## 不止于中国：印度市场的另一扇门

越南榴莲也在向中国以外的市场扩张。上月底，印度正式批准进口越南新鲜榴莲，为这种水果打开了一个人口超过14亿的庞大市场。不过，人口众多并不意味着榴莲必然畅销——邓福源坦言，对许多印度消费者而言，榴莲仍是陌生水果，进口税、消费口味与销售渠道都还需时间适应。多元化的出口版图，从来不是一纸批文就能铺就。

## 一颗榴莲的区域政治经济学

把视野拉远，榴莲是观察中国与东南亚农业耦合的微观标本。中国不断壮大的中等收入群体与进口胃口，驱动了越南的农业专业化：河内（Hanoi）利用气候、地缘临近与中国检疫规程的可及性迅速放量。对北京而言，这是热带水果进口的多元化；对河内而言，对单一邻国出口依赖的加深则暗含集中度风险。能否把上半年的势头延续为长期红利，关键仍在于持续满足中国的食品安全与检疫标准——一颗榴莲的甜蜜，最终要靠标准与信任来保鲜。

## 延伸阅读
**西原地区（Central Highlands）**：越南中南部高原，气候适宜，是全国最大的榴莲、咖啡等经济作物种植区。
**区域全面经济伙伴关系协定（RCEP）**：涵盖中国与东盟等15国的大型自贸安排，降低了区域内农产品贸易壁垒。
**农产品出口集中度（Export concentration）**：一国对某单一市场或品类依赖度过高的结构性风险，易受需求与检疫政策波动冲击。

# A Durian's Two Chessboards: The Industrial Logic Behind Vietnam's S$1.27bn Durian Exports to China

Data from Vietnam's General Department of Customs show that in the first half of 2026, Vietnam's durian exports to China reached US$988 million (roughly S$1.265 billion, about RMB 7.1 billion), up 43.4% year on year, accounting for 48.45% of Vietnam's fruit-and-vegetable exports to China — more than the combined total of dragon fruit, banana, jackfruit, coconut and mango. A spiky tropical fruit has become a window onto the China–Vietnam supply chain and the region's agricultural map.

## The structural shift behind the numbers

In the first half of the year, Vietnam's total fruit-and-vegetable exports to China reached US$2.04 billion, up nearly 25% year on year. That durian alone takes 48.45% — surpassing the sum of other leading fruits — signals more than a rise in volume and price; it marks a structural shift. Durian has become the single largest category in Vietnam's farm exports to China, and Chinese consumers' fruit bowls are redrawing a neighbour's cultivation map.

## The Central Highlands season and H2 headroom

Dang Phuoc Nguyen, secretary-general of the Vietnam Fruit and Vegetable Association, noted that the Central Highlands (西原地区) — the country's largest durian belt — is entering its main harvest, promising more supply for the second half. If current growth holds, full-year volume could exceed last year's, suggesting the strong first half may be only a prelude to the annual curve.

## Beyond China: India's other door

Vietnamese durian is also expanding beyond China. At the end of last month India formally approved imports of Vietnamese fresh durian, opening a market of more than 1.4 billion people. Yet population alone does not guarantee popularity — Dang acknowledged that durian remains unfamiliar to many Indian consumers, and tariffs, taste and distribution channels will take time to mature. A diversified export map is never laid down by a single approval paper.

## The regional political economy of one fruit

Seen from a distance, the durian is a micro-specimen of China–Southeast Asia agricultural coupling. China's growing middle-class demand and import appetite have driven Vietnam's agricultural specialisation: Hanoi (河内) leverages climate, proximity and access to China's quarantine protocols to scale up fast. For Beijing it means diversified tropical-fruit imports; for Hanoi, deepening export reliance on a single neighbour carries concentration risk. Whether the first-half momentum becomes a durable dividend hinges on consistently meeting China's food-safety and quarantine standards — the sweetness of a durian, in the end, is preserved by standards and trust.

## Further reading
**Central Highlands**: The plateau of south-central Vietnam, climatically suited to the country's largest durian and coffee plantations.
**Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP)**: A 15-economy free-trade arrangement covering China and ASEAN that lowers intra-regional agricultural trade barriers.
**Export concentration**: The structural risk of over-reliance on a single market or product, exposing a country to demand and quarantine-policy shocks.

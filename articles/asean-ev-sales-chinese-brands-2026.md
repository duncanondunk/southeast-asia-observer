# 车轮上的角力：东盟电车销量飙升，中国品牌拿下近半壁江山

据《日经亚洲》报道，2026年第二季度，东盟主要市场汽车销量同比回升约11%，其中印尼以34%的增幅领跑，单季售出227545辆。数字背后，是一场地缘经济意义上的换岗：中国品牌在电气化赛道上把日系车企从领跑位上拉了下来。表面是销量此消彼长，深层却是区域汽车工业的权力结构在重组。

## 数字：东盟车市二季度回暖，印尼领涨34%

把镜头拉到数据本身。据《日经亚洲》统计，2026年第二季度，东盟主要市场的汽车销量同比增长约11%；印尼一骑绝尘，季度销量227545辆，同比大涨34%。普华永道东盟汽车研究中心的数据更刺眼：2026年上半年，印尼、马来西亚、泰国、越南、菲律宾、新加坡六国电气化车辆（含纯电、插混与油电混动）总销量达55.7万辆，同比上涨62%。电气化渗透率一口气提升10个百分点，来到32%。这不是温和复苏，是结构性换挡。

## 主角换人：中国品牌拿下近半壁江山

谁在驱动这场换挡？答案写在榜单上。普华永道上半年榜单显示，销量前15的品牌里，中国品牌拿下8席，合计约26.18万辆，市场份额约47%，已逼近半壁江山。比亚迪以约8.2万辆位列第二，同比增长近50%，在印尼与泰国两线开花；越南本土的 VinFast 以约12.1万辆登顶，但96%的订单来自越南国内。丰田仍以约6万辆排名第三，却主要靠油电混动撑场，纯电占比偏低。一句话：日系的根基在燃油车，中国品牌的攻势全在电气化。

## 印尼：比亚迪夺冠与「油改电」的国民刚需

印尼是这场变局最生动的注脚。印尼汽车工业协会数据显示，2026年前九月批发销量679020辆，同比增长21%；9月单月批发79540辆，同比增28.1%。比亚迪前九月售出43727辆，市场份额6.4%，排名第五、且是榜单上唯一的纯电主力。更值得玩味的是结构：据印尼国家经济委员会副主席冯慧兰披露，电动汽车在印尼汽车总销量中的占比已接近20%，政府正全力推进两轮摩托车的全面电气化——那才是2.5亿人口的真实出行刚需。中国品牌的打法，不是硬碰丰田的燃油基本盘，而是绕到「油改电」的增量战场。

## 马来西亚与泰国：补贴与门槛之间的拉锯

马来西亚与泰国则上演另一出戏。据《日经亚洲》，马来西亚电动汽车销量已历史性超过油电混动，补贴与本土组装要求把市场推过临界点；泰国则用「EV 3.0/3.5」税收减免锚定产能，吸引比亚迪、长城、上汽南下设厂。但有趣的是，也正因中国车涌入太快，两国先后收紧纯电进口条件、要求本地生产。补贴喂大市场，门槛护住本土——这中间的拉锯，恰恰说明电动化已成国家战略资产，谁都不敢轻易拱手让人。

## 越南：VinFast的本土堡垒与出海初探

越南的故事由 VinFast 独写。它上半年售出约12.1万辆电气化车型，登顶区域第一，但96%依赖本土市场，出海尚在起步——4%的海外订单散落印尼与菲律宾。越南本土组装厂8月投产，首批车辆下线，是它从「内销王」迈向「区域玩家」的第一步。VinFast 的存在提醒我们：东盟不是中国品牌的独家猎场，本土势力同样在电气化窗口期疯狂生长。

## 新加坡：成熟市场里的另一套逻辑

新加坡是区域里的异类。据《日经亚洲》，它已是东盟最成熟的电动车市场，靠的不是低价，而是高人均收入、密集充电网络与强力政策激励。在这里，特斯拉与高端车型有一席之地，竞争逻辑与印尼、越南的「国民代步」截然不同。新加坡的启示是：东盟电气化没有单一剧本，六个市场六套打法。

## 日本车企：从领跑者到追赶者

最尴尬的或许是日本车企。曾几何时，丰田、本田、铃木在东盟燃油车市场说一不二；如今在电气化赛道，丰田仅排第三且纯电薄弱，现代、起亚甚至未进前15。日本品牌并非整体溃败——燃油基本盘仍在——但「电车慢半拍」的标签一旦贴上，就意味着下一代消费者的心智正在被中国品牌抢占。追赶可以靠混动续命，却难掩战略被动。

## 结语：电动化不是终点，是区域格局的重塑

回到开头的设问：东盟车市回暖，究竟是谁的春天？数据给出的答案是，这是电气化的春天，更是中国品牌的春天。但也要清醒：渗透率32%远未到终局，日系的混动护城河、各国的补贴退坡与本地化门槛，都是变量。电动化撕开的，不只是发动机与电池的技术裂缝，更是区域汽车工业半个世纪权力格局的重新洗牌。谁笑到最后，还要看下一程。

# Wheels of contest: ASEAN's EV sales surge as Chinese brands take nearly half the market

The numbers from Nikkei Asia tell a story larger than autos. In the second quarter of 2026, vehicle sales across major ASEAN markets rose about 11% year on year, led by Indonesia's 34% jump to 227,545 units. Beneath the headline figures lies a changing of the guard: on the electrified track, Chinese automakers have pulled Japan's incumbents off the lead. What looks like a routine shift in sales is, in truth, a restructuring of the region's automotive power.

## The numbers: ASEAN auto sales rebound in Q2, Indonesia surges 34%

Start with the data. According to Nikkei Asia, ASEAN major markets grew roughly 11% year on year in Q2 2026, with Indonesia surging 34% to 227,545 vehicles. PwC's ASEAN automotive research centre makes the picture sharper: in the first half of 2026, electrified vehicle sales (BEV, PHEV and HEV) across Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines and Singapore reached 557,000 units, up 62% year on year. Electrification penetration jumped 10 percentage points to 32%. This is not a gentle recovery; it is a structural gear change.

## A changing of the guard: Chinese brands take nearly half the market

Who is driving the shift? The answer is on the leaderboard. PwC's H1 ranking shows Chinese brands occupying 8 of the top 15, combining for roughly 261,790 units and about 47% of the market — already close to half. BYD, at about 82,000 units, ranked second with nearly 50% growth, winning in both Indonesia and Thailand. Vietnam's VinFast topped the table at about 121,000 units, yet 96% of its orders came from domestic Vietnam. Toyota held third at roughly 60,000, carried mostly by hybrids with a thin BEV share. In one line: Japan's strength is in combustion; China's assault is entirely electric.

## Indonesia: BYD's lead and the push to electrify two-wheelers

Indonesia is the liveliest footnote to this transformation. Data from the Indonesian Automotive Industry Association (Gaikindo) shows wholesale volume of 679,020 units in the first nine months of 2026, up 21% year on year; September alone reached 79,540, up 28.1%. BYD sold 43,727 units in the period — a 6.4% share, fifth overall, and the only pure-EV force in the top tier. The more telling detail is structural: Mari Pangestu, Indonesia's deputy minister for international trade and multilateral cooperation, noted that EVs already approach 20% of total vehicle sales, and the government is pushing to electrify two-wheelers — the real mobility need of a 250-million population. Chinese brands are not attacking Toyota's combustion base head-on; they are flanking it through the "fuel-to-electric" incremental battleground.

## Malaysia and Thailand: a tug-of-war between subsidies and barriers

Malaysia and Thailand stage a different play. Per Nikkei Asia, Malaysia's EV sales have historically surpassed those of hybrids, with subsidies and local-assembly rules pushing the market past a tipping point; Thailand anchors capacity with its "EV 3.0/3.5" tax breaks, drawing BYD, Great Wall and SAIC south to build plants. Yet precisely because Chinese cars arrived too fast, both countries tightened pure-EV import conditions and demanded local production. Subsidies grow the market; barriers shield the homeland. The tug-of-war shows electrification has become a strategic national asset no one will readily hand away.

## Vietnam: VinFast's home fortress and first steps abroad

Vietnam's story is written by VinFast alone. Its roughly 121,000 electrified units in H1 made it the regional number one, but 96% depended on the domestic market, with overseas orders — a mere 4% — scattered across Indonesia and the Philippines. A local assembly plant that began output in August, with the first vehicles rolling off the line, marks its first step from "domestic champion" toward "regional player." VinFast is a reminder that ASEAN is not China's exclusive hunting ground; local forces are also rushing the electrification window.

## Singapore: a different logic in a mature market

Singapore is the regional outlier. According to Nikkei Asia, it is already ASEAN's most mature EV market, powered not by low prices but by high per-capita income, a dense charging network and strong policy incentives. There, Tesla and premium models hold ground, and the competitive logic differs sharply from the "people's commuter" battles of Indonesia and Vietnam. Singapore's lesson: there is no single script for ASEAN electrification — six markets, six playbooks.

## Japanese automakers: from front-runner to follower

The most awkward position may belong to Japan's automakers. Not long ago Toyota, Honda and Suzuki dictated terms in ASEAN combustion markets; today, on the electrified track, Toyota ranks only third with a thin BEV presence, and Hyundai and Kia do not even make the top 15. Japanese brands have not collapsed — the combustion base endures — but the "half-a-beat-slow on EVs" label, once attached, means the next generation of consumers is being won over by Chinese brands. Hybrids can buy time; they cannot hide strategic passivity.

## The bottom line: electrification is reshaping the regional order

Return to the opening question: whose spring is this ASEAN recovery? The data answers — it is the spring of electrification, and of Chinese brands. But sobriety is warranted: 32% penetration is far from the endgame, and Japan's hybrid moat, subsidy rollbacks and localization barriers are all variables. What electrification has cracked open is not merely a technological seam between engine and battery, but a half-century reordering of the region's automotive power. Who laughs last depends on the next stretch of the road.

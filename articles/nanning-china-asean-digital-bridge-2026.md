# 南宁借服贸会搭数字桥：互联互通从硬件走向规则

2026年中国国际服务贸易交易会期间，一场以“数聚邕城·智联东盟”为主题的南宁数字出海推介活动在北京举行。据中国侨网报道，广西壮族自治区政府副秘书长郑愿东在活动上邀请各国企业赴南宁考察，在数字内容、数字技术、跨境服务等领域寻找商机；南宁市副市长侯劲则介绍，南宁正全力打造“南宁渠道”，搭建中国—东盟数字合作桥梁。作为西部陆海新通道的关键节点，南宁的区位叙事近年来从“面向东盟的窗口”升级为“国内国际双循环”的交汇点。在平陆运河、中国—东盟信息港等硬件枢纽之外，南宁正试图把面向东盟的连接从港口与班列，延伸到数据、平台与规则。

## 服贸会上的“邕城声音”

服贸会是服务贸易与数字规则的展示窗口。与侧重货物贸易的广交会不同，服贸会聚焦跨境服务、数字贸易与知识产权，恰好对应中国—东盟合作中增长最快的部分。东盟拥有逾6亿人口、过半为30岁以下的青年，是一个移动优先、平台驱动的消费市场，数字服务贸易的增量空间巨大，也因此成为各方争夺的增量腹地。南宁选择在此平台主动发声，本身释放信号：它不愿只做中国—东盟博览会的“会场”，而想成为数字服务的“出海口”。活动中，广西山海星辰传媒展示了“一站式AI内容生产平台Nova AI”等成果，本土企业也与沙特、巴基斯坦、阿塞拜疆客商达成初步合作意向。

## 从“办展会”到“建平台”

南宁的角色正在升级。作为东博会永久举办地，南宁过去十余年的城市叙事围绕“办展会、迎宾客”；如今“南宁渠道”的提法，意味着从一次性活动转向常态化机制——跨境金融、智慧物流、数字通关等服务体系被持续完善，为“经桂出海”提供支撑。东博会本身也在数字化转型，云上东博、线上洽谈等模块让“展会流量”沉淀为“平台留存”，这正是“场所”向“平台”跃迁的微观注脚。这种从“场所”到“平台”的转变，是地方城市把区位优势制度化的典型路径，也折射出广西借数字贸易重塑面向东盟枢纽地位的意图。

## 数字合作的几只抓手

中国—东盟数字合作已有清晰抓手。其一是跨境电商，南宁获批跨境电商综试区后，成为不少国货与东盟特色商品双向流通的节点；其二是算力与数据中心，中国—东盟信息港在南宁布局，作为“数字丝绸之路”面向东盟的落子，试图把西部算力优势转化为区域服务能力；其三是跨境支付与数字金融，人民币跨境结算与本地钱包互联互通逐步铺开，降低了中小商户的汇兑门槛；其四是智慧城市，南宁与东盟城市在政务、交通、口岸数字化上经验可复制。多条线并行，构成“数字桥”的实质内容。

## 数据跨境流动：最核心的难题

抓手易列，规则难立。数字合作真正的硬骨头是数据跨境流动：何种数据可出境、经何种安全评估、在何处存储，各国标准不一。中国已设立数据领域国际合作试点城市，南宁是首批之一，正是要为跨境数据流动探索“白名单”与“安全港”机制，其背后是《数据安全法》《个人信息保护法》构建的出境合规框架。但东盟十国数字治理水平参差，从新加坡的成熟立法到部分国家的空白，统一的区域数据规则远未成型。没有可预期的合规框架，算力与平台再多，也难沉淀为可持续的贸易流量。

## 与新加坡、马来西亚数字经济的竞合

南宁不是唯一的玩家。新加坡凭借法治、资本与英语优势，长期是中国—东盟数字服务的“总部节点”；马来西亚则在半导体、数据中心与数字政府上积累深厚，柔佛借助毗邻新加坡的区位，正以经济特区形态承接 hyperscale 算力基建。南宁的优势在“近”与“通”——地理距离近、面向东盟的政策通道成熟，且背靠中国完整的数字产业供应链。竞合之中，南宁更可能扮演“面向东盟的应用落地与中转”角色，而非与新加坡争夺高端总部功能；三方各有生态位，未必是零和。

## 东盟的数字主权顾虑

合作的另一面是顾虑。东盟国家对数据主权高度敏感：印尼、越南等国已出台数据本地化要求，并辅以数字税等工具，担忧数字平台沦为外部势力获取信息与影响舆论的渠道。政局不稳的泰国、局势复杂的缅甸，更把数据治理视作安全议题。南宁推动数字桥，若只谈商业效率而回避主权关切，易被解读为“数字影响力延伸”。如何在技术输出与主权尊重之间找平衡，决定合作能否走深，也考验中方“不干涉”原则在数字经济时代的成色。

## 华侨华商网络：落地的“中介层”

在官方平台之外，华侨华人网络是数字落地不可或缺的“中介层”。东南亚华商熟悉本地语言、法规与市场缝隙，是中国数字平台、内容产品进入东盟的天然渠道；东盟的华文媒体与短视频创作者，则把中国微短剧、电商直播等新业态翻译成本地可感的内容。反过来，东盟华商也借中国电商与支付体系把特色商品卖向全球。南宁本身侨乡底色不深，但广西毗邻粤港澳，可借大湾区侨商网络补位。这种“制度平台+商业网络”的双层结构，往往比单纯政府推动更经得起市场检验。

## 从硬件互联互通走向规则互联互通

互联互通正在换挡。过去十余年，中国与东盟的连接以铁路、港口、运河等硬件为主；平陆运河、中老铁路是这一阶段的标志，西部陆海新通道则把内陆与海岸连成一张网。而当硬件骨架渐成，合作的瓶颈转向规则、标准与数据——谁来定标准，谁就掌握下一阶段的主动权。南宁借服贸会搭数字桥，表面是城市营销，深层是中国—东盟关系从“通物流”迈向“通规则”的缩影。桥搭起来不难，难的是让两边的车都愿意、也都敢开过去；规则的桥，比钢筋水泥的桥更难合龙。

## 延伸阅读

**中国—东盟信息港（China–ASEAN Information Harbor）**：以广西南宁为核心、面向东盟的数字经济合作载体，涵盖大数据、云计算、跨境通信与智慧城市合作，是中国—东盟数字互联互通的重要基础设施。

**数据领域国际合作试点城市（Pilot City for International Data Cooperation）**：中国为探索数据跨境安全有序流动而设立的城市试点，南宁为首批入选城市之一，重点试验跨境数据分类管理与合规出境机制。

**南宁渠道（Nanning Channel）**：南宁市提出的面向东盟合作机制品牌，依托东博会永久举办地与区位优势，整合展会、跨境金融、数字通关等职能，打造中国—东盟常态化合作平台。

# Nanning's Digital Bridge at CIFTIS: From Hardware to Rules in China–ASEAN Connectivity

During the 2026 China International Fair for Trade in Services (CIFTIS), Nanning held a "Digital Going-Global" promotion in Beijing under the theme "Data Gathers in Yongcheng, Intelligence Links ASEAN." According to ChinaQW, Zheng Yuandong, deputy secretary-general of the Guangxi regional government, invited foreign firms to explore opportunities in Nanning across digital content, technology and cross-border services; Hou Jin, Nanning's vice mayor, said the city is building the "Nanning Channel" to bridge China–ASEAN digital cooperation. As a key node of the New Western Land–Sea Corridor, Nanning's location narrative has shifted in recent years from "window on ASEAN" to a junction of the "dual circulation" strategy. Beyond hardware hubs like the Pinglu Canal and the China–ASEAN Information Harbor, Nanning is trying to extend its ASEAN-facing links from ports and freight trains to data, platforms and rules.

## A "Yongcheng voice" at CIFTIS

CIFTIS is a window onto services trade and digital rules. Unlike the Canton Fair, which centres on goods, CIFTIS highlights cross-border services, digital trade and intellectual property—precisely the fastest-growing parts of China–ASEAN cooperation. ASEAN has over 600 million people, more than half under 30, a mobile-first, platform-driven consumer market with vast headroom in digital services trade, which is exactly why it has become contested incremental hinterland. That Nanning chose to speak up here signals intent: it does not want merely to host the China–ASEAN Expo but to become a digital-services "export port." At the event, Guangxi Sun-Hill Media showcased its "one-stop AI content platform Nova AI," and local firms reached preliminary cooperation deals with buyers from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Azerbaijan.

## From "hosting expos" to "building platforms"

Nanning's role is upgrading. As the permanent home of the China–ASEAN Expo, the city's narrative for over a decade revolved around hosting events and welcoming guests. The "Nanning Channel" framing shifts this from one-off gatherings to a standing mechanism—cross-border finance, smart logistics and digital customs systems are being built up to support "going global via Guangxi." The Expo itself is going digital: cloud editions and online matchmaking turn "event traffic" into "platform retention," a microcosm of the venue-to-platform leap. This move from venue to platform is a textbook case of a local city institutionalising its geographic advantage, and reflects Guangxi's ambition to remake its ASEAN-facing hub through digital trade.

## Concrete handles of digital cooperation

China–ASEAN digital cooperation already has clear handles. First, cross-border e-commerce: Nanning's cross-border e-commerce pilot zone has become a node for two-way flows of Chinese goods and ASEAN specialties. Second, computing power and data centres: the China–ASEAN Information Harbor, anchored in Nanning as a piece of the "Digital Silk Road" facing ASEAN, seeks to convert western China's compute advantage into regional service capacity. Third, cross-border payment and digital finance, where RMB settlement and local-wallet interoperability are expanding, lowering exchange barriers for small merchants. Fourth, smart cities, with replicable experience in e-governance, transport and port digitalisation. Multiple threads form the substance of the "digital bridge."

## Data flows: the hardest knot

Handles are easy to list; rules are hard to set. The real hard bone of digital cooperation is cross-border data flow: which data may leave, under what security review, and where it is stored—standards differ across countries. China has designated pilot cities for international data cooperation, with Nanning among the first, precisely to explore "whitelists" and "safe harbours" for data outflow, backed by the outbound-transfer framework of its Data Security Law and Personal Information Protection Law. But ASEAN's ten members vary widely in digital governance—from Singapore's mature legislation to gaps in others—and a unified regional data regime is far off. Without a predictable compliance framework, computing power and platforms cannot settle into durable trade flows.

## Competing and cooperating with Singapore and Malaysia

Nanning is not the only player. Singapore, with rule of law, capital and English, has long been the "headquarters node" of China–ASEAN digital services; Malaysia has deep strengths in semiconductors, data centres and digital government, with Johor, leveraging its position next to Singapore, absorbing hyperscale compute infrastructure through special-economic-zone forms. Nanning's edge is proximity and access—close geography and a mature policy corridor to ASEAN, backed by China's complete digital supply chain. In this rivalry, Nanning is more likely to be an "application-landing and transit hub" for ASEAN than to contest Singapore's high-end headquarters functions; the three occupy distinct niches rather than a zero-sum game.

## ASEAN's data-sovereignty anxieties

Cooperation has its mirror image in anxiety. ASEAN states are acutely sensitive to data sovereignty: Indonesia and Vietnam have enacted data-localisation requirements, backed by instruments like digital taxes, wary that digital platforms become channels for external information-gathering and influence. Politically unstable Thailand and conflict-ridden Myanmar treat data governance as a security matter. If Nanning's digital bridge talks only of commercial efficiency while dodging sovereignty concerns, it risks being read as "digital influence projection." Balancing technology export with respect for sovereignty decides how deep cooperation can go, and tests the mettle of China's "non-interference" principle in the digital age.

## The overseas Chinese as a "middle layer"

Beyond official platforms, overseas Chinese networks are an indispensable middle layer for digital landing. Southeast Asian Chinese businesses know local languages, regulations and market niches, serving as natural channels for Chinese platforms and content to enter ASEAN; the region's Chinese-language media and short-video creators translate China's micro-dramas and live-commerce formats into locally legible content. Conversely, ASEAN Chinese merchants use Chinese e-commerce and payment systems to sell regional goods worldwide. Nanning itself is not a classic qiaoxiang, but Guangxi borders the Greater Bay Area and can tap its diaspora business networks. This dual "institutional platform plus commercial network" structure often outlasts purely state-driven pushes.

## From hardware to rules in connectivity

Connectivity is shifting gears. For over a decade, China–ASEAN links centred on hardware—railways, ports, canals. The Pinglu Canal and the Laos–China railway mark that phase, and the New Western Land–Sea Corridor knits inland and coast into one network. As the hardware skeleton takes shape, the bottleneck moves to rules, standards and data—whoever sets the standards holds the initiative in the next phase. Nanning's digital bridge at CIFTIS looks like city marketing, but points to a deeper shift: China–ASEAN ties moving from "moving goods" to "moving rules." Building the bridge is easy; getting both sides' traffic to cross—willingly and safely—is the hard part. A rules bridge is harder to close than one of steel and concrete.

**Pilot City for International Data Cooperation**: A Chinese designation for cities experimenting with safe, orderly cross-border data flow; Nanning is among the first, testing classified data management and compliant outbound transfer.

**Nanning Channel**: Nanning's branded mechanism for ASEAN-facing cooperation, built on the Expo's permanent venue and location, integrating expos, cross-border finance and digital customs into a standing platform.

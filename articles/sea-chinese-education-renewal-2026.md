# 东南亚华文教育的守脉与向新：从南洋学堂到「中文+职业技能」

在马来西亚新纪元大学学院的校园里，校长莫顺宗常对学生说，先辈下南洋，最怕的是后人忘记自己从哪来。这句话道出东南亚华文教育百余年的底色：它不只是一门语言课，更是一条身份认同的命脉。

## 守脉：不识字也要学中文

马来西亚华文教育的发展并非一路坦途。早年华校几经低谷，林连玉、沈慕羽等华教先贤为守护华文教育身陷囹圄，却始终未改其志。许多普通华侨倾尽毕生积蓄捐助华校，只说自己有幸为华文教育出一份力。

正是这种代际接力，让马来西亚的华文教育"一直有花一直开"。如今，马来西亚的华文小学早已不只是华裔学子的课堂，非华裔学生比例持续上升。先辈将学堂定名为"华文学校"而非"华人学校"，不以民族、血统设边界，让华文教育成为包容各族的公共教育，也成了民心相通、文化交融的纽带。

## 破圈：从学中文到用中文立身

身为校长，莫顺宗不断争取让更多专业实现中文授课，并积极拓展与中国高校的联合培养机制。他试图打破单一的语言教学模式，让学生不只"学中文"，更能"用中文求学、以中文立身"。

早年华文小学几乎清一色是华裔学子，如今华校的非华裔面孔越来越多。在莫顺宗看来，这恰恰说明华文教育的生命力——它不靠血缘维系，而靠使用价值延续。

## 向新：当中文成为谋生的本领

在柬埔寨，华文教育的迭代走得更远。柬华理事总会秘书长钟耀辉介绍，从旧时的中文补习，到如今的"中文+职业技能"融合教育，海外华文教育已不仅是对乡愁的寄托，更成为青年掌握谋生本领的重要途径。

柬华理事总会联动中国职教资源，共建柬华应用科技大学，并推动多所华校转型升级，开设中文与电工、酒店管理、跨境电商、信息技术等结合的专业课程。有学生学起电商术语和跨境直播操作，计划回乡开办华文技能课堂；也有柬埔寨工人通过学习逐渐晋升为技术骨干，收入翻倍。

"从前，中文是海外游子不忘来路的乡愁；如今，中文是南洋青年奔赴山海的出路。"钟耀辉说。

## 延伸阅读

**华文学校（Chinese-medium school）**：以华语为教学语言的学校，东南亚多国华社自二十世纪初兴办，是海外华文教育的基层载体。

**柬华应用科技大学（Cambodian Applied Science and Technology University）**：由柬华理事总会联合中国职教资源共建，主打"中文+职业技能"融合培养。

**二十四节令鼓（24 Festive Drums）**：马来西亚华人创新的传统鼓乐，已入编马来西亚教育部课程，是华文教育文化输出的典型案例。

# The Continuity and Renewal of Chinese-Language Education in Southeast Asia: From Nanyang Classrooms to "Chinese Plus Vocational Skills"

On the campus of New Era University College in Malaysia, its president, Mok Soon Chong, often tells students that when the early Chinese migrants left their homeland, what they feared most was that later generations would forget where they came from. The line captures the century-long essence of Chinese-language education in Southeast Asia: more than a language course, it is a lifeline of identity.

## Holding the Line: Learning Chinese Even Without Literacy

The development of Chinese education in Malaysia was never smooth. In its early years, Chinese schools weathered repeated downturns. Pioneers of Chinese education such as Lim Lian Geok and Sim Mow Yu were imprisoned for defending the mother tongue yet never wavered. Countless ordinary overseas Chinese donated their life savings to Chinese schools, insisting only that they were fortunate to contribute.

It is precisely this intergenerational relay that has kept Malaysian Chinese education "ever blooming." Today, Chinese primary schools are no longer classrooms for ethnic Chinese alone; the share of non-Chinese students keeps rising. The forebears named these institutions "Chinese schools" rather than "ethnic Chinese schools," refusing to draw boundaries by ethnicity or bloodline, so that Chinese education became public education open to all communities—and a bridge of people-to-people and cultural exchange.

## Breaking Out: From Studying Chinese to Living by It

As president, Mok has kept pushing to deliver more programmes in the Chinese language and to expand joint-training mechanisms with universities in China. He aims to break the single-track language model, so students do more than "study Chinese"—they "learn through Chinese and stand by Chinese."

Decades ago, Chinese primary schools were almost entirely ethnic Chinese. Today, non-Chinese faces are increasingly common on campus. To Mok, this is exactly proof of the vitality of Chinese education: it endures not through blood ties but through practical value.

## Turning New: When Chinese Becomes a Livelihood

In Cambodia, the reinvention of Chinese education has gone further. Chong Yao Hui, secretary-general of the Cambodian Chinese Association, notes that from old-style Chinese tutoring to today's "Chinese plus vocational skills" model, overseas Chinese education is no longer merely a sentimental attachment to homeland—it has become a vital pathway for youth to acquire employable skills.

The association has partnered with Chinese vocational-education resources to co-build the Cambodian Applied Science and Technology University, and has upgraded several Chinese schools by adding programmes that combine Chinese with electrical work, hotel management, cross-border e-commerce and information technology. One student picked up e-commerce terms and cross-border livestreaming to plan a Chinese-language skills class back home; another Cambodian worker rose to become a technical backbone and doubled his income.

"Once, Chinese was the nostalgia of overseas travelers who remembered their roots; today, Chinese is the road that young Nanyang people take to the wider world," Chong says.

## Further reading

**Chinese-medium school**: A school using Mandarin as the language of instruction, pioneered by Southeast Asian Chinese communities since the early 20th century and the grassroots carrier of overseas Chinese education.

**Cambodian Applied Science and Technology University**: Co-built by the Cambodian Chinese Association with Chinese vocational-education partners, centered on "Chinese plus vocational skills" training.

**24 Festive Drums (二十四节令鼓)**: A drum ensemble invented by Malaysian Chinese, now in Malaysia's national school curriculum—a textbook case of cultural export from Chinese education.

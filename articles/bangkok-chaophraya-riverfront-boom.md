# 湄南河的第二春：当曼谷把河畔变成"东方滨海湾"

继总投资约 600 亿泰铢（约 120 亿元人民币）的暹罗天地（ICONSIAM）之后，曼谷滨河地带正迎来第二波开发潮。资产世界公司（AWC）、正大集团与多家国际酒店品牌沿湄南河落子，试图把这条河复制成"泰国（Thailand）的滨海湾"。但沿河土地几近见底，文保与商业、旅游与生活的拉扯，也被一并推到台前。

## 从一座商场到一条经济带

暹罗天地由暹罗皮瓦集团、MQDC 与正大集团联合投资，占地 55 莱，开业后带动 Charoen Nakhon 沿线土地价格由每平方哇约 25 万泰铢涨至 70 万泰铢，沿河商业增长超过 60%，周边一公里内新增房地产项目逾 60 个。它被视为泰国《湄南河愿景总体规划》的核心支点，也让"滨河更新"从单点项目升级为城市战略。

## 第二波：夜市、缆车与《阿凡达》

这轮热度由 AWC 的河滨码头夜市（ASIATIQUE）扩建点燃。公司计划投入约 100 亿泰铢（约 20 亿元人民币）将建筑面积由 4 万平方米扩至 22.4 万平方米，规模扩张约六倍；并引入《阿凡达：艾娃守护者》沉浸式体验（占地约 4000 平方米，计划 2027 年开放），其蓝海穹顶由普利兹克奖建筑师坂茂设计，原为 2025 年大阪世博会建筑、后迁建而来。配套还包括一座横跨湄南河、投资约 20 亿泰铢的环保缆车，以及携手澳大利亚 MONA 共建的沉浸式艺术馆，以缆车串联两岸。

## 酒店品牌沿河"抢滩"

高端酒店同步升温。AWC 拟投约 50 亿泰铢与万豪合作丽思卡尔顿河畔酒店（预计 2028 年）；另将"河滨花园"改造为雅典娜广场品牌超豪华酒店，投资约 29 亿泰铢、146 间客房、同样 2028 年开业，并入其连接 11 个目的地的"AWC 河畔之旅"。朗廷则联手 Rabbit Holdings，修复逾 130 年历史的海关大楼与旧邮局，以 60 亿泰铢打造曼谷首家莱佛士酒店，预计 2026 年底开业。与此同时，皇家兰花喜来登挂牌出售，业内估计成交价可达 60 至 70 亿泰铢。

## 土地见底之后的玩法

业内判断，湄南河沿岸已基本没有可供大规模开发的新土地，未来新增项目主要依赖历史建筑改造、资产升级与综合体更新。这意味着开发逻辑从"圈地"转向"织补"——把大皇宫、黎明寺、唐人街与当代艺术、生活方式街区缝进一条可步行的"河流路线"。AWC 此前提出的 100 层滨河综合体计划已暂缓，公司称将视全球经济与市场需求重新评估。

## 尾声：谁的城市，谁的河

曼谷想借河畔讲述一个"世界级城市"的故事，方向不无道理：新加坡滨海湾、上海外滩、伦敦南岸都证明，一条被善待的河能放大一整座城的吸引力。但河畔从来不止是土地与客流，它也是市民晨练的步道、摊贩的生计与水上的信仰。当资本把河岸一寸寸点亮，如何为普通人留下一扇看得见水的窗，才是第二春能否持久的关键。

- **暹罗天地（ICONSIAM）**：正大等联合投资、约 600 亿泰铢的滨河综合商业地标，带动两岸地价与商业跃升。

# The Chao Phraya's Second Spring: Can Bangkok Make Its River the "Marina Bay of the East"?

After ICONSIAM — a roughly 60-billion-baht (about US$1.7 billion) complex — Bangkok's riverfront is entering a second wave of development. Asset World Corp (AWC), Charoen Pokphand and several international hotel brands are staking claims along the Chao Phraya, hoping to turn the river into "Thailand's Marina Bay." But with riverfront land nearly exhausted, the tension between heritage and commerce, tourism and livelihood, is now front and centre.

## From One Mall to an Economic Belt

Developed by Siam Piwat, MQDC and Charoen Pokphand, ICONSIAM sits on 55 rai of land and lifted land prices along Charoen Nakhon from about 250,000 baht to 700,000 baht per square wa, drove riverfront commercial growth above 60%, and seeded over 60 new real-estate projects within a kilometre. It became the anchor of Thailand's "Chao Phraya River Vision" plan, upgrading waterfront renewal from a single project to urban strategy.

## Wave Two: Night Market, Cable Car and "Avatar"

The new momentum comes from AWC's expansion of the Asiatique riverfront night market. The company plans to invest about 100 billion baht (roughly US$2.8 billion) to grow floor area from 40,000 to 224,000 square metres — roughly sixfold — and to import "Avatar: The Tulkun Protector," a 4,000-square-metre immersive experience opening in 2027 inside a dome designed by Pritzker laureate Shigeru Ban, relocated from Expo 2025 Osaka. A roughly 2-billion-baht (about US$56 million) eco cable car will span the river and link to a MONA-backed immersive art museum.

## Hotel Brands Rush the Bank

Luxury hotels are heating up too. AWC plans a ~5-billion-baht (about US$140 million) Ritz-Carlton Riverside with Marriott (2028), and is converting "River Garden" into a 29-billion-baht (around US$810 million), 146-room Anantara ultra-luxury hotel (also 2028), folding it into its 11-stop "AWC River Journey." Langham, with Rabbit Holdings, is restoring the 130-year-old Customs House and old post office into Bangkok's first Raffles, at 6 billion baht (about US$170 million), targeted for late 2026. Meanwhile the Royal Orchid Sheraton is up for sale, with estimates of 6–7 billion baht (US$170–200 million).

## What Comes After the Land Runs Out

Analysts argue the riverfront has almost no large undeveloped plots left; future projects will rely on heritage retrofit, asset upgrades and mixed-use renewal. The logic shifts from "land grab" to "stitching" — weaving the Grand Palace, Wat Arun, Chinatown and contemporary art into one walkable "river route." AWC's earlier plan for a 100-storey riverfront complex has been shelved, pending global economic conditions.

## Coda: Whose City, Whose River

Bangkok wants the river to tell a "world-class city" story, and the instinct is sound: Singapore's Marina Bay, Shanghai's Bund and London's South Bank all show a well-kept river amplifies a whole city's pull. But a riverfront is never only land and footfall; it is also citizens' morning jog, vendors' livelihoods and riverside faith. As capital lights up the banks inch by inch, leaving ordinary people a window with a view of water may decide whether this second spring lasts.

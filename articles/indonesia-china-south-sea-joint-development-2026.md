# 把南海写进交易清单：印尼「共同开发」的真正考题

据《南华早报》（South China Morning Post）报道，印尼总统普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）11月20日在深圳会见中国国家主席习近平后表示，两国在南海「已经找到强有力的合作前景」，并称双方已在共同开发领域「交换了照会」。据该报道，同一场会晤还启动了印尼与中国全面经济伙伴关系协定谈判，并伴以总额约718亿美元（约517亿元人民币）的四项合作安排。把海域合作与经贸大单放进同一份外交成果单，意图很清楚：雅加达正在把南海议题从单纯的主权宣示，改写成一笔可以谈判、可以分期的生意。

点评：一句「交换照会」比「加强合作」更值得注意。前者是文书动作，意味着有文本、有经办部门、有下一步；但文书不等于条约，更不等于划界。真正的问题不是印尼有没有转向，而是它准备把转向写到什么程度。

## 换文不是条约：先把法律位置摆正

外交照会可以是共识的载体，在某些情况下也构成国际协议，但它的法律分量取决于内容：写了哪些海域、哪些活动、谁来执行。普拉博沃所说的是「共同开发领域的换文」，不是承认中国对南海的主张，也不是承认中国对相关海域拥有主权。两件事必须分开。印尼长期以「非声索国」自居，拒绝承认中国的九段线主张，这一立场并未在11月20日的会晤后被宣布修改。

微妙之处在于，不承认主张与接受合作项目可以在同一份政策里并存。雅加达的逻辑是：既然争议短期无法靠划界解决，就不如先把资源开发、渔业管理和海上执法的技术性安排做起来。点评：这种务实在理论上说得通，在实践里却极易被误读——对外发出「共同开发」信号，国内选民听到的却可能是「主权让步」。政治成本不会写在照会里，却会写进下一轮选举。

## 718亿美元的价签：经济账压过法律账

据《南华早报》另一篇报道，11月20日举行的印尼—中国商业论坛签下35项商业承诺，总额超过150亿美元（约1080亿元人民币）。四项战略合作安排的名义价值约718亿美元（约517亿元人民币），包括首批10座智慧城市约500亿美元（约3600亿元人民币）、与海信合作约100亿美元（约720亿元人民币）、基础设施融资框架约100亿美元（约720亿元人民币），以及煤制乙醇项目约18亿美元（约130亿元人民币）。两国2025年贸易额约1540亿美元（约1109亿元人民币），雅加达希望把这份体量转化为更高附加值的产业合作。

这些数字不能按现金到账来理解。它们混合了意向书、融资框架和已签约项目，落地取决于融资决定、审批与合同。但数字本身就是信号：当印尼拿出如此规模的产业清单时，南海共同开发就不再是孤立的外交动作，而是整套经济议程的配套件。用一个海上项目去给陆上的工业园、铁路和数据中心上保险，这是普拉博沃政府较之前几届更明显的交易风格。

## 纳土纳的底线：开发可以谈，权利不能含糊

争议的核心在纳土纳群岛附近海域。中国的九段线与印尼依据《联合国海洋法公约》享有的专属经济区权利在那里重叠。2024年11月两国联合声明出现「在主张重叠海域共同开发」的措辞，在印尼国内引发强烈质疑，外界担心这等于变相承认争议主张。印尼外交部随后重申不承认中国的南海主张，普拉博沃也表态将「始终维护我们的主权」。

因此，这次的考验不是口头重申，而是文本边界。共同开发若落在印尼专属经济区内，谁发渔业许可、谁收资源费、谁执行渔获限额、争议由谁裁决，都是不能回避的问题。对纳土纳沿海社区而言，协议不是抽象的地缘政治，而是渔船能否出海、海产能否稳定卖给加工厂、油价与补贴会不会变化的日常生活。点评：任何绕开当地渔民和地方政府的设计，都难以通过印尼国内的政治检验。

## 规则真空：缺的不是意愿，而是细则

共同开发在国际实践中并不罕见，失败原因也高度雷同：领导人达成原则共识，部门却在许可、执法和收益分配上各说各话。印尼和中国之间不缺合作意愿，缺的是一份能把意愿转化为可执行义务的文本。至少要回答七个问题：地理范围是哪里；允许哪些作业；由哪国发放许可；海上执法如何分工；收入如何分配；纠纷如何解决；环保和信息公开按什么标准。

若这些问题留白，「共同开发」就会被各自解释成不同的东西。一方可能理解为资源分成，另一方可能理解为准入便利；一方强调主权不受损，另一方强调历史权利。灰色地带看似为双方保留了面子，实际却把风险推给了执行层面——一旦发生扣押渔船或勘探纠纷，谁来灭火？这才是对雅加达外交能力的真正检验。

## 对东盟的溢出：双边交易会不会掏空行为准则

据《南华早报》早前报道，中国与东盟国家已就南海行为准则完成约77%的磋商，剩余分歧集中在约束对象等关键问题上。在这个节点推进中印尼双边共同开发，不可避免会带来外溢效应。若雅加达接受一套宽松的双边模板，其他国家会借机要求同等条件；若这套模板绕开东盟框架，区域规则就会被一份份双边合同切碎。

印尼的分量恰恰在于它不是主声索国。过去它常以维护东盟团结、反对阵营化的角色出现。如今它把海权议题纳入对中国的经济合作包，等于在考验自己的区域信誉：平衡外交若只换来一份条件不明的换文，其他东盟成员会重新计算「东盟中心性」的含金量。点评：区域秩序不是靠声明维持的，而是靠最克制的一方也不越界来维持的。

## 观察清单：从措辞走到合同

接下来的信号不在领导人讲话，而在三件小事。其一，换文内容是否公开到足以让印尼国会和公众审阅；其二，是否出现具体的招标、许可证或联合勘探合同，标明地点、作业方与收益安排；其三，国内反对声音是否被纳入政策说明，而不是被当作噪音处理。这三步如果走完，共同开发才算从口号进入治理；如果停在第一步，它就只是一次精明的外交包装。

印尼的非对齐传统不要求它在大国之间沉默，只要求它把条件写清楚。南海可以被写进交易清单，但清单必须由能被问责的条款组成。否则，今天换来的投资与项目，明天可能要用更大的主权解释成本去补账。

## 延伸阅读

**共同开发（Joint development）**：争议海域相邻国家在划界前就资源勘探、收益分配和执法安排所作的临时性合作；其效力取决于书面条款，而非政治宣示。

**专属经济区（Exclusive economic zone）**：沿海国依据《联合国海洋法公约》享有勘探、开发和管理自然资源主权权利的海域；它不等于领土主权，却构成资源管辖的核心依据。

**东盟方式（ASEAN way）**：以协商、不干涉与共识为基础的区域决策习惯；其优势是降低对抗，代价是执行松散、容易被双边安排稀释。

# Putting the South China Sea on a deal sheet: Indonesia's real joint-development test

According to the South China Morning Post, Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto said after meeting Chinese President Xi Jinping in Shenzhen on 20 November that the two countries had found "a strong future of cooperation in the South China Sea" and that notes had already been exchanged on joint development. The same meeting launched negotiations on a bilateral comprehensive economic partnership agreement and was accompanied by four cooperation packages with a headline value of about US$71.8 billion. Putting maritime cooperation and commercial deals on one diplomatic balance sheet makes the strategy legible: Jakarta is recasting the South China Sea from a declaration of sovereignty into a negotiable transaction — something that can be bargained over and delivered in instalments.

The phrase "exchange of notes" deserves more attention than another round of "strengthening cooperation". It implies a document, a responsible ministry and a next step. But a note is not a treaty, still less a boundary settlement. The question is not whether Indonesia has shifted, but how far it is willing to write that shift down.

## An exchange of notes is not a treaty

Diplomatic notes can record a shared understanding and, depending on their content, may constitute an international agreement. What matters is what they actually say: which waters, which activities, and who enforces the rules. What Prabowo described was an exchange of notes on joint development — not an acceptance of China's claims in the South China Sea, still less of Chinese sovereignty over the waters concerned. The two must be kept apart. Indonesia has long styled itself a non-claimant state and has refused to recognise China's nine-dash-line claim; nothing announced on 20 November formally changed that position.

The subtlety is that non-recognition and practical cooperation can coexist in one policy. Jakarta's reasoning is straightforward: if delimitation cannot be settled soon, start with the technical work on resource development, fisheries management and maritime enforcement. That is defensible in theory and easily misread in practice. A "joint development" signal sent outward can sound to domestic voters like a sovereignty concession. Political costs do not appear in the text of a note, but they do appear in the next election.

## The US$71.8 billion price tag

A separate South China Morning Post report says 35 business commitments worth more than US$15 billion were signed at the Indonesia–China Business Forum in Shenzhen on 20 November. The four strategic packages carry a combined indicative value of about US$71.8 billion: roughly US$50 billion for an initial ten smart cities, about US$10 billion tied to Hisense, around US$10 billion for an infrastructure financing framework, and US$1.8 billion for a coal-to-ethanol project. Bilateral trade reached US$154 billion in 2025, and Jakarta wants to convert that weight into higher-value industrial cooperation.

These figures should not be read as money arriving in an account. They mix letters of intent, financing frameworks and signed projects, and their fate depends on financing decisions, approvals and contracts. Yet the numbers are themselves a signal. Once Indonesia tables an industrial package of this size, South China Sea joint development ceases to be an isolated diplomatic gesture and becomes a complement to a wider economic agenda. Insuring onshore industrial parks, railways and data centres with a maritime project is a more transactional style of diplomacy than Jakarta has shown in the past.

## Where Natuna's red line lies

The dispute centres on waters near the Natuna Islands, where China's nine-dash line overlaps with the exclusive economic zone rights Indonesia holds under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. A November 2024 joint statement referring to "joint development in areas of overlapping claims" provoked sharp domestic criticism in Indonesia, because critics feared it implicitly accepted a contested claim. The foreign ministry promptly reaffirmed that Jakarta does not recognise China's claims, and Prabowo said Indonesia would "always safeguard our sovereignty".

The test this time is therefore not another oral reaffirmation but the boundary drawn in the text. If joint development takes place inside Indonesia's exclusive economic zone, who issues fishing licences, who collects resource fees, who enforces catch limits, and who adjudicates disputes cannot be left vague. For coastal communities near Natuna, an agreement is not abstract geopolitics: it determines whether boats can go to sea, whether landings reach processing plants reliably, and how local incomes respond to competition and regulation. Any design that bypasses local fishers and regional governments will struggle to survive Indonesia's domestic politics.

## The governance gap: will is not the missing piece

Joint development is not unusual in international practice, and it fails for predictable reasons: leaders agree on a principle, while agencies disagree over licences, enforcement and revenue. Indonesia and China do not lack the will to cooperate. They lack a text that turns will into enforceable obligations. At minimum, seven questions need answers: where the area is; what activities are allowed; which authority issues permits; how enforcement is divided at sea; how revenue is shared; how disputes are settled; and which environmental and disclosure standards apply.

Leave those questions blank and "joint development" becomes a phrase each side fills differently. One side may hear resource sharing; the other, access facilitation. One stresses that sovereignty is untouched; the other stresses historic rights. The grey area buys face-saving ambiguity but pushes risk down to the implementation level — and when a fishing vessel is detained or a survey ship is challenged, somebody has to put out the fire. That is the real test of Jakarta's diplomatic capacity.

## Spillover to ASEAN: do bilateral deals hollow out the code?

The South China Morning Post reported earlier that China and ASEAN countries had completed about 77 per cent of negotiations on a code of conduct in the South China Sea, with the remaining gaps centred on questions such as which parties the code binds. Advancing a bilateral joint-development arrangement with China at this stage inevitably has spillover effects. If Jakarta accepts a loose bilateral template, other states will ask for equivalent terms. If that template sits outside the ASEAN framework, regional rules will be sliced up by a series of bilateral contracts.

Indonesia's weight comes precisely from not being a primary claimant. It has often acted as a defender of ASEAN cohesion and of a region not organised into rival camps. By folding maritime issues into an economic package with China, it is testing its own regional credibility: if balancing buys only a note with unspecified terms, other ASEAN members will recalculate what "ASEAN centrality" is worth. Regional order is not sustained by declarations. It is sustained when even the most restrained actor declines to cross the line.

## What to watch: from wording to contracts

The next signals will not come from leadership speeches. They will come from three unglamorous things. First, whether the text of the exchange is disclosed enough for Indonesia's parliament and public to examine. Second, whether concrete tenders, licences or joint survey contracts appear, specifying location, operators and revenue terms. Third, whether domestic criticism is incorporated into policy explanation rather than treated as noise. If all three happen, joint development moves from slogan to governance. If the process stops at the first, it will have been a piece of clever diplomatic packaging.

Indonesia's non-aligned tradition does not require silence between great powers. It requires that the conditions be written down. The South China Sea can be put on a deal sheet, but that sheet must consist of clauses that can be held to account. Otherwise, the investment and projects won today may be paid for tomorrow with a much larger bill of interpretation over sovereignty.

## Further reading

**Joint development**: A temporary arrangement between neighbouring states in disputed waters to share exploration, revenue and enforcement before delimitation; its force lies in written terms, not political declarations.

**Exclusive economic zone**: A maritime zone in which a coastal state holds sovereign rights to explore, exploit, conserve and manage natural resources under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea. It is not territorial sovereignty, but it is the core basis for resource jurisdiction.

**ASEAN way**: The region's preference for consultation, non-interference and consensus. It lowers the cost of confrontation, but leaves implementation loose and vulnerable to dilution by bilateral deals.

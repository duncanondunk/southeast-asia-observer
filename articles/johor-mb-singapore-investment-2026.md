# 柔州务大臣访新招揽约16亿新元：柔新经济特区驶入兑现期

马来西亚柔佛州务大臣翁哈菲兹日前率团访问新加坡，与新加坡副总理兼贸工部长颜金勇会面，并与国家发展部长徐芳达共进早餐，其间吸引超过50亿令吉（约84亿元人民币，折合约16亿新元）潜在投资。在全球经济逆风之下，柔佛正试图把"新加坡+柔佛"的组合，包装成亚细安最具竞争力的投资与增长门户。这背后，是柔佛—新加坡经济特区（JS-SEZ）从概念走向落地的前奏。

## 一笔"约16亿新元"的潜在投资

据联合早报报道，翁哈菲兹在8月20日及21日连续于脸书发文，披露此行成果。潜在投资主要集中在高速光学技术、医疗科技与半导体制造设备等战略性、高科技领域。他强调，这些领域"至关重要，不仅带来投资金额，更将新技术、专业知识和就业机会引入柔佛"。

"潜在"二字值得玩味。它既不是已签约的确定订单，也不是空泛的意向书，而是投资者在面对面会谈后表露的可落地意向。对柔佛而言，在经济形势严峻时拿到这样一笔指向高科技的潜在盘子，本身就说明"新加坡邻居"的信用外溢正在发生——许多投资者是先被新加坡吸引，再被劝说到一桥之隔、成本更低的柔佛落子。

## 柔新经济特区：年底蓝图将至

会谈的重点之一，是推进预计于今年底推出的柔新经济特区投资蓝图与总体规划。翁哈菲兹与徐芳达还讨论了强化供应链与中小企业参与、简化柔新之间商业往来与货物流动手续等议题。

经济特区从来不是划一块地、给点税收优惠那么简单。JS-SEZ的真正卖点，在于把新加坡的资本、规则与全球 connectivity，和柔佛的土地、人力与成本优势拧成一股绳。一旦蓝图在年底推出，投资者看到的将不再是两个分割的市场，而是一条"前店后厂、上午开会下午验厂"的跨境生产链。能否把纸面规划转化为可执行的通关、签证与税收细则，是特区成败的分水岭。

## 供应链、中小企业与高技术领域

此次潜在投资聚焦高技术领域，与柔佛既有的产业基底形成呼应。半导体设备、医疗科技、光学技术，都是资本与技术密集、且能带动上下游的环节。翁哈菲兹特别点名"中小企业参与"，说明柔佛不只想当大型项目的承接地，更希望本地中小企嵌入跨境供应链，避免增长红利被跨国巨头独吞。

这恰是经济特区设计的难点：如何让大投资"渗漏"到本地经济。若仅有旗舰项目而缺中小企配套，特区很容易沦为飞地；若中小企能借势升级、成为跨国公司的二级甚至一级供应商，柔佛才真正完成从"低成本腹地"到"价值节点"的跃迁。

## 1436项咨询背后：新马合作的真实密度

一个被低估的信号是：自去年1月以来，马来西亚投资促进中心已接获1436项投资咨询，其中包括43项由新加坡经济发展局及企业发展局转介的潜在项目。数字不大，却揭示了一种常被忽视的合作密度——新加坡并非只在桥头扮演"总部"，也在主动把可外溢的产能与项目，往柔佛方向导流。

柔佛与新加坡的关系，历来在竞争与互补之间摇摆。但站在亚细安视角，这个"一桥两岸"的组合，正越来越像区域内部最成熟的一对产业协同体。当外部不确定性推高分散化布局的需求，靠近新加坡、又能压低成本的柔佛，自然会成为资本重新配置时的优先落点。翁哈菲兹此行招揽的，不只是一笔潜在投资，更是柔佛在亚细安价值链中重新定位的入场券。

## 延伸阅读

**经济特区（Special Economic Zone）**：在一国境内划定、实行特殊经济与监管政策的区域，旨在通过税收、通关与投资便利吸引产业集聚；柔佛—新加坡经济特区是东盟内跨境协同的最新试验。

**经济外交（Economic Diplomacy）**：以经贸议题为核心的外交活动，州务大臣率团招商、部长级会谈促成投资，均属次国家与国家级经济外交的日常形态。

**投资（Investment）**：资本为获取收益而投向生产与资产的行为；文中"潜在投资"指已表露意向、尚未签约的可落地资本，是观察区域产业动向的先行指标。

# Johor's Menteri Besar Courts ~S$1.6 Billion in Singapore: The Johor–Singapore SEZ Enters Its Delivery Phase

Malaysia's Johor Menteri Besar Onn Hafiz recently led a delegation to Singapore, meeting Deputy Prime Minister and Minister for Trade and Industry Gan Kim Yong and having breakfast with Minister for National Development Desmond Lee. The trip attracted over RM5 billion (around S$1.6 billion, roughly US$1.25 billion) in potential investment. Against global economic headwinds, Johor is trying to repackage the "Singapore plus Johor" combination into ASEAN's most competitive investment and growth gateway. Behind it lies the prelude of the Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone (JS-SEZ) moving from concept to ground.

## A "roughly S$1.6 billion" pot of potential investment

According to Lianhe Zaobao, Onn Hafiz disclosed the outcomes on Facebook on 20 and 21 August. The potential investment centres on strategic, high-tech fields — high-speed optical technology, medical technology and semiconductor manufacturing equipment. He stressed these areas are "crucial, bringing not just investment but new technology, expertise and jobs to Johor."

The word "potential" is telling. It is neither a signed, certain order nor a vague letter of intent, but concrete interest shown by investors after face-to-face talks. For Johor, securing such a high-tech-leaning pot amid a grim economy signals that the credit spillover of its "Singapore neighbour" is at work — many investors are first drawn to Singapore, then persuaded to plant flags in cost-cheaper Johor just across the bridge.

## The Johor–Singapore SEZ: a blueprint by year-end

A key focus of the talks was advancing the JS-SEZ investment blueprint and master plan expected to launch by year-end. Onn Hafiz and Desmond Lee also discussed strengthening supply chains and SME participation, and simplifying procedures for cross-border business and goods movement.

A special economic zone is never just drawing a line on a map and offering tax breaks. The JS-SEZ's real selling point is twisting Singapore's capital, rules and global connectivity together with Johor's land, labour and cost advantages. Once the blueprint lands by year-end, investors will see not two segmented markets but a cross-border production chain of "front shop, back factory — meeting in the morning, inspecting the plant in the afternoon." Whether paper planning turns into executable customs, visa and tax details is the watershed for the zone's success.

## Supply chains, SMEs and high-tech sectors

The high-tech tilt echoes Johor's existing industrial base. Semiconductor equipment, medtech and optical technology are capital- and knowledge-intensive and drag along upstream and downstream links. Onn Hafiz specifically named "SME participation," signalling Johor does not want to be merely a receiver of flagship projects but to embed local small firms into cross-border supply chains, avoiding growth dividends captured solely by multinationals.

This is exactly the SEZ design's hard part: how to let big investment "leak" into the local economy. With only flagship projects but no SME backing, a zone easily becomes an enclave; if local firms upgrade and become tier-one or tier-two suppliers, Johor truly completes its leap from "low-cost hinterland" to "value node."

## Behind 1,436 enquiries: the real density of Singapore–Malaysia ties

An underrated signal: since January last year, Malaysia's investment promotion agency has received 1,436 investment enquiries, including 43 potential projects referred by Singapore's EDB and Enterprise Singapore. The numbers are modest, yet they reveal an often-overlooked cooperation density — Singapore is not only a "headquarters" at the bridgehead but actively diverts spillover capacity and projects toward Johor.

Johor–Singapore relations have long swung between competition and complementarity. But from an ASEAN vantage point, this "two shores, one bridge" pair is increasingly the region's most mature industrial synergy. As external uncertainty raises the demand for diversified footprints, Johor — close to Singapore yet cheaper — naturally becomes a priority landing spot when capital reallocates. What Onn Hafiz courted on this trip is not just a pot of potential investment, but Johor's entry ticket to re-anchor itself in the ASEAN value chain.

## Further reading

**Special Economic Zone**: a demarcated area within a country applying special economic and regulatory policies to attract industrial clustering via tax, customs and investment facilitation; the Johor–Singapore SEZ is ASEAN's latest cross-border synergy experiment.
**Economic Diplomacy**: diplomatic activity centred on economic issues; a chief minister leading a trade mission and ministerial talks securing investment are everyday forms of sub-national and national economic diplomacy.
**Investment**: capital committed to production and assets for returns; the "potential investment" here denotes interest shown but not yet contracted — an advance indicator of regional industrial direction.

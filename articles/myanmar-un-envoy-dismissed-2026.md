# 觉莫吞被撤职：代表权之争是合法性之争的延伸

2026年9月13日，缅甸政府发表声明称，常驻联合国代表觉莫吞（Kyaw Moe Tun）因涉嫌从事"非法活动"遭撤职。路透社报道，军政府称其"不服从指示"，并指他早在2021年2月27日已被开除公职。这位由昂山素季政府委任、在2021年政变后拒绝承认军方并持续以民选政府代表身份发声的外交官，长期被军政府视为"叛徒"。五年来，围绕他联合国席位的拉锯从未停歇；而此次撤职，只是这场漫长角力的最新一幕。然而在联合国体系内，一纸来自内比的撤职令，法律效力几乎为零。

## 一纸"撤职令"的由来

觉莫吞的"反叛"始于政变次日。2021年2月26日，他在联合国大会发表激昂演说，呼吁国际社会不要承认通过政变夺权的军方。自此，他以支持被推翻的民选政府立场留任纽约，军方多次要求联合国替换其席位却未获接受。据联合早报报道，此次撤职声明把理由升级为"非法活动"——军方指责他在与影子政府"民族团结政府"（NUG）合作期间，未经授权使用国家财产及驻联合国使团资金，并正通过国际刑警组织、美国机构及外交渠道寻求对他采取法律行动。NUG则长期通过国际倡议与游说，协助觉莫吞维持在联合国的席位，海外缅甸侨民社群亦是其重要支撑。

## 国际法上的零效力

关键在于：谁有权决定联合国席位的归属。按《联合国宪章》与主权平等原则，成员国代表权的 credentials（全权证书）争议，由成员国组成的全权证书委员会处理，而非由成员国政府单方面宣布。该委员会自2021年以来持续推迟就缅甸代表权作决定，觉莫吞的代表身份因此得以维持。军政府可以发声明、可以寻求国际刑警协助，但无法仅凭一纸国内撤职令，让联合国秘书处或大会接受新的代表。换言之，撤职令改变的是叙事，而非席位；在国际法层面，它更像一场没有管辖权的"缺席审判"。

## 军政府为何此刻动手

时机耐人寻味。全权证书委员会预计在9月联合国大会结束后于纽约开会，届时可能讨论缅甸代表权争议。军政府在会前抛出撤职令，更像一次"先发制人"的舆论与法律铺垫：既向内展示"仍在行使国家权威"，也向外把觉莫吞塑造为"涉嫌侵吞公款"的被追诉者，而非单纯的异议外交官。这与军政府筹备大选、谋求外交正常化的节奏相互呼应——在内战僵持、合法性持续受质疑的背景下，抢先在 credentials 战场上布子，是其外交策略的一环。

## 全权证书之争的历史先例

代表权之争并非缅甸独有。1970年代，柬埔寨与阿富汗的政权更迭、以及长期的两岸代表权问题，都曾交由全权证书委员会或大会裁决。惯例是：委员会多倾向"推迟决定"，以避免在成员国内部政权更迭问题上替会员国做主，这一克制源于对《宪章》不干涉内政条款的谨慎。这种"不轻易剥夺席位"的传统，正是觉莫吞能长期留任的制度原因，也意味着军政府很难在程序上速胜；它最多争取到"推迟"变为"讨论"，却难以直接夺席。

## "五点共识"下的缅甸僵局

撤职风波发生在东盟"五点共识"彻底失灵的背景下。2021年政变后，东盟提出要求停止暴力、展开对话、人道准入等五项共识，却始终无法约束军政府，也未能让NUG或其代表进入谈判桌。东盟内部对是否邀请缅甸军方代表与会分歧严重，轮换主席国立场不一，一致性与执行力长期受质疑。觉莫吞在联合国的存在，反而成为东盟框架之外、国际社会与缅甸反对力量维持联系的一条缝隙——这恰是军政府不愿看到的，也暴露了东盟"不干涉"原则的双重困境。

## 大选与争取承认的节奏

军政府正筹备一场被西方与反对派视为"合法性包装"的大选，意在把既成事实转化为国际承认。这套选举框架建立在2008年军方主导的宪法之上，反对派与少数民族武装普遍拒斥其正当性。若选举按计划推进，军方势必在前后加大对代表权、外交承认的争夺：国内的"选举授权"与联合国的"席位归属"互为表里。此次对觉莫吞动手，可视为这套组合拳的前奏——先在国内法上"清除"异议代表，再试图以外交手腕让新代表入主纽约。

## 对内战各方的信号

对内，撤职令释放出强硬信号：即便在海外，不服从者也将被追讨。这对仍效忠民选政府、或在NUG体系中任职的海外缅甸人，是一种威慑；对军政府支持者，则是"国家机器仍在运转"的宣示。NUG方面料将继续力挺觉莫吞，把撤职令反衬为军方"心虚"的证据。但对正与军方交战的各民族地方武装与人民防卫军而言，此举几乎无实质影响——战场上的力量对比，远比一纸撤职令更能决定谁代表缅甸。

## 承认政治的碎片化

更深层的图景是承认政治的碎片化。缅甸的合法性之争已无法由单一机构裁决：联合国大会与全权证书委员会拖延、东盟分裂、西方承认NUG部分合法性、中国与俄罗斯等国则维持与军政府的务实接触，避免彻底孤立仰光。这种"多重承认"的格局，使任何一派都难以独占代表权，也令外部调停失去统一抓手。觉莫吞的去留，不过是这场碎片化博弈的一个节点。代表权之争从来不是程序问题，而是合法性之争的延伸——谁被承认为"缅甸"，谁就掌握了定义这个国家未来的权力。

## 延伸阅读

**全权证书委员会（Credentials Committee）**：联合国大会下属的九国委员会，负责审议成员国代表的全权证书是否合规；对存在政权更迭争议的国家，惯例倾向于推迟决定，避免替会员国裁定代表权。

**民族团结政府（National Unity Government, NUG）**：2021年政变后由缅甸联邦议会代表委任的影子政府，未获国际普遍承认，但在部分国家与联合国体系中维持着与觉莫吞等代表的事实联系。

**五点共识（Five-Point Consensus）**：2021年4月东盟就缅甸危机达成的共识，要求停暴、对话、人道准入、特使斡旋与各方磋商，但执行乏力，未能实质性缓和缅甸局势。

# Kyaw Moe Tun's Dismissal: The Seat Fight Is an Extension of the Legitimacy Fight

On 13 September 2026, Myanmar's government said its permanent representative to the UN, Kyaw Moe Tun, had been dismissed over alleged "illegal activities." Reuters reported the junta claimed he had "disobeyed instructions" and was stripped of his post as early as 27 February 2021. Appointed by Aung San Suu Kyi's government and refusing to recognise the military after the 2021 coup, Kyaw Moe Tun has spoken at the UN as the envoy of the ousted elected government—earning the junta's label of "traitor." For five years the tug-of-war over his UN seat has never ceased; this dismissal is merely the latest act in that protracted struggle. Yet within the UN system, a dismissal order from Naypyidaw carries almost no legal weight.

## Where the dismissal order comes from

Kyaw Moe Tun's defiance began the day after the coup. On 26 February 2021 he delivered an impassioned address at the UN General Assembly urging the world not to recognise the junta that seized power. He has since stayed in New York on the side of the ousted government, while the military repeatedly demanded his replacement without success. According to Lianhe Zaobao, the latest dismissal elevates the rationale to "illegal activities": the junta accuses him of unauthorised use of state property and mission funds while working with the shadow National Unity Government (NUG), and is pursuing legal action via Interpol, US agencies and diplomatic channels. The NUG, for its part, has long sustained his UN seat through international advocacy and lobbying, with the overseas Burmese diaspora a crucial backbone.

## Zero effect under international law

The crux is who decides a UN seat. Under the UN Charter and the principle of sovereign equality, disputes over a member state's credentials are handled by the Credentials Committee—composed of member states—not by a government's unilateral announcement. The committee has deferred a decision on Myanmar's representation since 2021, allowing Kyaw Moe Tun's status to stand. The junta may issue statements and seek Interpol help, but it cannot, by a domestic dismissal order alone, make the UN Secretariat or Assembly accept a new representative. The order changes the narrative, not the seat; in international law it is closer to an "in absentia trial" with no jurisdiction.

## Why the junta moves now

The timing is telling. The Credentials Committee is expected to meet in New York after the September General Assembly, possibly taking up the Myanmar dispute. Issuing the dismissal beforehand looks like pre-emptive legal and rhetorical groundwork: projecting domestic authority while reframing Kyaw Moe Tun as a suspect in alleged embezzlement rather than merely a dissident diplomat. This dovetails with the junta's election preparations and its quest for diplomatic normalisation—amid a stalemated civil war and persistent questions over legitimacy, planting flags on the credentials battlefield is part of the junta's diplomatic playbook.

## Historical precedents of credential fights

Seat disputes are not unique to Myanmar. In the 1970s, rival Cambodian and Afghan regimes, and the long-standing cross-strait question, were all referred to the Credentials Committee or the Assembly. The norm is to defer—avoiding a ruling on internal regime change, a restraint rooted in caution over the Charter's non-interference clause. This reluctance to strip seats is precisely why Kyaw Moe Tun has kept his post, and why a swift procedural win for the junta is unlikely; at best it may turn "deferral" into "discussion," not a direct seizure of the seat.

## The Myanmar impasse under the Five-Point Consensus

The dismissal unfolds against the total failure of ASEAN's Five-Point Consensus. Agreed in 2021, it called for an end to violence, dialogue, humanitarian access and an ASEAN special envoy—yet it never constrained the junta nor brought the NUG to the table. ASEAN itself is split over inviting the junta's representatives, with rotating chairs taking divergent lines and its coherence and enforceability long in doubt. Kyaw Moe Tun's UN presence has instead become a crack through which the international community stays linked to Myanmar's opposition—exactly what the junta resents, and a reveal of the double bind in ASEAN's non-interference principle.

## Elections and the rhythm of recognition

The junta is preparing a general election that the West and opponents dismiss as legitimacy theatre, aiming to convert fait accompli into international recognition. That electoral framework rests on the 2008 military-drafted constitution, whose legitimacy is rejected by opponents and ethnic armed groups alike. If polls proceed, the military will intensify its fight over representation and diplomatic recognition before and after: domestic "electoral mandate" and UN "seat" are two faces of one coin. Moving against Kyaw Moe Tun now reads as the opener of that combination—first "clearing" the dissident envoy under domestic law, then trying to seat a new representative in New York through diplomacy.

## Signals to the parties in the civil war

Domestically, the dismissal sends a hard line: even abroad, the disobedient will be pursued. For overseas Burmese still loyal to the elected government or serving the NUG, it is a deterrent; for junta supporters, a declaration that "the state machinery still runs." The NUG will likely keep backing Kyaw Moe Tun, turning the dismissal into evidence of the junta's "guilt." But for ethnic armed organisations and People's Defence Forces fighting the military, the move is near-irrelevant—the balance of force on the ground, far more than a dismissal order, decides who represents Myanmar.

## The fragmentation of recognition politics

The deeper picture is the fragmentation of recognition politics. Myanmar's legitimacy contest can no longer be adjudicated by a single body: the General Assembly and Credentials Committee stall, ASEAN is divided, the West grants the NUG partial legitimacy, while China, Russia and others keep pragmatic contact with the junta rather than isolating Yangon outright. This "multiple recognition" landscape makes it hard for any faction to monopolise representation, and deprives outside mediation of a unified handle. Kyaw Moe Tun's fate is but one node in this fragmented game. The seat fight was never merely procedural—it is an extension of the legitimacy fight. Who is recognised as "Myanmar" holds the power to define the country's future.

## Further reading

**Credentials Committee**: A nine-member committee of the UN General Assembly that examines whether a member state's credentials are in order; on states with contested regime change, it conventionally defers, avoiding a ruling on representation.

**National Unity Government (NUG)**: A shadow government formed after the 2021 coup by representatives of Myanmar's deposed legislature; not widely recognised but maintaining de facto links with envoys like Kyaw Moe Tun in parts of the UN system.

**Five-Point Consensus**: ASEAN's April 2021 agreement on Myanmar calling for cessation of violence, dialogue, humanitarian access, an envoy's mediation and inclusive consultation—weakly implemented and unable to substantially ease the crisis.

# 从捐资到组局：潮侨新生代如何成为亚太合作的无形基础设施

据中国侨网报道，亚太经合组织第三十三次领导人非正式会议不久前在深圳举行，主题是建设亚太共同体、促进共同繁荣。宣言里的词很大，落到企业层面却是一笔笔订单、一次次人才互通和一条条要打通的供应链。真正把框架变成合同的，往往不是讲台上的声音，而是一些熟悉两边办事方式的人。其中，潮籍侨胞的后代尤其值得看看：全球海外侨胞逾6000万，祖籍潮汕者逾1500万，约70%聚居东南亚。这不是一段怀旧插曲，而是一个关于区域合作由谁提供「连接件」的现实问题。

## 从旁听到组局：一场亚太会议背后的座次变化

这场会议把开放、创新、合作列为优先领域。中国侨网的观察是，潮侨新生代参与其中的位置变了：过去多是列席者、旁听者，如今越来越多人成了组局者。2025年1月，新加坡潮商总会成立，创会会长吴木兴说得很直白，潮商需要一个「纯粹围绕商业和发展」的平台。将于2027年举办的国际潮团联谊年会，也已经把数码转型、青年参与、可持续发展写进议题。

这个变化不该被当成一句漂亮话。侨团过去被衡量，常常看捐了多少钱、修了多少路、办了多少场联谊；现在它们开始抢议程设置权，这比摆一桌酒席难得多。组局很便宜，交付很贵——能把人召集起来是一回事，能把共识变成可执行的合作是另一回事。但座次确实变了：当青年侨胞开始决定谈什么、和谁谈、按什么规则谈，他们就不再只是传统的受益人，而是区域合作的参与者。

## 信任、双语与跨制度：难以复制的三样本钱

凭什么是他们？答案不在血脉神秘论，而在三样很具体的东西。第一是双语的深度，能把政策语言翻译成当地企业听得懂、能照着做的语言。第二是跨制度的经验，两边的办事方式、审批节奏都熟，知道一份文件在哪一个国家会卡住。第三是信任的存量，会馆的信用是祖辈一点点攒下来的，不是一份漂亮的商业计划书能替代的。

柬埔寨有3个潮团，老会馆通历史，新商会通当下，这种结构不是谁设计出来的，是几十年自然长成的。使馆可以签框架，跨国咨询公司可以写报告，前者未必能为一家供应商的履约能力背书，后者未必懂得一个家族在当地的口碑分量。侨团恰好站在这两者之间。也要看到，继承来的信任会折旧：年轻一代未必还会说潮语，非潮籍的合作伙伴也不天然分享这份信用。若不能靠透明的治理把信任重新挣回来，存量终究会坐吃山空。

## 华校的转身：从识字启蒙到「中文＋职业技能」

2025年8月，柬埔寨金边端华大学揭牌，首批百余名新生入学。这所学校源自1908年潮侨创办的私塾，如今在校生1.6万余人，新开设汉语言、物流运输、新能源汽车、建筑工程技术等专业，目标写得很明确：补齐跨境物流、产业园区和中资企业落地所需的本土化技能人才缺口。

这是所有变化中最有实质意义的一步。海外华校过去的首要任务是识字与传续文化，现在开始直接对接劳动力市场。这个转向是聪明的：只教语言，就业回报弱；语言加一门手艺，学生才真正有饭碗。中老铁路开通后，沿线华校家长关心的问题从「要不要学中文」变成「怎样学中文更有用」，就是同一逻辑。但风险也在旁边：如果「中文＋职业技能」被窄化成一条招聘管道，华校就会丢掉它作为社区文化锚点的人文中枢。技能可以衡量，归属感的产出却没办法写进就业统计表。

## 会馆的年轻化：把传统变成可参与的共同经验

2025年9月，新加坡潮州八邑会馆青年团办「行起来，家己人」跑步健行，350人冒雨参加，终点还设了潮语闯关游戏。团长陈俊元说，报名比上一届多了近一半，而且很多人并不是潮州人。柔佛潮州八邑会馆的「三月初三锣鼓响」已经办了24届，儿童庙会还立了一条规矩：童谣比赛必须与长辈搭档报名。

这不是把民俗锁进玻璃柜，而是把身份变成一种可以参与的社会接口。你听得懂我奶奶唱的那支歌，我们的文化认同就近了一层——它给不同国籍的同龄人一个共同话题。这里有个有趣的悖论：要让非潮籍青年愿意来，传统就得变得好玩、可携带，独特性因此会被稀释一些。可这总好过单向宣讲。文化在这里不是表演给人看，而是一件需要一起完成的事。

## 桥梁也有影子：侨团基础设施的边界

报道归纳了四个转向：从捐资到组局，2001年柔佛潮州八邑会馆时任会长李树藩提出摸清潮人拓殖柔佛的史料，会馆出钱请学者做田野，11家会馆资助出版；从回馈到双向，「潮商大师班」既帮本地企业看懂中国市场，也把技术引进中国企业；从代表社群到专业共同体，第48届董事会平均年龄57岁，位子给到专业人士；从守成到再生产，潮语做成闯关游戏，成年礼做成直播。

这些转向值得肯定，但也要问几个不客气的问题。专业化会不会把会馆变成与普通会员脱节的专家俱乐部？平均年龄57岁算不上青年治理，技术能力也不等于代表性。在东南亚，华人网络本来就容易被放到「外来影响」的放大镜下，越是高调组局，越可能被安全化。透明度、在地注册、多方参与的问责机制，不是装饰，而是桥梁的护栏。

区域框架要落成合同，总得有人翻译规则、为信用背书、培养能干活的人。潮侨新生代正在做这份工作。但桥梁的价值取决于它向谁负责：只有对所指涉的社群交得了账，它才算真正的基础设施。

## 延伸阅读

**侨团（overseas Chinese association）**：由地缘、血缘或业缘联结的海外华人组织，传统上办慈善与祭祀，如今也承担规则对接、专业培训与商业撮合。

**潮籍（Teochew origin）**：祖籍广东潮州、汕头、揭阳一带的群体，在东南亚分布集中，是观察华人离散网络与区域合作的重要个案。

**中文＋职业技能（Chinese plus vocational skills）**：海外华校把语言教学与物流、新能源、工程等专业训练结合，以回应在地产业链的人才需求。

# From Donors to Conveners: Teochew Diaspora Youth and the Quiet Infrastructure of Asia-Pacific Cooperation

According to China Qiao Wang, the 33rd APEC Leaders' Week was held in Shenzhen under the theme of building an Asia-Pacific community and shared prosperity. The language at the podium is grand; at company level, integration resolves into individual orders, visits by engineers, and supply chains that actually clear customs. The people who turn frameworks into contracts are rarely the ones giving the keynote. They are often people who know how things get done on both sides of a border. The descendants of Teochew-origin overseas Chinese deserve a closer look: of more than 60 million overseas Chinese worldwide, over 15 million trace their ancestry to the Chaoshan area, and about 70 percent of them live in Southeast Asia. This is not a nostalgic footnote. It is a question of who supplies the connective tissue when regional agreements meet factories.

## From observers to conveners: a change of seats behind an APEC week

The Shenzhen meeting set openness, innovation and cooperation as its priorities. The China Qiao Wang report makes a sharper observation: Teochew diaspora youth have changed seats. They used to sit in the audience; increasingly, they set the table. In January 2025 the Singapore Teochew Chamber of Commerce was founded, with founding chairman Wu Muxing arguing that Teochew business needed a platform "purely about business and development". The 2027 International Teochew Convention has already placed digital transformation, youth participation and sustainability on its agenda.

This should not be read as ceremonial optimism. Diaspora organisations were once measured by how much they donated, how many roads they built and how many banquets they hosted. Now they are competing for agenda-setting power, which is harder than hosting a dinner. Convening is cheap; delivery is expensive. Gathering people in a room is one thing, turning consensus into enforceable cooperation is another. Yet the change of seats is real: when young diaspora members decide what is discussed, who is invited and which rules apply, they cease to be passive beneficiaries of diplomacy and become its practitioners.

## Trust, bilingualism and institutional fluency: the three things others cannot easily copy

Why them? The answer is not bloodline mysticism but three concrete assets. First, deep bilingualism: the ability to translate policy language into something a local firm can understand and act on. Second, cross-institutional experience: familiarity with both administrative cultures, including the point at which a document tends to stall. Third, inherited trust: the credit that a chamber has accumulated over generations, which a polished business plan cannot replicate.

Cambodia has three Teochew associations; the older ones carry historical memory, the newer ones carry current commercial intelligence. Such layering was not designed in a conference room — it grew over decades. An embassy can sign a framework but cannot vouch for a supplier's reliability; a global consultancy can write a report but may not know how much a family's name is worth in a local market. Diaspora networks sit precisely between the two. Inherited trust, however, depreciates. The younger generation may no longer speak Teochew, and non-Teochew partners do not inherit it either. Unless trust is re-earned through transparent governance, the family savings will eventually run out.

## The school's pivot: from literacy to "Chinese plus skills"

In August 2025, Phnom Penh's Duan Hua University was formally unveiled, with its first cohort of more than one hundred students. The institution traces its origins to a private school founded by Teochew migrants in 1908; it now enrols more than 16,000 students and has added programmes in Chinese language, logistics and transport, new-energy vehicles and construction engineering technology. Its purpose is stated plainly: to close the local skills gap created by cross-border logistics, industrial parks and the arrival of Chinese-invested firms.

This is the most substantive shift in the report. Overseas Chinese schools once focused on literacy and cultural continuity; now they feed directly into labour markets. The pivot is smart. Language alone offers a weak employment return; language plus a trade gives a student something to live on. After the China–Laos Railway opened, parents near the line reportedly stopped asking whether their children should learn Chinese and started asking how to learn it usefully — the same logic. The risk sits next door. If "Chinese plus vocational skills" is narrowed into a recruitment pipeline, the school loses the humanistic centre that made it a community anchor. Skills are measurable; the production of belonging does not fit into an employment spreadsheet.

## Younger chambers: turning heritage into a shared, participatory experience

In September 2025 the youth wing of Singapore's Teochew Eight Districts Association organised a "Walk, Our People" run. Some 350 people took part in the rain, and Teochew-dialect games awaited them at the finish. Youth leader Chen Junyuan said registration was nearly half again as large as the previous edition, and many participants were not Teochew at all. In Johor, the "Third Month, Third Day Drumming" event has run for 24 editions, with one rule for the children's temple fair: folk-song contestants must register alongside an elder.

This is not folklore locked behind glass; it is identity turned into a social interface you can step into. "You understand the song my grandmother sang" creates common ground among people of different nationalities. There is a useful paradox here. To attract non-Teochew youth, the particular has to become playful and portable, and some distinctiveness thins out in the process. That is still better than a one-way lecture. Culture here is not performed for an audience; it is a task people complete together.

## Where the bridge casts a shadow: limits of diaspora infrastructure

The report identifies four shifts: from donations to convening — in 2001 the then-chairman of the Johor Teochew Eight Districts Association, Li Shufan, proposed documenting the Teochew pioneering of Johor, and the association paid scholars to do fieldwork, with eleven chambers funding publication; from giving back to two-way exchange, as the "Teochew Business Masterclass" helps local firms read the Chinese market while bringing technology into Chinese companies; from representing a community to becoming a professional body, with the 48th board averaging 57 years of age and reserving seats for professionals; and from preservation to reproduction, with dialect puzzles and livestreamed coming-of-age ceremonies.

These shifts deserve credit, but a few unflattering questions are in order. Could professionalisation turn a chamber into an experts' club disconnected from ordinary members? An average age of 57 is not youth governance, and technical competence is not legitimacy. In Southeast Asia, ethnic Chinese networks are already examined under the lens of foreign influence; the more visibly they convene, the more easily they can be securitised. Transparency, local registration and multi-stakeholder accountability are not decoration. They are the guardrails on the bridge.

If a regional framework is to become a contract, someone must translate the rules, vouch for credit and train people who can do the work. Teochew diaspora youth are doing that job. But a bridge's value depends on whom it answers to: only when it can render accounts to the community it claims to represent does it qualify as infrastructure.

## Further reading

**Overseas Chinese association** (侨团): an organisation built on place of origin, kinship or occupation; traditionally active in charity and ritual, increasingly in rule-brokering, training and business matchmaking.

**Teochew origin** (潮籍): descent from the Chaoshan area of Guangdong — Chaozhou, Shantou and Jieyang — a population heavily concentrated in Southeast Asia and a useful lens on diaspora networks.

**Chinese plus vocational skills** (中文＋职业技能): overseas Chinese schools pairing language teaching with training in logistics, new energy or engineering to meet the talent needs of local industrial chains.

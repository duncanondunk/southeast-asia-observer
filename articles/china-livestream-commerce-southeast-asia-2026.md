# 从香港珠宝展看中国直播带货出海东南亚（Southeast Asia）：半壁江山与华人商户的双刃剑

9月中旬，全球规模最大的珠宝展之一「珠宝首饰展览会」（JGW）在香港举行，约6万名采购商从世界各地涌来。据《日经亚洲》（Nikkei Asia）报道，展会上最引人注目的，是来自东南亚的直播带货主播——有参展商说，「最近五年左右，来自东南亚的直播主播比来自中国本土的还要多」。一句闲谈，道出中国电商出海的深层转向：直播带货这门在中国被打磨得极为成熟的生意，正加速向东南亚输出，而站在风口上的，既有中资平台，也有当地的华侨华人商户。

## 一面镜子：珠宝展上的东南亚主播

珠宝展本是看货、谈价的传统舞台，如今却被一部部智能手机改写了玩法。主播们举着手机在展位间穿梭，把珍珠项链、彩宝实时推给镜头另一端的东南亚观众。据《日经亚洲》观察，面向当地市场、由当地主播卖货的模式已在泰国（Thailand）、印度尼西亚（Indonesia）等地普及。行业已从「把中国货卖给中国人」走到「把中国模式教给当地人」——这正是中国企业向外输出直播电商经验的新阶段。

## 数字：东南亚成了直播电商的「全球实验室」

支撑这门生意的，是一组惊人的数字。TikTok 首席执行官周受资在2026年7月底披露，该平台在东南亚的月活跃用户超过3.25亿，企业账号达1500万个。调查机构的数据更显示，2025年 TikTok 在东南亚的商品交易总额（GMV）高达456亿美元（约3283亿元人民币），东南亚已成为这家公司规模最大的海外市场。一个区域，悄然变成全球直播带货的「实验室」：今天在雅加达（Jakarta）直播间跑通的打法，明天就成了西方平台抄作业的模板。

## 从「中国先行」到「东南亚主战场」

直播带货的先行者是中国。但国内流量见顶、竞争白热化，企业为寻找新增量，把下一个主战场锁定在东南亚。这里年轻人口多、社媒渗透率极高、习惯「一部手机走天下」，购物从「人找货」变成「人带人」——主播实时演示、答疑，把广告和推荐合二为一。娱乐与消费边界被抹平，静态网店难以匹敌。对在中国市场被「卷」出来的平台与商家而言，东南亚几乎是为这套模式量身定做的试验场。

## 中资平台的半壁江山

格局正在重排。新加坡冬海集团（Sea）旗下的 Shopee 长期稳坐东南亚电商头把交椅。但后来 TikTok、阿里巴巴旗下的 Lazada、快手科技等中资背景平台陆续入局。据《日经亚洲》援引的数据，借助直播电商，中国系平台的市场份额已接近五成，规模壮大到足以逼近 Shopee。也就是说，短短数年，中资军团从「外来者」变成了「近半壁江山」的搅局者，逼得区域内每一个零售商都不得不重新思考：怎么用视频和达人把货卖出去。

## 多频道网络出海：把「带货」变成一门手艺

撑起这场盛宴的，是一张由达人（关键意见卖家，KOS）织成的网。中国本土的多频道网络（MCN）机构已大举南下。直播电商企业广东遥望科技集团手握大量主播，不仅在国内招募，也把重心转向在东南亚当地招人；从事网红营销的天下秀数字科技早在2023年就上线面向海外的「ADream」平台，连接菲律宾（Philippines）、印度尼西亚、泰国、马来西亚（Malaysia）、越南（Vietnam）等国主播与想开拓东南亚的品牌方。这些机构把「如何卖货」变成了一门可复制、可培训的手艺，也把中国的内容电商方法论搬到了海外。

## 华侨华人的双刃剑

在这场变局里，华侨华人站在最微妙的位置。香港作为自由港，进出口免关税、无增值税，又是连通中国内地与东南亚的门户，展会上人流、货流、资金流的背后，处处是华人商贸网络的影子。一方面，熟悉两端市场、通语言的华人商户，最容易承接中资平台与本地消费者的对接，成为「双向桥」；另一方面，当中资平台以效率和低价横扫货架，那些曾经靠信息差、靠代理加价吃饭的传统华人贸易商，恰恰成了被冲击的对象。受益与承压，往往发生在同一群人身上。

## 隐忧：监管与本土反弹

势头虽猛，脚下并不稳。最清晰的风险是监管：印尼（Indonesia）已立法要求加密货币与股票推介须持牌，类似的合规收紧随时可能落到直播带货头上；各国对数据本地化、外资持股、内容审查的要求各不相同。更深一层是情绪——当中资平台凭借供应链效率压低价格，本土制造商与中小商户的生存空间被挤压，保护主义的大棒迟早会举起。真正可持续的出海，不是把低价商品无限倾销，而是与当地产业融合、带动就业与技术转移；否则今天装满购物车的繁荣，装下的可能就是明天针对中国制造的高墙。

## 结论：输出的是模式，沉淀的是连接

说到底，香港珠宝展上的那些东南亚主播，只是一个切面。它照见的是中国直播电商从「国内市场」走向「区域输出」的拐点：中资平台逼近 Shopee 的霸主地位，多频道网络把带货变手艺，华侨华人商户则在受益与承压之间腾挪。这门生意输出的不止是算法和话术，更是一张把中国产能与东南亚需求缝在一起的商业连接。而这张网能织多牢，取决于它能否真正在当地扎根，而非只把东南亚当成下一个「内卷」的战场。

## 延伸阅读

**商品交易总额（GMV）**：直播电商平台最常用的规模指标，指一定周期内商品成交的总金额，是衡量内容电商爆发力的核心数据。
**关键意见卖家（KOS）**：既做内容又做销售的主播型达人，区别于单纯的网红（KOL），是东南亚直播电商生态的人力发动机。
**冬海集团（Sea Group）**：新加坡上市的数字经济集团，旗下 Shopee 是东南亚电商龙头，长期面对中资平台的贴身竞争。

# From Hong Kong's Jewelry Show, a Glimpse of China's Livestream Commerce Conquering Southeast Asia

In mid-September, one of the world's largest jewelry fairs, Jewellery & Gem WORLD (JGW), drew some 60,000 buyers to Hong Kong. What caught the eye of Nikkei Asia was not the diamonds but the livestream hosts — many of them from Southeast Asia. As one exhibitor put it, "In the past five years or so, there have been more livestream hosts from Southeast Asia than from mainland China." That offhand remark captures a deeper shift in Chinese e-commerce going global: the livestream-selling model, polished to a high sheen at home, is now racing into Southeast Asia, and perched at the cusp are both Chinese platforms and the region's overseas-Chinese merchants.

## The fair as a mirror: Southeast Asian hosts take the stage

JGW was traditionally a stage for inspecting stones and haggling prices. Today a smartphone has rewritten the script. Hosts weave between booths, pushing pearl necklaces and coloured gems to viewers across Southeast Asia in real time. Nikkei Asia observed that selling to local markets through local hosts has already taken hold in Thailand, Indonesia and beyond. The industry has moved from "selling Chinese goods to Chinese people" to "teaching locals the Chinese playbook" — a new phase of exporting the livestream-commerce experience itself.

## The numbers: Southeast Asia as the world's livestream lab

Behind the buzz sits a startling set of figures. TikTok CEO Shou Zi Chew disclosed in late July 2026 that the platform has more than 325 million monthly active users in Southeast Asia and 15 million business accounts. Industry data cited by Nikkei Asia put TikTok's Southeast Asia gross merchandise value (GMV) at US$45.6 billion in 2025, making the region the company's single largest overseas market. Almost unnoticed, the region has become the world's laboratory for livestream selling: a tactic that works in a Jakarta live room today becomes a template Western platforms copy tomorrow.

## From China-first to Southeast Asia as the main battlefield

China pioneered livestream commerce. But with domestic traffic peaking and competition white-hot, firms hunting new growth have locked the next battlefield onto Southeast Asia. Young populations, extraordinary social-media penetration, and a mobile-first everything culture make shopping a matter of "people bringing people" rather than "people searching for goods" — hosts demo products live, answer questions in real time, and fuse advertising with recommendation. Entertainment and commerce blur into one experience a static online store cannot match. For platforms and merchants already "rolled flat" by China's cut-throat market, Southeast Asia looks tailor-made for the model.

## Chinese platforms take half the pie

The competitive map is being redrawn. Singapore's Sea Group and its Shopee have long sat atop Southeast Asia's e-commerce throne. But Chinese-backed entrants — TikTok, Alibaba's Lazada, and Kuaishou — moved in behind them. According to data Nikkei Asia cited, powered by livestream commerce, Chinese-origin platforms now command close to a 50 percent share, large enough to threaten Shopee. In just a few years the Chinese contingent went from outsider to near-half-the-market disruptor, forcing every regional retailer to rethink how to sell through video and creators.

## MCN agencies go global: turning "selling" into a craft

Propping up the boom is a web of creators the industry calls key opinion sellers (KOS) — people whose business is selling, not merely influencing. Chinese multi-channel networks (MCN) have marched south in force. Guangdong-based Yaowang Technology commands a large roster of hosts and has shifted its focus toward recruiting locally in Southeast Asia, not just in China. IMS, a Chinese influencer-marketing firm, launched its overseas platform "ADream" back in 2023, linking hosts in the Philippines, Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia and Vietnam with brands eager to enter the region. These agencies turn "how to sell" into a replicable, trainable craft and carry China's content-commerce methodology abroad.

## A double-edged sword for overseas Chinese

Overseas Chinese sit in the most delicate spot of this upheaval. Hong Kong, a free port with no tariffs or value-added tax and a gateway between mainland China and Southeast Asia, is threaded through with ethnic-Chinese commercial networks — in its flows of people, goods and capital. On one side, merchants fluent in both markets and languages are best placed to bridge Chinese platforms and local consumers, acting as a two-way bridge. On the other, when Chinese platforms sweep shelves with efficiency and low prices, the very traditional ethnic-Chinese traders who once lived off information gaps and markup arbitrage become the ones under pressure. Gain and strain often land on the same shoulders.

## The risks: regulation and local pushback

For all the momentum, the ground can shift. The clearest risk is regulatory. Indonesia has already legislated that promoting crypto and stocks requires a licence, and similar tightening could easily reach livestream commerce; rules on data localisation, foreign ownership and content scrutiny differ country by country. Deeper still is sentiment: when Chinese platforms squeeze prices with supply-chain efficiency, the survival space of local manufacturers and small merchants shrinks, and the club of protectionism will eventually be swung. Truly sustainable going-global is not dumping cheap goods without limit but fusing with local industry and driving local jobs and technology transfer — otherwise the prosperity filling today's shopping carts may be the high wall erected against Chinese-made goods tomorrow.

## Conclusion: exporting a model, depositing connections

In the end, those Southeast Asian hosts at the Hong Kong jewelry fair are only one facet. They reflect the inflection point where Chinese livestream commerce moves from a domestic market to a regional export: Chinese platforms closing in on Shopee's throne, MCN agencies turning selling into a craft, and overseas-Chinese merchants manoeuvring between gain and pressure. What this business exports is more than algorithms and scripts; it is a commercial connection stitching Chinese production capacity to Southeast Asian demand. How tightly that web holds depends on whether it truly takes root locally — rather than treating Southeast Asia as merely the next arena for "involution."

**Gross Merchandise Value (GMV)**: The standard scale metric for livestream-e-commerce platforms, the total value of goods transacted in a period — the core gauge of the sector's explosive growth.
**Key Opinion Seller (KOS)**: A host who both creates content and closes sales, distinct from the pure influencer (KOL), and the human engine of Southeast Asia's livestream-commerce ecosystem.
**Sea Group**: The Singapore-listed digital-economy group behind Shopee, Southeast Asia's e-commerce leader, long fending off the close competition of Chinese-origin platforms.

# 深圳直飞文莱航线开通：一条支线航线背后的中国—东盟空中互联

国庆黄金周前夕，深圳宝安国际机场悄悄补上了航线版图上的一块空白：深圳直飞文莱首都斯里巴加湾的航线正式开通。执飞方是深圳航空，每周四个往返。单看运量，它远不如京沪穗的洲际大线热闹；但放在中国—东盟互联互通的版图上，这条支线航线却别有意味——它把粤港澳大湾区与这个被南海环抱的油气小国，第一次用一条不经中转的空中通道直接连了起来。

## 一条被填补的空白航线

此前，从深圳前往文莱，旅客往往要在香港、新加坡或吉隆坡中转，耗时动辄大半天。新航线开通后，深圳与斯里巴加湾之间无需再绕道。据深圳机场方面介绍，该航线由深圳航空执飞，去程航班号ZH171，回程ZH172。这不是一条临时加班，而是常态化排班，意味着两地之间的商务、探亲与旅游往来，有了一副稳定的空中骨架。

值得玩味的是开通的时机。航线首发恰逢国庆出行高峰，深圳机场预计假期客流保持高位运行。一边是出境游热门目的地东南亚持续升温，一边是机场借黄金周把新线推向市场——这本身就是一张"供需两旺"的民生答卷。

## 数字：每周四班，不再中转

根据深圳航空公布的班期：去程ZH171计划每周一、二、四、六执行，16时30分从深圳起飞，19时55分抵达斯里巴加湾；回程ZH172计划每周二、三、五、日执行，9时55分从斯里巴加湾起飞，13时20分抵达深圳（以上均为当地时间）。

把这些数字摊开看，指向的是"频次够用、时刻友好"。四个往返覆盖了一周的大部分工作日与周末，对中国华南与文莱之间的短途差旅而言，基本能实现"本周去、本周回"。对一条新开的国际支线来说，这个排班密度已不算保守。

放到更大的坐标系里看：深圳机场目前国际及地区客运通航城市超过60个，其中通往APEC成员经济体的航线超过30条；而在东南亚方向，深圳已通达该区域20余个主要城市，每周计划出港航班超过250班。文莱一地的加入，补全的是这张网络里最后一个明显的缺口。

## 小国大通道：文莱为何被纳入"空中丝路"

文莱地处加里曼丹岛北部，国土不大，却是东南亚重要的油气生产国，也是东盟成员国和共建"一带一路"国家。深圳机场方面把这条航线明确定位为"持续拓展东盟航空市场、完善'一带一路'航线布局的又一积极进展"——话不多，但分量不轻。

为什么是文莱？第一是地缘位置特殊：文莱扼守南海南部的通航要道，与中国在南海的航运、科研与能源合作交集不少。第二是政治关系稳定：中文两国早已建立战略合作伙伴关系，高层往来频繁。第三是体量小、议价低成本、接入快——对一家中国枢纽机场而言，把文莱这样的小国串进航线网，边际投入有限，外交与象征收益却相当可观。一条支线，往往比一纸宣言更能把"互联互通"四个字落到实处。

## 不止是旅游：经贸与侨情的双向纽带

新闻通稿把意义概括为"经贸往来、文化交流和旅游合作"三件套。这绝非套话。文莱经济高度依赖油气出口，眼下正急于推动多元化，旅游与服务业是重点方向；深圳及大湾区则有资本、技术与客流外溢的现实需求。一条直飞航线，等于把"大湾区产能"与"文莱转型"放进了同一张可达性地图。

更隐蔽的一条线，是侨情。文莱华人约占全国人口一成，虽属少数，却长期主导零售、餐饮与中小型贸易，是两国经贸往来的天然中介。过去他们往返祖籍地，多要取道第三地中转；如今深圳直飞开通，等于在华南侨乡与文莱华人社会之间，添了一段更短、更顺的回家路。航空通道从来不只运客，也运人脉。

## 区域镜像：中国机场的东盟航线扩张潮

把视野拉出文莱，会发现深圳并非孤例。近年来，中国多座枢纽机场都在加密东盟航线：南宁、昆明借中国—东盟博览会与澜湄合作持续扩容，广州、深圳则依托大湾区与RCEP红利抢滩。东南亚凭"航程适中、旅游资源丰富、产业链黏性强"这三重优势，成了中国机场国际化最稳的基本盘。

这背后是一条清晰的逻辑：当中美航线因政治与运力而起伏不定，东盟正成为中国航空"走出去"的避风港与试验田。小国航线看似不起眼，却是这张网最敏感的神经末梢——哪里先通航，哪里的商贸与民间往来就先活起来。深圳—文莱一线，正是这套逻辑的又一次微观验证。

一条支线航线的开通，改变不了区域格局；但无数条这样的支线叠在一起，就是中国—东盟从"相邻"走向"相连"的日常注脚。对观察者而言，值得记住的不是某班飞机的起降，而是这张网仍在以肉眼可见的速度，往更小的国家、更偏的角落延伸。

## 延伸阅读

**文莱（Brunei）**：东南亚加里曼丹岛北部的油气君主国，东盟成员国，人口约45万，经济高度依赖油气出口，近年积极推进多元化。

**中国—东盟（China–ASEAN）**：中国与东南亚国家联盟之间的对话与合作框架，2021年建立全面战略伙伴关系，是当前亚太最具活力的区域合作机制之一。

**互联互通（Connectivity）**：指基础设施、规则与人员往来的系统性连通，是"一带一路"倡议与东盟互联互通总体规划共同的核心关切。

# Shenzhen Opens First Direct Flight to Brunei: A Minor Route with a Major Subtext

On the eve of China's National Day golden week, Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport quietly filled a gap on its route map: a direct service from Shenzhen to Bandar Seri Begawan, the capital of Brunei, has officially launched. The carrier is Shenzhen Airlines, with four round trips a week. By volume, the route is far less glamorous than the intercontinental trunk lines out of Beijing, Shanghai or Guangzhou. Yet on the map of China–ASEAN connectivity, this feeder route carries a different weight — it links the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area to a small, oil-and-gas-rich state ringed by the South China Sea, for the first time, by a single non-stop air corridor.

## A gap finally filled

Until now, a traveler flying from Shenzhen to Brunei usually had to transfer in Hong Kong, Singapore or Kuala Lumpur, with journeys often stretching across most of a day. With the new service, Shenzhen and Bandar Seri Begawan no longer need a detour. According to the airport, the route is operated by Shenzhen Airlines — outbound ZH171, return ZH172. This is not a seasonal chartered extra but a regular scheduled service, which means that business, visiting-family and leisure travel between the two places now has a stable aerial backbone.

The timing is telling. The launch coincides with the National Day travel peak, when Shenzhen airport expects passenger flows to stay high. On one side, outbound tourism to Southeast Asia keeps climbing; on the other, the airport uses the golden week to push the new line to market. In itself, that is a small "strong supply meets strong demand" answer sheet for ordinary travelers.

## The numbers: four weekly flights, no transfers

Per Shenzhen Airlines' published schedule: outbound ZH171 operates every Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Saturday, departing Shenzhen at 16:30 and arriving in Bandar Seri Begawan at 19:55; return ZH172 operates every Tuesday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday, leaving Bandar Seri Begawan at 09:55 and arriving in Shenzhen at 13:20 (all times local).

Laid out, the numbers point to "enough frequency, friendly hours." Four round trips cover most weekdays and the weekend; for short-haul business between South China and Brunei, a "go this week, back this week" trip is basically feasible. For a newly opened international feeder, this scheduling density is by no means conservative.

Zoom out, and the context is larger: Shenzhen airport now serves more than 60 international and regional passenger destinations, of which over 30 are to APEC economies; in the Southeast Asia direction alone, it reaches more than 20 major cities with over 250 planned outbound departures a week. Brunei's addition plugs the last obvious hole in that network.

## A small state, a big corridor: why Brunei joins the "air silk road"

Brunei sits on the northern part of the island of Borneo. Small in territory, it is nonetheless a significant Southeast Asian oil and gas producer, an ASEAN member and a Belt and Road partner. The airport explicitly framed the route as "another positive step in continuously expanding the ASEAN aviation market and improving the Belt and Road route layout" — few words, but not light ones.

Why Brunei? First, location: it guards the navigation thoroughfare in the southern South China Sea, where China's shipping, scientific research and energy cooperation frequently intersect. Second, stable politics: China and Brunei established a strategic cooperative partnership years ago, with frequent high-level exchanges. Third, small size, low bargaining cost and fast accession — for a Chinese hub airport, stringing a small state like Brunei into the network carries a limited marginal outlay yet a considerable diplomatic and symbolic payoff. A feeder route often puts the word "connectivity" into practice more concretely than a declaration does.

## More than tourism: trade and diaspora ties

The official release sums up the significance as the familiar trio of "economic and trade exchange, cultural exchange and tourism cooperation." This is no mere boilerplate. Brunei's economy relies heavily on hydrocarbon exports and is urgently pushing diversification, with tourism and services as priority directions; Shenzhen and the Greater Bay Area, in turn, have a real need to export capital, technology and tourist flows. A direct flight drops "Greater Bay Area capacity" and "Brunei's transition" onto the same map of accessibility.

A more hidden thread is the diaspora. Ethnic Chinese make up roughly one tenth of Brunei's population — a minority, yet one that has long dominated retail, catering and small- and medium-sized trade, serving as a natural intermediary for bilateral business. In the past they returned to their ancestral hometowns via third places; now the Shenzhen non-stop adds a shorter, smoother road home between South China's qiaoxiang and Brunei's Chinese community. Air corridors never carry passengers alone; they carry connections too.

## A regional mirror: China's ASEAN route expansion

Step back from Brunei, and Shenzhen is no outlier. In recent years, multiple Chinese hub airports have been thickening their ASEAN routes: Nanning and Kunming keep expanding on the back of the China–ASEAN Expo and Lancang–Mekong cooperation, while Guangzhou and Shenzhen pitch on the strength of the Greater Bay Area and RCEP dividends. Southeast Asia, with the triple advantage of "moderate flight time, rich tourism resources and strong industrial-chain stickiness," has become the steadiest bedrock of Chinese airports' internationalization.

Behind this sits a clear logic: as China–US routes fluctuate under politics and capacity constraints, ASEAN is becoming both a "safe haven" and a "test bed" for China's aviation going global. Small-state routes look inconspicuous, yet they are the most sensitive nerve endings of the network — wherever they open first, commerce and people-to-people exchange come alive first. The Shenzhen–Brunei line is one more micro-validation of that logic.

The launch of a single feeder route changes no regional order; but stacked together, countless such feeders become the everyday footnote of China and ASEAN moving from "neighbors" to "connected." For the observer, what is worth remembering is not the takeoff and landing of any one flight, but that this network keeps extending, at a visible speed, toward smaller countries and more out-of-the-way corners.

## Further reading

**Brunei**: An oil-and-gas sultanate on the northern coast of Borneo in Southeast Asia, an ASEAN member with a population of around 450,000; its economy depends heavily on hydrocarbon exports while actively pursuing diversification.

**China–ASEAN**: The dialogue and cooperation framework between China and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations; the comprehensive strategic partnership established in 2021 makes it one of the most dynamic regional mechanisms in the Asia-Pacific.

**Connectivity**: The systematic linkage of infrastructure, rules and people-to-people exchange — a shared core concern of both the Belt and Road Initiative and ASEAN's Master Plan on ASEAN Connectivity.

# 越南拟将北宁、广宁升格为中央直辖市：三星枢纽与下龙湾背后的增长赌注

越南正试图把北宁（Bac Ninh）与广宁（Quang Ninh）两座外资重镇，升格为直属于中央政府的直辖市。据日经亚洲报道，这一举动旨在为三星（Samsung）制造枢纽与下龙湾（Ha Long Bay）所在的增长极注入加速度——在“中国+1（China+1）”的产业链重构中，河内想把最肥沃的两块引资田地，直接攥进中央手里。

## 三星的北宁：一座为出口而生的城市

北宁与三星的绑定始于二〇〇八年。那一年，这家韩国电子巨头在越南开设了第一座手机工厂，此后的北宁便逐渐成为其全球制造版图的核心。截至二〇二四年底，三星在越南累计投资已达二百三十二亿美元（约一千六百七十亿元人民币），并计划再追加二十亿美元（约一百四十四亿元人民币）。今日的北宁，已是越南最大的出口地：二〇二五年其出口额约九百三十亿美元（约六千七百亿元人民币），接近全国出口总值的两成。富士康（Foxconn）、歌尔（Goertek）、立讯精密（Luxshare）等也沿三星而来，聚成电子集群。二〇二六年上半年，北宁地区生产总值增速达百分之十点五六；规划中的嘉平（Gia Binh）国际机场将于二〇二七年投运，以缓解河内内排机场的拥堵。

## 广宁：从煤矿到家门口的“中国+1”门户

与北宁并立的广宁，则走着另一条路。这里坐拥下龙湾——一处被联合国教科文组织列入名录的遗产，石灰岩岛屿在碧水中排布如画卷。但广宁的故事不止于旅游：去年它以百分之十一点九的增速，成为越南最具活力的经济体之一。更关键的是它的位置——与中国的广西壮族自治区接壤，凭借港口与工业区，正成为中越供应链里的一扇门户。规划中的河内—下龙高铁预计二〇二八年底前通车，届时两地车程将从约两个半小时压缩到二十三分钟。对想在北方的“中国+1”节点上落子的企业而言，广宁的吸引力正快速上升。

## 升格的逻辑：把招商权收到中央

越南目前有七座中央直辖市。若提案获国会通过，北宁与广宁将加入这一行列。升格的意义，远不止一块城市招牌。中央直辖市在预算、基建与政策上直通中央政府，少了中间层级，决策与落地的摩擦更小。咨询机构人士解读，这等于中央明确宣告：这两地是国家战略资产。放在外资犹豫、全球产业链重组的当口，这是一种稳预期的信号——河内要的不是一时的招商热潮，而是一张可长期兑现的工业蓝图。

## 中国+1 下的越南赌注

把两座城市升格，本质是一场关于“中国+1”的赌注。中美博弈之下，大批产能与订单涌向越南，北宁的工厂与广宁的港口正是承接者。但赌注也有另一面：过度依赖单一投资者，会让本地经济随一家企业的周期起伏；而代工虽带来就业与出口，核心技术、研发与品牌仍握在别人手里。越南要真正从“世界工厂的车间”走向“区域技术枢纽”，需要的不是更多的厂房，而是更深的本地配套、更稳的规则与更高的人均附加值。升格只是起点，能不能把外资红利沉淀为内生能力，才是这场增长实验真正的考题。

## 延伸阅读

**外商直接投资（FDI, Foreign Direct Investment）**：指一国企业或个人在海外长期持有并经营资产的投资行为，是越南承接产业转移、拉动出口的主要资金来源。

**中国+1（China+1）**：跨国企业在对华布局之外，另设东南亚等备选生产基地的策略，意在分散地缘与关税风险；越南是主要承接国之一。

**北宁（Bac Ninh）与广宁（Quang Ninh）**：越南北方两省，前者为三星电子制造重镇，后者以下龙湾与临边区位成为中越供应链门户。

# Vietnam's Bet on Two New Cities: Samsung's Hub and Ha Long Bay as Growth Poles

Vietnam is seeking to turn Bac Ninh and Quang Ninh — two of its biggest FDI magnets — into centrally governed cities answering directly to Hanoi. According to Nikkei Asia, the move is meant to accelerate the Samsung manufacturing hub and the Ha Long Bay growth pole: in the supply-chain rewiring of "China+1", Hanoi wants to pull its two richest investment fields directly into central hands.

## Samsung's Bac Ninh: a city built to export

Bac Ninh's bond with Samsung began in 2008, when the Korean electronics giant opened its first phone factory in Vietnam; the province has since become the core of its global manufacturing footprint. By the end of 2024, Samsung's cumulative investment in Vietnam had reached US$23.2 billion, with a further US$2 billion planned. Today Bac Ninh is Vietnam's largest exporting locality: in 2025 its exports reached about US$93 billion, close to a fifth of the national total. Foxconn, Goertek and Luxshare followed Samsung in, forming an electronics cluster. In the first half of 2026 Bac Ninh's GRDP grew 10.56%; the planned Gia Binh international airport, due in 2027, will ease congestion at Hanoi's Noi Bai.

## Quang Ninh: from coal to a "China+1" gateway next door

Quang Ninh tells a different story. It holds Ha Long Bay, a UNESCO-listed seascape of limestone karsts. But Quang Ninh is more than tourism: last year, with 11.9% growth, it was among Vietnam's most dynamic economies. Its location matters most — bordering China's Guangxi, with ports and industrial zones, it is becoming a gateway in the Vietnam–China supply chain. A Hanoi–Ha Long high-speed railway, expected by end-2028, will cut travel from about two and a half hours to twenty-three minutes. For firms seeking a northern "China+1" node, Quang Ninh's pull is rising fast.

## The logic of upgrading: pulling investment power to the centre

Vietnam currently has seven centrally governed cities. If the National Assembly approves, Bac Ninh and Quang Ninh join them. The upgrade is far more than a city title. Centrally governed status links budget, infrastructure and policy straight to the central government, trimming the friction of intermediate layers. Analysts read it as Hanoi declaring these two localities "strategic national assets." Amid wary foreign investors and a reorganising global supply chain, it is a signal of stability — not a one-off investment rush, but a long-horizon industrial blueprint.

## Vietnam's bet under China+1

Upgrading two cities is, at root, a bet on China+1. As US–China rivalry pushes capacity and orders toward Vietnam, Bac Ninh's factories and Quang Ninh's ports are the recipients. But the wager has a flip side: over-reliance on a single investor leaves the local economy exposed to one company's cycle, and contract manufacturing brings jobs and exports while core technology, R&D and brands stay elsewhere. For Vietnam to move from "workshop of the world" to "regional technology hub", it needs deeper local supply chains, steadier rules and higher per-capita value-add — not more factory floors. The upgrade is a start; whether foreign-investment dividends harden into endogenous capacity is the real test.

## Further reading

**Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: Long-term investment by a foreign firm or individual that operates assets abroad; the main source of capital behind Vietnam's industrial relocation and export surge.

**China+1**: A strategy in which multinationals keep a China base but add alternative production sites in places like Vietnam, to spread geopolitical and tariff risk; Vietnam is a leading recipient.

**Bac Ninh and Quang Ninh**: Two northern Vietnamese provinces — the former Samsung's manufacturing heartland, the latter a Vietnam–China supply-chain gateway built around Ha Long Bay and its border location.

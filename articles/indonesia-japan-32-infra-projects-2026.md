# 32个项目的东京清单：印尼如何把基建做成外交

8月底，雅加达在东京办了一场"招商会"——印度尼西亚—日本交通与基础设施商业论坛暨对接会。印尼端出32个国企与地方国企牵头的战略基建项目，标的总体规模约207.64万亿印尼盾（约合125亿美元，约900亿元人民币），递给36家日本企业的50名代表。在2029年冲8%增长的口号下，这场推介既是生意，也是外交。

## 32个项目，一份东京清单

清单由9家国企与地方国企（BUMD）打包，涵盖12条收费公路与公路、10处房地产与以交通为导向的开发（TOD）、6个铁路及TOD项目、2个港口与物流设施、2处钢结构制造。从雅加达大众捷运（MRT）到巴蒂班港，印尼把最成熟的现金流资产摆上货架，目标明确：用已验证的收益，换日本的技术、纪律与长期资本。

## 从雅加达MRT到巴蒂班港

日本早已是印尼基建的"老搭档"。雅加达大众捷运（MRT）网络、西爪哇巴蒂班港，都是东京留下的标志性工程。印尼驻东京临时代办玛利亚在论坛上强调，日本是"最一致、最可信"的伙伴。对印尼而言，续写这些合作，不只是建项目，更是把本国工程标准、运维体系与日本深度绑定。

## 5.61%增速下的招商逻辑

印尼今年上半年经济增速5.61%，通胀受控于3.34%，上半年实际投资已超1010万亿印尼盾（约5729亿美元，约4.1万亿元人民币）。在全球不确定性的背景下，这套基本面成了招商话术的底气。印尼想借日本资本填补基建缺口，同时换取技术转移与就业——这正是其"8%增长"野心的支点。

## 为什么是日本

在众多金主中挑日本，有其算计。日本提供的不只是资金，还有工程品质、按期交付的纪律、技术转移与人才培训。对印尼来说，中国节奏快、规模大，但日本在项目治理与长期伙伴关系上的口碑，恰是主权信用评级（标普维持BBB、展望稳定）看重的软指标。多元融资，本就是发展中国家的理性选择。

## 8%增长目标与外资依赖

佐科（Jokowi）时代的"下游化"叙事之后，普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）政府把8%增长设为2029年硬目标，而基建投资被寄予厚望。但8%的胃口，单靠财政与国企扛不动，必须引入外资。印尼一边对日推介，一边也在对中西多方招商——32个项目只是清单之一，真正的考验是落地率，而非发布会的热度。

## 国企资产包的双刃剑

把9家国企的成熟资产打包出让，好处是降低投资风险、缩短回本周期；隐忧是优质资产外流、未来收益被分走。印尼用"已验证现金流"换资本，本质是拿存量博增量。如何定价、如何保留战略控制权，将决定这轮招商是壮大本国企业，还是把下金蛋的鹅提前卖了。

## 区域基建竞赛中的印尼牌

在东南亚，越南靠制造、泰国靠汽车与数据中心、马来西亚靠AI枢纽争抢资本；印尼的牌是体量、资源与年轻人口。把基建做成对日外交，是印尼在区域竞赛中巩固"节点"地位的一手。日本的参与，也使其在与中国、西方的基建博弈中，多了一张东南亚的稳定牌。

## 一场双向的耐心游戏

日企态度审慎而持续：2025年JETRO调查显示，69.1%在印尼日企预期盈利。但大型项目周期长、汇率与政策风险并存。这场对接不是一锤子买卖，而是日本用技术换市场、印尼用资产换资本的长期博弈。谁更有耐心，谁就笑到最后。

## 延伸阅读

**印尼—日本交通与基础设施商业论坛**：2026年8月27—28日在东京印尼驻日使馆举办，由印尼经济统筹部与驻日使馆共同组织，意在连接项目业主与日本投资者。

**巴蒂班港（Patimban Port）**：西爪哇深水港，日本援建标志性项目之一，旨在疏解雅加达丹戎不碌港拥堵、服务制造业出口。

**标普BBB评级**：国际评级机构标普给予印尼的主权信用评级，展望"稳定"，反映其经济基本面与外资信心。

# Indonesia's Tokyo List of 32 Projects: Turning Infrastructure into Diplomacy

In late August, Jakarta staged a "roadshow" in Tokyo — the Indonesia–Japan Transportation and Infrastructure Business Forum and matching event. Indonesia tabled 32 strategic infrastructure projects led by state and regional state-owned enterprises, with an indicative value of about Rp207.64 trillion (roughly US$12.5 billion). The pitch went to 50 representatives from 36 Japanese firms. Beneath the 2029 ambition of 8% growth, this was both business and statecraft.

## 32 projects, a Tokyo shopping list

The package was assembled by nine state and regional enterprises, spanning 12 toll and arterial roads, 10 property and transit-oriented development (TOD) sites, six railway and TOD projects, two ports and logistics facilities, and two steel-fabrication plants. From Jakarta's MRT to Patimban Port, Indonesia put its most cash-generating assets on the shelf — the aim was clear: trade proven revenue streams for Japanese technology, discipline and long-term capital.

## From Jakarta's MRT to Patimban Port

Japan is already Indonesia's veteran infrastructure partner. The Jakarta Mass Rapid Transit network and West Java's Patimban Port are Tokyo's signature projects. Indonesia's chargé d'affaires in Tokyo called Japan its "most consistent and trusted" partner. For Jakarta, extending these ties binds its engineering standards and operations to Japan — more than building, it is institutional alignment.

## The investment logic behind 5.61% growth

Indonesia grew 5.61% in the first half of 2026, with inflation contained at 3.34% and realised investment exceeding Rp1,010 trillion (about US$57.3 billion). Amid global uncertainty, those fundamentals became the pitch's backbone. Jakarta wants Japanese capital to fill the infrastructure gap while extracting technology transfer and jobs — the very lever behind its 8% ambition.

## Why Japan

Among many funders, Japan was chosen with calculation. It offers not just money but engineering quality, on-time delivery discipline, technology transfer and training. China moves faster and bigger, yet Japan's reputation for project governance and long-term partnership is exactly the soft indicator sovereign raters (S&P holds Indonesia at BBB, stable) reward. Diversifying funders is rational for any developing economy.

## The 8% target and the foreign-capital dependency

After the Jokowi-era "downstreaming" narrative, the Prabowo government set 8% growth as a 2029 hard target, with infrastructure investment shouldering heavy hopes. But that appetite cannot be carried by the budget and state firms alone; foreign capital is essential. Jakarta is courting Japan, China and the West simultaneously — 32 projects are one list, and the real test is conversion rate, not launch-stage heat.

## The double-edged SOE asset package

Bundling nine SOEs' mature assets lowers investment risk and shortens payback; the downside is that prime assets leave domestic hands and future earnings are shared away. Trading "proven cash flow" for capital is, at heart, leveraging existing stock for new growth. How to price and retain strategic control will decide whether this round strengthens domestic firms or sells the golden geese early.

## Indonesia's hand in the regional infrastructure race

In Southeast Asia, Vietnam competes on manufacturing, Thailand on autos and data centres, Malaysia on AI hubs. Indonesia's cards are scale, resources and a young population. Turning infrastructure into Japan-facing diplomacy consolidates its "node" status in the regional race. Japan's participation also gives it a stable Southeast Asian chip amid infrastructure rivalry with China and the West.

## A two-way game of patience

Japanese firms are cautious but steady: a 2025 JETRO survey found 69.1% of Japanese companies in Indonesia expected operating profit. Yet mega-projects are long-cycled, with currency and policy risks. This matching is no one-off deal but a long game of Japan swapping technology for market, Indonesia swapping assets for capital. The more patient side wins.

# 菲律宾南海政策转向观察

> 马尼拉在华盛顿与北京之间的钟摆。

菲律宾的南海政策，像一根钟摆——杜特尔特时期向北京倾斜，小马科斯执政后又回摆向华盛顿。这种钟摆式摆动，既折射出马尼拉在大国间的战略两难，也映射出整个东盟在地缘竞争中的处境。

## 钟摆的两次摆动

### 第一次：杜特尔特的"亲华疏美"

2016 年上台的杜特尔特，选择了一条与前任阿基诺三世截然不同的路径。他淡化 2016 年南海仲裁案的成果，搁置与北京的主权争议，换取中国承诺的大规模基础设施投资。

> 这一度被视为小国在大国间"逆操作"的成功案例。然而兑现的投资规模，远低于承诺。

### 第二次：小马科斯的"回归美菲同盟"

2022 年上台的小马科斯，迅速扭转方向。EDCA（加强防务合作协议）下美军可使用的菲律宾基地增至 9 个，美菲联合巡航常态化，马尼拉在仁爱礁、黄岩岛等问题上的姿态也明显趋硬。

| 维度 | 杜特尔特时期 | 小马科斯时期 |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| 对华基调 | 友好搁置 | 趋硬对峙 |
| 美军基地 | 5 个 | 9 个 |
| 仲裁案立场 | 淡化 | 重申 |
| 经贸重心 | 中国 | 美日韩 |

## 钟摆的逻辑

钟摆不是随意的。它背后是菲律宾国内三股力量的拉锯：

1. **军方与安全集团**——历史上亲美，是 EDCA 扩张的主要推动者。
2. **经济与地方势力**——依赖对华贸易与中国游客、中国投资。
3. **民族主义舆论**——在主权议题上对任何让步都高度敏感。

三股力量的强弱随国内外环境变化，决定了钟摆的方向。**理解钟摆，比判断哪一摆"正确"更重要。**

## 对东盟的隐喻

菲律宾的钟摆，是整个东盟处境的隐喻。东盟国家普遍面临同一个困境：安全上无法完全摆脱美国体系，经济上又与中国深度绑定，而在主权议题上又必须维持民族主义的底线。

东盟式的"对冲与模糊"，在中美关系尚可时游刃有余；一旦中美进入对抗区间，模糊空间就被压缩，"选边"的压力骤增。

## 结语

菲律宾的钟摆仍会摆动。下一摆朝哪边，取决于大国竞争的烈度、国内政治的更替，以及南海局势的演变。

但有一点是确定的：**对一个发展中国家而言，在主权与利益、安全与经济之间反复权衡，不是软弱，而是生存的常态。** 只是这种常态，代价高昂。

# Watching the Philippines' South China Sea Pivot

> Manila's pendulum between Washington and Beijing.

The Philippines' South China Sea policy is a pendulum — swinging toward Beijing under Duterte, then back toward Washington under Marcos Jr. That pendular motion reflects Manila's strategic dilemma between the great powers and mirrors ASEAN's predicament amid geopolitical competition.

## Two Swings of the Pendulum

### First Swing: Duterte's "China Tilt, America Drift"

Taking office in 2016, Rodrigo Duterte chose a path sharply different from his predecessor Benigno Aquino III. He downplayed the 2016 South China Sea arbitration outcome, shelved sovereignty disputes with Beijing, and traded for China's promised large-scale infrastructure investment.

> For a time this was hailed as a successful "reverse play" by a small state between great powers. The investment that materialized, however, fell far short of the promises.

### Second Swing: Marcos Jr.'s "Return to the US Alliance"

Ferdinand Marcos Jr., in office from 2022, swiftly reversed course. Under EDCA (Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement), the number of Philippine bases accessible to US forces rose to nine, joint US–Philippine patrols became routine, and Manila's posture on Second Thomas Shoal and Scarborough Shoal hardened markedly.

| Dimension | Duterte era | Marcos Jr. era |
|------|--------------|--------------|
| Posture toward China | Friendly, shelved | Hardening standoff |
| US bases | 5 | 9 |
| Arbitration stance | Downplayed | Reaffirmed |
| Economic focus | China | US, Japan, South Korea |

## The Logic of the Pendulum

The swing is not arbitrary. Behind it is the tug of three domestic forces:

1. **The military and security establishment** — historically pro-US, the main driver of EDCA's expansion.
2. **Economic and local interests** — dependent on trade with China, Chinese tourists, and Chinese investment.
3. **Nationalist opinion** — highly sensitive to any concession on sovereignty.

The relative strength of these three shifts with the domestic and international environment, setting the pendulum's direction. **Understanding the pendulum matters more than judging which swing was "right."**

## A Metaphor for ASEAN

The Philippine pendulum is a metaphor for ASEAN's predicament as a whole. ASEAN states broadly face the same dilemma: they cannot fully exit the US security system, are deeply bound to China economically, and must uphold a nationalist baseline on sovereignty.

ASEAN-style "hedging and ambiguity" works smoothly when US–China relations are tolerable; once they slip into confrontation, the ambiguous space compresses and the pressure to "choose sides" spikes.

## Conclusion

The Philippine pendulum will keep swinging. The next swing's direction depends on the intensity of great-power competition, domestic political turnover, and the evolution of the South China Sea situation.

But one thing is certain: **for a developing country, weighing sovereignty against interest, security against economy, again and again, is not weakness — it is the norm of survival.** And that norm comes at a high cost.

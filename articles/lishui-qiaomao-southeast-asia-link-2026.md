# 以侨为桥 贸通世界：丽水侨贸"百千万"工程开辟东南亚新链路

近日，浙江丽水企业纳爱斯集团价值12万美元（约86万元人民币）的3柜日化产品顺利发往柬埔寨，被柬埔寨浙江总商会会长、丽水青田籍华侨施永平称为"丽水企业首次试单开辟东南亚全新侨贸链路"。这笔看似寻常的出口，背后是丽水正在统筹推进的侨贸"百千万"工程——以侨为桥，把散落全球的侨胞商业网络，变成可触达、可转化的外贸新通道。

## 一笔12万美元的试单，撬动一条新链路

据中国侨网报道，纳爱斯这批货物并非通过传统外贸代理商出海，而是在侨联组织的牵线搭桥下，直接对接柬埔寨当地的侨商渠道。施永平长期从事柬埔寨与浙江之间的经贸往来，他口中的"全新链路"，指的是过去丽水日用化工品多经第三方贸易商转口，而此次经由侨胞自有分销网络，实现了从工厂到东南亚终端更短的路径。

单看金额，12万美元（约86万元人民币）并不算大；但作为一种"试单"模式，它的意义在于验证了一条可由侨胞网络复制的出口通道。对许多中小制造企业而言，东南亚市场最大的门槛不是产能，而是渠道、信用与本地合规——而这些恰恰是长期扎根当地的侨商所擅长的。

## 青田侨胞：散在柬埔寨的"桥"

丽水下辖的青田县，是中国著名的侨乡，数十万青田籍侨胞遍布欧洲与东南亚。在柬埔寨，青田籍商人早已深耕批发、零售与物流环节，形成了成熟的侨商社群。施永平这样的纽带人物，既是商会领袖，也是家乡企业与住在国市场之间的"翻译器"——他熟悉两地规则、信用与需求，能把一句家乡的招商口号，变成具体的订单与货架。

这正是"以侨为桥"最朴素的含义：侨胞不只是走出去的劳动力或资本，更是连接两个市场的制度性接口。当国内企业想触达陌生的东南亚，最省力的方式未必是重新搭建一套外贸体系，而是借力已经长在当地的侨商网络。

## 从"百千万"工程看侨贸一体化逻辑

侨贸"百千万"工程，意在统筹推进侨贸一体化——百个侨团、千家企业、万家网点，把分散的侨胞资源组织成系统性的贸易能力。与单纯依赖跨境电商平台不同，侨贸更强调"人"的信任与在地服务：货物到了，有人清关；到了货架，有人推销；遇到纠纷，有人协调。

这种模式在不确定性上升的全球贸易环境中反而显出韧性。当中美博弈抬高部分转口成本、当东南亚各国对原产地与合规审查趋严，由侨胞背书、熟悉本地规则的链路，往往比纯平台化出口更稳。侨贸不是要替代既有外贸体系，而是补上"最后一公里"的人情与信用。

## 东南亚供应链重组中的侨网红利

更大的背景是东南亚供应链的重组。劳动力与关税的再配置，让越南、柬埔寨、马来西亚成为新的制造与集散节点；而中国与这些节点之间，不仅需要货物通道，更需要理解与信任的通道。青田侨胞在柬埔寨的百年积淀，恰好提供了这条软通道。

丽水此番试单，看似只是一个地级市的招商小动作，却折射出"侨"作为战略资源的重新被发现。当"以侨为桥、贸通世界"从标语走向订单，侨贸一体化的红利，或许才刚刚开始兑现。

*话题参考："中国侨网"（chinaqw.com）侨商投资频道——"丽水实施侨贸'百千万'工程，统筹推进侨贸一体化——以侨为桥 贸通世界"。*

## 延伸阅读

**侨贸（Qiaomao）**：指依托海外华侨华人商业网络开展的贸易活动，强调以侨胞信任与在地渠道降低跨境交易成本，是中国"以侨为桥"经贸战略的具体载体。

**青田侨乡（Qingtian Qiaoxiang）**：浙江丽水下辖的著名侨乡，数十万青田籍侨胞遍布欧洲与东南亚，在住在国批发、零售与物流领域积淀深厚，是侨贸网络的重要节点。

**供应链（Supply Chain）**：产品从原材料到终端消费者的全链路体系；东南亚供应链重组正使该区域成为新的制造与集散中心，侨胞网络则在其中提供软性信任通道。

# Bridging Homeland and Diaspora: Lishui's "Hundred-Thousand-Ten-Thousand" Qiaomao Program Opens a New Southeast Asia Trade Link

Recently, three containers of daily-care products worth US$120,000 (about RMB 860,000) from Lishui's Nice Group in Zhejiang were shipped to Cambodia. Shi Yongping, head of the Cambodia Zhejiang Chamber of Commerce and a Qingtian-born overseas Chinese from Lishui, called it "Lishui enterprises' first trial opening a brand-new qiaomao (overseas-Chinese-trade) link to Southeast Asia." A seemingly ordinary export, it sits on top of Lishui's "Hundred-Thousand-Ten-Thousand" qiaomao program — using the diaspora as a bridge to turn globally scattered overseas-Chinese business networks into reachable, convertible trade channels.

## A US$120,000 trial order pries open a new link

According to China Qiaowang, Nice Group's shipment did not go overseas through a conventional trading agent. Instead, with the federation of returned overseas Chinese acting as matchmaker, it connected directly to Cambodian overseas-Chinese distribution channels. The "brand-new link" in Shi's words refers to a shorter path from factory to Southeast Asian shelf: in the past Lishui's daily-care goods were re-exported via third-party traders, but this time they moved through diaspora-owned networks.

In pure value terms, US$120,000 (about RMB 860,000) is modest. Yet as a "trial" model, its value lies in proving a replicable export channel built on diaspora networks. For many small and mid-sized manufacturers, the real barrier to the Southeast Asian market is not production capacity but channels, credit and local compliance — precisely what overseas-Chinese merchants rooted locally do best.

## Qingtian compatriots: the "bridge" scattered across Cambodia

Qingtian county under Lishui is a famed qiaoxiang (ancestral home of overseas Chinese), with hundreds of thousands of Qingtian-born compatriots across Europe and Southeast Asia. In Cambodia, Qingtian businessmen have long operated in wholesale, retail and logistics, forming a mature overseas-Chinese community. Figures like Shi are both chamber leaders and "translators" between hometown firms and the host-market — fluent in the rules, credit systems and demands of both sides, able to turn a hometown investment slogan into concrete orders and shelf space.

This is the plainest meaning of "using the diaspora as a bridge": overseas Chinese are not merely labour or capital that went abroad, but institutional interfaces connecting two markets. When domestic firms want to reach an unfamiliar Southeast Asia, the easiest route may not be building a new trade system from scratch, but leveraging diaspora networks already planted locally.

## The logic of qiaomao integration behind the "Hundred-Thousand-Ten-Thousand" program

The "Hundred-Thousand-Ten-Thousand" program aims to integrate qiaomao systematically — a hundred clansmen associations, a thousand enterprises, ten thousand outlets — organizing scattered diaspora resources into structured trade capacity. Unlike pure cross-border e-commerce platforms, qiaomao stresses the trust and local service of "people": when goods arrive, someone clears customs; on the shelf, someone sells; in a dispute, someone mediates.

This model shows resilience precisely when global trade uncertainty rises. As the US–China rivalry lifts some re-export costs and Southeast Asian countries tighten rules of origin and compliance, a link endorsed by compatriots and familiar with local rules is often steadier than purely platform-based exports. Qiaomao does not replace the existing trade system; it supplies the human trust of the "last mile."

## The diaspora dividend amid Southeast Asia's supply-chain rewiring

The larger backdrop is the rewiring of Southeast Asia's supply chains. The reallocation of labour and tariffs is turning Vietnam, Cambodia and Malaysia into new manufacturing and distribution hubs; what China needs with these nodes is not only a goods corridor but a channel of understanding and trust. The century-deep Qingtian presence in Cambodia happens to provide that soft corridor.

Lishui's trial order looks like a small investment-promotion move by a prefecture-level city, yet it reflects the rediscovery of the "diaspora" as a strategic resource. When "using the diaspora as a bridge to connect the world" moves from slogan to order, the dividend of qiaomao integration may only just be starting to pay out.

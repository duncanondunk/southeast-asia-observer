# 缅甸总统敏昂莱访越：军政府"合法性外交"的又一站

9月1日，越南外交部宣布，缅甸总统敏昂莱及夫人应越共中央总书记、国家主席苏林邀请，将于9月4日至6日对越南进行正式访问。这是敏昂莱自2026年4月10日出任总统以来首次访越，也是其"地区再接触"外交攻势的最新一站。在东盟因缅甸危机而将其政治代表排除在峰会之外的当下，这场访问既折射出缅甸军政府争取国际承认的努力，也考验着河内在务实交往与政治立场之间的平衡术。

## 事件：河内之行的时点与规格

据越南政府新闻网与缅甸《环球新光》报披露，敏昂莱此行将与苏林举行双边会谈，并分别会见越南总理陈红河与国会主席。访问规格被刻意抬高：他携夫人同行，并计划出席"缅越商业论坛2026"，参观数字与电子科技产业。缅甸方面称此访旨在促进"两国政府间互利共赢的合作"。

时点同样耐人寻味。8月下旬，越南外长黎怀忠刚刚访问内比都，成为2021年政变以来首位到访缅甸的越南外长；双方据报讨论了扩大双边贸易与投资，并提及"在国际舞台相互支持"。河内此番高规格接待，可视为这一铺垫的延续与回报。

## 脉络：一场精心编排的地区巡回

敏昂莱的河内之行，并非孤立事件，而是一场按节奏展开的外交连续剧。自4月通过军方主导的大选就任总统后，他先后访问中国、印度（6月），以及俄罗斯、白俄罗斯、哈萨克斯坦（8月）；在东盟内部，已接待他的国家包括老挝（7月）、泰国（8月），柬埔寨亦据报将于9月初接访。越南是他就任后正式到访的第七个国家。

这套打法的逻辑清晰：通过一连串国事访问、商务论坛与同现任元首的合影，把军政府包装成"正常运作、行使日常外交"的合法政府，从而冲淡2021年政变推翻昂山素季民选政府留下的"贱民"色彩。每一次出访，不论东道主如何措辞，都在为军政府希望构建的叙事添砖加瓦——即其已获国际社会当作缅甸合法声音接纳。

## 东盟困境：接触与集体冷处理的张力

东盟自身的处境因而格外尴尬。一方面，缅甸危机源于军方2021年政变，东盟的"五点共识"要求止暴并展开包容政治对话，而军政府被普遍认为基本未落实；正因如此，东盟持续将缅甸政治代表排除在峰会之外，仅允许非政治性参与。另一方面，个别成员国（尤以泰国为甚）选择双边接触，泰国官员甚至强调支持缅甸"充分、平等参与"东盟机制。

这种"双轨"带来张力：双边交往能维持沟通、缓解人道危机，却也模糊了"重新接触"与"背书"的界线。正如有东南亚学者在敏昂莱上月曼谷之行时所言，两者之间只有"一线之隔"，而越南如今须自行拿捏分寸。

## 越南的平衡：务实交往而非政治背书

对河内而言，此次访问首先是扩大经贸与投资的机遇，被框定为"务实外交"而非政治背书。越南与缅甸1975年建交，近年双边贸易虽体量有限，却在农产品、电信、基建等领域互补；缅甸领导人更公开感谢越南在其发展与东盟事务中的"一贯支持"。

但越南的平衡术并不轻松。作为东盟轮值主席年（2020）后持续推进"四不"防御政策与多边主义的国家，河内既要维持与东盟一致的危机立场，又要保住与缅甸军政府的务实通道——后者关乎其在缅投资（电信、能源）与区域影响力。高规格接待敏昂莱，因而是一场地缘算计，而非价值认同。

## 经贸牌：双边论坛背后的算计

"缅越商业论坛2026"是此访最具实质性的抓手。缅甸方面释放出明确信号：将继续支持在缅经营的中资与外商，并在电信、教育培训、农业、食品加工、物流、数字化转型与科技等领域提供政策便利。对越南企业而言，缅甸市场虽小，却是在东盟腹地延伸产业链、规避部分贸易壁垒的潜在支点。

更深一层，缅越经贸合作也服务于两国各自的"去风险"逻辑：越南在美中之间寻求战略自主，缅甸则渴望外部经济支点以缓解制裁压力。论坛本身或许签不下大单，却足以向外界释放"正常化"信号——这正是军政府最看重的非物质收益。

## 风险：在"重新接触"与"背书"之间走钢丝

越南接过这一站，风险同样清楚。其一，高规格接待可能被解读为对军政府的变相承认，招致西方与东盟内温和派的不满；其二，若缅甸国内局势在访问前后生变（如冲突升级、针对异议者的镇压），河内将陷入被动；其三，军政府"选举"的合法性广受质疑——主要反对党被排除、大片冲突区无法投票、东盟拒绝派观察员，这一切使任何"正常化"叙事都显得脆弱。

河内能做的，是把交往严格限定在经济与人文层面，以"务实"对冲"背书"的观感。但国际叙事从不完全由东道主定义——敏昂莱此行带回的合影与公报，终将被军政府用以证明其"被接纳"。

## 冷思考：访问频率能否兑换成承认

归根结底，敏昂莱的访问频率，未必能直接兑换为国际承认。东盟作为一个整体、以及仍维持制裁的西方国家，是否愿跟随个别成员国脚步，才是关键变量。河内这次接访，更多是地区大国在"接触"与"原则"之间的一次例行权衡，而非缅甸危机的转折点。

对东南亚观察而言，这趟河内之行提供的，是一面观察东盟"双轨"应对缅甸问题的镜子：它既显示个别成员国不愿坐视缅甸彻底孤立，也暴露出集体共识与单边务实之间的持久裂痕。无论敏昂莱走多少个国家，真正决定缅甸命运的，仍是冲突本身能否走向对话——而这，远非几场国事访问所能解决。

## 延伸阅读

**五点共识（Five-Point Consensus）**：东盟2021年就缅甸危机达成的调解框架，要求止暴并启动包容政治对话，军政府被指基本未落实。

**环球新光（Global New Light of Myanmar）**：缅甸军政府控制的官方英文报纸，常发布国家元首外访与政策表态。

**东盟轮值主席国（ASEAN Chair）**：东盟年度轮值机制，因缅甸危机持续，东盟仅允许缅方非政治性参与峰会。

# Myanmar President Min Aung Hlaing Visits Vietnam: Another Stop on the Junta's "Legitimacy Diplomacy"

On September 1, Vietnam's Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced that Myanmar's President Min Aung Hlaing and his spouse would pay an official visit to Vietnam from September 4 to 6, at the invitation of General Secretary and President To Lam. It is Min Aung Hlaing's first visit to Vietnam since assuming the presidency on April 10, 2026, and the latest stop in the junta's "regional re-engagement" offensive. With ASEAN having excluded Myanmar's political representatives from its summits over the crisis, the trip both reflects the military government's bid for international recognition and tests Hanoi's balancing act between pragmatic engagement and political stance.

## The event: timing and protocol of the Hanoi visit

According to Vietnam Government News and Myanmar's Global New Light, Min Aung Hlaing will hold bilateral talks with To Lam and separately meet Prime Minister Le Minh Hung and the National Assembly chairman. The protocol is deliberately elevated: he travels with his wife and is scheduled to attend the "Myanmar–Vietnam Business Forum 2026" and visit digital and electronic-technology industries. Myanmar described the visit as promoting "mutually beneficial cooperation between the two governments."

The timing is also telling. In late August, Vietnamese Foreign Minister Le Hoai Trung visited Naypyidaw — the first Vietnamese foreign minister to do so since the 2021 coup; the two sides reportedly discussed expanding bilateral trade and investment and "mutual support on the international stage." Hanoi's high-profile reception can be read as a continuation and回报 of that groundwork.

## The context: a carefully choreographed regional tour

Min Aung Hlaing's Hanoi trip is not isolated but an act in a paced diplomatic serial. Since taking the presidency through a military-organised election in April, he has visited China and India (June), then Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan (August); within ASEAN, Laos (July) and Thailand (August) have hosted him, with Cambodia also reported to receive him in early September. Vietnam is the seventh country he has officially visited since taking office.

The logic is clear: through a stream of state visits, business forums and photo opportunities with sitting heads of state, the junta is packaged as a "normally functioning government conducting routine diplomacy," diluting the "pariah" stigma left by the 2021 coup that ousted Aung San Suu Kyi's elected administration. Every outbound visit, regardless of the host's framing, adds to the narrative the junta hopes to construct — that it is being accepted as Myanmar's legitimate voice.

## ASEAN's dilemma: tension between engagement and collective cold-shoulder

ASEAN's own position is thus awkward. On one hand, the crisis stems from the military's 2021 coup; the bloc's Five-Point Consensus calls for an end to violence and inclusive political dialogue, which the junta is widely seen as having largely ignored. That is why ASEAN keeps Myanmar's political representatives out of summits, permitting only non-political participation. On the other hand, individual members — Thailand most visibly — choose bilateral engagement, with Thai officials even stressing support for Myanmar's "full and equal participation" in ASEAN mechanisms.

This "two-track" creates tension: bilateral contact maintains communication and eases humanitarian crises, yet blurs the line between "re-engaging" and "endorsing." As one Southeast Asia scholar put it during Min Aung Hlaing's Bangkok trip last month, the two are separated by "a fine line," and Vietnam must now calibrate its own measure.

## Vietnam's balance: pragmatic contact, not political endorsement

For Hanoi, the visit is first an opportunity to expand trade and investment, framed as "pragmatic diplomacy" rather than political endorsement. Vietnam and Myanmar established ties in 1975; although bilateral trade is modest, the two complement each other in agriculture, telecommunications and infrastructure. The Myanmar leader publicly thanked Vietnam for its "consistent support" in his country's development and at ASEAN and regional forums.

But Hanoi's balancing is not easy. As a country that has advanced the "Four No's" defence policy and multilateralism since its 2020 chairmanship, Vietnam must both uphold ASEAN's collective crisis stance and preserve a pragmatic channel to the Myanmar junta — the latter bears on its investments (telecom, energy) and regional influence. Hosting Min Aung Hlaing at high protocol is therefore geopolitical calculus, not value alignment.

## The economic card: the calculus behind the business forum

The "Myanmar–Vietnam Business Forum 2026" is the most substantive lever of the trip. Myanmar sent a clear signal: it will continue to support Chinese-invested and foreign firms operating there, offering policy facilitation in telecommunications, education and training, agriculture, food processing, logistics, digital transformation and technology. For Vietnamese firms, Myanmar's market is small but a potential pivot to extend supply chains into the ASEAN heartland and circumvent some trade barriers.

Deeper still, Myanmar–Vietnam economic cooperation serves both countries' "de-risking" logic: Vietnam seeks strategic autonomy between the US and China, while Myanmar craves external economic anchors to ease sanction pressure. The forum may sign few big deals, but it suffices to signal "normalisation" to the outside world — precisely the non-material dividend the junta values most.

## The risk: walking the tightrope between re-engagement and endorsement

By taking this stop, Vietnam's risks are clear. First, high-protocol reception may be read as implicit recognition of the junta, drawing displeasure from the West and ASEAN moderates. Second, should Myanmar's domestic situation shift around the visit — escalated conflict, crackdowns on dissidents — Hanoi would be left exposed. Third, the legitimacy of the junta's "election" is widely questioned: major opposition parties were excluded, large conflict zones could not vote, and ASEAN refused to send observers, making any "normalisation" narrative fragile.

What Hanoi can do is confine contact strictly to economic and people-to-people spheres, using "pragmatism" to counter the impression of "endorsement." But international narratives are never defined solely by the host — the photos and communiqués Min Aung Hlaing brings home will ultimately be used by the junta to prove its "acceptance."

## A cool note: can visit frequency buy recognition?

In the end, Min Aung Hlaing's visit frequency may not directly buy international recognition. Whether ASEAN as a bloc, and Western states that maintain sanctions, are willing to follow individual members' lead is the key variable. Hanoi's reception is more a routine weighing between "engagement" and "principle" by a regional power than a turning point in the Myanmar crisis.

For Southeast Asia observers, this Hanoi trip offers a mirror on ASEAN's "two-track" response to Myanmar: it shows some members' reluctance to let Myanmar drift into total isolation, yet also exposes the persistent rift between collective consensus and unilateral pragmatism. No matter how many countries Min Aung Hlaing visits, what truly decides Myanmar's fate remains whether the conflict can move toward dialogue — something no number of state visits can resolve.

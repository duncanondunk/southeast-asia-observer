# 中新"海上合作-2026"联演在湛江海域举行：小多边默契里的战略信号

9月3日，中国国防部发布消息：中国和新加坡海军将于9月上旬在广东湛江附近海空域举行"海上合作-2026"联合演习，科目包括实弹射击、海上补给与联合搜救，旨在增进互信、维护地区稳定。在一连串地区安全事件频发的背景下，这场看似例行的联演，实则承载着中新两国对"小国与大国如何相处"的长期探索。

## 一场"例行性"的联演

"海上合作"系列是中新两国海军机制化的联合训练品牌，多聚焦非传统安全与海上意外相遇处置，强调专业性与低敏感。本次演习地点选在广东湛江附近海空域，时间定在9月上旬，科目涵盖实弹射击、海上补给与联合搜救三大类。官方表述直白：增进互信、维护地区稳定。外界不必过度解读，但也不应忽略其连续性。

## 科目里的信号

实弹射击检验协同精度，海上补给考验在航保障能力，联合搜救则指向人道主义与灾难响应。这三类科目共同指向一个能力画像：两国海军能在复杂海况下安全地"并肩作业"。对一个高度依赖海上通道的城市国家与一个有全球利益的大国而言，这种可预测的协作能力，本身就是一种稳定器。

## 中新防务合作的厚度

中新防务关系并非一日之功。两国有着高频的军种互访、联合演习与军官培训传统，新加坡长期是中国周边少数保持密集防务往来的国家之一。这种厚度使双方在南海等敏感议题上即便立场不尽一致，也留有专业沟通的空间，避免误判升级。机制化往来，正是大国与中小国家管控分歧的低成本方式。

## 为什么是湛江

湛江是中国南部重要的海军与远海训练基地，也是面向南海的前沿支点。选择在此海域联演，既是对中方保障能力的实地展示，也便利新方舰艇就近参加。地理上的象征意义同样清晰：演习在中国近海而非争议水域进行，本身就是一种"把合作放在可控空间"的克制表达。

## 小国与大国如何相处

新加坡的外交哲学素来是在大国间保持平衡、以规则维系自主。与中国的常态化防务合作，不构成选边，而是其"全面交往"策略的一环——既参与美国的演习体系，也与中方保持联合训练。对北京而言，与新加坡这样的中等强国保持专业军事往来，有助于把"周边外交"从口号落到可操作的互信机制上。

## 与区域安全网络的咬合

中新联演并非孤立存在。它与中国同东盟多国的防长会、海上联演、联合搜救机制共同构成一张非正式的安全网络。当双边默契足够多，南海行为准则谈判与危机管控就有了更厚实的底层支撑。这也是为什么一场规模有限的演习，会被区域观察者反复解读。

## 不只演习：机制化往来

值得注意的，是"海上合作"系列的稳定性——它不像某些一次性表演性军演，而是年复一年地延续。稳定性意味着可预期性，可预期性降低交易成本。对依赖规则生存的小国尤其如此：它们要的不是每次都重新谈判关系，而是一套能自动运行的往来程序。

## 叙事之外，看什么

外界常把中外联演简化为"秀肌肉"或"站队"两种叙事，二者都失之简单。中新"海上合作-2026"更像一则治理样本：两个体量悬殊的国家，如何通过低敏感科目、年复一年的机制化训练，把潜在摩擦转化为可管理的协作。在南海舆情极易升温的当下，这种"把信任做进流程里"的耐心，或许比任何声明都更稀缺。

## 延伸阅读

**海上意外相遇规则（CUES）**：西太平洋海军论坛通过的专业守则，规范各国军舰在海上的通信、动作与避让，旨在降低误判与擦枪走火风险。

**全面交往（Full Engagement）**：新加坡在大国间保持等距接触、以多边规则维系自主的外交策略，体现为同时参与不同大国的演习与对话机制。

**南海行为准则（COC）**：中国与东盟谈判中的区域性海上行为规范文件，旨在以规则管控分歧、维护南海稳定。

# China and Singapore Launch "Maritime Cooperation-2026" in Waters off Zhanjiang

On 3 September, China's Ministry of National Defence announced that the Chinese and Singaporean navies would hold the "Maritime Cooperation-2026" joint exercise in the sea and airspace off Zhanjiang, Guangdong, in early September. The drill covers live-fire shooting, underway replenishment and joint search-and-rescue, aiming to build mutual trust and uphold regional stability. Amid a string of regional security incidents, this seemingly routine exercise carries the two countries' long-running exploration of how a small state and a great power should get along.

## A "routine" exercise

The "Maritime Cooperation" series is a institutionalised brand of China–Singapore naval training, mostly focused on non-traditional security and professional encounters at sea, emphasising professionalism and low sensitivity. This iteration is set in waters off Zhanjiang in early September, with three categories: live-fire, replenishment and joint search-and-rescue. The official framing is plain: enhance mutual trust, safeguard stability. Outsiders need not over-read it, but should not ignore its continuity either.

## Signals in the subjects

Live-fire tests coordination precision; underway replenishment tests in-mission sustainment; joint search-and-rescue points to humanitarian and disaster response. Together they sketch a capability portrait: the two navies can "operate shoulder to shoulder" safely in complex conditions. For a city-state dependent on sea lines and a great power with global interests, this predictable capacity is itself a stabiliser.

## The depth of China–Singapore defence ties

China–Singapore defence relations are not built in a day. The two sides share a tradition of frequent service visits, joint exercises and officer training; Singapore is among the few of China's neighbours maintaining dense defence exchanges. This depth leaves professional communication space even when positions on sensitive issues such as the South China Sea diverge, preventing miscalculation from escalating. Institutionalised exchange is precisely the low-cost way for great and small powers to manage differences.

## Why Zhanjiang

Zhanjiang is a major southern naval and far-seas training base, and a forward pivot facing the South China Sea. Holding the exercise in these waters showcases China's sustainment capability on the spot and lets Singaporean ships join nearby. The geographic symbolism is also clear: the drill takes place in China's near seas rather than disputed waters — a restrained expression of "keeping cooperation in controllable space".

## How a small state meets a great power

Singapore's diplomatic philosophy has long been to balance among great powers and preserve autonomy through rules. Routine defence cooperation with China is not bandwagoning but one strand of its "full engagement" strategy — joining US-led exercise systems while maintaining joint training with Beijing. For Beijing, sustained professional military contact with a middle power like Singapore helps turn "peripheral diplomacy" from slogan into operable trust mechanisms.

## Meshing with the regional security web

The China–Singapore drill is not isolated. It forms, with China's defence ministers' meetings, maritime exercises and search-and-rescue mechanisms with multiple ASEAN states, an informal security web. When bilateral tacit understandings accumulate, the Code of Conduct negotiations and crisis management gain a thicker substrate. That is why an exercise of limited scale is read and re-read by regional observers.

## More than a drill: institutionalised exchange

Notable is the stability of the "Maritime Cooperation" series — unlike one-off demonstrative exercises, it recurs year after year. Stability means predictability; predictability lowers transaction costs. For small states that survive on rules, what they want is not renegotiating the relationship each time, but a set of procedures that run automatically.

## Beyond the narrative, what to watch

Observers often reduce Sino-foreign exercises to two narratives — "muscle-flexing" or "taking sides" — both simplistic. "Maritime Cooperation-2026" reads more like a governance sample: two states of vastly different weight, through low-sensitivity subjects and year-after-year institutional training, converting potential friction into manageable cooperation. As South China Sea sentiment easily overheats, this patience of "building trust into process" may be scarcer than any statement.

## Further reading

**CUES (Code for Unplanned Encounters at Sea)**: A professional code adopted by the Western Pacific Naval Symposium governing communication, manoeuvre and avoidance between warships, aimed at reducing miscalculation and accidental escalation.

**Full Engagement**: Singapore's diplomatic strategy of equidistant contact among great powers and rule-based autonomy, embodied in joining different powers' exercise and dialogue mechanisms simultaneously.

**Code of Conduct in the South China Sea (COC)**: A regional maritime behaviour document negotiated between China and ASEAN to manage differences through rules and uphold stability.

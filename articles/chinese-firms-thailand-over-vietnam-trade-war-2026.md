# 贸易战2.0下中企"中国+1"：为何从越南转向泰国

《南华早报（South China Morning Post）》观察，在贸易战2.0与转运审查收紧的双重压力下，部分中企正把产能从中国经越南，再向泰国做二次转移。这并非简单的区位切换，而是全球产业链"避险逻辑"的一次再校准。

## 一组正在重画的地图

自2018年中美贸易摩擦起，"中国+1（China Plus One）"先是董事会里的流行词，到2025年已成制造企业的基线风控。越南凭借廉价劳动力与便利出口，成为中企出海的第一站。但到了2026年，地图开始重画：部分企业把目光从越南移向泰国，曼谷也变得审慎——尤其警惕美国限制中企借道转运的措施。

一条原本"中国→越南"的转移线，正在被拉长成"中国→越南→泰国"的二次曲线。

## 背景：越南为何"失宠"

越南并非不好，而是到了天花板。它胜在劳动力便宜、出口政策便利、营商环境相对稳定，是低端产能的首选。但产业链配套不够深，核心零部件仍依赖中国进口，难以承接中高端制造。

更关键的是关税风险。越南对美贸易顺差在2024年创下1230亿美元（约8856亿元人民币）的历史新高，美国已将转运审查扩至贸易顺差较大的国家。特朗普政府据信将更严格执行原产地规则、扩大海关调查，堵住借道越南"洗产地"的漏洞。越南的便宜，正被它的顺差反噬。

## 数字：泰国凭什么接住

泰国能接住这波转移，靠的是工业生态而非单纯低价。泰国投资促进委员会（BOI）数据显示，2024年来自中国的促投申请达982份，占外国投资项目总数的40%，高居第一，总投资额达1.72万亿泰铢（约344亿元人民币）；2026年第一季度，中国仍遥遥领先，促投项目155个、投资额1732.7亿泰铢（约34.7亿元人民币）。

阿努廷（Anutin）执政周年高规格访华期间，四家中国公司更宣布对泰投资合计超7000亿泰铢（约140亿元人民币），其中5000亿泰铢用于扩大现有投资。东部的东部经济走廊（EEC）覆盖春武里、罗勇、北柳三府，面积达3万平方公里，是泰国承接高端制造的旗舰园区。

## 泰国的底气：生态与稳定

泰国不靠劳动力成本取胜，而靠供应商深度、基础设施、投资激励与政治稳定"通吃"。作为东南亚最大汽车生产国，其供应链成熟度仅次于新加坡与马来西亚，中企落地无需从零搭建配套。

进入2024年后，中国整车厂、电池、电控及零部件企业扎堆落地泰国，形成规模化集聚。对许多工业买家而言，泰国赢在一切，唯独不赢在人工——而这恰恰是高端制造最看重的"生态密度"。

## 隐忧：转运红线与"洗产地"困局

但泰国也并非没有包袱。曼谷比谁都清楚，美国限制中企借道转运的红线就在头顶。纯粹把越南当"换标签"跳板的做法已难以为继，企业必须在泰国做真正本土化生产，否则随时可能撞上原产地核查。

这意味着"中国+1"的泰国版本，成本与合规门槛都更高。对押注泰国的中企而言，便宜的窗口正在收窄，留下的必须是真产能，而非空壳转运。

## 谁在改写东南亚的产业版图

把镜头拉远，这场迁移是一场多国竞速。印尼动作最密集，2025年吸收中国投资54亿美元、2026年上半年近40亿美元，却因政策摇摆把部分观望中资推走；马来西亚凭半导体基础争夺中高端，但体量偏小；越南仍在低成本路线上坚守。

东南亚各国都清楚，承接中国产能的窗口期有限。谁先把营商环境、供应链与合规三件事做扎实，谁就能在"中国+1"的下一程里，接住真正的产业，而不只是接住一阵风。

## 延伸阅读

**中国+1（China Plus One）**：企业在保留中国产能的同时于第二国建立平行供应链，以规避关税与地缘风险；东南亚是主要承接方，泰国、越南、马来西亚各凭优势分羹。

**友岸外包（Friend-shoring）**：将产能与供应链布局于政治互信、规则相近的伙伴国家，以换取地缘安全溢价；常与"中国+1"叠加出现于美国友岸外包战略中。

# China Plus One 2.0: Why Chinese Firms Are Picking Thailand Over Vietnam

The South China Morning Post observes that, under the twin pressures of trade-war 2.0 and tighter transshipment scrutiny, some Chinese firms are making a second relocation — from China via Vietnam, onward to Thailand. This is no mere site switch, but a recalibration of the global supply chain's "risk-avoidance logic."

## A map being redrawn

Since the 2018 US–China trade frictions, "China Plus One" evolved from a boardroom buzzword into a baseline risk rule for manufacturers by 2025. Vietnam, with cheap labour and convenient exports, was the first stop. But in 2026 the map is redrawn: some firms shift their gaze from Vietnam to Thailand, and Bangkok has grown cautious — especially wary of US measures to limit Chinese transshipment.

A transfer line once read "China → Vietnam"; it is being stretched into a second curve, "China → Vietnam → Thailand."

## Why Vietnam fell out of favour

Vietnam is not bad; it has hit a ceiling. It wins on cheap labour, export convenience and relative stability — the default for low-end capacity. But its supplier ecosystem is shallow, and core components still depend on Chinese imports, limiting its ability to host mid- and high-end manufacturing.

More decisive is tariff risk. Vietnam's trade surplus with the US hit a record US$123 billion in 2024; Washington has extended transshipment scrutiny to surplus-heavy countries. The Trump administration is expected to enforce rules of origin more strictly and widen customs probes, closing the "origin-washing" loophole via Vietnam. Vietnam's cheapness is turning into its own surplus backlash.

## What lets Thailand catch the wave

Thailand catches the spillover through industrial ecosystem, not bare low cost. Data from the Board of Investment show Chinese-promoted applications reached 982 in 2024 — 40 per cent of all foreign projects, the largest share — with total investment of B1.72 trillion (about US$51 billion); in Q1 2026 China still led with 155 projects worth B173.27 billion (about US$5.1 billion).

During Anutin's high-profile China visit marking his first anniversary, four Chinese companies announced combined investment exceeding B700 billion (about US$20.7 billion), B500 billion of it to expand existing commitments. The Eastern Economic Corridor (EEC) spanning Chonburi, Rayong and Chachoengsao — 30,000 square kilometres — is the flagship zone for high-value manufacturing.

## Thailand's ballast: ecosystem and stability

Thailand does not win on labour cost but on supplier depth, infrastructure, incentives and stability combined. As Southeast Asia's largest auto producer, its supply-chain maturity trails only Singapore and Malaysia, so Chinese firms land without building ecosystems from scratch.

From 2024, Chinese carmakers, battery, electronics-control and parts firms clustered in Thailand, creating scale. For many industrial buyers, Thailand wins on everything except wages — precisely the "ecosystem density" high-end manufacturing prizes.

## The catch: transshipment red lines

But Thailand carries baggage too. Bangkok knows the US red line on Chinese transshipment sits right overhead. Purely using Vietnam as a "relabelling" springboard no longer works; firms must do genuine local production in Thailand or risk origin verification at any moment.

This means Thailand's version of China Plus One carries higher cost and compliance thresholds. For firms betting on Thailand, the cheap window is narrowing; what remains must be real capacity, not an empty-shell transshipment shell.

## Who is redrawing Southeast Asia's industrial map

Zoom out, and this migration is a multi-country race. Indonesia moved most aggressively — US$5.4 billion of Chinese investment in 2025 and nearly US$4 billion in H1 2026 — yet policy swings pushed some wavering capital away. Malaysia competes for high-end via its semiconductor base but is small in scale; Vietnam holds the low-cost line.

Every Southeast Asian state knows the window to absorb Chinese capacity is finite. Whoever first builds solid business environment, supply chain and compliance will catch real industry in the next leg of China Plus One — not just a passing wind.

## Further reading

**China Plus One**: Firms keep Chinese capacity while building a parallel supply chain in a second country to hedge tariffs and geopolitics; Southeast Asia is the main recipient, with Thailand, Vietnam and Malaysia each carving a share.

**Friend-shoring**: Placing capacity and supply chains in politically trusted, rule-aligned partner countries to buy geopolitical safety; often layered onto China Plus One in US friend-shoring strategy.

# 政变五年，缅甸如何沦为亚洲冰毒工厂

8月，印度尼西亚海关人员在峇淡岛码头一艘货船上查获2.6吨液态冰毒。这不过是一次单独的拦截，却指向一股任何单次查缉都挡不住的区域暗潮。联合国毒品和犯罪问题办公室（UNODC）的数据显示，2025年东亚与东南亚查缉的冰毒达到349吨，较上一年激增48%，是十年前的五倍有余。分析人士公认，根本的驱动力就摆在缅甸的国境之内。

## 数字：创纪录的查缉量

联合国毒品和犯罪问题办公室于2026年发布的《东亚及东南亚合成毒品：最新发展与挑战》报告，勾勒出这场浪潮的规模。2025年，东亚与东南亚查缉的冰毒达到349吨，为该区域有记录以来最高；氯胺酮（K粉）查缉量也创下52.5吨的纪录，一年之内激增185%。泰国一国的查缉量就接近区域总量的一半，超过159吨；马来西亚的查缉量几乎翻了三倍，菲律宾翻了近两番，印度尼西亚也增长约80%。正如联合国毒品和犯罪问题办公室首席分析师沈仁植（Inshik Sim）所言，查缉量攀升与其说是执法成功的信号，不如说是供给扩张的症状——警方缴获得越多，说明毒贩生产得越多。

## 缅甸如何沦为区域冰毒工厂

地处缅甸、泰国与老挝交界的金三角（Golden Triangle），数十年来一直是鸦片与海洛因的主要产地。自1990年代起，毒贩逐渐转向可在实验室大规模量产的冰毒。2021年缅甸军方夺取政权引发的内战，让这一趋势急剧恶化。各少数民族地方武装在2023年后不断扩张控制区，发现冰毒是筹措购武资金的一条可靠财源。这些武装与军方之间的交火，进一步掏空了本就薄弱的执法能力。其结果是一片治理真空：犯罪网络得以肆无忌惮地设立制毒工厂，并经陆路和海路将成品运出。

## 前体化学品：管道上的漏洞

冰毒离不开前体化学品，而中国（China）与印度（India）是这类原料的主要供应国。经由中国云南省瑞丽市（Ruili）等边境通道进入缅甸的路线，被视为最主要的走私走廊。北京方面已收紧前体化学品的出口管制，但其中许多物质具有合法工业用途、属于两用化学品，犯罪团伙仍能持续获取。中国有关部门估计，每年有超过10万吨前体化学品流入缅甸边境地区，按他们的说法，足以生产1200吨冰毒。教训很直白：只要化学品还在流入，工厂就不会停工。

## 价格下跌，危险上升

若供给真的在收紧，价格应当上涨。现实却相反，价格一路下跌。在缅甸，冰毒零售价从每克6美元（约43元）降至5.55美元（约40元）；在泰国，从20美元（约144元）降至13.5美元（约97元）；在印度尼西亚，从134美元（约965元）降至79美元（约569元），跌幅超过四成。价格下跌讲的是一个简单的故事：市场已被淹没，再严厉的执法也撼动不了庞大的生产与分销网络。朱拉隆功大学（Chulalongkorn University）的研究员萨亚莫尔说得对——泰国已成为区域最大的合成毒品市场，买家动动手指就能网购到毒品，价格更是跌到十年前的五分之一。

## 毒品与诈骗：同一个犯罪生态

2026年报告最令人警惕的发现，是各类犯罪正在合流。调查人员在缅甸北部发现，诈骗据点与大型制毒设施相距不到1公里，二者共享保护伞、物流与金融网络。在掸邦（Shan State）山区边境小镇大其力（Tachileik），缅甸当局称今年已逮捕超过500名中国公民，他们与缅甸公民及其他外籍人员一同活动。联合国毒品和犯罪问题办公室与中国有关部门都警告，毒品贩运与网络诈骗运营正融合为同一个犯罪生态——生产、运输、资金转移与线上支付，全部纳入同一套分工体系。这种合流，让整张网络远比任何单线执法所能瓦解的复杂。

## 单靠查缉无法取胜

创纪录的查缉量，并没有扭转曲线。根子在于结构：制毒恰恰在最缺乏国家权威的地方最为猖獗。国际危机组织（International Crisis Group）的理查德·霍西（Richard Horsey）直言，缅甸"处于东南亚跨国犯罪的核心"。朱拉隆功大学的娜努伊·特丽拉（Nualnoi Treerat）警告，只要冲突与治理真空持续，任何一次严打之后，生产都能迅速恢复。专家认为，逮捕与查缉必要但远远不够。真正的着力点在于金融：掐断洗钱渠道、地下银行体系与线上支付通道，才能切断这套生态的变现能力。与此同时，公共卫生同样不可忽视——要治疗使用者，而不只是惩罚他们。一个用查缉吨位衡量成败、价格却持续下跌的区域，是在治标，而非治病。

## 延伸阅读

**甲基苯丙胺（Methamphetamine，俗称冰毒）**：强效中枢神经兴奋剂；金三角地区自1990年代起由鸦片转向其实验室量产，缅甸掸邦是当前主要生产基地。

**前体化学品（Precursor Chemicals）**：制造毒品所需原料，多为两用工业化学品，中国、印度为主要供应国，走私管控难度极高。

**金三角（Golden Triangle）**：缅甸、泰国、老挝三国交界的传统禁毒重点地区，如今亦是亚洲冰毒的主要源头。

# Five Years After the Coup, How Myanmar Became Asia's Meth Factory

In August, Indonesian customs officers seized 2.6 tonnes of liquid methamphetamine from a cargo ship at Batam port. It was a single interception, yet it points to a regional tide that no single haul can turn. According to the UN Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC), East and Southeast Asia seized a record 349 tonnes of methamphetamine in 2025 — up 48 per cent from the year before and more than five times the amount a decade ago. Analysts broadly agree that the fundamental driver sits inside Myanmar's borders.

## The numbers: a record haul

UNODC's 2026 report, Synthetic Drugs in East and Southeast Asia: Latest Developments and Challenges, lays out the scale. The 349 tonnes of meth seized in 2025 mark the highest total ever recorded for the region. Ketamine seizures also hit a record 52.5 tonnes, up 185 per cent in a single year. Thailand alone accounted for nearly half of the meth haul, seizing more than 159 tonnes. Malaysia's seizures nearly tripled, the Philippines nearly quadrupled, and Indonesia's rose about 80 per cent. As UNODC lead analyst Inshik Sim puts it, rising seizures are less a sign of success than a symptom of supply: the more drugs law enforcement catches, the more traffickers are evidently producing.

## How Myanmar became the region's meth factory

The Golden Triangle — where Myanmar, Thailand and Laos meet — has been a centre of opium and heroin production for decades. Since the 1990s, traffickers shifted toward methamphetamine, which can be mass-produced in laboratories. The 2021 military coup accelerated the collapse. Civil war fragmented the country, and ethnic armed organisations that expanded their territory after 2023 found in meth a reliable way to fund weapons purchases. Fighting between these groups and the military has hollowed out whatever enforcement capacity remained. The result is a governance vacuum in which criminal networks set up labs with impunity and ship product out by land and sea.

## Precursor chemicals: the leak in the pipeline

Meth needs precursor chemicals, and China and India are the main suppliers. Routes running through border crossings such as Ruili in China's Yunnan province into Myanmar are treated as a primary smuggling corridor. Beijing has tightened export controls on precursors, but many are dual-use substances with legitimate industrial applications, letting syndicates keep sourcing. Chinese authorities estimate that more than 100,000 tonnes of precursor chemicals flow into the Myanmar border area each year — enough, they say, to produce 1,200 tonnes of meth. The lesson is blunt: as long as the chemicals keep flowing, the labs keep running.

## Falling prices, rising danger

If supply were tightening, prices would rise. Instead they are falling. In Myanmar, retail meth dropped from US$6 to US$5.55 per gram in 2025; in Thailand, from US$20 to US$13.50; in Indonesia, from US$134 to US$79 — a drop of more than 40 per cent. Falling prices tell a simple story: the market is flooded, and even intensified enforcement cannot dent the production and distribution networks. Chulalongkorn University researcher Saya Moe is right that Thailand has become the region's largest synthetic-drug market, where buyers can order online and prices have collapsed to a fifth of what they were a decade ago.

## Drugs and scams: one criminal ecosystem

The 2026 report's most striking finding is convergence. Investigators in northern Myanmar found scam compounds and large-scale meth labs less than a kilometre apart, sharing protection, logistics and financial networks. In Tachileik, a border town in the Shan hills, regime authorities say they have arrested more than 500 Chinese nationals this year operating alongside Myanmar citizens and other foreigners. Both UNODC and Chinese authorities warn that drug trafficking and online-scam operations are merging into a single criminal ecosystem — production, transport, money transfer and online payment all under one division of labour. That fusion makes the network far harder to dismantle than any single thread of enforcement could achieve.

## Why seizures alone won't win

Record seizures have not bent the curve. The reason is structural: production thrives precisely where state authority is weakest. International Crisis Group's Richard Horsey notes that Myanmar "is at the heart of criminality in Southeast Asia." Chulalongkorn's Nualnoi Treerat warns that as long as conflict and governance vacuums persist, production can rebound quickly after any crackdown. Experts argue that arrests and seizures are necessary but not sufficient. The real levers are financial: choking the money-laundering channels, the underground banking systems and the online payment rails that let the ecosystem monetise. Alongside that sits a public-health imperative — treating users, not just punishing them. A region that measures success by tonnage seized, while prices keep falling, is fighting the symptom and neglecting the disease.

# 一条提醒让中国游客少了13.6%：泰国（Thailand）旅游的安全账，谁来买单？

## 数字：2月以来首次下滑

据《南华早报》（South China Morning Post）报道，泰国旅游与体育部数据显示，2026年10月赴泰中国游客为308942人次，同比下降13.6%，这是自2月以来该市场首次出现同比萎缩。9月这一数字还是349182人次。放进全年坐标看，反差更刺眼：今年前10个月，中国仍以4244376人次稳居泰国最大客源国，同比增长12.4%。换言之，一次单月回落，打断的是一条刚刚修复的曲线。

据泰国旅游与体育部长素拉萨11月5日透露，泰中双方正筹备成立联合工作组。泰方承认，中国游客的信心已经受到影响。点评一句：游客不会阅读统计口径，他们只记住「安全」两个字。当这两个字与一个国家绑定，数据拐点往往比政策转弯来得更早。

## 一条提醒为何如此管用

泰国旅游局给出的解释有三重：10月16日中国驻泰使馆发布安全提醒、黄金周后的「正常」回落，以及曼谷9月底那场三天降下一个月雨量的洪灾。使馆提醒的核心，是警惕以高薪工作为诱饵的诈骗，以及在多起针对中国公民的高调绑架案后注意交通安全。中国社交平台上，不少网友干脆改口「国内游更安全」。

一条领事提醒，为什么能撬动十几万人的行程？因为它传递的不是风险概率，而是风险归属——当使馆点名「中国公民」，等于为不确定性盖了官方的章。这与泰国长期塑造的「微笑国度」叙事正面相撞。据新加坡旅游营销科技公司（China Trading Desk）首席执行官萨布拉马尼亚·巴特（Subramania Bhatt）分析，安全和信心本就是中国游客选择目的地的关键变量，而东南亚（Southeast Asia）又不缺替代选项。团体游最先取消——因为组织者比散客更怕承担责任。

## 灰产标签的反噬

更值得玩味的是标签的流向。一些中国游客在网上抱怨，泰国人把中国人与「灰产」划等号，以为中国生意都是灰色地带。一位广东网友11月3日写道：他每年去泰国几次，却越来越感到这种刻板印象的压力。

这里藏着一个不对称：诈骗网络的主责不在普通游客，却在游客的名声上结账。使馆提醒中特别点名网红和艺人，因为高薪工作骗局最爱拿「出镜机会」当饵；与此同时，部分中国籍人员本身也在参与诱骗同胞，泰国人亦有卷入非法带人越境。当「中国公民」同时出现在受害者与加害者两个栏目里，民间信任的损耗就不是一纸公告能补的。泰方若只做安全承诺，不做身份区分，等于把治理成本转嫁给守规矩的大多数。

## 问题在妙瓦底，不在曼谷

把地图摊开，问题就更清楚了。真正的诈骗园区集中在缅甸妙瓦底一带，靠近泰缅边境；泰国更多是中转通道。素拉萨说得直白：自愿越境的人最难识别，因为他们在口岸看起来与游客无异；只有被捆绑、被胁迫、走非正式通道者，检查站才能当场拦下。

这句话点破了治理的死结：犯罪的跨国性，决定了任何单边口岸管控都只能管住一小段。泰国加强边境巡逻是对的，但把缅甸境内的园区当成泰国的治安问题来解决，既不现实，也不公平。真正需要被追问的，是那些把人从曼谷机场一路送到边境的中介网络——它们的触角，恰恰伸在泰国境内。

## 联合工作组及其边界

据素拉萨披露，中国驻泰大使张建伟11月5日与他会谈，双方拟成立联合工作组，交换情报、协调救助，重点打击把中国公民骗往妙瓦底的骗局。中方态度明确：不支持在泰违法的中国公民，希望泰方依法严处；同时愿意分享人员信息，帮泰方把违法者与正常游客分开。张建伟也提醒，使馆并非首次发提醒，只是这次被中国媒体放大成了大事件。

这段对话值得记上一笔。它说明双方都意识到一个事实：单纯的「提醒」正在变成双刃剑——保护了公民，也吓退了客流。联合工作组若能把识别做在前端，把劝阻做在出发之前，提醒就不必总在半途拉响。但信息分享牵涉隐私与司法主权，能做多深，取决于政治互信，而非技术能力。

## 游客可以转身去别处

旅游业是一门「可替代性」生意。中国游客熟悉这条算术：航程两三个小时、落地签、物价适中——这些泰国长期享有的优势，正在被安全折价一点点抵消。今年前10个月，赴泰外国游客25464306人次，同比下降4.59%；旅游收入1.241万亿泰铢（约合2600亿元人民币），同比下降2.85%。同一时期，韩国赴泰游客下降26.86%，马来西亚（Malaysia）下降16.34%。这不是泰国一家的困境，而是整个区域在重新定价「安全感」。

据泰国旅行社协会主席塔纳蓬·奇瓦拉塔纳帕隆估计，11月和12月中国游客可能同比下降20%，团体游降幅至少40%；若信心迟迟不恢复，明年春节团体游甚至可能腰斩。这些数字未必精确，却传递了一个清楚的信号：泰国正在为边境另一侧的犯罪，支付本国的旅游账单。

## 信心这笔账

泰国政府打算借11月18日至19日深圳亚太经合组织领导人非正式会议的机会，向国际社会展示「已做好准备」。泰国旅游局也说，要用主动的安全沟通、定制体验和与中国媒体的合作来「重建信心、刺激需求」。方向没错，但重心仍在沟通，而非结构。

真正能重建信心的，不是又一支人工智能生成的宣传片——2025年1月中国演员王星被绑架后，时任总理佩通坦就曾用这种方式喊话——而是让游客在口岸、在街头、在景区，看到看得见的变化。中国游客从2024年的673万人次跌到2025年的447万人次，已经用脚投过一次票。若这次仍把问题归结为「媒体放大」，那么下一次下滑，恐怕不再有12.4%的累计增幅来兜底。

## 延伸阅读

**旅游安全（Travel Safety）**：指目的地通过预警、警务与保险安排降低游客风险的治理能力，已成为影响跨境客流的关键变量。

**灰产（Grey-area businesses）**：介于合法与非法之间的经营活动，在东南亚部分中文语境中被泛化为对华人商户的污名标签。

**领事保护（Consular Protection）**：本国政府通过使领馆为海外公民提供的协助与预警机制，主动预警正成为风险治理的常规工具。

# One advisory, 13.6% fewer visitors: who pays Thailand's safety bill?

## The numbers: the first drop since February

According to the South China Morning Post, Thailand logged 308,942 arrivals from China in October, 13.6 per cent fewer than a year earlier — the first year-on-year contraction since February. The month before had brought 349,182. The reversal is sharper against the full-year picture: in the first ten months of 2026 China remained Thailand's largest source market with 4,244,376 visitors, up 12.4 per cent. A single month's decline interrupted a curve that had only just begun to recover.

Tourism and Sports Minister Surasak Phancharoenworakul said on November 5 that Thailand and China were preparing a joint working group. Even the minister acknowledged that visitor confidence had been shaken. Tourists, after all, do not read statistical definitions; they remember one word — safety. Once that word attaches itself to a country, the data tends to turn before the policy does.

## Why one advisory matters

The Tourism Authority of Thailand offers three explanations: the Chinese embassy's October 16 advisory, the normal post-Golden Week lull, and late-September floods that dumped a month of rain on Bangkok in three days. The advisory warned against scams promising high-paying jobs and urged caution on transport security after a string of high-profile kidnappings of Chinese nationals. On Chinese social media, many travellers simply switched to domestic destinations.

Why does a consular notice move so many itineraries? Because it communicates not the probability of risk but its ownership: when an embassy names its own citizens, it stamps uncertainty with official authority. That collides head-on with the "land of smiles" narrative Thailand has spent decades cultivating. Subramania Bhatt, chief executive of Singapore-based travel marketing firm China Trading Desk, notes that safety and confidence are decisive factors for Chinese travellers, who have no shortage of alternative Southeast Asian destinations. Group tours are the first to cancel — organisers, more than individuals, fear being held responsible.

## The grey-industry stigma

More revealing is where the stigma lands. Some Chinese visitors complain that Thais lump them together with "grey-area" businesses, assuming all Chinese commerce is suspect. One Guangdong-based traveller wrote on November 3 that although he visits Thailand several times a year, the stereotype increasingly weighs on him.

Here lies the asymmetry: the criminal networks are not made up of ordinary tourists, yet ordinary tourists pay the reputational bill. The embassy singled out influencers and artists because scam recruiters dangle on-camera opportunities as bait; at the same time, some Chinese nationals are themselves involved in deceiving compatriots, and Thais in facilitating illegal crossings. When "Chinese citizens" appear in both the victim and perpetrator columns, the erosion of trust cannot be repaired by an announcement. If Bangkok promises safety without distinguishing identities, it shifts the cost of governance onto the law-abiding majority.

## Myawaddy, not Bangkok

Spread out the map and the picture clarifies. The scam compounds cluster around Myawaddy in Myanmar, near the Thai border; Thailand is more a transit route than the final destination for the crime. Surasak was blunt: voluntary crossers are hardest to identify because they look like any other traveller at the checkpoint; only those bound hand and foot, coerced, or moved through informal routes can be stopped on the spot.

That sentence exposes the governance knot. The transnational character of the crime means any unilateral border control covers only one segment. Tightening Thai patrols is correct, but treating compounds on the Myanmar side as a Thai policing problem is neither realistic nor fair. The actors who deserve scrutiny are the brokers who move people from Bangkok airport all the way to the border — their reach lies squarely inside Thailand.

## A joint working group, with limits

According to Surasak, Chinese Ambassador to Thailand Zhang Jianwei discussed the matter with him on November 5, and the two sides plan a joint working group to exchange intelligence, coordinate assistance, and target scams that lure Chinese nationals to Myawaddy. Beijing's position is clear: it does not support Chinese citizens who break the law in Thailand and wants firm action; it is also willing to share information so that offenders can be separated from legitimate tourists. Zhang noted that the embassy had issued similar advisories before, and that Chinese media had amplified this one into a major story.

That exchange is worth recording. Both sides understand that an advisory has become a double-edged tool: it protects citizens and deters visitors at once. If a working group can move identification upstream and warning before departure, notices need not sound mid-journey. But information-sharing touches privacy and judicial sovereignty; how deep it goes depends on political trust, not technical capacity.

## Tourists can choose elsewhere

Tourism is a business of substitutability. Chinese travellers know the arithmetic: a two-or-three-hour flight, visa-free entry, moderate prices — advantages Thailand has long enjoyed are being discounted, bit by bit, by safety concerns. In the first ten months, Thailand welcomed 25,464,306 foreign visitors, down 4.59 per cent, with tourism revenue of 1.241 trillion baht (about US$34.5 billion), down 2.85 per cent. South Korean arrivals fell 26.86 per cent and Malaysian arrivals 16.34 per cent. This is not Thailand's predicament alone; the whole region is repricing the sense of security.

Thanapol Cheewarattanaporn, president of the Association of Thai Travel Agents, estimates Chinese arrivals could fall 20 per cent year on year in November and December, with group tours down at least 40 per cent; if confidence is not restored, group bookings for next year's Lunar New Year could halve. The figures may be imprecise, but the signal is clear: Thailand is paying a domestic tourism bill for crime committed across its border.

## The confidence arithmetic

The government plans to use the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen on November 18–19 to showcase its readiness. The tourism authority says it will rebuild confidence through proactive safety communication, curated experiences and partnerships with Chinese media. The direction is right, but the emphasis remains on messaging rather than structure.

What restores confidence is not another AI-generated promotional video — after the January 2025 kidnapping of Chinese actor Wang Xing, then-prime minister Paetongtarn Shinawatra used exactly that device — but visible change at the checkpoint, on the street and in the resorts. Chinese arrivals fell from 6.73 million in 2024 to 4.47 million in 2025; travellers have already voted once with their feet. If this episode is again dismissed as media exaggeration, the next decline may not have a 12.4 per cent cumulative gain to cushion it.

## Further reading

**Travel safety**: the capacity of a destination to reduce visitor risk through warnings, policing and insurance; now a decisive variable in cross-border flows.

**Grey-area businesses**: activities straddling legality and illegality, often generalised in Chinese-language discourse in Southeast Asia into a stigma against ethnic-Chinese merchants.

**Consular protection**: assistance and advance warnings offered by a government to its citizens abroad; proactive alerts have become a routine risk-governance tool.

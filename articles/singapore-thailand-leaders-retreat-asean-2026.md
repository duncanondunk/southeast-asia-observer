# 新加坡与泰国重启领导人非正式会晤：一份跨东盟轮值主席年的"多年议程"

9月2日，新加坡总理黄循财与泰国总理阿努廷在曼谷举行新加坡—泰国领导人非正式会晤（Leaders' Retreat）。这是两国自2015年以来首次重启这一最高层级双边机制，也是两任总理的首次正式会晤。在联合记者会上，黄循财把两国关系描述为"建立在信任、尊重与友谊之上"，并抛出一个更具雄心的设想：新加坡将于2027年担任东盟轮值主席国，泰国接棒2028年，两国团队应着手制定一份"多年议程"，让重要倡议跨主席年延续势头。

## 背景：时隔十一年的再次"领袖之约"

领导人非正式会晤是新加坡与泰国之间最高级别的双边平台，上一次举行还要追溯到2015年，由时任总理李显龙与巴育在狮城主持。十余年间，区域格局与两国政局均已剧变：新加坡完成了领导层代际交接，泰国则经历了反复的政治重组，阿努廷于2026年出任总理。此次会晤选择曼谷，且以"非正式"为名，既有淡化仪式的务实考量，也释放出两国愿意恢复制度化对话的信号。

黄循财与阿努廷此前已数次碰面——2025年11月阿努廷访新，2026年5月两人又于菲律宾宿务的东盟峰会期间会面。但唯有此次，双方得以坐下来系统盘点既有合作、并规划可落地的项目。

## 框架：为2027与2028架桥

会晤最核心的成果，是双方同意为东盟制定一份跨年度的共同议程。新加坡2027年、泰国2028年相继担任主席，意味着两个"志同道合"的东盟核心国家，将在连续两届主席任期内掌握议程设置的主导权。黄循财直言，某些区域性倡议需要数年持续努力，单靠一届主席国难以毕其功于一役；若能以"多年议程"承上启下，就能在粮食、能源、科技等议题上保持惯性。

这一安排的战略含义在于：它把双边信任外溢为区域公共产品。当新泰两国在主席年间步调一致，东盟内部更易就关键议题形成合力，也一定程度上对冲了大国博弈对区域议程的干扰。

## 粮能与科技：三大合作主轴

双方确定了三大优先合作领域：粮食与能源安全、新兴技术、实体连通。在粮食安全上，泰国是主要粮食生产国，新加坡是物流与贸易枢纽，两国计划通过生产商与贸易商的更紧密协作强化供应链；在能源上，则推进东盟电网（ASEAN Power Grid）并深化碳信用合作。新兴技术尤其是人工智能，被双方视为下一个协同前沿。

黄循财特别提到，两国在制造、医疗、旅游等优势产业都有AI落地空间，可就"安全且负责任地部署AI"交换经验，在鼓励创新的同时管控风险。这一定位，与两国近月各自的AI布局遥相呼应。

## 电网：东盟电网的雏形落地

能源合作中最具实质性的进展，是老挝—泰国—马来西亚—新加坡电力一体化项目（LTMS-PIP）的推进。该项目的首期已使新加坡得以经泰国、马来西亚，从老挝进口最多100兆瓦可再生水电。这看似不大的数字，却是东盟电网从纸面走向现实的标志性一步——它证明了跨境绿电贸易在技术与制度上可行。

在伊朗战争阴影推高能源安全焦虑的当下，区域电网的意义被重新放大。对新加坡而言，进口清洁电力既是脱碳工具，也是供应多元化手段；对泰国，则是把自身定位为区域能源枢纽的机遇。双方同意在主席年间继续推动该议程，显然意在把示范工程扩展为网络。

## 人工智能：从国家计划到双边协同

人工智能是此次会晤的新亮点。新加坡已于2026年1月承诺五年内投入10亿新元（约合人民币54亿元）提升公共AI研究能力；泰国则于8月31日推出"TH-AI 护照"（TH-AI Passport）计划，向500万公民免费开放AI工具一年。两国不约而同把AI上升为国家战略，为双边协同提供了基础。

黄循财指出，双方可在制造、医疗、旅游等领域开发实用应用，并分享"安全负责任部署"的经验。这意味着新泰AI合作不会停留在宣言，而可能走向标准互认、人才交流与联合应用场景。对一个高度依赖外部技术与信任的区域而言，这种"志同道合者先行"的模式，或将成为东盟数字一体化的模板。

## 连通：支付与基建的下一步

在实体连通方面，新泰被视为东盟金融连通的领跑者。两国早在2021年4月就上线了新泰即时支付系统（PayNow—PromptPay）连接，成为全球首个实现国家级实时支付系统直连的案例——用户仅凭手机号即可跨境转账。如今双方计划把这一模式通过多边框架，连接更多国家。

与此同时，泰国正升级工业中心、交通网络与港口，新加坡企业（包括PSA国际港务集团）凭其能力与经验可成为天然伙伴。从数字支付到港口物流，连通议题把两国的经济互补性直接转化为项目清单。

## 冷思考：善意之下仍有变量

会晤释放的善意毋庸置疑，但变量同样存在。其一，阿努廷政府的执政基础相对脆弱，泰国国内政治一旦生变，双边议程的连续性可能受影响。其二，东盟内部对"多年议程"的接纳程度不一，若其他成员国缺乏同步意愿，新泰的领跑可能变成"小圈子"。其三，能源与AI合作都高度依赖区域安全环境，中东冲突的外溢效应仍是最大不确定性。

更现实的是落实难题：领导人会晤确定了方向，真正考验在于部委层面的项目转化。新加坡以行政高效见长，泰国则常受制于官僚与协调成本。能否把"愿景文件"变成"交付清单"，将决定这份多年议程是流于仪式，还是真正重塑区域合作节奏。

# Singapore and Thailand Revive Leaders' Retreat: A "Multi-Year Agenda" Spanning ASEAN Chairmanships

On September 2, Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong and Thailand's Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul held the Singapore–Thailand Leaders' Retreat in Bangkok. It was the first revival of the highest-level bilateral mechanism since 2015, and the first formal meeting between the two premiers. At the joint press conference, Wong described the relationship as "built on trust, respect and friendship," and floated a more ambitious idea: Singapore will chair ASEAN in 2027 and Thailand in 2028, so the two teams should draft a "multi-year agenda" that carries key initiatives across chairmanships to sustain momentum.

## The backdrop: a "leaders' date" after eleven years

The Leaders' Retreat is the top bilateral platform between Singapore and Thailand; the previous one was held in 2015 in Singapore by then-PM Lee Hsien Loong and Prayut Chan-o-cha. Over the intervening decade, both the regional landscape and the two countries' politics have shifted sharply: Singapore completed a generational leadership handover, while Thailand went through repeated political重组, with Anutin becoming premier in 2026. Holding the retreat in Bangkok under an "informal" label was both a pragmatic nod to lighter ceremony and a signal that the two sides wanted to restore institutionalised dialogue.

Wong and Anutin had already met several times — Anutin visited Singapore in November 2025, and the two appeared together on the sidelines of the ASEAN Summit in Cebu, Philippines, in May 2026. But only this retreat allowed them to take stock of existing cooperation and plan concrete projects.

## The framework: bridging 2027 and 2028

The retreat's core outcome was the agreement to draft a cross-year joint agenda for ASEAN. With Singapore chairing in 2027 and Thailand in 2028, two "like-minded" core members will hold the agenda-setting initiative for two consecutive terms. Wong argued plainly that some regional initiatives require years of sustained effort and cannot be finished in a single chairmanship; a "multi-year agenda" would preserve inertia on food, energy and technology.

The strategic implication is that bilateral trust spills over into a regional public good. When Singapore and Thailand move in step during their chair years, ASEAN finds it easier to build collective force on key issues, and to some extent hedges against great-power rivalry distorting the regional agenda.

## Food, energy, technology: three pillars

The two sides identified three priority areas: food and energy security, emerging technology, and physical connectivity. On food, Thailand is a major producer and Singapore a logistics and trading hub; the two plan to strengthen supply chains through closer cooperation between producers and traders. On energy, they will advance the ASEAN Power Grid and deepen carbon-credit cooperation. Emerging technology — especially artificial intelligence — was cast as the next frontier of synergy.

Wong specifically noted that both countries have room to deploy AI in manufacturing, healthcare and tourism, and can exchange experience on "safe and responsible deployment" while encouraging innovation and managing risk. This framing echoes each country's AI moves in recent months.

## The grid: the ASEAN Power Grid takes shape

The most substantive energy progress is the Lao PDR–Thailand–Malaysia–Singapore Power Integration Project (LTMS-PIP). Its first phase already lets Singapore import up to 100 MW of renewable hydropower from Laos via Thailand and Malaysia. The number looks small, but it is a landmark step from paper to reality — proof that cross-border green-power trade is technically and institutionally feasible.

With the Iran war shadow raising energy-security anxiety, regional grids are being revalued. For Singapore, importing clean power is both a decarbonisation tool and a diversification of supply; for Thailand, it is a chance to position itself as a regional energy hub. The two agreed to keep pushing the agenda through their chair years, clearly intending to expand the pilot into a network.

## Artificial intelligence: from national plans to bilateral synergy

AI was a fresh highlight. Singapore committed in January 2026 to S$1 billion (about RMB 5.4 billion) over five years to boost public AI research; Thailand launched its "TH-AI Passport" on August 31, giving five million citizens free AI tools for a year. Both have elevated AI to national strategy, laying the ground for bilateral synergy.

Wong noted the two can develop practical applications in manufacturing, healthcare and tourism, and share "safe and responsible deployment" experience. This implies the cooperation will not stall at declarations but may move toward standards mutual recognition, talent exchange and joint use-cases. For a region highly dependent on external technology and trust, this "like-minded go first" model could become a template for ASEAN digital integration.

## Connectivity: the next step in payments and infrastructure

On physical connectivity, Singapore and Thailand are seen as ASEAN's financial-connectivity frontrunners. They launched PayNow–PromptPay linkage in April 2021, the world's first direct link between national real-time payment systems — users transfer across borders with just a phone number. Now they plan to extend this model to more countries through a common multilateral framework.

At the same time, Thailand is upgrading industrial centres, transport networks and ports, where Singaporean firms including PSA International can be natural partners with their capabilities and experience. From digital payments to port logistics, the connectivity agenda turns the two economies' complementarity directly into a project list.

## A cool note: variables beneath the goodwill

The goodwill is unquestionable, but variables remain. First, Anutin's government rests on a relatively fragile base; any domestic political shift in Thailand could affect the continuity of the bilateral agenda. Second, ASEAN members differ in their appetite for a "multi-year agenda" — if others lack the will to sync, the Singapore–Thailand lead could become a "small circle." Third, both energy and AI cooperation depend heavily on the regional security environment, with spillover from the Middle East conflict the biggest uncertainty.

More practically, implementation is the test. The retreat set direction; the real challenge is project conversion at the ministerial level. Singapore is known for efficient administration, while Thailand is often constrained by bureaucracy and coordination costs. Whether a "vision document" becomes a "delivery list" will decide if this multi-year agenda is mere ritual or a genuine reset of regional cooperation节奏.

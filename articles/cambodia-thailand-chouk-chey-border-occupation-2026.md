# 铁丝网后的村庄：柬泰停火何以在 Chouk Chey 裂开

二〇二六年七月二十八日停火协议生效不过一周，柬泰边境的火药味便重新升腾。在班迭棉吉省（Banteay Meanchey）奥贝胶（O'Beichoan）乡一带，泰国军队于 Chouk Chey（又作 Chok Chey）村周边铺设铁丝网、竖起"限期撤离"通告牌，要求约一百七十户柬埔寨家庭离开世代居住的土地，否则依泰国法律惩处。推搡与对峙随即爆发，金边两度递交外交抗议，斥泰方"以军事手段单边划界"。一个被铁丝网割裂的村庄，撕开了停火协议脆弱的表层。

## 事发：铁丝网与"限期撤离"通告

据柬埔寨方面叙述，泰方人员在 Chouk Chey 村一带架设带刺铁丝网、渔网与轮胎路障，并在村落入口立起以泰文、高棉文与英文写就的告示牌。告示称，占据泰国境内土地者"违反泰国司法管辖"，限令"拆除并撤离"，否则将依泰国《移民法》与《森林法》提起诉讼与惩处。

沙缴府（Sa Kaeo）省长帕里尼亚（Parinya Photisat）签署的指令，要求搬迁的家庭分布于"班农湛"（Ban Nong Chan）与"班农雅胶"（Ban Nong Ya Kaeo）等村落。现场目击称，泰国军人由警察支援，与企图阻止设障的村民发生肢体摩擦，柬埔寨士兵则在柬侧土地保护本国民众。一段二十分钟的"取物时限"，成为许多家庭与家园的最后告别。

## 金边的抗议：单边划界违反协议

班迭棉吉省长奥姆·列提（Oum Reatrey）致函泰方，拒绝单边行动。他在九月二日的回函中援引一九九五年《边境合作协定》与二〇〇〇年《陆界勘界备忘录》，强调边界管理与界桩安设属联合边界委员会（JBC）与一般边界委员会（GBC）的职权，任何影响现居村民的单边举动，都违反上述协议、今年七月二十八日停火，以及八月七日在马来西亚举行的 GBC 特别会议与八月二十二日区域边界委员会（RBC）会议的精神。

金边更将措辞升级为"吞并"。柬埔寨新闻部长涅·皮克拉（Neth Pheaktra）早前斥泰军以集装箱与铁丝网构筑"边境围栏"、展示国旗，是"以武力片面主张主权"，并声明"绝不承认任何以武力造成的边界变更"。九月三日，外交部正式声明，指泰方在奥贝胶乡两村架设障碍，已令众多柬埔寨家庭被逐出家园与农田。

## 法理脉络：二〇〇〇年备忘录与停火

争议的枢纽，是二〇〇〇年《关于陆界勘测与划界的谅解备忘录》（MOU）。该文件确立以联合机制解决分歧，并冻结单边改变现状的行为。柬方坚持，任何划界都须循 JBC/GBC 渠道，而非由前线驻军以铁丝网"生米煮成熟饭"。

七月二十八日停火本意在止战，却未触及界碑归属这一根子问题。东盟观察团与多次双边会议试图巩固停火，但 Chouk Chey 事件表明：当军事对峙转为"低强度占据"，停火条文难以约束日常的边界推进。法理框架存在，执行力却悬空。

## 历史病灶：殖民地图与未定界

柬泰约八百公里边境，多依法国殖民时期（法属印度支那与暹罗）所绘地图划定，许多地段至今未联合勘定。国际法院一九六二年裁定柏威夏寺（Preah Vihear）归柬埔寨，但未就其他争议区划线，埋下今日冲突的种子。

Chouk Chey 一带恰是典型：双方各执一词，泰国称收回"固有领土"，柬埔寨指其人居此数十年。前"农湛"（Nong Chan）难民营旧址成为新焦点，泰方声索范围涵盖 Chok Chey 与 Prey Chan 大部分土地。殖民遗产叠加民族叙事，使每一寸土地都裹挟历史情绪。

## 人道代价：被切断的家园与生计

铁丝网隔开的不只是国界，更是生活。据英国广播公司（BBC）走访，约三千人居住在该争议地带，其中十三户被隔在铁丝网另一侧，无法返回房屋与田产。一名村民称，泰军只给二十分钟取走锅碗。

更隐蔽的代价在账面上。班迭棉吉省长估计，边境关闭令当地每天损失约一百万美元关税收入；农产品与跨境贸易几近停摆，柬埔寨劳工离泰、泰国游客止步，新落成的暹粒机场门可罗雀。和平的红利尚未兑现，对峙的成本已先到账。

## 区域反应：东盟轮值主席介入

作为二〇二六年东盟轮值主席，菲律宾激活磋商机制，并强化东盟观察团（AOT）授权，意在防止对抗复燃。外交官被安排实地察看，见证 Chouk Chey、Prey Chan 与 Boeung Trakoun 等地平民所受冲击。

但东盟的"不干涉"传统与协商一致惯性，使其调停多停留于劝和。面对一方指控"非法占据"、另一方辩称"恢复管辖"，主席国能提供的，更多是道义关注与程序性监督，而非强制划界。这对苦候归家的家庭，远水难救近火。

## 冷思考：停火易，划界难

Chouk Chey 的铁丝网，是柬泰关系的一面镜子：武力可以换来短暂的停火，却换不来被殖民地图与民族记忆缠绕的边界和解。二〇〇〇年备忘录与联合机制仍在，但纸面共识需要两国政治意愿共同"落地"，而非由前线士兵以路障代行。

对中国与区域而言，这起事件提醒我们：东南亚的和平不只取决于高层握手，也取决于每一根被悄悄拉起的铁丝网能否被及时劝回。停火是起点，划界才是终点——而这条路，显然比一纸协议漫长得多。

## 延伸阅读

**二〇〇〇年谅解备忘录（2000 MOU）**：柬埔寨与泰国关于陆界勘测与划界的框架文件，确立以联合边界委员会（JBC）解决分歧、冻结单边改变现状行为。

**联合边界委员会（JBC, Joint Boundary Committee）**：两国依备忘录设立的勘界机制，负责界桩安设与边界管理，是化解 Chouk Chey 类争议的正规渠道。

**东盟观察团（AOT, ASEAN Observer Team）**：由东盟轮值主席国授权、监督停火与人道状况的机制；二〇二六年由菲律宾作为主席国强化其授权。

# The Village Behind the Wire: How the Cambodia–Thailand Ceasefire Cracked at Chouk Chey

Barely a week after the ceasefire of 28 July 2026 took effect, the smell of gunpowder returned to the Cambodia–Thailand border. In O'Beichoan commune, Banteay Meanchey province, Thai troops laid barbed wire and erected "vacate-by-deadline" notice boards around the village of Chouk Chey (also Chok Chey), ordering some 170 Cambodian families to leave land they had lived on for generations or face punishment under Thai law. Scuffles and standoffs followed; Phnom Penh lodged protests twice, accusing Bangkok of "unilateral demarcation by military means." A village split by wire tore through the fragile surface of the truce.

## What happened: wire and an "evacuate-by-deadline" order

According to the Cambodian account, Thai personnel strung barbed wire, nets and tyre barricades around Chouk Chey and raised notice boards in Thai, Khmer and English. The notices declared that those occupying land inside the Kingdom of Thailand were "committing an offence," ordering them to "dismantle and vacate" or face prosecution and penalties under Thailand's Immigration Act and Forest Act.

The directive, signed by Sa Kaeo province governor Parinya Photisat, targeted families in villages such as Ban Nong Chan and Ban Nong Ya Kaeo. Witnesses said Thai soldiers, backed by police, scuffled with villagers trying to block the barriers, while Cambodian soldiers protected their citizens on Cambodian soil. A twenty-minute "grab-your-things" window became many families' last farewell to home.

## Phnom Penh's protest: unilateral demarcation violates agreements

Banteay Meanchey governor Oum Reatrey wrote back rejecting the unilateral move. In his 2 September letter he invoked the 1995 Agreement on Border Cooperation and the 2000 Memorandum of Understanding on land-boundary demarcation, stressing that border management and marker placement fall to the Joint Boundary Committee (JBC) and General Border Committee (GBC); any one-sided action affecting resident villagers violates those pacts, the 28 July 2026 ceasefire, and the spirit of the 7 August GBC special meeting in Malaysia and the 22 August Regional Border Committee (RBC) meeting.

Phnom Penh elevated the wording to "annexation." Information Minister Neth Pheaktra had earlier condemned Thai troops for building a "border fence" of containers and wire and displaying the flag as "unilateral assertion of sovereignty by force," declaring Cambodia "will not recognise any border changes resulting from the use of force." On 3 September the foreign ministry formally stated that Thai barriers in two O'Beichoan villages had driven many Cambodian families from their homes and farms.

## The legal thread: the 2000 MOU and the ceasefire

The hinge of the dispute is the 2000 Memorandum of Understanding on the Survey and Demarcation of the Land Boundary. It established resolution through joint mechanisms and a freeze on unilateral changes to the status quo. Cambodia insists any demarcation must run through the JBC/GBC, not be presented as a fait accompli by front-line troops.

The 28 July ceasefire was meant to stop fighting, not to settle the question of who owns which marker. ASEAN observer teams and repeated bilateral meetings tried to consolidate it, but Chouk Chey shows that when military standoff turns into "low-intensity occupation," ceasefire text struggles to constrain everyday boundary creep. The legal framework exists; enforcement is hollow.

## The historical lesion: colonial maps and undemarcated lines

The roughly 800 km Cambodia–Thailand border mostly follows maps drawn in the colonial era (French Indochina versus Siam), and many stretches remain jointly undemarcated. The International Court of Justice ruled in 1962 that Preah Vihear temple belongs to Cambodia but drew no line for other contested areas, sowing today's seeds.

Chouk Chey is typical: both sides claim right; Thailand says it is recovering "inherent territory," Cambodia says its people have lived there for decades. The former Nong Chan refugee-camp site became the new flashpoint, with Thai claims covering most of Chok Chey and Prey Chan. Colonial legacy fused with nationalist narrative makes every inch emotionally charged.

## The human cost: homes and livelihoods cut off

The wire splits more than a frontier—it severs lives. BBC reporting from the ground found about 3,000 people living in the disputed belt, with 13 families cut off on the far side of the wire, unable to return to houses and fields. One villager said soldiers allowed just 20 minutes to fetch cooking pots.

The subtler cost is on the ledger. The Banteay Meanchey governor estimates the border closure costs the locality about US$1 million a day in customs revenue; farm produce and cross-border trade have nearly stalled, Cambodian workers have left Thailand and Thai tourists stopped coming, leaving the new Siem Reap airport all but empty. The dividend of peace has not arrived; the cost of standoff has.

## Regional reaction: the ASEAN chair steps in

As 2026 ASEAN chair, the Philippines activated consultation mechanisms and strengthened the mandate of the ASEAN Observer Team (AOT), aiming to prevent renewed confrontation. Diplomats were arranged to witness first-hand the impact on civilians in Chouk Chey, Prey Chan and Boeung Trakoun.

Yet ASEAN's tradition of non-interference and consensus inertia keeps mediation largely at the level of exhortation. Faced with one side alleging "illegal occupation" and the other claiming "restoration of jurisdiction," the chair can offer moral attention and procedural oversight rather than compulsory demarcation—cold comfort for families awaiting homecoming.

## A cool note: ceasefires are easy, borders are hard

The wire at Chouk Chey is a mirror of Cambodia–Thailand relations: force can buy a fleeting ceasefire but not a reconciliation of borders entangled with colonial maps and national memory. The 2000 MOU and joint mechanisms still stand, but paper consensus needs both governments' political will to "land," not front-line soldiers substituting barriers for diplomacy.

For China and the region, the incident is a reminder that Southeast Asian peace rests not only on handshakes at the top but on whether every quietly raised roll of wire can be talked down in time. A ceasefire is a starting line; demarcation is the finish—and that road is plainly longer than a single agreement.

## Further reading

**2000 MOU**: The Cambodia–Thailand Memorandum of Understanding on the Survey and Demarcation of the Land Boundary, establishing resolution via the Joint Boundary Committee (JBC) and a freeze on unilateral changes to the status quo.

**Joint Boundary Committee (JBC)**: The bilateral demarcation mechanism set up under the MOU, responsible for marker placement and border management—the proper channel for disputes like Chouk Chey.

**ASEAN Observer Team (AOT)**: A mechanism mandated by the ASEAN chair to monitor ceasefires and humanitarian conditions; in 2026 strengthened under the Philippine chairmanship.

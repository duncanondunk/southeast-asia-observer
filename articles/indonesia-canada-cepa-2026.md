# 印尼（Indonesia）与加拿大自贸协定12月生效：数千税目归零，雅加达（Jakarta）在下一盘什么棋

印尼与加拿大的全面经济伙伴关系协定（ICA-CEPA）将于12月1日正式生效，双方取消对3100个税目的进口关税。据联合早报（Lianhe Zaobao）报道，印尼经济事务统筹部长艾尔朗加11月15日表示，印尼已完成核准程序并通过外交照会通知加拿大——这是印尼首次与北美国家达成此类协定。表面看，这是一份双边贸易"小事"：去年双边贸易额43亿美元（约合310亿元人民币），在印尼的外贸版图中并不起眼。但若把视野拉长，从11月21日签署的印尼—欧盟全面经济伙伴关系协定，到计划明年3月收官的东盟（ASEAN）—加拿大自由贸易协定，雅加达显然是在下一盘贸易多元化的大棋。真正的问题不是关税降了多少，而是印尼能否把"市场准入"兑现为"产业升级"。

## 数字：从43亿到3100个税目

先把账算清楚。印尼与加拿大2025年双边贸易额为43亿美元（约合310亿元人民币），其中印尼出口17亿美元（约合122亿元人民币）；今年1月至9月，双边贸易额已达40亿3000万美元（约合290亿元人民币），同比增长19.76%。印尼对加拿大的出口从2021年的10亿6000万美元（约合76亿元人民币）稳步增至去年的17亿美元。

协定生效后，双方将取消3100个税目的关税。据印尼贸易部口径，加拿大将对印尼90.55%的税目给予优惠待遇，印尼则逐步调降或取消加拿大85.54%税目的进口关税。印尼经济统筹部评估，协定有望带来约16亿美元（约合115亿元人民币）的出口增长潜力，受益产业包括矿产金属、化工、汽车、木材橡胶、机械电子、食品饮料与水产品。

数字看着漂亮，但值得追问：3100个税目里，有多少是印尼真正有竞争力、能立刻扩大出口的品类？关税归零只是把门推开，能不能走进去，取决于产品本身。

## 首次拥抱北美：为何是现在

这份协定的第一个标签是"首次"——印尼第一次与北美国家签署全面经济伙伴关系协定。对加拿大而言，这也是它与东南亚（Southeast Asia）国家签下的首个双边贸易协定。两个都在寻找新市场的中等强国，走到一起并不意外。

更关键的是时机。近年来贸易保护主义抬头，供应链被地缘政治反复切割，雅加达显然不愿把出口押在少数几个传统市场上。就在同一时期，印尼贸易部长布迪将于11月21日率团赴布鲁塞尔签署谈了十年的印尼—欧盟全面经济伙伴关系协定；印尼还推动东盟—加拿大自由贸易协定在明年3月前完成谈判。双边、区域、跨洲，三条线同时推进，逻辑只有一个：分散风险。

## 逆差之问：谁更需要这份协定

不过，观察家不该只看官方新闻稿。一个不容回避的事实是：印尼在对加拿大的贸易中长期处于逆差。2025年，印尼对加出口17亿美元，自加进口却高达26亿7000万美元（约合192亿元人民币）；今年前八个月逆差约12亿7000万美元（约合91亿元人民币）。印尼卖过去的是手机、电子设备、天然橡胶、可可脂和鞋类，买回来的则是小麦、肥料、纸浆和矿物燃料。

这就引出一个尖锐的设问：关税互免，究竟谁更受益？印尼的出口主力是劳动密集与资源型产品，价格敏感度高，关税下调确实能提升竞争力；但加拿大的小麦与肥料同样具有成本优势，降税之后也会更容易进入印尼。对逆差一方而言，自由贸易从来是把双刃剑——降低别人进入本国的门槛，和打开别国市场同样重要。印尼制造业能否顶住进口冲击，才是这份协定的隐藏考卷。

## 产业链的短板：出口结构里的隐忧

细看印尼对加出口的构成，短板一目了然。2025年，印尼主要出口手机与电子设备（约1亿1400万美元）、电机（约1亿300万美元）、天然橡胶（8900万美元）、可可脂（6500万美元）与鞋类（6300万美元）。

这些品类有一个共同点：大多处在产业链中下游，技术含量与附加值有限。印尼经济统筹部自己列出的受益产业——矿物金属、化工、汽车、木材橡胶、机械电子——同样集中在资源加工与组装环节。一份自贸协定可以改变价格，却改变不了产业结构。如果三年后印尼对加出口仍是这几样"老面孔"，那么所谓"扩大市场准入"就只是一句漂亮的口号。

## 关键矿产与电动车电池：真正的战略意图

如果说前几项还停留在贸易层面，那么印尼在投资领域抛出的清单，才暴露了协定的战略野心。据印尼官方通讯社安塔拉（Antara）报道，印尼邀请加拿大矿业与加工企业参与关键矿产、工业下游化、符合环境、社会与治理（ESG）标准的采矿，以及电动车电池生态建设；同时希望加方在可再生能源、电网现代化与小型模块化反应堆（SMR）等清洁能源项目上提供技术转让与融资。印尼还鼓励加拿大的机构投资者与加拿大出口发展公司（EDC），扩大对基础设施、数字经济、物流、农业技术与肥料供应链的投资。

这份清单与印尼近年力推的"下游化"战略一脉相承：不再满足于出口镍矿等原材料，而是要用外资和技术把加工环节留在国内。加拿大在矿业、核能与养老基金长期投资上有专长，正好补印尼的短板。但邀请归邀请，落地才是关键——资本不会因一纸协定就蜂拥而至，它要看的是政策稳定、法律确定性和回报率。

## 从双边到区域：东盟—加拿大自贸的伏笔

印尼—加拿大协定还有一层区域意义。两国已商定于2027年举办贸易与投资峰会，为协定全面落实造势；同时就东盟—加拿大自由贸易协定交换意见，印尼推动在明年3月前完成谈判。

值得注意的是，加拿大与东南亚的双边自贸安排此前几乎空白。印尼抢先与加拿大达成全面伙伴关系，实际上是为更大规模的东盟—加拿大协定探路：先在本国范围内测试规则对接、原产地标准与服务开放，再推广到区域。若明年3月区域谈判如期收官，印尼这份双边协定就成了铺路石；若谈判拖延，它也不至于白签——至少保住了一个北美落脚点。

## 延伸阅读

**全面经济伙伴关系协定（Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement）**：比传统自贸协定覆盖范围更广，除货物关税外还涉及服务贸易、投资、知识产权与供应链合作。

**贸易多元化（Trade Diversification）**：通过增加贸易伙伴与产品种类来分散外部风险的策略，是中等经济体应对保护主义与供应链重构的常见选择。

**贸易逆差（Trade Deficit）**：一国在一定时期内进口额大于出口额的状态，长期逆差往往反映两国在产业结构与竞争力上的不对称。

# Indonesia–Canada FTA Takes Effect in December: Thousands of Tariff Lines Zeroed — What Game Is Jakarta Playing?

The Indonesia–Canada Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (ICA-CEPA) will enter into force on 1 December, with both sides eliminating import tariffs on 3,100 tariff lines. According to Lianhe Zaobao, Indonesian Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs Airlangga said on 15 November that Indonesia had completed its ratification process and formally notified Canada through a diplomatic note — the first such agreement Jakarta has struck with a North American country. On the surface this is a modest bilateral affair: last year's two-way trade stood at US$4.3 billion, a modest slice of Indonesia's foreign trade. But widen the lens — from the Indonesia–EU CEPA to be signed on 21 November, to the ASEAN–Canada FTA targeted for completion by March next year — and Jakarta is clearly playing a long game of trade diversification. The real question is not how many tariffs fell, but whether Indonesia can convert "market access" into genuine industrial upgrading.

## The numbers: from US$4.3 billion to 3,100 tariff lines

Let us start with the ledger. Indonesia–Canada trade reached US$4.3 billion in 2025, of which Indonesian exports accounted for US$1.70 billion. In the first nine months of this year, two-way trade hit US$4.03 billion, up 19.76 percent year on year. Indonesian exports to Canada have climbed steadily from US$1.06 billion in 2021 to US$1.70 billion last year.

Once the agreement takes effect, tariffs on 3,100 tariff lines will be removed. By Indonesia's trade ministry, Canada will grant preferential treatment to 90.55 percent of tariff lines for Indonesian goods, while Indonesia will gradually cut or eliminate duties on 85.54 percent of Canadian lines. Indonesia's economic coordination ministry estimates the pact could unlock about US$1.6 billion in additional exports, with beneficiaries including minerals and metals, chemicals, autos, wood and rubber, machinery and electronics, food and beverages, and aquatic products.

The figures look impressive. But one question is worth pressing: of those 3,100 lines, how many cover categories where Indonesia is genuinely competitive and can expand exports immediately? Zero tariffs merely open the door; walking through it depends on the product itself.

## A first embrace of North America: why now

The first label on this agreement is "first" — Indonesia's first comprehensive partnership with a North American country. For Canada, it is likewise the first bilateral trade deal with a Southeast Asian nation. Two middle powers in search of new markets finding each other is hardly surprising.

The timing matters more. With protectionism on the rise and supply chains repeatedly carved up by geopolitics, Jakarta plainly does not want to bet its exports on a handful of traditional markets. In the same window, Indonesian Trade Minister Budi will lead a delegation to Brussels on 21 November to sign the Indonesia–EU CEPA after a decade of talks, while Indonesia pushes to conclude ASEAN–Canada FTA negotiations by March next year. Bilateral, regional, intercontinental — three tracks advanced in parallel, driven by a single logic: spread the risk.

## The deficit question: who needs this deal more?

Observers, however, should look past the official press releases. One uncomfortable fact stands out: Indonesia runs a persistent trade deficit with Canada. In 2025, Indonesia exported US$1.70 billion to Canada but imported US$2.67 billion; in the first eight months of this year the deficit stood at roughly US$1.27 billion. Indonesia sends phones, electronics, natural rubber, cocoa butter and footwear; it buys wheat, fertiliser, pulp and mineral fuels.

That raises a sharp question: with tariffs mutually eliminated, who benefits more? Indonesia's export staples are labour-intensive and resource-based, highly price-sensitive, so lower duties should help. But Canadian wheat and fertiliser also enjoy cost advantages and will find it easier to enter Indonesia. For the deficit side, free trade is always a double-edged sword — lowering the threshold for others to enter your market matters just as much as opening theirs. Whether Indonesian manufacturers can withstand import competition is the hidden exam of this agreement.

## The industrial-chain weak spot

Look closely at Indonesia's export mix to Canada and the weakness is plain. In 2025, the leading categories were mobile phones and electronic devices (about US$114 million), electrical machinery (US$103 million), natural rubber (US$89 million), cocoa butter (US$65 million) and footwear (US$63 million).

These products share one trait: most sit in the middle and lower reaches of value chains, with limited technology and added value. The beneficiary sectors listed by Indonesia's own economic coordination ministry — minerals and metals, chemicals, autos, wood and rubber, machinery and electronics — are likewise concentrated in resource processing and assembly. An FTA can change prices but not industrial structure. If three years from now Indonesia is still exporting the same old lineup to Canada, then "expanded market access" will have been just a fine-sounding slogan.

## Critical minerals and EV batteries: the real strategic intent

If the previous items stay at the level of trade, the investment wish-list Indonesia has tabled reveals the agreement's strategic ambition. According to state news agency Antara, Indonesia has invited Canadian mining and processing firms to collaborate on critical minerals, industrial downstreaming, mining that meets environmental, social and governance (ESG) standards, and the electric-vehicle battery ecosystem. It also wants Canadian technology transfer and financing for renewable energy, grid modernisation and small modular reactors (SMRs), and has encouraged Canadian institutional investors and Export Development Canada (EDC) to expand funding for infrastructure, the digital economy, logistics, agricultural technology and fertiliser supply chains.

That list fits seamlessly with Indonesia's much-touted downstreaming strategy: no longer content to export raw nickel and other ores, Jakarta wants foreign capital and technology to keep processing at home. Canada has genuine strengths in mining, nuclear power and long-horizon pension investment — a complement to Indonesia's gaps. But invitation is not implementation. Capital will not flock in on the strength of a single agreement; it looks to policy stability, legal certainty and returns.

## From bilateral to regional: the ASEAN–Canada subplot

The ICA-CEPA also carries a regional dimension. The two countries have agreed to hold a Trade and Investment Summit in 2027 to support full implementation, and have exchanged views on the ASEAN–Canada Free Trade Agreement, with Indonesia pushing to conclude negotiations by March next year.

Worth noting: Canada had almost no bilateral free-trade arrangements with Southeast Asia. By moving first, Jakarta is effectively scouting the path for a larger ASEAN–Canada deal — testing regulatory alignment, rules of origin and services liberalisation at home before scaling them up regionally. If regional talks close on schedule in March, this bilateral pact becomes a stepping stone; if they stall, Indonesia still keeps a foothold in North America.

## Further reading

**Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA)**: A broader arrangement than a conventional FTA, covering services, investment, intellectual property and supply-chain cooperation in addition to tariff cuts.

**Trade Diversification**: A strategy of reducing external risk by multiplying trade partners and products — a common choice for middle powers facing protectionism and supply-chain restructuring.

**Trade Deficit**: A situation in which a country's imports exceed its exports over a given period; persistent deficits often reflect asymmetry in industrial structure and competitiveness.

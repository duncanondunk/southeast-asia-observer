# 侨企探路磨憨—磨丁：侨资如何成为中老互联互通的“双向桥”

如果不是中老铁路，磨憨大概还只是西双版纳勐腊县一个安静的边境口岸。据中国新闻网报道，这个位于云南省西双版纳傣族自治州勐腊县的边境小镇，如今已是中老铁路的关键节点，并于二〇二二年四月起由昆明市托管，被纳入省会城市的产业与招商版图。

一个长期以过境贸易为主的边陲，为何突然成了“投资热土”？答案在于区位与政策的叠加：磨憨一端连着中国的庞大腹地，另一端经老挝直达东南亚（Southeast Asia）。昆明接管后，资源调度、园区建设和招商推介明显提速。此次近三十名来自北京（Beijing）、昆明、成都的企业及侨商代表走进南坡国际产业示范园和磨憨公路口岸，本身就是这种“提速”的注脚。

但热土不等于变现。产业园建起来了，企业愿不愿意来、来了能不能赚到钱，才是真正的试金石。

## 侨企为何而来

此次考察由昆明市二〇二六年“法治宣传边关行”系列活动引出，参与者除了内地企业，更有一批侨商代表。昆明市归国华侨联合会副主席张胜在考察时直言：“磨憨连接中国与东南亚市场，交通便利、产业配套日趋完善，是一片投资热土。”

这句话点出了侨企的核心诉求：他们需要的是连接中国与东南亚的“接口”。许多东南亚华商本就深耕当地市场，拥有渠道、人脉与跨境经营经验；而磨憨—磨丁合作区恰好提供了一个把这种经验“反哺”到祖国边境、再借铁路辐射中南半岛的支点。换句话说，侨资在这里不是单向的“引进来”，而是双向的“桥”——一头连着中国产能，一头连着东盟（ASEAN）需求。

## 产业园与口岸：硬联通的进展

硬指标最见说服力。据合作区方面介绍，南坡国际产业合作区规划面积约七平方公里，聚焦出口导向型、劳动密集型产业，重点吸引纺织服装、装备制造、农产品加工等企业集聚。截至今年九月，合作区已建成标准化厂房十九点八万平方米，入驻产业类项目三十个，部分已投产。

口岸侧的变化更具象：磨憨公路口岸正实施综合提升改造，原有二车道卡口拓宽至十二车道，新建卡口已具备启用条件，并配套信息化查验设施。这意味着中老之间的货车通行与查验能力将大幅跃升。

从二车道到十二车道，数字背后是两国货物流量的真实增长，也折射出磨憨从“过路站”向“枢纽站”的转型。但也要看到，劳动密集型产业的集聚能否持续，取决于东盟市场的消化能力与区域产业链的真实协同，而非单靠园区硬件。

## 不只是钱：跨境医疗与生命健康的想象

值得玩味的是，此次探路的侨企并非都是传统制造或贸易商。张胜所在企业主营干细胞存储，他看好磨憨在“生命健康产业协作、跨境健康服务”方面的潜力；昆明鹏瑞利医院免疫中心主任王强则提出，可在跨境医疗康养、养老护理人才培训等领域探索合作。

这透露出一个信号：侨企看中的不只是低成本厂房，还有中老之间日益频繁的人员往来所催生的“软需求”——医疗、康养、商旅服务。随着中老铁路带旺跨境客流，边境城市完全可能从“物流枢纽”延伸出“健康与文旅枢纽”。只不过，这类高端服务的落地周期更长、监管更复杂，需要比建厂房更耐心的制度磨合。

## 法治与侨务：看不见的软基础设施

此次活动的全称是“法治宣传边关行”，并配套“侨企心系边关·走进磨憨”招商推介会。昆明市侨联党组书记、主席尹朝晖表示，将持续跟进企业合作意向，依托当地为侨服务及法律服务机制，为企业投资准入、跨境经营、纠纷解决等提供支持。

这一点往往被忽视，却至关重要。跨境投资最大的不确定性来自规则差异与纠纷解决成本。把“为侨服务”和“法律服务”前置到招商环节，实质是在搭建一套面向侨企的软基础设施：让企业敢来、来了有规可依、出问题有路可诉。对于习惯在东南亚复杂营商环境中运作的华商而言，这种确定性本身，可能比税收优惠更具吸引力。

磨憨—磨丁的故事，本质上是中老命运共同体在边境线上的一个微观样本。侨企的探路，能否从“考察热”走向“落地实”，既取决于园区与口岸的硬联通，也取决于法治与侨务的软联通。双向的桥，要两头都牢固，才算真正架通。

## 延伸阅读

- **中老铁路（China–Laos Railway）**：连接中国昆明与老挝万象的电气化铁路，二〇二一年十二月全线通车，是泛亚铁路中线关键段，极大缩短了中国西南与中南半岛的陆运时间。
- **磨憨—磨丁经济合作区（Mohan–Boten Economic Cooperation Zone）**：中老两国边境跨境合作区，中方一侧为云南磨憨、老方一侧为琅南塔省磨丁，是“一带一路（Belt and Road Initiative）”标志性跨境合作平台之一。
- **归国华侨联合会（Returned Overseas Chinese Federation）**：中国联系海外侨胞与归侨的群众团体，在引资引智、为侨服务与跨境投资对接中扮演桥梁角色。

# Overseas Chinese firms scout Mohan–Boten: how diaspora capital becomes a two-way bridge for China–Laos connectivity

Without the China–Laos Railway, Mohan would likely still be a quiet border crossing in Mengla County, Xishuangbanna. According to China News Service, this town in Yunnan Province is now a key node on the railway and, since April 2022, has been placed under the administration of Kunming, the provincial capital, folding it into the city's industrial and investment map.

Why would a frontier long defined by transit trade suddenly be called an "investment hotspot"? The answer lies in the stacking of location and policy: Mohan connects China's vast hinterland at one end and, via Laos, Southeast Asia at the other. After Kunming took over, resource allocation, park construction and investment promotion visibly accelerated. The fact that nearly 30 enterprise and overseas Chinese business representatives from Beijing, Kunming and Chengdu walked through the Nanpo International Industry Demonstration Park and the Mohan highway port is itself a footnote to that acceleration.

But a hotspot is not the same as a payoff. Parks may be built; whether firms actually come, and whether they can earn, is the real test.

## Why the overseas Chinese firms came

The visit was staged under Kunming's 2026 "Rule-of-Law Publicity at the Border" campaign, and the participants included, beyond mainland enterprises, a contingent of overseas Chinese business representatives. Zhang Sheng, vice chairman of the Kunming Returned Overseas Chinese Federation, put it plainly during the tour: "Mohan connects the Chinese and Southeast Asian markets, with convenient transport and improving industrial support — it is a land of investment opportunity."

That line captures the core appeal for diaspora firms: they need an "interface" linking China and Southeast Asia. Many ethnic Chinese businesspeople in Southeast Asia already have deep roots in local markets, with distribution channels, networks and cross-border operating experience. The Mohan–Boten zone offers a pivot to channel that experience back to the motherland's border and, via the railway, radiate across the Indochinese Peninsula. In other words, diaspora capital here is not a one-way "bringing in" but a two-way "bridge" — one end anchored to Chinese production capacity, the other to ASEAN demand.

## The park and the port: hard connectivity

Hard numbers make the case. Per the cooperation zone, the Nanpo International Industry Cooperation Park is planned at roughly seven square kilometres, focused on export-oriented and labour-intensive industries, attracting textiles and apparel, equipment manufacturing and agro-processing. By this September, 198,000 square metres of standardised workshops had been built, with 30 industrial projects settled in and some already in production.

The port tells a more tangible story. The Mohan highway port is undergoing a comprehensive upgrade: its original two-lane checkpoint is being widened to twelve lanes, the new checkpoint is ready for operation, and informatised inspection facilities are in place. That means a sharp jump in truck throughput and inspection capacity between China and Laos.

From two lanes to twelve, the figure reflects real growth in bilateral freight — and Mohan's shift from a "pass-through station" to a "hub station." Yet labour-intensive clustering will hold only if ASEAN markets can absorb the output and regional supply chains truly sync, not merely on the strength of hardware.

## More than money: cross-border healthcare

Tellingly, not all the scouting firms were traditional manufacturers or traders. Zhang's own company handles stem-cell storage, and he sees promise in "life-health industry collaboration and cross-border health services"; Wang Qiang, director of the immunology centre at Kunming's PuraPharm Hospital, proposed exploring cross-border medical wellness and elderly-care nursing training.

This signals something: diaspora firms are eyeing not just cheap workshops but the "soft demand" spawned by intensifying people-to-people flows — healthcare, wellness, business travel. As the railway boosts cross-border passenger traffic, a border city could extend from "logistics hub" to "health and tourism hub." Such high-end services, however, take longer to land and involve heavier regulation — demanding more patient institutional alignment than building a workshop.

## Rule of law and qiao affairs: the soft infrastructure

The campaign's full name is "Rule-of-Law Publicity at the Border," paired with an investment promotion session, "Overseas Chinese Enterprises Care for the Border · Entering Mohan." Yin Zhaohui, Party secretary and chairman of the Kunming Returned Overseas Chinese Federation, said the federation will keep following up on cooperation intentions and, relying on local mechanisms for overseas Chinese services and legal support, back firms on market access, cross-border operations and dispute resolution.

This is often overlooked yet vital. The biggest uncertainty in cross-border investment comes from divergent rules and the cost of dispute resolution. Putting "services for overseas Chinese" and "legal support" up front in the investment process is, in effect, building a soft infrastructure for diaspora firms: making them willing to come, giving them rules to rely on, and a path to litigate when things go wrong. For ethnic Chinese businesspeople accustomed to complex Southeast Asian business environments, that certainty may be more attractive than a tax break.

Mohan–Boten is, at heart, a microscopic sample of the China–Laos community with a shared future, traced onto a border line. Whether the diaspora's scouting moves from "inspection fever" to "real landing" depends as much on the hard connectivity of parks and ports as on the soft connectivity of rule of law and qiao affairs. A two-way bridge only truly spans when both ends are solid.

## Further reading

- **China–Laos Railway**: An electrified railway linking Kunming with Vientiane, opened in full in December 2021; a key segment of the central Pan-Asian rail line that has sharply cut overland travel time between southwest China and the Indochinese Peninsula.
- **Mohan–Boten Economic Cooperation Zone**: A China–Laos cross-border cooperation zone straddling the border — Mohan on the Chinese side in Yunnan, Boten in Laos's Luang Namtha Province — and a flagship platform of the Belt and Road Initiative.
- **Returned Overseas Chinese Federation**: A mass organisation connecting China with overseas compatriots and returned emigrants, acting as a bridge in investment and talent attraction, overseas Chinese services and cross-border investment matchmaking.

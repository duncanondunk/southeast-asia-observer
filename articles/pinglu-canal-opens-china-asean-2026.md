# 平陆运河今日通航：凿通西南向海新通道，重塑中国—东盟物流版图

2026年9月16日，平陆运河正式通航。这条纵贯广西、全长134.2公里的人工运河，是新中国成立以来第一条在国家层面统筹建设的通江达海运河。它的意义远不止于把西江航运干线连到北部湾。据《日经亚洲》9月12日报道，它的到来恰逢一个微妙的节点：中国产能正加速向东南亚转移，而这条运河正悄悄被写进这个故事里。

## 一条迟到百年的"通江达海"运河

数十年来，广西虽近海，却长期"面朝大海、背对海洋"。西江流域的物资要出海，须绕行珠江口、取道广东，路远且贵。平陆运河把这套地理逻辑整个翻转：船舶自西津库区平塘江口出发，向南经陆屋镇、沿钦江直下北部湾，较原先绕行珠三角的路线缩短约560公里，综合物流成本有望下降18%至30%。

这不是小工程。项目自2022年8月28日开工，从山体与河道中挖出约3.15亿立方米土石方，足以填满"鸟巢"体育场105次。总投资约727亿元（约102亿美元）。对于一个长期被形容为"近海而不向海"的西南内陆而言，这无异于一次经济地理的"换向"。

## 数字：改写西南物流的硬指标

平陆运河按内河Ⅰ级标准建设，可通航5000吨级船舶。这一项指标分量不小：它让西南制造得以不经早已饱和的珠三角港口而直抵大海。对煤炭南运、矿产与建材北上的低值大宗货物而言，运费每降一分，竞争力便增一分。

天风证券两年前估计，到2035年，运河每年可为北部湾港带来2亿吨新增货运量，而该港去年货物吞吐量为3.58亿吨。为吸引货流，2026年剩余时间内船舶过闸免收通行费；此后收费标准定为每总吨每道船闸1元人民币。

## 孙中山的蓝图，2026年才落地

这一设想比运河本身更古老。《日经亚洲》指出，该构想普遍被认为源自孙中山——他在1920年的《实业计划》中曾提出，修建一条通往北部湾钦州港的航运通道，以带动云南、贵州等内陆省份经济发展。百年之后，蓝图终成钢筋混凝土。

平陆运河也建立在近年中国的内河建设脉络之上。全长355公里的江淮运河于2023年建成，而连接长江与西江的湘桂运河、连接长江与珠江的赣粤运河也在规划之中。这显示出一种清晰的取向：把货运更多地交给水运，而非只倚重公路、铁路、大坝与机场。

## 工程之难：65米落差与"中国芯"船闸

最大的工程难题是水位落差。从上游西江到出海口，全线落差达65米。工程师以三组水力船闸化解，可升降载重5000吨的船舶，闸门可在30秒内迅速关闭。船闸水力操作系统的关键部件均为国内设计制造。

《日经亚洲》列举了若干重大技术突破：采用节水池垂直叠层布置技术，并以3D打印制造水力闸门的控制机构。对如此量级的工程而言，其意味不在"炫技"，而在贸易通道核心装备的自主可控。

## 真正的命题：为中国产能南移凿通新航道

这才是《日经亚洲》真正想说的、也最值得认真对待的论点。东南亚已超越北美与欧洲，成为中国最大的贸易伙伴；与此同时，部分中国企业把生产活动迁往东南亚——既为利用当地较低的劳动力与投入成本，也为规避美国及其他出口市场针对"中国制造"产品不断增加的壁垒。

在此视角下，运河的价值不只是缩短中国自身的路程。它强化的是一套区域制造体系的"连接组织"：中国与东南亚经济正日益咬合在一起。总部位于新加坡的研究公司Asia Decoded董事兼首席经济学家普里扬卡·基肖尔对《日经亚洲》表示，从更大的层面看，运河将强化东盟作为具有成本效率的制造业中心的地位。

## 成本账：比巴拿马更"便宜"的捷径

这笔经济账天然引来比较。平陆运河每总吨每道船闸1元的收费，放在巴拿马运河面前几乎可以忽略——后者的通行时段拍卖价格有时超过250万美元（约1780万元人民币）。二者的意义不在于争夺同一批货流（它们并不竞争），而在于北京把这条新走廊的定价，摆出了"让人用起来"而非"仅供瞻仰"的姿态。

近期的引力来自国内：西南企业获得560公里捷径，据观察者测算每年可节省最高52亿元人民币（约7.3亿美元）物流开支。而从更长时间看，更大的红利是一条更稳定、更便宜的南向动脉，服务于正跨境迁移的供应链。

## 新加坡资本已先行一步

通航并非只是中国的故事。北部湾港最繁忙的码头，由一家与新加坡国有企业PSA国际港务集团合资成立的企业运营。这个细节耐人寻味：在首趟满载货轮启航之前，东盟资本早已嵌入运河的商业肌理。

它无声地提醒人们，与东南亚的"互联互通"日益成为双向。运河给中国一条更快触达东盟市场的线路；而新加坡港务专长出现在一座中国门户港之内，则意味着这种一体化比关税表走得更深。

## 结语：一条河与区域一体化的再定义

平陆运河不是万能药。生态学者会盯紧跨流域调水与航运用水，运营方会盯紧货物是否真的选择这条线路。基础设施只是前提，而非保证——让货物"愿意走、走得划算"，才是真正的检验。

但象征意义是真实的。一条百年前草拟的水道，如今把中国西南与北部湾、进而与东南亚系在一起。正如《日经亚洲》所框定的，运河或在中国产能加速向区域迁移的进程中发挥作用。换言之，这条捷径本身也是一则命题：区域一体化正被一条条可通航的水道，重新浇筑成型。

## 延伸阅读

**平陆运河（Pinglu Canal）**：纵贯广西、连通西江航运干线至北部湾的人工运河，全长134.2公里，是新中国成立以来第一条国家层面统筹建设的通江达海运河。

**北部湾（Beibu Gulf）**：中国南海西北部海域，环抱广西、广东、海南，是面向东盟的重要出海口。

**西部陆海新通道（Western Land-Sea New Corridor）**：中国引导西南内陆货物出海并衔接东南亚的物流战略，平陆运河是其骨干工程之一。

# China's Pinglu Canal opens, redrawing the southwest's trade route to Southeast Asia

On 16 September 2026, the Pinglu Canal begins operation. The 134.2-kilometre artificial waterway through Guangxi — the first sea-access canal built at the national level since the founding of the People's Republic — does more than connect the Xijiang river system to the Beibu Gulf. As Nikkei Asia reported on 12 September, it arrives at a precise moment: China's supply chains are accelerating their shift toward Southeast Asia, and the canal is quietly being written into that story.

## A century-late waterway to the sea

For decades, Guangxi sat close to the coast yet faced inland. Cargo from the Xijiang basin had to detour through the Pearl River estuary around Guangdong to reach the sea, a long, costly loop. Pinglu flips that geometry. Ships can now leave the Xijiang at Hengzhou, head south through Qinxian, and discharge directly into the Beibu Gulf — cutting roughly 560 kilometres off the route to ASEAN ports and lowering comprehensive logistics costs by an estimated 18% to 30%.

The project is not small. Construction, which began on 28 August 2022, moved about 315 million cubic metres of earth and rock — enough to fill the Bird's Nest stadium 105 times. Total investment is about 72.7 billion yuan (roughly US$10.2 billion). For a southwest interior long described as "facing the sea but turning its back on it," this is a literal reorientation.

## The numbers: hard metrics that redraw southwest logistics

The canal is built to Class I inland-water standards, navigable by 5,000-tonne vessels. That single specification matters: it lets southwestern manufacturing reach the sea without routing through the Pearl River Delta's saturated ports. For bulk commodities — coal moving south, minerals and building materials moving north — every yuan saved in freight is a point of competitiveness.

Analysts at TF Securities estimated two years ago that by 2035 the canal could add 200 million tonnes of annual cargo to the Beibu Gulf port, on top of the 358 million tonnes it handled last year. To pull cargo in, passage through the canal's locks is free for the remainder of 2026; thereafter the fee is set at 1 yuan per gross tonne per lock.

## Sun Yat-sen's blueprint, realized only in 2026

The idea is older than the canal itself. Nikkei Asia notes the concept is widely traced to Sun Yat-sen, who in his 1920 "Industrial Plan" proposed a shipping channel to Qinzhou port on the Beibu Gulf to develop the economies of inland provinces such as Yunnan and Guizhou. A century later, the vision is concrete.

Pinglu also builds on China's recent waterway push. The 355-kilometre Jianghuai Canal, completed in 2023, and planning for two further north–south canals — the Xiang-Guangxi link between the Yangtze and Xijiang, and the Gan-Guangdong link between the Yangtze and Pearl — show a deliberate strategy of moving freight by water rather than only by road, rail, dam and airport.

## The engineering hurdle: a 65-metre drop and "home-grown" locks

The hardest problem was elevation. The route drops 65 metres from the upper Xijiang to the sea. Engineers solved it with three sets of hydraulic ship locks capable of lifting and lowering 5,000-tonne vessels, with gates that can close in 30 seconds. Key components of the lock hydraulic systems were designed and built domestically.

Nikkei Asia highlights several technical breakthroughs: a vertical, stacked water-saving-basin layout, and the use of 3D-printing to manufacture the control mechanisms of the lock gates. For a project of this scale, the implication is less about prestige than about self-reliance in the machinery that keeps a trade corridor running.

## The real thesis: carving a new channel for China's production shift south

Here is the argument Nikkei Asia actually makes, and it is the one worth taking seriously. Southeast Asia has overtaken North America and Europe to become China's largest trading partner. At the same time, some Chinese firms are moving production to Southeast Asia — partly for lower labour and input costs, partly to circumvent rising barriers on "Made in China" goods in the US and other export markets.

The canal's value, in this reading, is not only that it shortens China's own routes. It strengthens the connective tissue of a regional manufacturing system in which Chinese and Southeast Asian economies are increasingly interlocked. Priyanka Kishore, chief economist and director at Singapore-based Asia Decoded, told Nikkei that at a larger level the canal will reinforce ASEAN's position as a cost-efficient manufacturing centre.

## The cost equation: a shortcut cheaper than Panama

The economics invite comparison. Pinglu's lock fee of 1 yuan per gross tonne per lock is a rounding error next to the Panama Canal, where auction prices for transit slots have at times exceeded US$2.5 million. The point is not that the two canals compete for the same traffic — they do not — but that Beijing is pricing its new corridor to be used, not merely admired.

The near-term pull is domestic: Southwest firms gain a 560-kilometre shortcut and, by observers' estimates, up to 5.2 billion yuan (about US$730 million) in annual logistics savings. Over time, the bigger prize is a steadier, cheaper southbound artery for the supply chains relocating across the border.

## Singapore capital has already moved in

The opening is not only a Chinese story. The busiest terminal at the Beibu Gulf port is operated by a joint venture with PSA International, Singapore's state-linked port operator. That detail matters: ASEAN capital is already embedded in the canal's commercial fabric before the first full-scale shipment sails.

It is a quiet reminder that "connectivity" with Southeast Asia is increasingly two-way. The canal gives China a faster line to ASEAN markets; the presence of Singaporean port expertise inside a Chinese gateway suggests the integration runs deeper than tariff tables.

## Redefining integration, one river at a time

Pinglu is not a panacea. Ecologists will watch cross-basin water transfer and navigation withdrawals; operators will watch whether cargo actually chooses the route. Infrastructure is a prerequisite, not a guarantee — getting goods to "want to go, and to go cheaply," is the real test.

But the symbolism is real. A waterway first sketched a century ago now binds China's southwest to the Beibu Gulf, and through it to Southeast Asia. As Nikkei Asia frames it, the canal may play a role in the accelerating relocation of Chinese supply chains into the region. The shortcut, in other words, is also a thesis: that regional integration is being rebuilt, quite literally, as navigable water.

## Further reading

**Pinglu Canal (平陆运河)**: A 134.2-km artificial canal in Guangxi linking the Xijiang waterway to the Beibu Gulf, the first sea-access canal built at China's national level since 1949.

**Beibu Gulf (北部湾)**: The northwestern section of the South China Sea bordering Guangxi, Guangdong and Hainan; a key gateway to ASEAN.

**Western Land-Sea New Corridor (西部陆海新通道)**: A Chinese logistics strategy channelling southwest inland cargo to sea and onward to Southeast Asia, of which Pinglu is a backbone piece.

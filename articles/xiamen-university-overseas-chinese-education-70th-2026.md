# 厦门大学海外教育暨国际中文教育70周年：真正的教育是双向奔赴

10月3日，厦门大学海外教育暨国际中文教育70周年高质量发展大会在翔安校区举行。一场以“嘉庚文脉润四海，文明互鉴耀五洲”为题的纪念，把人们的目光重新拉回这所因侨而生的大学。70年过去，厦大海外教育从一封封跨海函授信件，长成了覆盖南洋、辐射全球的汉语课堂。它提醒我们：当国际中文教育被反复讨论是否为“软实力输出”时，厦大给出的答案很朴素——真正的教育，从来都是双向奔赴。

## 因侨而生：嘉庚遗产与“面向东南亚华侨”的办学原点

厦门大学与华侨华人的缘分，写在基因里。1921年，南洋侨领陈嘉庚倾资创办厦大，这所大学从诞生那天起就带着“面向南洋”的底色。1956年，紧扣国家“面向东南亚华侨”的办学方向，厦大创办华侨函授部，成为国内最早专门从事海外华文教育的高校之一。

为什么是厦大？因为近代以来，闽南子弟“下南洋”，东南亚本就是厦门最大的侨乡腹地。厦大办海外教育，不是凭空起念，而是回应一个庞大离散族群“留根”的文化需求。这一点，今天的很多分析都容易忽略：中国高校的国际中文教育，起点恰恰是华侨华人自己的需求，而非国家的对外宣传工程。

## 七十年三阶段：从跨海函授到全球课堂

校长胡文平在大会上回顾，七十年薪火相传，学院逐步形成了汉语国际教育与文化传播并重、学历与非学历兼备、面授与远程并举、理论与应用并行的格局。院长陈志伟将其概括为“因侨而生、因中文而兴、因文明互鉴而远”，并形成了国际中文教育、孔子学院建设、南方基地三位一体的协同格局。

从1956年的函授部，到今天面向印度尼西亚、菲律宾、泰国、马来西亚等国设立海外教学点，厦大的海外教育走过的是一条“由近及远”的路线——先服务南洋侨社，再借孔子学院走向欧美，如今又在新一轮中国—东盟人文交流中回到东南亚深耕。这种“三阶段”的节奏，恰是华侨华人网络随时代重心迁移的缩影。

## “双向奔赴”：国际中文教育的话语转向

美籍教授潘维廉的致辞，是这场大会最有温度的注脚。1988年他从美国来厦大学汉语，后留校任教，1992年成为福建首位拿到“中国绿卡”的外国人。他说：“我亲眼见证中国向世界广泛学习。但真正的教育，从来都是双向奔赴，世界同样有许多东西可以向中国学习。”

这句话耐人寻味。过去谈国际中文教育，外界常把它简化为“文化输出”甚至“渗透”。厦大用七十年实践提出的“双向奔赴”，实质是一种话语转向：把汉语教学从“我教你”的单线叙事，调整为“互学互鉴”的双向关系。在东南亚，这一点更有说服力——当地华社主动办学、主动送教师来华进修，需求是内生的，不是外部强加的。

## 马来西亚华文教师奖学金：离散社群反哺师资

大会上的捐赠环节值得单独拎出来说。马来西亚大智慧教育基金会主席王培坤，继2025年捐资500万元人民币设立马来西亚华文教师奖学金后，2026年再增资500万元人民币。这笔钱用于支持马来西亚籍学生来厦大学习、毕业后回国任教，为海外华文师资培养汇聚社会力量。

这正是观察华侨华人议题时最容易被数据淹没的细节：离散社群不是被动的“文化接收端”。他们自己出钱、出力，把子弟送回祖籍地的高校，再送回住在国讲台。一个马来西亚华人基金会，连续两年向中国的大学注资，培养的对象却是马来西亚本土的华文教师——这是一条自下而上的“反哺链”，比任何官方叙事都更能说明华文教育在东南亚的生命力。

## 南方基地再落子：东南亚办学网络扩容

此次大会还启动了新一批海外教学点／教育实践基地，合作单位来自印度尼西亚、菲律宾、泰国、马来西亚，外加中亚的吉尔吉斯斯坦。把东南亚四国与孔子学院网络叠加，厦大“南方基地”在区域内的办学触角进一步加密。

放在更大的图景里看，这正是中国—东盟“民心相通”在教育和语言层面的落地。当经贸、基建合作已趋于成熟，人文交流就成了维持双边关系韧性的关键变量。厦大这类高校凭借地缘与侨缘优势，天然成为中国—东盟教育合作的支点——它做的不是短期项目，而是把几代人的师生、校友网络编织进区域关系之中。

## 延伸阅读

- **陈嘉庚（Tan Kah Kee）**：南洋华侨领袖，1910年代起在新马兴办教育，1921年倾资创办厦门大学与集美学校，被毛泽东誉为“华侨旗帜、民族光辉”，是东南亚华侨与中国近代教育纽带的核心人物。
- **孔子学院（Confucius Institute）**：由中国与海外高校合作设立的语言文化推广机构，自2004年起全球铺开；其“借船出海”的合作模式常被当作国际中文教育“走出去”的代名词。
- **南方基地（Southern Base）**：教育部依托厦大等高校在福建设立的国际中文教育与侨务人才培养平台，侧重服务东南亚及港澳台侨，是“因侨而生”办学传统的制度性延续。

# Xiamen University's 70 Years of Overseas and Chinese-Medium Education: True Education Is Always a Two-Way Journey

On 3 October, Xiamen University (XMU) held a high-quality development conference at its Xiang'an campus to mark the 70th anniversary of its overseas education and international Chinese-language education. Framed by the theme "The Kah Kee legacy nourishes the four seas; mutual learning among civilisations shines across the five continents," the event turned attention back to a university that was, in a sense, born for the diaspora. Seven decades on, XMU's overseas education has grown from letters of cross-sea correspondence courses into Chinese-language classrooms spanning Nanyang and reaching the globe. It is a reminder that, at a moment when international Chinese education is repeatedly cast as "soft-power export," XMU's answer is plain: true education has always been a two-way journey.

## Born for the diaspora: the Tan Kah Kee legacy and the "Southeast Asian overseas Chinese" founding mission

XMU's bond with overseas Chinese is written into its DNA. In 1921, the Nanyang tycoon and community leader Tan Kah Kee (Chen Jiageng) founded the university with his own fortune, and from its first day XMU carried the colour of "facing Nanyang." In 1956, responding to the state's directive of "serving Southeast Asian overseas Chinese," XMU established its Correspondence Department for Overseas Chinese, becoming one of the earliest domestic universities devoted specifically to overseas Chinese-medium education.

Why XMU? Because from the modern era onward, Minnan (southern Fujian) sons and daughters "went down to Nanyang," and Southeast Asia was always Xiamen's vast qiaoxiang hinterland. XMU's overseas education was not an abstract invention; it answered a vast diaspora's cultural need to "keep its roots." This is too often missed in today's analysis: the starting point of Chinese universities' international Chinese education was the overseas Chinese communities' own demand, not a state external-propagation project.

## Seven decades, three stages: from cross-sea correspondence to a global classroom

In his address, President Hu Wenping recalled that over seventy years the college has developed a distinctive model — pairing Chinese international education with cultural dissemination, combining degree and non-degree programmes, blending in-person and distance teaching, and uniting theory with application. Dean Chen Zhiwei summarised it as "born for the diaspora, thriving through Chinese, reaching far through mutual learning," converging into a tripartite synergy of international Chinese education, Confucius Institute building, and the Southern Base.

From the 1956 correspondence department to today's overseas teaching centres in Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand and Malaysia, XMU's overseas education has followed a "near-to-far" path — first serving Nanyang communities, then reaching Europe and America through Confucius Institutes, and now returning to Southeast Asia to deepen roots amid a new round of China–ASEAN people-to-people exchange. This three-stage rhythm is precisely a microcosm of how the overseas Chinese network has shifted its centre of gravity with the times.

## "A two-way journey": a discursive shift in international Chinese education

The most humane footnote to the conference was the speech by William N. Brown, an American professor at XMU. He came from the United States in 1988 to study Chinese at the university that was, in the 1980s, among the first in China authorised to enrol foreigners for Chinese-language study. He stayed, joined the faculty of the School of Management, and in 1992 became the first foreigner in Fujian to receive a Chinese "green card." "I have witnessed China learning widely from the world," he said. "But true education has never been one-way; the world, too, has much to learn from China."

The remark is telling. International Chinese education has often been simplified abroad into "cultural export," even "penetration." The "two-way journey" that XMU proposes through seventy years of practice is, in essence, a discursive shift — reframing Chinese-language teaching from a one-line "I teach you" narrative into a two-way relationship of mutual learning. In Southeast Asia this lands with particular force: local Chinese communities run their own schools and send their own teachers to China for training; the demand is endogenous, not externally imposed.

## The Malaysian Chinese-teacher scholarship: diaspora communities reinvesting in their own teachers

One donation moment at the conference deserves its own mention. Wong Pui-kwan, chairman of Malaysia's Dazhihui Education Foundation, after donating 5 million RMB (roughly US$700,000) in 2025 to establish a Malaysian Chinese-teacher scholarship, added another 5 million RMB (roughly US$700,000) in 2026. The fund supports Malaysian students studying at XMU and returning home to teach, pooling social strength for the training of overseas Chinese-medium teachers.

This is precisely the kind of detail most easily drowned out by aggregate data when observing overseas Chinese issues: the diaspora is not a passive "cultural receiving end." They put up their own money and effort, send their youth back to ancestral universities, and then send them back to the classrooms of their home countries. A Malaysian Chinese foundation injecting capital into a Chinese university for two consecutive years — to train, of all things, Malaysian local Chinese teachers — is a bottom-up "reinvestment chain" that speaks more powerfully than any official narrative about the vitality of Chinese-medium education in Southeast Asia.

## The Southern Base expands: Southeast Asia's teaching network grows

The conference also launched a new batch of overseas teaching points and practice bases, with partner institutions from Indonesia, the Philippines, Thailand and Malaysia, plus Kyrgyzstan in Central Asia. Stacking these four Southeast Asian countries onto the Confucius Institute network, XMU's "Southern Base" further densifies its regional educational footprint.

Seen against the larger picture, this is exactly how China–ASEAN "people-to-people bonds" land at the level of education and language. As economic, trade and infrastructure cooperation matures, people-to-people exchange becomes the key variable sustaining the resilience of bilateral relations. Universities like XMU, leveraging their geographic and qiaoyuan (diaspora) advantages, are natural pivots for China–ASEAN educational cooperation — and what they build is not short-term projects but multi-generational networks of teachers, students and alumni woven into the fabric of regional relations.

## Further reading

- **Tan Kah Kee (陈嘉庚)**: A Nanyang overseas Chinese leader who built schools across Singapore and Malaysia from the 1910s and founded Xiamen University and the Jimei schools in 1921 with his personal fortune; praised by Mao as the "banner of overseas Chinese and glory of the nation," he is the central figure linking Southeast Asian Chinese to modern Chinese education.
- **Confucius Institute**: A language-and-culture promotion body jointly run by China and overseas universities since 2004; its "borrowing a boat to go to sea" partnership model is often treated as the shorthand for international Chinese education "going global."
- **Southern Base (南方基地)**: A platform for international Chinese education and diaspora talent training established by the Ministry of Education in Fujian with universities such as XMU, focused on serving Southeast Asia and the Hong Kong–Macao–Taiwan communities; the institutional continuation of the "born for the diaspora" tradition.

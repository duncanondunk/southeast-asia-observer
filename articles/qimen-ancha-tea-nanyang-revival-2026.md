# 百年侨销安茶重走南洋路

近日，在安徽祁门县芦溪乡，一场「安茶夜露」活动让一篾包老茶重新站到了聚光灯下。马来西亚留学生杨艺艺端起橙红茶汤，脱口而出：「和我们马来西亚家里的茶很不一样。」一句话，道尽了这盏茶与南洋之间跨越百年的隐秘纽带。

## 从祁门到南洋：一篓茶漂过百年

祁门安茶，历史上鼎鼎大名的「侨销茶」——专为海外侨胞聚居地而制的茶。它创制于明代中晚期，比后来名声更响的祁门红茶还要早。清末民初，安茶顺着阊江水系、经广州口岸，一路销往港澳与东南亚。

值得玩味的是，安茶当年出海，靠的不是资本炒作，而是实实在在的「有用」。南洋气候湿热，疫病时有流行，当地有医生以安茶为药引，治好了不少人。久而久之，它成了侨胞家中常备的康养茶饮，被尊为「圣茶」「神仙茶」。一篾篓紧压的安茶，既是祭祖、婚嫁的礼俗之饮，也装着背井离乡者化不开的乡愁。

## 侨销茶的底色：凉性、祛湿与「圣茶」信仰

安茶性凉祛湿，向来被当作暑天调理之饮。在缺医少药的移民社会里，这样一种「能当药喝的茶」，天然契合了华侨华人对健康与故乡的双重渴求。即便今天，马来西亚许多老华侨乃至华裔新生代，对安茶的认知仍优于国内年轻人——这是一种被离散生活反复强化过的味觉记忆。

这恰是观察「侨销茶」现象的一个切口：它从来不是单纯的农产品，而是一套包裹在茶饮里的身份符号。茶好喝是表，乡愁记忆才是里。

## 断裂与重生：抗战停摆，一九九二年古法复原

好景并未一直延续。抗战时期，水路中断，安茶生产一度停滞。直到上世纪八十年代，一位香港茶人写信寻访安茶，孙义顺老茶号的后人与老茶工才在一九九二年成功复原整套古法制茶工艺。

其中最金贵的一道工序叫「夜露」：每年白露前后，晴夜之中，制茶人把提香后的干茶匀摊在露天竹席上，借山间秋露浸润、昼夜温差退火去燥，自然触发后期发酵。经日晒夜露、箬叶竹篓封装，安茶便越陈越香，民间素有「三年为药、五年为宝」之说。非遗代表性传承人徐乾说，安茶的价值，正在于接续华侨华人的乡愁记忆。

## 重走南洋路：区域全面经济伙伴关系协定框架下的味觉返乡

时隔百年，安茶再度南下。依托《区域全面经济伙伴关系协定》（RCEP）带来的合作机遇，祁门县近年先后赴马来西亚、日本以及中国港澳地区推介安茶，并与马来西亚相关协会签下销售框架协议。

但要说清楚：安茶「重走南洋路」，关税红利只是表层，真正的底层逻辑是离散族群的情感刚需。当返乡青年茶人用短视频记录采茶、编篓、夜露，当夜露活动把制茶体验、中医问诊与热茶品鉴打包成年轻人爱逛的市集，这盏老茶才真正接上了时代的地气。

## 茶之外：乡愁经济与软实力的轻量化路径

一组数据很能说明问题：安茶年产量已从几年前的约200吨增至目前的近1000吨；2026年，祁门县安茶产值约2亿元人民币。安徽省农业科学院茶叶研究所所长张必桦提醒，守传统的同时要加科研、推标准、借电商与国际展会，让「侨销茶」成为消费者认可的「国际茶」。

这正是值得玩味之处。相比孔子学院式自上而下的文化输出，安茶走的是一条轻量化、市场驱动的软实力路径：它不靠宣讲，而靠一杯茶里熟悉的味道，让南洋的华人家庭主动回望故土。当然，情怀能卖一时，品牌要立长久，标准化与年轻化仍是绕不开的考题。

## 延伸阅读

- **侨销茶（Qiaoxiao Tea）**：历史上专门销往海外侨胞聚居区的中国茶，安茶、六堡茶等均属此类，是离散经贸与乡愁记忆的双重载体。
- **南洋（Nanyang）**：东南亚的旧称，特指中国东南沿海移民在南洋群岛与中南半岛形成的华人聚居网络，是理解华侨华人史的核心概念。
- **夜露工艺（Night-Dew Process）**：安茶独有的核心工序，于白露前后露天摊茶借秋露与昼夜温差触发后期发酵，是其「越陈越香」风味的来源。

# Century-old qiaoxiao tea re-walks the Nanyang route

Recently, in Luxi Township of Qimen County, Anhui, a "Night-Dew Ancha" event thrust a basket of woven-packaged old tea back into the spotlight. Sipping the orange-red brew, Malaysian student Yang Yiyi blurted out: "It's nothing like the tea we drink at home in Malaysia." In that single line lies a century-spanning, half-hidden bond between this tea and Nanyang.

## From Qimen to Nanyang: a basket of tea across a century

Qimen Ancha is historically one of China's most famous qiaoxiao teas — teas made specifically for overseas Chinese enclaves. It was created in the mid-to-late Ming dynasty, earlier than the better-known Keemun black tea. In the late Qing and early Republican period, Ancha travelled down the Chang River system, through the port of Canton, and on to Hong Kong, Macao, and Southeast Asia.

What is worth noting is that Ancha went abroad not on the back of capital speculation, but on its sheer usefulness. Nanyang's hot, humid climate bred frequent epidemics, and local doctors used Ancha as a medicinal guide, curing many patients. Over time it became a staple health drink in overseas Chinese homes, revered as the "holy tea" or "immortal tea." A single bamboo-basket of compressed Ancha served both as a ritual drink for ancestor worship and weddings, and as the irreplaceable vessel of a migrant's homesickness.

## The undercurrent of qiaoxiao tea: cooling nature, dampness relief, and the "holy tea" belief

Ancha is cooling and dampness-relieving, long valued as a summer restorative. In immigrant societies where doctors were scarce, a "tea you could also use as medicine" naturally answered both the diaspora's hunger for health and its longing for home. Even today, many elderly Malaysian Chinese — and even younger generations — know Ancha better than young people on the mainland. This is a taste memory repeatedly reinforced by the experience of dispersion.

This is precisely the lens through which to read the qiaoxiao phenomenon: it was never merely an agricultural product, but a set of identity symbols wrapped inside a beverage. That it tastes good is the surface; the nostalgia memory is the substance.

## Rupture and rebirth: wartime halt, the 1992 revival of ancient craft

The good times did not last. During the War of Resistance, water routes were severed and Ancha production stalled. It was only in the 1980s, when a Hong Kong tea connoisseur wrote seeking the lost tea, that the descendants of the Sun Yishun teahouse and veteran tea workers successfully restored the entire ancient craft in 1992.

The most precious step is called "night dew" (yelu): around the White Dew solar term, on clear nights, makers spread the fragrance-lifted dry leaves evenly on open-air bamboo mats, letting the mountain dew and the day-night temperature swing mellow the fire and trigger natural post-fermentation. Sun-dried then night-dewed, sealed in indocalamus leaves and bamboo baskets, the tea improves with age — folklore holds that "three years makes medicine, five years makes treasure." As inheritor Xu Qian puts it, Ancha's value lies precisely in carrying forward the nostalgia memory of overseas Chinese.

## Re-walking the Nanyang route: taste-homecoming under the RCEP framework

A century on, Ancha is heading south again. Riding the opportunities opened by the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), Qimen County has in recent years promoted Ancha in Malaysia, Japan, Hong Kong, and Macao, and signed a sales framework agreement with a Malaysian association.

But let us be clear: Ancha's "re-walk of the Nanyang route" owes its tariff dividends only to the surface. The deeper logic is the emotional hard demand of diaspora communities. When a returning young tea maker documents plucking, basket-weaving, and night-dew on short video, when the night-dew festival packages tea-making, traditional-medicine consultation, and hot-tea tasting into a bazaar the young actually want to attend, this old tea finally reconnects with the spirit of the times.

## Beyond the tea: the nostalgia economy and a lightweight path of soft power

The numbers tell the story. Annual Ancha output has climbed from roughly 200 tons a few years ago to nearly 1,000 tons today; in 2026, Qimen County's Ancha output value reached about 200 million yuan (≈US$28 million). Zhang Bihua, director of the Tea Research Institute at the Anhui Academy of Agricultural Sciences, cautions that while preserving tradition the county must add research, push standardization, and leverage e-commerce and international exhibitions to turn "qiaoxiao tea" into an "international tea" consumers actually recognize.

This is the intriguing part. Compared with the top-down cultural export of Confucius Institutes, Ancha walks a lightweight, market-driven path of soft power: it preaches nothing, and instead lets a familiar flavour in a cup lead Nanyang's Chinese families to look back toward their ancestral land on their own. Sentiment can sell for a season, but a brand must endure — and standardization and youth appeal remain unavoidable exams.

## Further reading

- **Qiaoxiao Tea (Overseas-Sold Tea):** Historically, Chinese teas made specifically for overseas Chinese enclaves — Ancha and Liubao both qualify — serving as a dual carrier of diaspora trade and nostalgia memory.
- **Nanyang:** The old Chinese term for Southeast Asia, referring specifically to the Chinese migrant networks across the Nanyang islands and Indochina peninsula; a core concept for understanding overseas Chinese history.
- **Night-Dew Process (yelu):** Ancha's signature step, spreading leaves outdoors around White Dew to trigger post-fermentation via dew and temperature swings — the source of its "better with age" character.

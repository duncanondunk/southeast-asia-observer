# 火围新都：印尼"智慧森林城"撞上自己烧着的泥炭地

印尼正经历十一年来最猛的林火季，而火舌这一次咬向了一个象征意义极强的目标——正在加里曼丹岛兴建的新首都努山塔拉（Ibu Kota Nusantara, IKN）。据南华早报报道，截至9月初，努山塔拉规划用地上已有超过400公顷土地发现火点；总统普拉博沃已直接下令，调集灭火无人机，并动用印尼从意大利获赠、定于9月17日抵港的首艘航空母舰"朱塞佩·加里波第"号前往加里曼丹参与灭火。一座尚未竣工的首都，要靠航母来灭火，这画面本身就够荒诞了。

## 现场：火，烧到了未来首都的门口

这不是孤例，却格外刺眼。新首都本是印尼迁都雄心的地标，如今火却先一步"进驻"其外围。努山塔拉首都管理局（OIKN）称，火情仍局限在距主施工区约35公里的外围缓冲带，核心政务区未见热点。话虽如此，一座以"生态智慧"为卖点的未来之城，连周边缓冲带都护不住，招牌先糊了一半。更讽刺的是，这场灭火还动用了尚未正式服役的航母——国家最昂贵的资产被拉来补最基础的防火短板，足见地方消防与空中灭火能力的窘迫。

## 数字：十一年来最猛的林火季

印尼森林与泥炭地监测平台Sipongi的数据显示，今年1至6月全国已有近20.2万公顷森林和泥炭地被焚毁；8月1日至9月9日录得逾1.4万个热点，而去年同期仅1033个。火，正以指数级的速度蔓延。邻近努山塔拉的潘贾姆北帕瑟县，热点数从7月的6个飙到8月的136个；库台卡塔内加拉县更从86个蹿到1188个。数字不会说谎：气候的弦，已经绷到极限。把视野拉回全国，七省30家企业的特许经营区内失火被叫停，说明这并非努山塔拉一地的偶然，而是整片泥炭地貌的季节性宿命。

## 应对：无人机、航母与总统的命令

普拉博沃在9月7日的内阁救灾会议上放话："绝不让火烧到核心区。"这话听着硬气，却也透着急。OIKN环境与自然资源的副手米尔娜·阿斯纳瓦蒂·萨菲特里辩称，火情局限在距主施工区约35公里的外围，核心政务区自7月以来空气质量总体良好。但那片外围本就是19.9万公顷的"环境与粮食安全带"——火若烧穿它，所谓"智慧森林城"的叙事便先塌一角。

## 矛盾：建在泥炭地上的"智慧森林城"

问题不在火有多大，而在城建在哪儿。泥炭地修复监测组织潘陶甘布（Pantau Gambut）的倡导者瓦尤·佩尔达纳点破要害：努山塔拉选址于敏感的湿地与泥炭地，本就是"易燃地基"。泥炭地若遭破坏，雨季涝、旱季燃，这是水文常识。可印尼偏要把承载着政治迁都雄心的"智慧森林城"压在这块海绵上。规划者当初的乐观，如今被地下的余烬一一反驳。

## 根源：厄尔尼诺遇上点火的人

OIKN把锅甩给厄尔尼诺带来的极端干旱，这没说错，却只说了一半。米尔娜自己承认，"林地火灾的主因是烧荒造地"。努山塔拉管理局已对一名涉嫌非法烧荒者提起刑诉，并处罚一家引发火情的种植园企业、责令其复绿；边界上的三家矿企也被移交环境部。全国范围，七省30家特许经营区内失火的企业被临时叫停。可见天灾之下，人祸从未缺席。说到底，烧荒是低成本扩张的惯性，而迁都这样的国家级工程，竟没能从规划阶段就掐断这把火，本身就是治理的失分。

## 拷问：旗舰工程遭遇气候压力

水弹灭火看似壮观，对泥炭火却近乎徒劳——水浇一两遍，反而把地下阴燃的烟憋得更浓。努山塔拉总投资估算约466万亿印尼盾（按当前汇率约2030亿元人民币），迁都本是为给下沉的雅加达"解套"。可若新都自己先被火围、被烟呛，这场世纪工程的政治账与生态账，恐怕都得重算。迁都的政治逻辑是"均衡群岛、减轻爪哇负担"，可若新中心自己成了气候脆弱点，这笔账怎么算都不划算。一座号称"森林里的智慧城市"，首先得证明自己不会被森林烧掉。

## 延伸阅读

**努山塔拉（Nusantara）**：印尼规划中的新首都，位于加里曼丹岛东加里曼丹省，2019年由时任总统佐科提出，旨在纾解雅加达下沉与拥堵之困，目前仍处建设期。

**泥炭地（Peatland）**：富含有机质的湿地土壤，排水开垦后极易燃，燃烧时阴燃地下、释放大量PM2.5与跨境烟霾，是东南亚林火的核心隐患。

**潘陶甘布（Pantau Gambut）**：印尼泥炭地修复监测非政府组织，长期追踪泥炭地火灾与修复进展，对努山塔拉选址的生态风险多有警示。

# Wildfires at the Gates of Nusantara: Indonesia's 'Smart Forest City' Meets Its Own Burning Peatland

Indonesia is living through its worst wildfire season in 11 years, and this time the flames are licking at a symbolically loaded target — Nusantara, the new capital rising on the island of Borneo. According to the South China Morning Post, more than 400 hectares of land earmarked for Nusantara had recorded fires by early September. President Prabowo Subianto has personally ordered water-bombing drones deployed and even pressed into service the Giuseppe Garibaldi, the country's first aircraft carrier, donated by Italy and due to arrive on September 17, to join the firefight in Kalimantan. A capital not yet finished, fighting its fires with an aircraft carrier — the image alone tells you how serious this is.

## The scene: fire at the doorstep of a future capital

This is no isolated blaze, yet it is especially stark. The new capital was meant to be the landmark of Indonesia's relocation ambition; instead, fire has "moved in" to its perimeter first. The Nusantara Capital City Authority says the fires are confined to a buffer zone roughly 35 km from the main construction site, with no hotspots in the core governmental area. Be that as it may, a future city sold on its "eco-smart" brand cannot even protect its own buffer — and the brand is already half-charred.

## The numbers: the worst fire season in 11 years

Data from Sipongi, the forestry ministry's monitoring platform, show nearly 202,000 hectares of forest and peatland razed nationwide from January to June. Between August 1 and September 9 alone, more than 14,000 hotspots were recorded — against just 1,033 in the same window a year earlier. The fire is spreading exponentially. In Penajam North Paser, next to Nusantara, hotspots jumped from 6 in July to 136 in August; in Kutai Kartanegara, from 86 to 1,188. The numbers do not lie: the climate string has been pulled to its limit.

## The response: drones, a carrier, and a presidential order

At a cabinet disaster meeting on September 7, Prabowo drew a hard line: "Do not let the fires reach the core zone." The words sound firm; they also betray a flicker of panic. Myrna Asnawati Safitri, the authority's deputy for environment and natural resources, insists the fires are confined to a buffer about 35 km out, with air quality "generally good since July" in the core area. But that buffer is itself a 199,000-hectare "environmental and food-security belt." If the fire burns through it, the "smart forest city" narrative loses a corner first.

## The contradiction: a 'smart forest city' built on peatland

The problem is not how big the fire is, but where the city was planted. Wahyu Perdana, a campaigner at the peatland-restoration NGO Pantau Gambut, lays it bare: Nusantara sits on sensitive wetlands and peatland — a "flammable foundation" by any hydrological logic. Damaged peatland floods in the rainy season and ignites in the dry. Yet Indonesia chose to drop its politically charged "smart forest city" onto this very sponge. The planners' early optimism is now being rebutted, ember by ember, from underground.

## The root cause: El Niño meets the match

OIKN blames the extreme drought driven by El Niño — true, but only half the story. Myrna herself concedes that "the main cause of land and forest fires is land clearing by burning." The authority has launched criminal proceedings against one suspected illegal burner, sanctioned a plantation company and ordered it to restore the land, and reported three mining firms on the Nusantara boundary to the environment ministry. Nationwide, operations of 30 companies across seven provinces have been suspended after fires broke out in their concessions. Beneath the "natural disaster," human arson was never absent.

## The reckoning: a flagship project under climate stress

Water bombing looks impressive, yet on peatland fires it is close to futile — douse it once or twice and you simply trap the underground smoulder into thicker haze. Nusantara was estimated to cost around 466 trillion rupiah (about US$28.6 billion), conceived to relieve a sinking, gridlocked Jakarta. But if the new capital is itself ringed by fire and choked by smoke, the political and ecological arithmetic of this century project will have to be redone. A city that calls itself "smart forest" must first prove it will not be burned by the forest.

## Further reading

**Nusantara**: Indonesia's planned new capital on Borneo, unveiled by then-president Joko Widodo in 2019 to ease Jakarta's subsidence and congestion; still under construction.

**Peatland**: Organic wetland soil that turns highly flammable once drained and cleared, smouldering underground when burned and releasing vast PM2.5 and transboundary haze — the core driver of Southeast Asia's fire crises.

**Pantau Gambut**: An Indonesian peatland-restoration monitoring NGO that tracks peatland fires and rehabilitation and has long warned of the ecological risk in Nusantara's siting.

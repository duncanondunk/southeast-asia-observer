# 从"高薪文职"到乌克兰战壕：印尼人为何成了大国战争的炮灰

据《南华早报》报道，乌克兰情报机构于8月27日披露，8名印尼公民被以"待遇优厚的民用岗位"为饵诱骗，最终被送上俄乌战场、为俄方作战。雅加达方面已确认知情。分析人士警告，这8人恐怕只是冰山一角——东南亚既有的诈骗与人口贩运网络，正被俄方招募者当成现成的"狩猎场"。一条原本用于诱骗年轻人进园区敲键盘的流水线，如今把人直接送进了战壕。

## 现场：一份"高薪文职"把人送上了战场

乌克兰情报部门在通报中还原了套路：招募者许诺的是"高薪、安全、非战斗"的文职工作，等受害者跨过边境、交出护照，才发现自己被塞进了俄军的队列。他们几乎没受过像样训练，就被推向顿巴斯前线。更刺眼的是，此前已有个案浮出水面——一名化名为"约翰·帕特里克"的菲律宾人，被乌方指认在俄占顿涅茨克地区阵亡；印尼方面也确认，早有两名前军人（一名前海军陆战队员、一名前机动旅警员）私自脱队加入俄军。其中一人自称拿到一次性奖金200万卢布（约合17.5万元人民币）、月薪21万卢布（约合1.84万元人民币）。换句话说，"被骗"与"主动投奔"两股水流，正汇成同一片暗河。

## 数字：8人只是冰山一角

8，是乌方点名的数字，却远非全貌。乌克兰人权组织"真相猎犬"统计，截至今年1月，至少已有44名来自东南亚的招募者落入这条通道，覆盖印度尼西亚、越南、柬埔寨、菲律宾、马来西亚与缅甸六国。若把全球范围算上，俄方借以搜罗"外国炮灰"的剧本，已在数十个国家演练过。问题不在个别骗子，而在一条已经工业化的供应链。

## 骗局：从招聘广告到战壕只有一步

这套把戏并不新鲜，新鲜的是它的"出口"。过去几年，东南亚的诈骗园区以"高薪海外工作"诱骗本国及邻国青年，扣留证件、逼签债务、施以殴打，已是公开的秘密。如今同一套话术被平移到军事招募：先用"文职""翻译""后勤"的名目把人骗出国，护照一收、债条一签，人就成了俄军填线的耗材。受害者普遍被剥夺证件、身负招募者逼出的高额债务——所谓招募者，就是那些早就在园区里吃人血馒头的掮客。诈骗经济的"产能"，被战争直接征用。

## 软肋：劳务移民为何成了猎物

为什么是东南亚？答案藏在地区结构里。印尼有2.8亿人口，是全世界最大的穆斯林国家，也是劳务输出大国；越南、菲律宾、缅甸的青年同样面临就业与外汇的双重压力。当正规渠道狭窄、海外高薪的诱惑巨大，最容易上当的，恰恰是那些急于改变命运、却缺少信息与保护的普通人。大国战争需要的"廉价人力"，与东南亚青年谋生无着的困境，在同一道裂缝里碰头。

## 跨界：诈骗园区如何转做"征兵"

更值得警惕的是"跨界"。东南亚的诈骗与贩运网络，原本是区域安全的老大难，如今被外部大国当作现成的征兵中介。俄方看中的，正是这些网络触达底层、能伪造证件、敢跨境运作的能力。当战争机器发现，它不必自己建招募站，只要接手已有的犯罪基础设施就能低成本填线，东南亚的青年便从"被诈骗"升级为"被送上战场"。这是一种危险的耦合：区域治理的失灵，正被远方的冲突所利用。

## 困境：雅加达能做什么

对雅加达而言，这是一道难解的方程。印尼政府已确认知情，却缺乏有效抓手：与俄罗斯没有引渡条约，领事探视在前线几乎不可能，而受害者往往在被骗之初就自愿跨出了国门，法律上的"被害人"身份并不好认定。既有的海外劳工保护机制，本就对付不了正规雇主之外的灰色掮客；当掮客背后连着一场战争，常规外交手段更显苍白。

## 失语：东盟为何集体缺席

更刺眼的是集体的失语。东盟素来强调"以人民为中心"，口中的公民保护，遇到成员国公民在第三国被卷入大国战争时，却拿不出任何协调动作。六国皆有受害者，却没有一份区域层面的预警、没有统一的领事联动、没有对俄方的集体交涉。对比欧盟对公民海外安全的密集护盘，东盟的"不干涉"原则，在这里成了弱者被各个击破的漏洞。

## 棋局：大国战争如何伸进了东南亚

把镜头拉远，这8名印尼人不过是全球南方被卷入欧洲战事的一个切片。俄方招募外国兵，既是填线，也是宣传——用"多国志愿参战"的影像，为这场战争披上"得道多助"的外衣。而东南亚青年之所以成为目标，正是因为他们的脆弱被精确计算：经济困顿、信息落差、治理薄弱，每一条都降低了招募成本。当一场发生在万里之外的战争，开始从东南亚的村庄与城中村抽人，所谓"大国博弈"，就不再是抽象的地缘名词。

## 延伸阅读

**外国雇佣兵（Foreign Fighters / Mercenaries）**：指越过国界、为并非本国的交战方作战的人员；现代冲突中常以"志愿""合同工"名义招募，法律与保护地位模糊，极易成为被弃之不顾的消耗品。

**真相猎犬（Truth Hounds）**：乌克兰本土人权组织，长期记录武装冲突中的战争罪与平民受害，本案中统计了至少44名东南亚籍被招募者，是揭示"外国炮灰"规模的关键信源。

**劳务移民保护（Migrant Worker Protection）**：指输出国与输入国对跨境务工者的权益、证件、薪值与归国保障的制度安排；东南亚多国长期面临中介灰产与保护缺位的结构性难题。

# From "well-paid office jobs" to Ukrainian trenches: how Indonesians became cannon fodder for a great-power war

South China Morning Post reports that Ukraine's intelligence agency revealed on 27 August that eight Indonesian citizens were lured with promises of "well-paid civilian jobs" and ended up fighting for Russia in Ukraine. Jakarta has confirmed it is aware of the case. Analysts warn that these eight are likely the tip of an iceberg — and that Southeast Asia's entrenched scam and trafficking networks are being repurposed as a ready-made hunting ground for Russian recruiters. A pipeline built to trick young people into typing behind compound walls is now funneling them straight into the trenches.

## The scene: a "civilian job offer" that ended on the front line

Ukraine's intelligence disclose the mechanics: recruiters dangle "high-paying, safe, non-combat" clerical work; only after the victim crosses a border and surrenders their passport do they discover they have been slotted into the ranks of the Russian military. With almost no real training, they are pushed toward the Donbas front. The pattern is not isolated. Earlier cases have surfaced — a Filipino identified only as "John Patrick" was named by Ukrainian intelligence among those killed in occupied Donetsk; Indonesia itself has confirmed two former servicemen — an ex-Navy marine and an ex-mobile-brigade police officer — who deserted to join Russian forces. In one such case a former Aceh police officer told his unit he had joined the Wagner group, claiming an initial bonus of 2 million rubles (about US$25,000) and a monthly salary of 210,000 rubles (about US$2,600). In other words, the streams of "deceived" and "volunteered" are merging into the same dark river.

## The numbers: eight is only the beginning

Eight is the figure Ukrainian intelligence named — far from the whole picture. The Ukrainian human-rights group Truth Hounds has documented at least 44 recruits from Southeast Asia drawn into this channel as of January, spanning Indonesia, Vietnam, Cambodia, the Philippines, Malaysia and Myanmar. Expand the lens globally and the Kremlin's playbook for sourcing "foreign cannon fodder" has already been rehearsed in dozens of countries. The problem is not a few rogue fraudsters; it is an industrialised supply chain.

## The trap: from a job advert to a trench in one step

The trick itself is not new; what is new is its export destination. For years, Southeast Asia's scam compounds lured youth from home and neighbouring countries with "high-paid overseas jobs," confiscating documents, forcing debt and meting out beatings — an open secret. Now the same script is transplanted onto military recruitment: first bait them out with titles like "clerk," "translator," "logistics"; once the passport is taken and the debt note signed, the person becomes expendable filler for the Russian line. Victims are routinely stripped of documents and saddled with heavy debts to their recruiters — the very brokers who have long profited from the region's scam economy. The "capacity" of that economy has been conscripted directly by the war.

## The soft underbelly: why migrant workers became prey

Why Southeast Asia? The answer lies in regional structure. Indonesia, with its 280 million people, is the world's largest Muslim-majority state and a major labour exporter; the youth of Vietnam, the Philippines and Myanmar face the twin pressures of employment and remittances. When legal channels are narrow and the lure of overseas wages is vast, the easiest to deceive are precisely those ordinary people desperate to change their fate yet lacking information and protection. The "cheap manpower" a great-power war demands meets the precarity of Southeast Asian youth in the same crack.

## The crossover: how scam compounds turned into recruiting stations

What deserves real alarm is the "crossover." Southeast Asia's scam and trafficking networks — long a regional-security headache — are now being used by an external great power as off-the-shelf enlistment brokers. What Moscow wants is exactly the networks' ability to reach the grassroots, forge documents and operate across borders. When the war machine discovers it need not build its own recruitment posts but can simply take over existing criminal infrastructure to fill the line cheaply, the region's youth are upgraded from "scam victims" to "cannon fodder." This is a dangerous coupling: the failure of regional governance is being exploited by a distant conflict.

## Jakarta's dilemma: what can the capital actually do

For Jakarta, this is a hard equation. The government has confirmed it is aware, yet lacks effective levers: there is no extradition treaty with Russia, consular visits at the front are near impossible, and because victims often stepped across the border "voluntarily" at first, their legal status as "victims" is hard to establish. The existing overseas-labour protection machinery was never built to handle grey brokers outside formal employment; when those brokers are backed by a war, ordinary diplomacy looks even paler.

## The silence: why ASEAN is absent

More glaring still is the collective silence. ASEAN routinely stresses a "people-centred" approach and speaks of citizen protection, yet when nationals of member states are dragged into a great-power war in a third country, it produces no coordinated move. Six countries have victims, but there is no regional early-warning, no unified consular linkage, no collective demarche toward Moscow. Set against the European Union's dense protective apparatus for its citizens abroad, ASEAN's "non-interference" principle here becomes a loophole through which the weak are picked off one by one.

## The bigger board: how a great-power war reached into Southeast Asia

Pull the camera back and these eight Indonesians are but a slice of the Global South drawn into a European war. Russia's recruitment of foreign fighters serves both to fill lines and to propagandise — the imagery of "volunteers from many nations" drapes the war in a cloak of supposed global endorsement. That Southeast Asian youth are targeted is no accident: their vulnerability is precisely calculated — economic distress, information gaps, weak governance, each lowering the cost of recruitment. When a war fought ten thousand miles away begins pulling people from Southeast Asian villages and urban kampongs, "great-power competition" ceases to be an abstract geopolitical term.

## Further reading

**Foreign Fighters / Mercenaries**: persons who cross borders to fight for a party not their own; in modern conflicts often recruited under the labels "volunteer" or "contractor," with ambiguous legal and protective status, making them easily discardable expendables.

**Truth Hounds**: a Ukrainian human-rights organisation that documents war crimes and civilian harm in armed conflict; in this case it tallied at least 44 Southeast Asian recruits, becoming the key source exposing the scale of "foreign cannon fodder."

**Migrant Worker Protection**: the institutional arrangements by sending and receiving states for the rights, documents, wages and repatriation of cross-border workers; Southeast Asian states have long faced the structural problem of grey recruitment agencies and weak protection.

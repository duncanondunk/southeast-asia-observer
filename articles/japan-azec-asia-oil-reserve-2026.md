# 日本借「石油储备」重塑亚洲能源秩序

10月8日，由日本主导的「亚洲零排放共同体」（AZEC）部长级会议在菲律宾马尼拉收官。会议抛出一份扩大亚洲石油储备的工作计划，也顺手递出了东京重回亚洲舞台中央的一把钥匙。名义是脱碳与储备，底色却是战略博弈。

## 一场在马尼拉达成的「储备」共识

10月8日，由日本主导的「亚洲零排放共同体」（AZEC）部长级会议在菲律宾马尼拉落下帷幕。会议汇总出一份扩大亚洲石油储备的工作计划：面对中东局势动荡长期化的风险，各成员国将完善相关制度与基础设施，建立危机发生时协同释放石油储备的机制。沙特阿拉伯与阿联酋的副部长级官员也专程到场背书，承诺协助亚洲国家建设储备，并在紧急状态下优先供应。

表面看，这是一场关于「能源安全」的技术性磋商。但把镜头拉远，这条新闻的弦外之音，远比「储备」二字沉重——它勾勒出一幅日本以能源为支点、重新编织亚洲地缘版图的清晰轮廓。

## 「电力亚洲」：百亿美元能源外交的底牌

这场布局并非临时起意。今年4月，日本首相高市早苗抛出「电力亚洲」（Power Asia）能源合作构想，计划以总规模100亿美元（约720亿元人民币，折合约1.6万亿日元）的金融支援为核心，向亚洲国家输出储备技术与资金。马尼拉会议上，日本经济产业副大臣高木启直言：「各国对拥有丰富储备经验的日本寄予极高期待。」

更值得注意的是动作的落点。日本与菲律宾签署备忘录，协助后者引进核电；日本经济产业省还将与新加坡在海上风电领域展开合作。援助清单从储备罐一路延伸到核反应堆与风机叶片，能源合作就此成了东京重塑区域影响力最顺手的抓手。

## 数字：东南亚的储备有多薄

日本的底气，来自半个世纪的积累。自1970年代石油危机以来，日本持续建设石油储备，规模至今超过200天消费量，居全球前列。反观东南亚，家底要单薄得多：据日本资源能源厅统计，截至今年3月，泰国原油及油品储备仅相当于61天消费量，菲律宾为53天，越南更少，只有30天。

这串数字戳中了东南亚的命门。2026年2月美国对伊朗发动攻击，伊朗随即报复并试图封锁霍尔木兹海峡（Hormuz），石油供应中断的风险骤然升高。储备本就单薄的国家被迫限制加油、抑制消费，经济脆弱性暴露无遗。日本此时递上「储备经验」，恰是踩在了痛点上。

## 弦外之音：能源合作还是战略围堵

据日经亚洲报道，东京此番慷慨，藏着一层没有说透的盘算——日本希望通过向亚洲各国提供支持，以牵制中国在亚洲影响力的不断增强。一句话，把「能源援助」的面纱掀开了一角。

这并不意外。近年来中国在东南亚的能源与基础设施存在感一路走高，从电网到炼油再到可再生能源，合作项目遍地开花。日本以「储备经验」和「脱碳融资」为礼包，既填补了东南亚的真实短板，又在不点名的情况下划出一条战略防线。能源，俨然成了地缘博弈的新前线。

## 日本的软肋：被东南亚卡住的生命线

不过，这场棋局里日本并非只有筹码，也有软肋。据日经亚洲报道，日本医用橡胶手套和人工透析用管材超过99%依赖海外生产，主要进口自东南亚。中东危机一度引发医用耗材短缺，日本政府不得不紧急释放手套储备应急。换句话说，日本引以为傲的高端制造与公共卫生，同样被东南亚的产业链卡住了咽喉。

这恰恰说明，「牵制」与「被牵制」是一体两面。东京想用能源把东南亚拉进自己的轨道，但自身性命攸关的供应链，也攥在东南亚手里。相互依存不是口号，而是赤裸裸的现实。

## 沙特与阿联酋入局：产油国的算盘

值得玩味的是产油国的态度。沙特与阿联酋不仅派员出席，还主动承诺在紧急情况下优先向亚洲供应原油。对利雅得和阿布扎比而言，中东局势一旦失控，其原油出口本就会因霍尔木兹受阻而下滑；若能借「合作储备」机制把原油提前存进消费国的储罐，即便海运中断，也能通过储备罐继续外供。

这是一笔双赢的买卖：产油国锁定了需求与储罐，消费国拿到了应急保险。而日本居中斡旋的角色，恰好让这场「储油外交」得以顺理成章地落地。

## 脱碳外衣下的现实逻辑

AZEC名义上以实现脱碳为目标，但面对人工智能（AI）普及带来的电力需求激增，亚洲短期内根本无法摆脱对火电与化石燃料的依赖。会议重心从绿色转型（GX）悄悄滑向能源安保这一事实本身，就足以说明问题。日本主推的「中长期脱碳路径」，更像是一张用能源安全包装的现实主义地图。

## 余波：东南亚该信谁

站在东南亚的立场，这场「储备」棋局既是机遇也是考验。真实的能源短板需要外部助力，日本的技术与资金确有价值；但把鸡蛋放进单一援助方的篮子，无异于连同战略主动权一并让渡。当「脱碳」与「围堵」共用一套话术，东南亚要做的，是在拥抱合作的同时守住自己选边站队的权利。能源从来不只是能源——它是二十一世纪亚洲秩序最诚实的注脚。

## 延伸阅读

**亚洲零排放共同体（AZEC）**：日本于2022年发起的能源合作框架，涵盖东南亚等11个国家，名义上推动脱碳，实际兼具能源安全与地缘布局功能。

**霍尔木兹海峡（Strait of Hormuz）**：连接波斯湾与阿曼湾的石油运输咽喉，全球约五分之一的海运原油经此通过，一旦被封锁将直接冲击亚洲能源供给。

**电力亚洲（Power Asia）**：日本首相高市早苗2026年4月提出的能源援助构想，以约100亿美元金融支援为核心，意在强化日本对亚洲能源合作的主导权。

# Japan repackages energy security as Asia's new order

On 8 October, the ministerial meeting of the Asia Zero Emission Community (AZEC), a framework led by Japan, closed in Manila, the Philippines. It produced a work plan to expand Asia's oil reserves — and, with it, a key for Tokyo to return to the centre of the Asian stage. The label is decarbonisation and reserves; the undertone is strategic competition.

## A 'reserve' consensus struck in Manila

On 8 October, the ministerial meeting of the Asia Zero Emission Community (AZEC), a framework led by Japan, drew to a close in Manila, the Philippines. It wrapped up a work plan to expand Asia's oil reserves: facing the risk of a prolonged Middle East crisis, member states will build the institutions and infrastructure needed to release oil reserves in a coordinated way during emergencies. Deputy-minister-level officials from Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates made a point of attending to endorse the plan, pledging to help Asian countries build reserves and to prioritise supply in times of crisis.

On its face, this is a technical discussion about "energy security." But zoom out, and the subtext is far heavier than the word "reserve" suggests — it sketches a clear outline of Japan redrawing Asia's geopolitical map with energy as the lever.

## 'Power Asia': the trump card of a US$10bn energy diplomacy

This layout is no improvisation. In April this year, Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae unveiled the "Power Asia" energy cooperation concept, built around a financial support package totalling US$10 billion (around ¥1.6 trillion). At the Manila meeting, Japan's vice-minister of economy, trade and industry, Takagi Hiroshi, put it bluntly: "Countries have extremely high expectations of Japan, which has rich reserve experience."

What is more telling is where the effort lands. Japan signed a memorandum with the Philippines to help it introduce nuclear power; METI will also cooperate with Singapore on offshore wind. The aid menu runs from storage tanks to nuclear reactors and turbine blades, making energy cooperation the most convenient instrument for Tokyo to rebuild its regional clout.

## The numbers: how thin Southeast Asia's buffers are

Japan's confidence comes from half a century of accumulation. Since the oil crises of the 1970s, Japan has built oil reserves exceeding 200 days of consumption, among the highest in the world. Southeast Asia, by contrast, is far thinner: according to Japan's Agency for Natural Resources and Energy, as of March this year Thailand held reserves equal to just 61 days of consumption, the Philippines 53 days, and Vietnam a mere 30 days.

Those figures expose the region's vulnerability. After the United States struck Iran in February 2026 and Iran retaliated by attempting to block the Strait of Hormuz, the risk of supply disruption spiked. Countries with already thin reserves were forced to limit refuelling and suppress consumption, laying their economic fragility bare. Japan's offer of "reserve experience" lands precisely on that sore point.

## Reading between the lines: cooperation or containment

According to Nikkei Asia, Tokyo's generosity hides a calculation it did not quite say out loud — Japan hopes to contain China's growing influence in Asia by providing support to Asian countries. That one sentence lifts a corner of the "energy aid" veil.

It is hardly a surprise. In recent years China's footprint in Southeast Asia's energy and infrastructure has risen steadily, from power grids and refineries to renewable energy, with projects blooming across the region. Japan's gift box of "reserve experience" and "decarbonisation finance" both fills a real gap in Southeast Asia and, without naming names, draws a strategic line of defence. Energy has become the new front line of geopolitical competition.

## Japan's own soft spot: a lifeline held by Southeast Asia

Yet in this chess game Japan is not all chips and no weakness. According to Nikkei Asia, more than 99% of Japan's medical rubber gloves and dialysis tubing depend on overseas production, imported mainly from Southeast Asia. The Middle East crisis once triggered a shortage of medical consumables, forcing the Japanese government to release glove reserves in an emergency. In other words, Japan's prized high-end manufacturing and public health are likewise throttled by Southeast Asia's supply chains.

This shows precisely that "containment" and "being contained" are two sides of one coin. Tokyo wants to pull Southeast Asia into its orbit through energy, but the supply chains vital to its own survival are gripped by the region. Interdependence is not a slogan; it is a naked reality.

## Saudi and UAE join in: the producers' calculus

What is intriguing is the producers' stance. Saudi Arabia and the UAE did not merely send representatives; they proactively promised to prioritise crude supply to Asia in emergencies. For Riyadh and Abu Dhabi, a loss of control over the Middle East would itself cut their crude exports as Hormuz is disrupted; if they can park crude in consumers' tanks in advance through a "cooperative reserve" mechanism, they can keep supplying even when sea lanes break down.

It is a win–win trade: producers lock in demand and storage, consumers buy insurance. And Japan's role as broker makes this "oil-storage diplomacy" fall neatly into place.

## The real logic beneath the decarbonisation cloak

AZEC is nominally committed to decarbonisation, but with the surge in power demand driven by artificial intelligence (AI), Asia cannot shed its dependence on thermal power and fossil fuels in the short term. The very fact that the meeting's centre of gravity quietly slid from green transition (GX) to energy security says it all. The "medium- to long-term decarbonisation path" that Japan leads looks more like a realist map wrapped in energy-security packaging.

## Aftermath: whom should Southeast Asia trust

From Southeast Asia's vantage point, this "reserve" game is both opportunity and test. Real energy gaps need outside help, and Japan's technology and capital are genuinely valuable; but putting all eggs in one donor's basket amounts to handing over strategic initiative as well. When "decarbonisation" and "containment" share the same vocabulary, what Southeast Asia must do is embrace cooperation while guarding its right to choose sides. Energy was never only energy — it is the most honest footnote to Asia's order in the twenty-first century.

## Further reading

**Asia Zero Emission Community (AZEC):** A Japan-initiated energy cooperation framework launched in 2022 covering 11 countries including Southeast Asia; nominally for decarbonisation, it also serves energy-security and geopolitical purposes.

**Strait of Hormuz:** The chokepoint linking the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman, through which roughly one fifth of seaborne crude passes; any blockade would directly hit Asian energy supply.

**Power Asia:** The energy-aid concept unveiled by Prime Minister Takaichi in April 2026, centred on about US$10 billion in financial support, intended to strengthen Japan's lead in Asian energy cooperation.

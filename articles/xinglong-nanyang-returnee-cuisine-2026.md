# 味道的返程：兴隆四代归侨与一门被“非遗化”的南洋手艺

## 酱料比族谱更诚实

据中新网报道，海南万宁兴隆小镇的一条小巷里，“侨欣源”餐厅飘出咖喱、香茅、南姜与斑斓叶的气味。掌勺的蔡雪英与丈夫林春华守着祖辈从印尼带回的手艺，到他们这一代已是第四代。一盘叁巴酱炒空心菜，酱底用的是印尼发酵虾膏，配虾米、香茅、南姜等10多种香料慢火熬成；那股初闻有些冲的独特气息，是老归侨“刻在舌尖的记忆”。

族谱会写谁在哪一年跨海，却写不出一勺虾膏的分量。味道的价值恰恰在这里：它不是被反复讲述的祖先故事，而是每天被称、被炒、被端上桌的实践。蔡雪英坚持自己熬咖喱酱——黄姜、香茅、南姜、沙姜的配比是不外传的家庭秘方，尽管市面上直接买成品酱料既省事又省钱。她说，“做餐饮不能糊弄客人，再难也要坚持”。这句朴素的话，其实是在拒绝把一门活的手艺替换成标准化的工业口味。

## “回国”不是终点

需要把“归侨”这个词拆开看。林春华的太奶奶在20世纪50年代举家回国、扎根兴隆；他的爷爷早年在印尼经营餐馆，归国后把酱料与菜式一代代传下来。这不是今天人们熟悉的“海归”叙事——带着资本与学历回流、寻找更大的市场。那一代人回来时面对的是物资紧缺、肉食难得：儿时的林春华常听奶奶讲，归侨们就地采木薯叶、芭蕉芯，配上椰浆咖喱焖咸鱼头，便已是难得的美味。

一碗椰浆素咖喱，把这段艰苦岁月留在了味觉里。研究离散群体的人习惯把注意力放在“出去”：出洋谋生、汇款回家、落地生根。但兴隆提醒我们，回流同样是离散史的一部分，而且往往更不容易。带回来的不是行李，而是酱料配比、种植经验和一整套生活技术。香茅、斑兰叶、胡椒、咖啡，都是归侨从南洋引种、在兴隆扎下根的物产。所谓“侨乡”，从来不只是有人出国的地方，也是有人带着异乡的生活回来、并把它变成新家常的地方。

## 当记忆被命名为“非遗”

故事的另一半，是国家对这门手艺的认定。蔡雪英如今是万宁市级非物质文化遗产代表性项目“兴隆南洋风味制作技艺”的代表性传承人。拿到这个身份，意味着手艺从家族厨房进入公共名录，有了传承人、展示渠道和政策语言。

这是好事，但值得追问一句：认定保护的是手艺，还是手艺被冻结的那一个瞬间？非遗的逻辑偏向“原真性”，而饮食的生命力恰恰在于变化。叁巴酱用的是当年从印尼带回来的方子，但端上桌的空心菜、咖喱牛腩、斑兰豆腐，服务的是今天的食客。把一门仍在生长的手艺钉进展柜，很容易让它变成表演——游客拍照、师傅演示、味道停留在“据说当年”。蔡雪英似乎意识到这层风险。她计划建一座华侨历史体验馆，把餐厅、糕点工坊和研学空间合在一起，让访客不只是看，而是亲手熬咖喱、做娘惹糕点。体验能不能避免沦为布景，取决于讲述是否还包括垦荒、匮乏与适应，而不只是一张香料表。

## 自贸港把乡愁变成客流

据中新网报道，随着海南自贸港建设推进，兴隆侨乡文旅不断升温。这给了南洋味道一个新身份：它不再只是老归侨的自我安慰，也是一座小镇在旅游经济里可以被识别的标签。蔡雪英四处寻访收集侨乡老物件，用老照片、墙绘留住归侨往事；她还计划收徒，且“优先招收热爱侨文化的年轻人”。

把乡愁变成客流，本身不需要道德紧张。真正的问题是谁从中获益、谁被呈现。定居深圳的老归侨林煌兴每次到兴隆都来点一份咖喱鸡饭，离开时还不忘带上几罐店里自制酱料——他说，“还是当年的味道，吃着心里踏实”。这类回头客说明，需求并不只来自猎奇的游客，也来自需要确认自身记忆的离散家庭。侨乡文旅如果只把归侨当作布景，就会失去最懂这门手艺的观众；如果能把这批人当作叙事的共同作者，一碗咖喱才可能成为跨越几代人的对话场所。

## 第四代的考题

最见功力的细节，是蔡雪英给学徒定的第一课：不是下厨，而是带着徒弟走遍侨乡街巷，读懂味道背后的岁月。这句话点出了传承的真正难点。配方可以抄，香料可以买，难的是下一代人是否还知道木薯叶与咸鱼头为什么会出现在那锅咖喱里。

但家族内部的诚意，解决不了所有问题。手艺从家庭厨房走向体验馆和学徒制，必然要面对标准化、卫生规范、原料供应和讲述方式这些“不浪漫”的环节。一旦咖喱酱要按罐带走、要供几十桌人食用，家庭秘方的边界在哪里？既不愿糊弄客人，又不可能永远只靠家人守灶，这门手艺最终需要在“不变味”和“能复制”之间找到制度性的平衡。

兴隆的价值，正在于它把一段常被忽略的离散史摆上了餐桌。过去谈华侨华人，习惯谈他们如何走出去、如何把中国的味道带到南洋；兴隆讲的是相反方向的流动——南洋的味道回来了，在一个并非原乡的地方活了下来，四代人之后仍在被重新解释。看一座侨乡有没有生命力，别只看它修了多少展馆，先看还有没有人愿意为一勺酱料较真，还有没有年轻人肯先走一遍老街再拿起锅铲。

## 延伸阅读

**归侨（returned overseas Chinese）**：因求学、工作、政治归属或家庭原因从海外回到中国定居的人群；20世纪50年代印尼归侨的回流，是冷战与去殖民背景下的大规模人口移动。

**非物质文化遗产代表性项目（intangible cultural heritage designation）**：由政府认定并列入名录的传统技艺、表演与民俗，通常配套传承人制度与展示传播资源，其风险是把仍在变化的活态实践固定为“原真”样本。

**侨乡文旅（diaspora hometown tourism）**：以侨乡的移民记忆、建筑与饮食为资源发展旅游的模式；它的成败取决于当地人究竟是被展示的对象，还是叙事的作者。

# A Taste that Came Home: Four Generations of Indonesian Returnees in Xinglong

## A migration history in one jar of sauce

According to China News Service, in a narrow lane of Xinglong, a small town in Wanning on Hainan, the restaurant Qiaoxinyuan gives off the smell of curry, lemongrass, galangal and pandan. Cai Xueying and her husband Lin Chunhua cook the food their ancestors brought back from Indonesia; they are the fourth generation. A plate of stir-fried morning glory in sambal begins with a paste made from fermented Indonesian shrimp paste, dried shrimp, lemongrass, galangal and more than ten other spices, simmered slowly. That initial, pungent aroma is, as the report puts it, "a memory etched on the taste buds" of the older returnees.

A genealogy records who crossed the sea and when. It cannot record the exact measure of a spoonful of shrimp paste. That is where taste becomes valuable: it is not an ancestral story retold, but a practice weighed, stirred and served every day. Cai insists on making her own curry paste — the ratios of turmeric, lemongrass, galangal and lesser galangal are a family secret — even though buying ready-made sauce would be cheaper and easier. "In the restaurant business you cannot cut corners on your guests," she says. "However hard it is, we keep to it." In plain words, she is refusing to replace a living craft with a standardised industrial flavour.

## "Coming home" was never the end of the story

The term "returned overseas Chinese" deserves to be unpacked. Lin's great-grandmother moved back to China with her family in the 1950s and settled in Xinglong. His grandfather had run a restaurant in Indonesia and, after returning, passed the sauces and dishes down the generations. This is not the familiar narrative of today's "returnees" — people coming back with capital and degrees in search of a larger market. That generation returned to shortages, when meat was a rarity. As a child, Lin often heard his grandmother describe how the returnees gathered cassava leaves and banana heart, then stewed them in coconut curry with salted fish head: a rare treat in those years.

A bowl of vegetarian coconut curry holds that hardship in the sense of taste. Students of diaspora usually focus on the outgoing half of the story: leaving to earn a living, remitting money home, putting down roots elsewhere. Xinglong is a reminder that return is also part of diaspora history, and often the harder part. What came back was not luggage but paste ratios, farming knowledge and an entire repertoire of daily technique. Lemongrass, pandan, pepper and coffee were all introduced from Nanyang by returnees and took root in Xinglong. A "qiaoxiang", a hometown of emigrants, is never merely a place people left. It is also a place where people brought a foreign way of living home and turned it into the ordinary domestic routine.

## When memory is given an official name

The other half of the story is the state's recognition of the craft. Cai is now a designated representative inheritor of Xinglong Nanyang-style cuisine, an intangible cultural heritage item at the municipal level in Wanning. That status moves the skill out of the family kitchen and into a public register, complete with inheritors, display channels and the language of policy.

That is welcome. It is also worth asking what exactly the designation protects: the craft, or one frozen moment of it? Heritage logic favours authenticity, while the life of food lies in change. The sambal follows a recipe brought back from Indonesia, but the morning glory, beef brisket curry and pandan tofu served at the table are for diners today. Pin a still-growing craft behind glass and it easily becomes performance: tourists take photographs, a master demonstrates, and the taste remains stuck in "as they say it used to be". Cai seems aware of the risk. She plans an overseas Chinese history experience centre combining the restaurant, a pastry workshop and a study space, where visitors do not merely watch but cook curry and make nyonya kuih themselves. Whether that experience avoids becoming a stage set depends on whether the telling includes land clearing, scarcity and adaptation — not just a list of spices.

## A free trade port turns nostalgia into footfall

As Hainan's free trade port develops, the report notes, tourism in the Xinglong emigrant hometown has been picking up. That gives Nanyang food a new identity: no longer only the private consolation of elderly returnees, it is a label a town can be recognised by in a tourist economy. Cai has spent years collecting old objects from the emigrant community, preserving stories in photographs and wall paintings, and plans to take apprentices, giving priority to young people who love the culture of overseas Chinese.

There is nothing morally suspect about turning nostalgia into footfall. The real question is who benefits and who is put on display. Lin Huangxing, an elderly returnee settled in Shenzhen, orders curry chicken rice every time he visits Xinglong and leaves with several jars of the house sauce. "It still tastes the way it did," he says. "Eating it puts my mind at ease." Such repeat customers show that demand comes not only from curious tourists but from diaspora families needing to confirm their own memory. If diaspora-town tourism treats returnees as scenery, it loses the very audience that understands the craft best. If it treats them as co-authors of the story, a bowl of curry can become a place of conversation across generations.

## The fourth generation's real test

The sharpest detail in the report is the first lesson Cai gives her apprentices: not cooking, but walking through the lanes of the emigrant town to understand the years behind the taste. That is where transmission actually becomes difficult. A recipe can be copied and spices can be bought; what is hard is whether the next generation still knows why cassava leaves and salted fish head ended up in that pot of curry.

Goodwill within a family cannot settle everything. As the craft moves from the home kitchen to an experience centre and an apprenticeship, it must confront the unromantic business of standardisation, hygiene rules, ingredient supply and narration. Once curry sauce is sold by the jar and cooked for dozens of tables, where does the family secret end? Unwilling to cut corners yet unable to rely forever on relatives at the stove, the craft will eventually need an institutional balance between "tasting the same" and "being replicable".

Xinglong's value lies in putting an often-overlooked chapter of diaspora history on the table. We usually talk about overseas Chinese in terms of how they went out and carried Chinese flavours to Nanyang. Xinglong tells the story in the opposite direction: a Nanyang taste came back, took root in a place that was not an ancestral home, and is still being reinterpreted four generations later. To judge whether a qiaoxiang is alive, do not count its exhibition halls. See whether anyone is still particular about a spoonful of sauce, and whether any young person is willing to walk the old streets before picking up a wok.

## Further reading

**Returned overseas Chinese (归侨)**: people who settled back in China after living abroad, for reasons of study, work, political allegiance or family. The return of Indonesian Chinese in the 1950s was a large population movement shaped by decolonisation and the Cold War.

**Intangible cultural heritage designation (非物质文化遗产代表性项目)**: a government-recognised register of traditional crafts, performances and customs, usually accompanied by inheritor systems and display resources; its risk is freezing a still-changing living practice into an "authentic" specimen.

**Diaspora hometown tourism (侨乡文旅)**: a development model that turns a town's migration memories, architecture and food into tourist assets. Its success depends on whether locals are treated as exhibits or as authors of the narrative.

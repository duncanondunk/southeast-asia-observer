# 澳门妈祖文化旅游节将启：一座城的海洋信俗如何变成文旅名片

## 妈祖信俗：跨越海域的纽带

每年农历三月廿三前后，从湄洲岛到南洋各埠，妈祖诞的香火总会同时点燃。这位被尊为"海上保护神"的女神，是联结中国东南沿海与东南亚华侨华人的最强文化纽带之一。2026年，澳门宣布将于10月举办妈祖文化旅游节，把这一源自闽南的海洋信俗，正式包装为城市级文旅IP。对一座以博彩与葡式风情闻名的城市而言，亮出妈祖牌，既是回归文化根脉，也是在多元文旅叙事中抢占"华人精神家园"的定位。

## 澳门为何成为妈祖文化枢纽

澳门与妈祖的渊源，比赌城的历史更长。早在开埠之前，闽商与渔民便在半岛上建起妈阁庙，"Macau"一词即由"妈阁"的葡语转音而来。今天的澳门，既有列入国家级非遗的妈祖信俗，也有全球最密集的妈祖庙群之一。这种"地名即源于神"的罕见 case，使澳门天然具备讲述妈祖故事的话语权。当福建莆田的祖庙、台湾的妈祖绕境与澳门的庙会连成一条"海上朝圣廊道"，澳门便不再是线路终点，而成为枢纽节点。

## 旅游节如何"以节造城"

文旅部门对妈祖节的期待，远不止一场庙会。参照澳门此前的民俗节庆运营，旅游节通常融合巡游、论坛、非遗展演与跨境朝圣线路，目标是以节庆为磁极，拉长游客停留、带动酒店餐饮与文创消费。在访澳旅客结构转向"体验型"的背景下，单纯观光已难支撑增长，文化节庆成了差异化抓手。妈祖节的特殊性在于情感黏性高——对闽籍与东南亚华人而言，它自带"回娘家"的朝圣动机，转化率和复游率都优于普通景点。

## 从信俗到文旅的转化逻辑

把信仰变成旅游产品，尺度极难拿捏。过度商业化会灼伤信众情感，过度保守则难成产业。澳门的经验，是在"核心仪式不动"的前提下，向外延展体验层：绕境保持庄重，配套的学术论坛、青年工作坊、文创市集则承担传播与消费功能。这种"内核守正、外延创新"的打法，与泰国华人节庆的公共化路径异曲同工。关键在于让参与者既感到被尊重，又愿意为周边消费买单——文化尊严与商业可持续，本可同向而行。

## 华侨华人与妈祖的情感结构

对散居东南亚的华人而言，妈祖不只是神祇，更是"上岸"与"平安"的集体记忆。早期移民离港前必到妈祖庙祈福，抵岸后第一件事往往是建庙安奉，信俗因此成为移民社会的凝聚力内核。今天，东南亚多国的妈祖宫庙仍是宗乡组织的活动中心。澳门妈祖节若能把"侨"的元素做实——例如邀请东南亚宫庙参与绕境、设华侨论坛——便不只是本地节庆，而会成为跨国产学交流与情感确认的场域。

## 粤港澳大湾区下的文化协同

妈祖节的另一个坐标，是大湾区文化协同。粤港澳三地均有深厚的妈祖信仰与庙宇网络，香港佛堂门、深圳赤湾、珠海淇澳的妈祖遗迹，与澳门妈阁庙同属一条文化带。在大湾区"人文湾区"建设框架下，以妈祖为共同符号整合线路，既能避免各地节庆同质化内卷，又能形成区域文旅合力。对澳门来说，借大湾区流量反哺自身文化品牌，是一笔顺理成章的流量生意。

## 节庆的公共外交价值

节庆从来有外交维度。澳门作为中西文化交汇之城，妈祖节同时向两种受众说话：对内强化华人身份与文化自信，对外展示"一国两制"下澳门的文化活力。当东南亚政商与宗乡领袖因节庆聚首，民间层面的互信便多了一层润滑。对北京而言，一个能在国际舞台讲好自己的中华文化故事的澳门，本身就是"一国两制"成功叙事的注脚。

## 对传统节庆"活化"的启示

澳门妈祖节给传统节庆活化提供了一条可参照的路径：其一，深挖"唯一性"——从地名溯源到庙群密度，找到不可复制的叙事锚点；其二，守住核心仪式的庄重，以此换取外延创新的合法性；其三，把"侨"与"湾"两类网络同时接入，放大跨境乘数效应。当然，活化不是万能药，若配套交通、住宿与体验设计跟不上，节庆仍可能沦为"办完即散"的烟花。可持续，才是检验成功的最终标尺。

## 延伸阅读

**妈祖信俗**：以湄洲妈祖祖庙为源头、以"海上保护神"信仰为核心的传统民俗，2009年列入联合国教科文组织人类非物质文化遗产代表作名录，在闽粤沿海与东南亚华人社会广泛流传。

**Macau（澳门）地名由来**：主流说法认为"Macau"源于"妈阁"（妈祖庙）的葡萄牙语发音，反映早期闽商渔民在半岛的活动痕迹。

# Macau's Mazu Cultural Tourism Festival: How an Ocean Faith Became a City's Travel Card

## Mazu belief: a bond across seas

Around the 23rd day of the third lunar month each year, from Meizhou Island to the Nanyang ports, the incense of Mazu's birthday lights up simultaneously. Revered as the "protector goddess of the sea," she is among the strongest cultural ties linking China's southeast coast with overseas Chinese in Southeast Asia. In 2026 Macau announced a Mazu Cultural Tourism Festival for October, formally packaging this Fujian-rooted maritime folk belief into a city-level cultural-IP. For a city known for gaming and Portuguese flavour, playing the Mazu card is both a return to cultural roots and a bid to claim the position of "spiritual homeland of the Chinese" in a diversified tourism narrative.

## Why Macau became a Mazu hub

Macau's bond with Mazu predates its gambling city status. Before the port opened, Fujian merchants and fishers built the A-Ma Temple on the peninsula; the word "Macau" is widely held to derive from the Portuguese transliteration of "A-Ma" (Mazu). Today Macau holds both the state-level intangible heritage listing of Mazu belief and one of the world's densest clusters of Mazu temples. This rare case — where the place name itself originates from the goddess — gives Macau natural authority to tell the Mazu story. When the ancestral temple in Putian, Taiwan's Mazu processions and Macau's temple fairs form a "maritime pilgrimage corridor," Macau becomes not an endpoint but a hub node.

## How the festival "builds a city with a festival"

The cultural-tourism bureau expects far more than a temple fair. Following Macau's past folk-event playbook, the festival typically blends parades, forums, intangible-heritage shows and cross-border pilgrimage routes, aiming to use the event as a magnet that lengthens stays and drives hotel, dining and creative-consumption spend. As visitor structure shifts toward "experience-seekers," plain sightseeing no longer sustains growth, and cultural festivals become a differentiation lever. Mazu's edge is high emotional stickiness — for Fujian-origin and Southeast Asian Chinese it carries an innate "returning home" pilgrimage motive, with conversion and repeat-visit rates superior to ordinary attractions.

## The logic of turning faith into tourism

Turning belief into a travel product demands delicate calibration. Over-commercialisation burns devotees' feelings; over-conservatism fails to build an industry. Macau's experience is to keep the "core ritual intact" while extending the experience layer outward: the procession stays solemn, while paired academic forums, youth workshops and creative markets carry the communication and consumption functions. This "core upright, periphery innovative" approach mirrors the publicisation path of Thai-Chinese festivals. The key is letting participants feel respected yet willing to pay for surrounding consumption — cultural dignity and commercial sustainability can run the same direction.

## Overseas Chinese and the emotional structure of Mazu

For Chinese dispersed across Southeast Asia, Mazu is more than a deity — she is the collective memory of "landing safely" and "peace." Early migrants prayed at a Mazu temple before departure and built one to enshrine her as their first act upon arrival, making the belief the cohesion core of immigrant society. Today Mazu temples across Southeast Asia remain centres of clan and hometown associations. If Macau's festival makes the "qiao" (overseas Chinese) element real — inviting Southeast Asian temples to join the procession, holding an overseas-Chinese forum — it becomes not a local event but a venue for transnational exchange and emotional affirmation.

## Cultural synergy under the Greater Bay Area

Another coordinate for the Mazu festival is Greater Bay Area cultural synergy. Guangdong, Hong Kong and Macau all carry deep Mazu faith and temple networks; Hong Kong's Fat Tong Mun, Shenzhen's Chiwan and Zhuhai's Qi'ao Mazu relics sit on the same cultural belt as Macau's A-Ma Temple. Under the "Cultural Bay Area" framework, integrating routes around Mazu as a shared symbol avoids homogeneous infighting among local festivals and builds regional tourism synergy. For Macau, leveraging Greater Bay traffic to feed its own cultural brand is a logical flow business.

## The public-diplomacy value of a festival

Festivals always carry diplomatic dimensions. As a meeting point of Chinese and Western cultures, Macau's Mazu festival speaks to two audiences at once: internally reinforcing Chinese identity and cultural confidence, externally showing the cultural vitality of Macau under "one country, two systems." When Southeast Asian political, business and clan leaders gather for the festival, people-to-people trust gains a lubricant. For Beijing, a Macau that can tell its own Chinese-culture story on the international stage is itself a footnote to the success narrative of "one country, two systems."

## Lessons for "revitalising" traditional festivals

Macau's Mazu festival offers a reference path for revitalisation: first, dig out "uniqueness" — from toponym origin to temple density, find an irreplaceable narrative anchor; second, keep core rituals solemn to earn legitimacy for peripheral innovation; third, plug in both the "qiao" and the "bay" networks to amplify cross-border multipliers. Revitalisation is no panacea, though — without matching transport, accommodation and experience design, a festival may still scatter like fireworks after the show. Sustainability is the final yardstick of success.

## Further reading

**Mazu belief**: a traditional folklore centred on the "sea-protecting goddess," originating from the Meizhou Mazu ancestral temple; inscribed in 2009 on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, widespread along the Fujian-Guangdong coast and among Southeast Asian Chinese.

**Origin of "Macau"**: the prevailing view holds that "Macau" derives from the Portuguese transliteration of "A-Ma" (Mazu), reflecting traces of early Fujian merchants and fishers on the peninsula.

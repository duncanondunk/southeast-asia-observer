# 普拉博沃提名德斯特莉执掌印尼央行：稳住印尼盾，也稳住政治账本

8月10日，印尼总统普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）向议会提交央行行长提名人选，代行长德斯特莉（Destry Damayanti）成为唯一候选人。从前行长佩里（Perry Warjiyo）7月25日无预警请辞，到两周内火速敲定接班者，这一连串动作背后，是印尼政府在市场信心与政治账本之间的一次精巧平衡。

## 两周敲定，且只此一人

普拉博沃的提名有几个耐人寻味的细节。其一是速度：按法律，总统有三个月时间提名行长，他却只用了两周。其二是排他性：他本可提名多达三人，却只推德斯特莉一人。尤索夫伊萨东南亚研究院（ISEAS-Yusof Ishak Institute）客座研究员阿安（A'an Suryana）的解读一针见血——只提一人，意在"向议会施压，通过这项提名"。

这不是普通的人事更迭。佩里在风口浪尖上闪辞，本就为市场投下震撼弹：有报道指他在经济增长战略与流动性政策上，与财长普尔巴亚（Purbaya）发生激烈矛盾。提名公布后，印尼盾兑美元应声升值0.7%，触及一个多月来高点——市场用最直接的方式，表达了对"政策延续性"的欢迎。

## 印尼盾的"信任缺口"

真正的隐患，藏在汇率里。受财政纪律与资金外流担忧拖累，印尼股市今年已流失约三成市值，印尼盾更数度创下兑美元历史新低。佩里留下的，不只是一个职位空缺，更是一道"信任缺口"。

德斯特莉被视作务实、熟悉央行运作且对市场友好的技术官僚。她2019年出任印尼央行（Bank Indonesia）副行长，长期监管货币市场与支付系统，是代行行长后呼声最高的接棒者。帕拉玛地纳大学（Paramadina University）经济学者维查扬托（Wijayanto）认为，她会延续当前立场，但也会比前任更"迎合政府要求"。换句话说，市场买账的是她的专业度，而这份专业度能否在政治上保持独立，仍是问号。

## 独立性的老问题

央行独立性（central bank independence）并非印尼独有的难题。但在普拉博沃任内，这道老问题被推到台前：政府要求央行支持经济增长议程，议会也通过法案，赋权国会对央行进行绩效评估。维查扬托指出，这些举措意味着政府有了更多介入空间，外界对央行独立性的顾虑不会消散。

阿安说得更直白："德斯特莉是获普拉博沃提名，不排除她可能对普拉博沃心怀感激。如何保持独立，是她得做的功课。"印尼议会自2008年以来不曾否决在任总统提名的央行行长，而当前绝大多数议席由执政联盟掌控，德斯特莉的提名料将顺利通过。程序上没有悬念，悬念在程序之外。

## 稳住货币，也稳住叙事

把这件事放回区域视野，它折射的是新兴市场经济体共同的张力：当"保增长"的政治诉求撞上"稳货币"的专业纪律，央行往往被推到两者中间。印尼盾的每一次波动，既关乎资本流向，也关乎投资者对一个年轻民主政体制度质量的判断。

普拉博沃用一次干净利落的提名，暂时稳住了印尼盾，也稳住了"尊重市场"的叙事。但真正的考验在提名通过之后——当增长目标与货币纪律再次冲突，这位由总统亲手选定的行长，能否守住那道独立的底线。市场会一直盯着，因为信任一旦流失，远比0.7%的升值更难追回。

## 延伸阅读

**印尼央行（Bank Indonesia）**：印度尼西亚的中央银行，负责货币政策、汇率与支付系统监管；行长由总统提名、议会表决通过，任期五年。

**印尼盾（Indonesian rupiah, IDR）**：印尼法定货币；2026年兑美元数度承压并创历史新低，被视为观察印尼经济信心的关键风向标。

**央行独立性（Central bank independence）**：指央行免受短期政治干预、自主制定货币政策的原则；在发展中国家，它常与"保增长"的政治诉求形成明显张力。

# Prabowo names Destry Damayanti to lead Bank Indonesia: steadying the rupiah, and the political ledger

On 10 August, Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto submitted his nomination for central bank governor to parliament, with acting governor Destry Damayanti as the sole candidate. The sequence — from former governor Perry Warjiyo's abrupt resignation on 25 July to a successor locked in within two weeks — reflects a deft balancing act between market confidence and the political ledger.

## Two weeks, one name

Prabowo's nomination carries telling details. First, the speed: the law gives the president three months to name a governor, yet he took just two weeks. Second, the exclusivity: he could have nominated up to three candidates but put forward only Destry. A'an Suryana, a visiting senior fellow at the ISEAS-Yusof Ishak Institute, read it sharply — naming a single candidate is a way "to pressure parliament into approving the nomination."

This was no ordinary reshuffle. Perry's exit at the worst possible moment had already rattled markets: reports suggested he clashed sharply with Finance Minister Purbaya over growth strategy and liquidity policy. Upon the nomination, the rupiah rose 0.7% against the US dollar to a more than one-month high — the market's most direct vote of confidence in "continuity."

## The rupiah's trust gap

The real vulnerability lies in the exchange rate. Weighed down by concerns over fiscal discipline and capital outflows, Indonesian equities have shed roughly 30% of their market value this year, and the rupiah has repeatedly hit record lows against the dollar. What Perry left behind was not merely a vacant seat but a "trust gap."

Destry is seen as a pragmatic, market-friendly technocrat who knows the central bank inside out. She became deputy governor of Bank Indonesia in 2019 and long supervised money markets and payment systems, making her the front-runner once she took over as acting governor. Wijayanto of Paramadina University argues she will sustain the current stance but prove "more accommodating to the government" than her predecessor. In other words, the market buys her professionalism; whether that professionalism stays independent under political pressure remains the open question.

## The old question of independence

Central bank independence is hardly an Indonesian peculiarity. Yet under Prabowo it has been pushed to the foreground: the government has demanded that the bank support its growth agenda, and parliament passed a law empowering it to conduct performance reviews of the central bank. Wijayanto notes these moves give the government more room to intervene, and outside unease about independence will not fade.

A'an put it bluntly: "Destry was nominated by Prabowo; one cannot rule out that she may feel grateful to him. Keeping her independence is the homework she must do." Indonesia's parliament has not rejected a president's central-bank nominee since 2008, and the ruling coalition holds most seats, so her confirmation is expected. The procedure holds no suspense; the suspense lies beyond it.

## Steadying the currency, and the narrative

In a regional frame, this episode captures a tension common to emerging economies: when the political imperative to "grow" collides with the professional discipline to "stabilize," the central bank is thrust into the middle. Every swing of the rupiah speaks both to capital flows and to investors' judgment of a young democracy's institutional quality.

With one clean nomination, Prabowo has temporarily steadied the rupiah and the narrative of "respecting markets." The real test comes after confirmation — when growth targets and monetary discipline clash again, whether this president-chosen governor holds the independent line. The market will be watching, because trust, once lost, is far harder to recover than a 0.7% gain.

## Further reading

**Bank Indonesia**: the central bank of Indonesia, responsible for monetary policy, exchange rates and payment-system oversight; its governor is nominated by the president and confirmed by parliament for a five-year term.

**Indonesian rupiah (IDR)**: Indonesia's fiat currency; under repeated pressure against the US dollar in 2026, it hit record lows and became a bellwether of market confidence.

**Central bank independence**: the principle that a central bank sets monetary policy free from short-term political interference; in developing economies it often clashes with growth-first political agendas.

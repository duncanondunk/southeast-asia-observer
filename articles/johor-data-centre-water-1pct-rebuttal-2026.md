# 1.05%能堵住质疑吗：柔佛19座数据中心与一场关于水的算术争论

柔佛州政府拿出一个百分比，试图为算力热潮降温。据联合早报报道，11月20日，州公共工程、交通、基础设施及通讯行政议员莫哈末法兹里在州议会答复议员质询时宣布：全州19座营运中的数据中心，获批用水量合计每天2127.7万公升，仅占全州净水日产量20.34亿公升的1.05%，占工业用水的3.35%。他同时反驳外界把水坝水位下降归咎于数据中心的说法，敦促“不负责任者停止用捏造的消息吓唬民众”。

数字看起来干净利落。但一场关于水的公共争论，从来不是靠一个百分比收场的。真正的问题在于：这个1.05%到底回答了什么，又刻意绕开了什么。

## 一场州议会里的辟谣

据《星报》与《南洋商报》报道，提出质询的是三名国阵州议员——巴西古当区的巴哈鲁丁、哥打伊斯干达区的潘达克，以及武吉巴杜区的库马兰，问题直指数据中心发展对州内水电供应的冲击。

州政府的答复相当具体。这19座数据中心最初申请的供水量合计每天7308.8万公升，最终只批了2127.7万公升，削掉约七成。以柔佛每天20.34亿公升的净水产量计算，其中9.04亿公升供家庭、6.35亿公升供工业，数据中心的份额分别是全州的1.05%和工业用水的3.35%。莫哈末法兹里承认数字不大，但强调州政府仍会谨慎监管新增需求，确保不影响家庭供水。

这里值得点一句：官方一面把质疑定性为“扭曲事实”，一面又在议会里承认需要“谨慎监管”。这两句话放在一起，恰恰说明争议并非空穴来风——如果一切都在掌控之中，何来谨慎的必要？辟谣的语气越硬，账本本身就越要经得起追问。

## 分母里的文章：1.05%究竟在说什么

一个百分比要成立，分母的选择就是一切。柔佛的20.34亿公升是全省、全行业、全年平均的净水产量，把家庭、工业、商业用水统统装进同一个容器。而水压力从来不是均匀分布的：它是局部的、季节性的。当21座里的绝大多数机房集中在伊斯干达、士芒加滤水站与柔佛河沿岸的走廊地带，用全州总量去稀释单一流域的负担，在统计上合法，在政治上却难免取巧。

更关键的是，“获批”不等于“实耗”。联合早报的中文报道写的是“获批用水量”，英文口径却时有“消费量”的表述，两者不是一回事。分配额度是纸面许可，实际取水是运行曲线；数据中心可以在拿到批文后按年份逐步爬坡，也可以在旱季满负荷运转。把额度当成实际负担公布，等于用一个上限去论证“影响很小”。

再看申请与获批的落差。7308.8万公升的原始胃口，是最终获批量的3.4倍。州政府砍掉七成当然值得肯定，但这个数字同时暴露了行业自己的预期——如果没有那道审批闸门，算力扩张想要的水是多少。1.05%是一张快照，不是天花板。按同样的结构再落50座、100座机房，份额就会向3%、5%爬升。用今天的比例去回答明天的容量问题，本身就是错位。

## 水坝背锅：一次技术正确的澄清

关于水坝，官方的说法站得住脚。受重点监测的六座水坝——马什、勒班、蒲莱山第二、小笨珍、上拉央与下拉央——水位下降主因是长期干旱与厄尔尼诺（El Niño）现象，影响预计持续到2027年3月。其中马什和勒班根本不向任何营运中的数据中心供水；蒲莱山第二与小笨珍主要服务笨珍一带；上下拉央供应巴西古当、避兰东、班兰及福林园的家庭与工业用户。现有19座机房实际由士芒加滤水站和柔佛河滤水站供水，原水都取自柔佛河。

把水坝与机房分开记账，技术上准确。但技术正确不等于公共沟通成功。水坝与河流是同一个流域的两套账本，机房取水口在柔佛河上，不等于它与全州水安全无关。公众看到的是水位一天天下降、机房一座座竖起，官方回应却说“这两件事没有因果关系”——这种回答缺少的是可自行验证的中间环节。

官方批评舆论“扭曲事实”，可真正被扭曲的是激励结构：当单座机房的批准量、实际取水量、旱季曲线都不对外公开，民众只能拿水坝水位当作代用指标。谣言的养料，往往正是信息的空白。

## 九座“零耗水”：冷却技术的文字游戏

莫哈末法兹里列出的节水技术清单很长：风冷式冷冻机、直接晶片液冷、冷板液冷、T级风冷冷冻机，以及浸没式冷却（immersion cooling）。他称19座中有9座采用了这些“可零耗水运行”的系统，另有11座每日耗水低于100万公升，其余介于100万至417.2万公升之间。

先把算术摆出来：9座加11座，再加“其余”，已经超过19座。这说明三组分类的口径互相重叠，外界无法据此还原真实的用水分布。一处对不清的账，比没有账更消耗信任。

再说“零耗水”这个词。官方用的是“能够零耗水运行”，这是设备能力，不是运行结果。浸没式冷却把散热从蒸发转移到了液体循环，机房内的水表读数确实可能下降，但发电、制晶圆、制造服务器的上游水耗并没有消失，只是离开了柔佛的账本。对于一台人工智能（artificial intelligence）服务器，真正的命题是每单位算力的水足迹，而不是某一栋建筑门口的水表。把“零耗水”当成免责金牌，是营销语言冒充水文事实。

## 20个机构的委员会：审批权与知情权

柔佛并非没有治理工具。州政府在2024年4月29日成立柔佛州数据中心发展协调委员会，由莫哈末法兹里主持，成员涵盖20个州级与联邦机构，首次会议在同年6月20日召开。项目须先接受水电容量、绿色科技与用水能效的综合评估，评估方包括柔佛水资源管理机构、柔州水务公司、柔佛特别水务公司与国家电力公司；高耗水业者还须通过柔佛特别水务公司使用替代水源，包括经处理的污水。评估过关，才能向地方政府申请规划许可。

这套机制听起来严密。但它管的是“批不批”，不是“公开什么”。审批权完备，不等于知情权落实；一张许可证能证明项目合规，不能证明社区认可。

反过来看，州政府能在州议会一次性拿出申请量、批准量、冷却技术分类、供水来源与未来工程清单，说明这些数据一直掌握在手里。既然如此，常态化公布每座机房的年度用水台账就不是技术难题，而是意愿问题。社会许可（social licence to operate）不是批文，它要靠可核查的持续披露一点点攒出来。

## 柔新经济特区的另一本账

真正需要摊开的，是未来的账。州政府同时宣布四项原水工程，包括乌鲁素里里大水坝、大笨珍河拦河坝及柔佛河蓄水工程，总供水能力11.066亿公升，预计2027年至2031年间陆续完工；柔州水务公司另推六项滤水站新建与扩建，将增加10.48亿公升净水产能。官方说，柔佛—新加坡经济特区（Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone）将继续按规划推进，让高价值投资与基础设施容量、民众福祉同步。

这正是争议的核心。当公共资本为算力扩张去修水坝、建滤水站，1.05%就不再是一句免责声明，而可能是公共补贴的起点。新增的水，优先供给谁？水价如何反映稀缺程度？旱季来临，居民与机房谁先被限？这三个问题，比1.05%重要得多。

新加坡自身的水资源约束，把越来越多的算力需求推向一河之隔的柔佛。柔佛要算的因此不只是水厂容量，还有财政代价与社会承受力。经济特区的“民众福祉”四个字，最终要落在水价单和用水优先级上，而不是落在新闻稿里。

## 透明才是硬约束

州政府赢了算术，未必赢了争论。反谣言最有效的办法，从来不是敦促别人“停止造谣”，而是把可核查的事实持续摆出来：逐座机房的批准量与实耗量、旱季取水曲线、替代水源占比、新增项目的容量上限，以及违反用水条款的处置记录。

算力可以落地，账本不能落进黑箱。柔佛若能成为第一个把数据中心水账公开到机房级别的州，它拿出的就不只是1.05%这个数字，而是一套让邻国和投资者都能读懂的规则。相反，如果每次解释都只有一个百分比，那么下一座机房竖起来时，质疑只会更响。

## 延伸阅读

**水资源压力（Water stress）**：指用水量接近或超过可用水资源承载力的状态，衡量的是局部流域与特定季节的供需关系，不能用一个全区域年平均比例替代。

**社会许可（Social licence to operate）**：项目除法定审批外，还需获得社区与公众的持续认可；它不写进许可证，却决定项目能否长期运转。

**冷却技术（Cooling technology）**：数据中心散热方式，从风冷、冷板液冷到浸没式冷却，差异在于水在机房内的消耗量；评估其环境成本须把发电与硬件制造的上游水耗一并计入。

# Can 1.05% Settle It? Johor's 19 Data Centres and the Arithmetic of Water

Johor's state government reached for a percentage to cool a heated debate. According to Lianhe Zaobao, on 20 November the state executive councillor for public works, transport, infrastructure and communications, Mohamad Fazli Mohamad Salleh, told the state assembly that the 19 data centres currently operating in Johor had been approved for a combined 21.277 million litres of water a day — just 1.05 per cent of the state's treated water output of 2,034 million litres, and 3.35 per cent of industrial use. He also rejected claims that falling dam levels were the fault of data centres, urging "irresponsible parties" to stop frightening people with fabricated news.

The number is tidy. But a public argument about water is never settled by a single percentage. The question is what that 1.05 per cent actually answers — and what it quietly leaves out.

## A rebuttal in the state assembly

The Star and Nanyang Siang Pau reported that the questions came from three Barisan Nasional assemblymen — Baharudin Mohamed Taib of Permas, Datuk Pandak Ahmad of Kota Iskandar, and R. Kumaran of Bukit Batu — who asked directly about the impact of data-centre growth on water and electricity supply.

The answer was detailed. The 19 centres had initially applied for a combined 73.088 million litres per day (MLD), but were approved for only 21.277 MLD — roughly 70 per cent cut. Of Johor's average 2,034 MLD of treated water, 904 MLD goes to domestic users and 635 MLD to industry; the data centres' allocation therefore amounts to 1.05 per cent of the total and 3.35 per cent of industrial consumption. Mohamad Fazli conceded the share was small, but stressed that the state would keep a close watch on new demand so that household supply would not be affected.

Worth noting: the official response both dismissed the criticism as a distortion of facts and promised "cautious regulation" of future demand. Put those two sentences together and the controversy stops looking baseless — if everything were under control, why the caution? The firmer the rebuttal, the sturdier the underlying ledger needs to be.

## What the denominator actually says

A percentage only means as much as its denominator. Johor's 2,034 MLD is a statewide, all-sector, annual-average figure that folds domestic, industrial and commercial use into one container. Water stress, however, is never evenly spread: it is local and seasonal. When most of the server halls cluster along the Iskandar, Semangar and Johor River corridor, using a statewide total to dilute the load on a single river basin is statistically legitimate but politically convenient.

More important, "approved" is not "consumed." The Chinese-language report speaks of an approved allocation, while English versions sometimes describe the same figure as consumption. They are not the same thing. An allocation is a paper permission; actual abstraction follows an operating curve. A data centre can ramp up gradually after receiving approval, or run at full load in a dry season. Publishing an entitlement as if it were a realized burden amounts to using a ceiling to argue that the impact is small.

Then there is the gap between the request and the approval. The original appetite of 73.088 MLD was 3.4 times what was granted. Cutting around 70 per cent deserves credit, but the figure also reveals what the industry expected before the gate existed. The 1.05 per cent is a snapshot, not a ceiling. Add 50 or 100 more halls on the same pattern and the share climbs towards 3 or 5 per cent. Using today's ratio to answer tomorrow's capacity question is a category error.

## A technically correct clarification about the dams

On the dams, the official account holds up. The six monitored reservoirs — Machap, Lebam, Gunung Pulai 2, Pontian Kechil, Upper Layang and Lower Layang — have been falling because of prolonged drought and El Niño, with the effect expected to last until March 2027. Machap and Lebam supply no operating data centre at all; Gunung Pulai 2 and Pontian Kechil mainly serve Pontian; Upper and Lower Layang supply households and industry in Pasir Gudang, Plentong, Pandan and Taman Daya. The 19 existing centres are supplied by the Semangar and Sungai Johor treatment plants, both drawing raw water from the Johor River.

Separating dams from server halls in the books is technically accurate. Technical accuracy is not the same as successful public communication. Dams and rivers are two ledgers of the same basin: the fact that a centre's intake sits on the Johor River does not make it irrelevant to statewide water security. What the public sees is water levels falling week by week while more halls go up, and an official answer saying the two have no causal link. What is missing is an intermediate, independently checkable chain of evidence.

Officials criticised the media for distorting facts. Yet the thing truly distorted is the incentive structure: when no facility-level approval, abstraction rate or dry-season curve is published, people have no choice but to use dam levels as a proxy. Rumours feed on exactly this kind of information vacuum.

## From 1.05 per cent to zero-water: the cooling-tech word game

The state listed an impressive menu of efficient cooling systems: air-cooled chillers, direct-to-chip liquid cooling, cold-plate liquid cooling, T-class air-cooled chillers and immersion cooling. Nine of the 19 centres, Mohamad Fazli said, use systems "capable of operating with zero water consumption"; another 11 use less than 1 MLD; the rest consume between 1 and 4.172 MLD.

First, the arithmetic: nine plus eleven plus "the rest" already exceeds nineteen. The three categories overlap, so outsiders cannot reconstruct the actual distribution of water use. A ledger that does not add up consumes more trust than no ledger at all.

Second, the phrase itself. Officials said "capable of operating with zero water" — a description of equipment capability, not of measured results. Immersion cooling moves heat rejection from evaporation into a liquid loop; the meter at the building may indeed fall, but the water consumed upstream to generate electricity, fabricate wafers and build servers has not disappeared. It has simply left Johor's books. For an artificial intelligence server, the meaningful measure is the water footprint per unit of compute, not the meter outside one building. Treating "zero water" as a waiver is marketing language passing itself off as hydrology.

## Twenty agencies, one committee: approval without disclosure

Johor is not short of governance tools. On 29 April 2024 the state created the Johor State Data Centre Development Coordination Committee, chaired by Mohamad Fazli, bringing together 20 state and federal agencies; its first meeting was on 20 June 2024. Proposed centres must pass an integrated assessment of water and power capacity, green technology and water and energy efficiency, conducted with the Johor Water Regulatory Body, Ranhill SAJ, Johor Special Water and Tenaga Nasional. High-intensity operators must also use alternative sources through Johor Special Water, including treated sewage. Only those that pass may apply to local authorities for planning permission.

The machinery sounds rigorous. But it governs whether a project is approved, not what gets disclosed. A complete approval process does not amount to an informed public; a permit proves compliance, not community acceptance.

Look at it from the other side. Because the state could produce application volumes, approvals, cooling classifications, supply sources and future projects in one assembly sitting, the data clearly exist. Publishing an annual water ledger for every facility would then be a question of will, not capability. A social licence to operate is not a permit — it is accumulated through continuous, verifiable disclosure.

## The other ledger: Johor–Singapore SEZ

The account that most needs opening is the future one. The state simultaneously announced four raw-water projects, including the Ulu Sayong dam, the Pontian Besar barrage and the Johor River storage scheme, with a combined capacity of 1.1066 billion litres, to be completed between 2027 and 2031. Ranhill SAJ is also pursuing six treatment-plant projects that will add 1.048 billion litres of capacity. Officials say development under the Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone will proceed in a planned and integrated way, so that high-value investment advances alongside infrastructure capacity and the well-being of Johor's people.

That is where the real dispute lies. When public capital builds dams and treatment plants to accommodate compute expansion, 1.05 per cent stops being a disclaimer and may become the starting point of a public subsidy. Who gets the new water first? How does the tariff reflect scarcity? When a dry season arrives, households or server halls — who is rationed first? Those three questions matter far more than the percentage.

Singapore's own water constraints are pushing more computing demand across the causeway into Johor. Johor therefore has to count more than treatment capacity; it must count the fiscal cost and the social carrying load. The phrase "well-being of the people" in the special economic zone will ultimately be judged on water bills and allocation priorities, not on press releases.

## Transparency is the only hard constraint

The state won the arithmetic. It has not necessarily won the argument. The best answer to a rumour has never been an exhortation to stop spreading it, but the steady publication of checkable facts: approved and actual abstraction for each facility, dry-season withdrawal curves, the share of alternative water, caps on new capacity, and what happens when a water condition is breached.

Compute can be allowed to land. The ledger must not disappear into a black box. If Johor becomes the first state to publish data-centre water accounts down to the level of each hall, it will have offered more than the 1.05 per cent figure — it will have offered a set of rules that neighbours and investors can actually read. If every explanation remains a single percentage, then the next time a hall goes up, the questions will only be louder.

## Further reading

**Water stress**: A condition in which demand approaches or exceeds available supply, measured at the level of a particular basin and season; a single regional annual average cannot substitute for it.

**Social licence to operate**: The ongoing acceptance of a project by communities and the public beyond its legal permits. It is not written into any licence, yet it determines whether a project can keep running.

**Cooling technology**: The means by which a data centre sheds heat, ranging from air cooling and cold plates to immersion cooling. They differ in on-site water use, but a proper environmental assessment must also count the water consumed upstream in power generation and hardware manufacturing.

# "广州茶居"走向37个国家和地区：侨务网络如何连接故土与住在国

广州素以"千年商都"与重点侨乡著称。最新数据显示，广州市侨联共建的"广州茶居"已扩容至51家，遍布全球37个国家和地区；而广州222万名海外侨胞，分散在130多个国家和地区。2024年起，广州市侨联在海外侨胞集中的城市与涉侨机构共建"广州茶居"，本意是为远行人留一处"乡愁驿站"。但数年之间，这方以饮茶为名的空间，逐渐长成了连接故土与住在国的多层网络——侨牵友谊、侨牵商贸、侨牵科技、侨牵人才、侨牵公益，五条线同时延展。

## 现象：一杯茶连接起的全球侨网

9月1日，第四批"广州茶居"题匾颁发仪式在广州市侨联海外青年侨领研修活动启动式上举行，菲律宾马尼拉、老挝万象、爱尔兰都柏林、牙买加金斯顿等地的新一批海外侨团接过了匾额。从岭南的茶桌到五大洲的会客厅，茶居把"饮茶"这一最日常的广府习俗，变成了海外乡亲认祖、叙旧、议事的共同场域。老挝中华总商会副会长姚迩晓接过题匾时说，老挝与中国广东同样有浓厚的茶文化，她会把匾额带回老挝，悬挂在茶居馆内，打造老中侨亲对话的空间。

这背后是一种朴素的逻辑：在异国他乡，同饮一壶茶的人，往往最容易信任彼此。茶居因此超越了餐饮功能，成为侨团凝聚、信息互通、资源对接的"软枢纽"。

## 数字：从一座城市到37个国度的布局

"广州茶居"的扩张速度值得玩味。2024年起步时，它只是广州侨联面向重点侨乡城市的试探性布点；到2026年9月，数量已达51家、覆盖37个国家和地区。这一密度，意味着广州的侨务网络已不再依赖少数传统枢纽（如东南亚的老牌会馆），而是向欧洲、美洲、加勒比乃至大洋洲的次级节点渗透。

数据显示，广州222万海外侨胞遍布130多个国家和地区，构成了这张网最底层的"节点"。茶居的意义，不在于覆盖所有人，而在于为分散的节点提供可触达的"锚点"——无论乡亲身在马尼拉还是都柏林，都能找到一个物理空间，与广州保持稳定的情感与事务联系。

## 功能：茶居不只是乡愁驿站

广州侨联对茶居的定位，早已超出"喝茶叙旧"。它被设计为五个功能的复合体：侨牵友谊，巩固乡亲与故土的情感纽带；侨牵商贸，为中小企业对接大湾区市场牵线；侨牵科技，引入科创资源与人才项目；侨牵人才，发掘并服务海外青年专业人士；侨牵公益，组织慈善与志愿服务。

这种"一站多能"的设计，回应了当代侨务的现实需求。早期侨务多聚焦于寻根、联谊等情感层面；而当新一代移民在住在国成长为专业人士、企业家，他们更需要的是事业上的接口。茶居把情感资本转化为社会资本与经济资本，正是其生命力所在。

## 青年：研修营里的第三代侨领

与茶居扩张同步推进的，是海外青年侨领的梯队建设。本次研修活动吸引了来自美国、加拿大、英国、法国、澳大利亚、韩国、马来西亚、新加坡、泰国等35个国家和地区的43位青年侨领，展开为期5天的研修。他们中近九成来自青年侨团与海外商会，意味着广州正有意识地把交往对象从"老侨领"切换到"新世代"。

印尼广东省总商会创会会长孙继飞在印尼投资创业20年，作为学员代表发言时表示，将以此次研修为起点，当好中印尼经贸合作的推动者、湾区故事的讲述者。澳门归侨总会副理事长刘兆爵则分享了随澳门招商投资促进局代表团走访新加坡、印尼、马来西亚的商务经历，邀请青年侨领了解粤港澳大湾区。这种代际交接，决定了未来十年侨务网络的活跃度。

## 商贸：侨牵湾区与住在国的双向通道

茶居最具想象力的功能，是成为大湾区与住在国之间的双向商贸通道。对住在国而言，茶居是当地企业接触中国市场的"前哨"；对大湾区而言，茶居是本土企业出海的"支点"。姚迩晓就表示，将依托商会与茶居阵地，打通老挝与中国广东的资源对接渠道，并创作展现大湾区的文旅演艺作品。

在逆全球化与供应链重构的背景下，这种"以侨为桥"的民间通道显示出独特韧性。它不依赖政府间协议，而是建立在宗族、乡谊与长期信任之上，能够在正式渠道不畅时提供替代性的信息与交易网络。这正是侨务经济价值被反复重提的原因。

## 机制：侨联如何织起这张网

"广州茶居"并非孤立的挂牌行为，而是一套有意识的机制设计。广州市侨联作为枢纽，负责筛选海外合作伙伴、统一品牌与标识、组织研修与互访；海外侨团则提供场地、社群与本地化运营。匾额、研修营、商务代表团三者环环相扣，形成"挂牌—培训—对接"的闭环。

值得注意的是，这一模式与广东其他侨乡的打法一脉相承。从潮汕的侨批文化到江门的银信遗产，岭南历来擅长把离散网络转化为发展资源。茶居可视为这一传统的当代升级：用最轻量的文化符号（一杯茶），撬动最厚重的跨国联结。

## 冷观察：网络热的限度与方向

当然，茶居网络也有其边界。其一，它高度依赖个别热心侨领的投入，若缺乏制度化激励，热度可能随个人精力起伏。其二，覆盖37个国家和地区不等于深度渗透，许多节点仍是"点"而非"网"，彼此之间尚未形成强连接。其三，在部分对华人社团敏感的国家，茶居的政治意涵需被谨慎管理，避免被误读为外部势力的延伸。

更根本的问题是：如何让这张网从"联谊型"走向"生产型"？答案或许在于把茶居与粤港澳大湾区的产业、科创、教育体系更深地绑定，使每一次饮茶都可能是一次合作的开端。唯有如此，茶居才不只是乡愁的容器，更是发展的引擎。

# "Guangzhou Tea House" Reaches 37 Countries and Regions: How Diaspora Networks Bind Hometown and Host Societies

Guangzhou is known both as a "millennium commercial city" and as a major qiaoxiang, or ancestral homeland of emigrants. The latest figures show that the "Guangzhou Tea House" platform, jointly built by the city's federation of returned overseas Chinese, has expanded to 51 outlets across 37 countries and regions, while Guangzhou's 2.22 million overseas compatriots are scattered across more than 130 countries and territories. Since 2024, the federation has co-established these tea houses with overseas Chinese institutions in cities with dense diaspora populations. The original intent was simply to leave a "home-sickness station" for those far from home. Within a few years, however, this space named after tea-drinking has grown into a multi-layered network linking the homeland and host societies — extending simultaneously along five lines: friendship, commerce, science and technology, talent and public welfare.

## The phenomenon: a cup of tea connecting a global diaspora web

On September 1, the fourth batch of "Guangzhou Tea House" plaques was awarded at the launch of the federation's overseas young leaders programme, with new overseas associations from Manila, Vientiane, Dublin and Kingston receiving their plaques. From Lingnan tea tables to living rooms across five continents, the tea house has turned the most everyday Cantonese custom — drinking tea — into a shared arena where overseas compatriots recognise their roots, swap news and deliberate on common affairs. Yao Erxiao, vice-chair of the Lao Chinese General Chamber of Commerce, said upon receiving the plaque that Laos and China's Guangdong share a deep tea culture, and she would hang it in the tea-house hall in Laos to build a space for dialogue between Lao and Chinese kin.

Behind this lies a simple logic: abroad, those who share a pot of tea are usually the easiest to trust. The tea house thus transcends its dining function and becomes a "soft hub" for diaspora cohesion, information exchange and resource matching.

## The numbers: from one city to 37 nations

The speed of the expansion is telling. When it started in 2024, the platform was a tentative pilot in key qiaoxiang cities; by September 2026 it had reached 51 outlets covering 37 countries and regions. This density means Guangzhou's diaspora network no longer relies solely on a few traditional hubs — such as the old-established associations of Southeast Asia — but penetrates secondary nodes in Europe, the Americas, the Caribbean and Oceania.

The data show that Guangzhou's 2.22 million overseas compatriots, spread across more than 130 countries, form the underlying "nodes" of this web. The value of the tea house is not to cover everyone, but to give dispersed nodes a reachable "anchor" — whether in Manila or Dublin, compatriots can find a physical space to maintain stable emotional and practical ties with Guangzhou.

## The function: more than a home-sickness station

The federation's positioning of the tea house has long exceeded "tea and reminiscence." It is designed as a five-in-one platform: friendship consolidates the emotional bond with the homeland; commerce links small and medium enterprises to the Greater Bay Area market; technology brings in innovation resources and talent projects; talent discovers and serves young overseas professionals; and public welfare organises charity and volunteering.

This "one-stop, multi-function" design responds to contemporary diaspora needs. Early diaspora work focused on emotional dimensions such as root-seeking and fellowship; as a new generation of migrants matures into professionals and entrepreneurs in host societies, what they need more is a professional interface. Converting emotional capital into social and economic capital is precisely where the tea house draws its vitality.

## The youth: a third-generation leadership in the training camp

Running in parallel with the expansion is the succession of young overseas leaders. The current programme drew 43 young leaders from 35 countries and regions — the United States, Canada, Britain, France, Australia, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and others — for a five-day study tour. Nearly 90 percent come from youth associations and overseas chambers, signalling that Guangzhou is deliberately shifting its interlocutors from "old leaders" to "the new generation."

Sun Jifei, founding chair of the Indonesia Guangdong Chamber of Commerce who has invested in Indonesia for 20 years, said as a trainee representative that he would act as a promoter of China–Indonesia economic cooperation and a narrator of the Bay Area story. Lau Siu-keung of the Macao Returned Overseas Chinese Association shared his business delegation's visits to Singapore, Indonesia and Malaysia, inviting young leaders to understand the Greater Bay Area. This generational handover will determine the vibrancy of the diaspora network for the next decade.

## Commerce: a two-way corridor between the Bay Area and host societies

The most imaginative function of the tea house is as a two-way commercial corridor between the Greater Bay Area and host societies. For the host country, it is an "outpost" for local firms to reach the Chinese market; for the Bay Area, it is a "fulcrum" for local firms going global. Yao Erxiao said she would use the chamber and tea-house base to connect resources between Laos and Guangdong, and create cultural-tourism performances showcasing the Bay Area.

Amid deglobalisation and supply-chain restructuring, this "using the diaspora as a bridge" civil channel shows unique resilience. It does not depend on inter-governmental agreements but on kinship, native-place ties and long-term trust, providing an alternative information and transaction network when formal channels stall. That is exactly why the economic value of diaspora affairs is repeatedly revisited.

## The mechanism: how the federation weaves the web

The "Guangzhou Tea House" is not an isolated plaque-awarding exercise but a deliberate mechanism. The Guangzhou federation acts as the hub — screening overseas partners, unifying brand and identity, organising study tours and exchanges — while overseas associations provide venues, communities and localised operation. Plaques, training camps and business delegations form a closed loop of "branding — training — matching."

Notably, this model echoes the approach of other Guangdong qiaoxiang. From the Teochew qiaopi (remittance-letter) culture to Jiangmen's silver-letter heritage, Lingnan has long excelled at converting dispersed networks into development resources. The tea house can be seen as a contemporary upgrade of this tradition: using the lightest cultural symbol — a cup of tea — to leverage the heaviest transnational bonds.

## A cool note: the limits and direction of the network boom

Of course, the tea-house network has its boundaries. First, it relies heavily on the commitment of individual enthusiastic leaders; without institutional incentives, the heat may rise and fall with personal energy. Second, covering 37 countries does not equal deep penetration — many nodes are still "points" rather than a "web," with weak connections among themselves. Third, in countries sensitive to Chinese associations, the political implication of the tea house must be managed carefully to avoid misreading as an extension of external influence.

The more fundamental question is how to move this web from "fellowship-type" to "productive-type." The answer may lie in binding the tea house more deeply to the industrial, innovation and education systems of the Greater Bay Area, so that every cup of tea could be the start of a cooperation. Only then will the tea house be not merely a vessel of nostalgia but an engine of development.

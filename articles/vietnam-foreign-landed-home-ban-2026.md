## 意象：一道新的围墙

在越南（Vietnam）人的世界里，土地从不是冷冰冰的资产。一句「有土斯有财」的古老信条，至今仍刻在从河内到胡志明市的门楣上。外籍人士若想在这片土地上拥有带地契的住宅，过去十余年里曾被视为开放与信心的象征；而如今，一道新的围墙正被悄然砌起。

## 条文：公寓之外再筑墙

越南政府正着手大幅修订《住房法》，拟全面禁止外国人购买包括独立别墅、联排住宅与排屋在内的所有「有地住宅」，仅保留高层公寓这一通道。根据《日经亚洲》（Nikkei Asia）与泰国（Thailand）《民族报》的报道，新草案维持既有规则：单栋公寓楼的外资持有比例上限为30%，外国买家的产权期限最长50年，在特定条件下可申请延长一次、同样不超过50年；涉及国家安全与国防的敏感区域，依旧严禁外资进入。

这一转向，意味着2014年越南首度向外国人敞开房地大门时那份「公寓与有地住宅皆可买」的宽松，将被大幅收回。

## 转折：从2014年开放到如今收紧

回溯脉络，越南在2014年修订法律，允许外籍个人购买公寓，并在符合条件的项目中购置有地住宅；2023年《住房法》延续了这一框架。然而，2026年7月越共中央委员会通过的第21号决议，重新强调「土地归全民所有，由国家代表行使管理」的宪法原则，明确禁止土地私有化，并不允许外国人以任何形式受让土地使用权。住房法修订草案，正是这一政治定调在房地产领域的具体落地。

## 数字：房价已是收入十九倍

收紧并非无的放矢。伴随经济快速扩张，越南房地产市场持续过热，主要城市房价已攀升至当地居民中位数收入的十九倍以上，普通家庭望房兴叹。建设部数据显示，截至修订草案公布时，外籍组织与个人已购买并持有约6188套住宅，其中自2024年8月1日（2023年《住房法》生效日）起成交的公寓为1519套。

与此同时，一套岘港海滨别墅标价约120万美元（约合860万元人民币），远非本地工薪阶层所能企及，却长期是外籍与海外越南裔买家追逐的标的。

## 逻辑：土地全民所有的红线

越南从未承认土地私有。在这套宪法逻辑里，土地是「全民所有」、由国家统一管理的公共资源，个人与组织获得的只是「土地使用权」而非所有权。外国人被挡在有地住宅之外，本质上是在守住这条红线——不让境外资本通过购置带地物业，间接掌握国家的土地资源。

## 隐忧：代持与透明度的代价

咨询机构阿米翁（Avison Young）越南及柬埔寨业务负责人戴维·杰克逊指出，更严格的限制将直接冲击面向国际买家的奢华别墅与海滨度假房产。但更大的隐忧在于「按下葫芦浮起瓢」：当正规通道收窄，部分外国买家可能转向代理人、挂名公司或委托书等隐蔽手段代持房产。这不仅会降低市场透明度，也会放大法律纠纷与洗钱风险。

## 影响：谁会离开，谁会留下

短期看，受冲击最明显的是高端有地住宅与旅游地产的外籍需求；长期看，越南对外资的态度信号更值得玩味。与泰国、马来西亚（Malaysia）等邻国相比，越南原本对外国人置业已设下30%的公寓比例上限与50年产权期限，如今进一步收紧有地住宅，折射出在吸引资本与守护土地主权之间的微妙平衡。

对于真正想在越南安家、养老或经商的长期居留者而言，高层公寓仍是合法且稳定的选项；但对于把越南物业当作投机筹码的短线资本，门正在关上。一道围墙，隔开的不只是砖瓦，更是一个国家对「谁能动土」的底线宣示。

## 延伸阅读

**住房法（Housing Law）**：越南2023年《住房法》规范住房所有权，含外国人持有比例与期限等条款，本次修订拟进一步收紧。

**红皮书（Pink Book）**：越南《土地使用权与房屋所有权证书》的通称，是确认房产权属的关键凭证，外籍买家能否取得直接决定处置与转让权利。

**土地全民所有（Land-for-all）**：越南宪法原则，规定土地归全民所有、由国家管理，构成限制外国人取得土地使用权的根本依据。

## The image: a new wall

In the Vietnamese imagination, land has never been a mere asset. An old maxim — to own land is to own security — still hangs above doorways from Hanoi to Ho Chi Minh City. For more than a decade, allowing foreigners to own homes with land titles was treated as a symbol of openness and confidence. Now, quietly, a new wall is being built.

## The text: a wall beyond apartments

Vietnam's government is moving to substantially revise its Housing Law, drafting a blanket ban on foreigners buying all "landed homes" — detached villas, townhouses and shophouses — and leaving only high-rise apartments as a channel. Reporting by Nikkei Asia and The Nation says the draft keeps existing rules: the foreign ownership cap stays at 30% of units in any single apartment building, titles run for a maximum of 50 years and may be extended once by up to another 50 years, and properties in national-security or defence-sensitive zones remain off-limits to foreign capital.

The shift rolls back the 2014 opening, when Vietnam first opened its property market and let foreigners buy both apartments and landed homes.

## The turn: from the 2014 opening to today's tightening

Vietnam amended its law in 2014 to let foreign individuals buy apartments and, in qualifying projects, landed homes; the 2023 Housing Law carried that framework forward. But in July 2026 the Communist Party of Vietnam Central Committee passed Resolution 21, reaffirming the constitutional principle that "land belongs to all the people, managed by the state," explicitly ruling out land privatisation and forbidding any transfer of land-use rights to foreigners. The housing-law draft is that political signal made concrete in real estate.

## The numbers: prices at 19 times income

The tightening is not without cause. As the economy expanded rapidly, Vietnam's property market overheated; in major cities prices have climbed to more than 19 times median household income, putting homes out of reach for ordinary families. The Ministry of Construction reports that foreign organisations and individuals had bought and held about 6,188 homes as of the draft's publication, of which 1,519 apartments were transacted after 1 August 2024, when the 2023 law took effect.

A Da Nang beachfront villa, meanwhile, is often listed at around S$1.6 million (~US$1.2 million) — far beyond local wage earners, yet long a target for foreign and overseas-Vietnamese buyers.

## The logic: the red line of land-for-all

Vietnam has never recognised private land ownership. Under its constitutional logic, land is a public resource "owned by all the people" and managed by the state; what individuals and organisations hold is a right to use land, not own it. Barring foreigners from landed homes is, at root, defending that red line — keeping overseas capital from indirectly controlling the country's land through property with title.

## The worry: nominees and the cost to transparency

David Jackson, head of Avison Young for Vietnam and Cambodia, notes that stricter limits will hit head-on the luxury villas and beachside resorts aimed at international buyers. The deeper worry is displacement: as the formal channel narrows, some foreign buyers may turn to nominees, nominee companies or powers of attorney to hold property covertly. That lowers market transparency and amplifies the risk of legal disputes and money-laundering.

## The impact: who leaves, who stays

In the short run, the clearest hit falls on foreign demand for high-end landed homes and tourism property; in the longer run, Vietnam's signal on foreign capital deserves more attention. Compared with neighbours such as Thailand and Malaysia, Vietnam already capped foreign apartment ownership at 30% and titles at 50 years; tightening landed homes further reflects a delicate balance between courting capital and guarding land sovereignty.

For long-term residents who truly wish to settle, retire or do business in Vietnam, high-rise apartments remain a lawful and stable option; for short-term capital treating Vietnamese property as a speculative chip, the door is closing. A wall, in the end, separates more than brick and mortar — it is a state's pronouncement of where the line on "who may break ground" is drawn.

## Further reading

**Housing Law**: Vietnam's 2023 Housing Law governs homeownership, including foreign ownership caps and tenure; the current revision would tighten it further.

**Pink Book**: The popular name for Vietnam's Certificate of Land Use Rights and House Ownership — the crucial proof of title. Whether a foreign buyer can obtain it decides their right to dispose of or transfer the property.

**Land-for-all**: A Vietnamese constitutional principle that land belongs to all the people and is managed by the state, forming the fundamental basis for restricting foreigners' access to land-use rights.

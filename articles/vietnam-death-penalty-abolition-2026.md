# 越南刑法修正草案拟废除六项死刑 刑罚改革迈出关键一步

越南政府向国会提交刑法修正草案，提议对叛乱、强奸未满16岁者、贩毒、危害人类和战争罪等六项罪名废除死刑，同时保留叛国、谋杀、恐怖主义与非法生产毒品的死刑。公安部称此举符合"立法进步趋势"，预计国会10月表决、2027年3月生效。

## 六项罪名拟免死 四项保留

据越南之声报道，公安部长梁心光8月20日在国会说明，草案旨在进一步区分刑事责任，使罪名与刑罚与行为的性质、严重程度更相称。拟废除死刑的六项包括：叛乱、强奸未满16岁者、贩毒、危害人类与战争罪等。

保留死刑的则包括叛国、谋杀、恐怖主义与非法生产毒品。草案还扩大非监禁刑罚至十种罪行，并大幅提高罚款与量刑金额门槛，同时取消对被判有罪商业法人的资本处罚。

## 从去死刑化到司法合作

公安部本周声明称，缩小死刑适用范围"使党的政策制度化，符合立法进步趋势，并提升越南在刑事司法合作、引渡和司法互助方面的地位"。这一定位，将刑罚改革与越南融入国际司法体系挂钩。

去年7月生效的刑法已对贪污及颠覆政府活动在内的八项罪行取消死刑，使房地产大亨张美兰免于死刑——她因涉嫌约120亿美元（约860亿元人民币）诈骗案被判有罪。在越南，死刑数据属国家机密，自2011年废除枪决后，注射死刑为唯一执行方式。

## 背景与争议

改革背后是越南逐步靠拢国际人权标准的长期努力。但批评者指出，越南的命题更接近于"如何在不放弃刑罚威慑的前提下，缩小死刑的适用范围"，而非简单地全面废死。

草案预计在未来几周辩论，并于10月国会会议表决。若通过，修订后的刑法将于2027年3月生效。对于一个人权记录仍受西方审视的国家，这一步既是内部法律技术的调整，也是对外信号的管理。

## 延伸阅读

**死刑（Death Penalty / Capital Punishment）**：对最严重犯罪剥夺生命的刑罚；越南现行执行方式为注射死刑，相关数据被列为国家机密。

**刑法修正（Penal Code Amendment）**：立法机关对刑法典的局部修订；越南近年以"区分刑事责任"为名，逐步取消部分罪名的死刑。

**司法互助（Mutual Legal Assistance）**：国与国在调查、取证、引渡等刑事事务上的协作机制；缩小死刑常被视为提升此类合作的条件之一。

# Vietnam's Draft Penal Code Revision Would Abolish the Death Penalty for Six Crimes

Vietnam's government submitted a draft penal-code revision to the National Assembly proposing to abolish the death penalty for six crimes — among them rebellion, rape of minors under 16, drug trafficking, crimes against humanity and war crimes — while retaining it for treason, murder, terrorism and illicit drug production. The Ministry of Public Security called the move consistent with a "trend of legislative progress"; a vote is expected in October, with the law taking effect in March 2027.

## Six crimes spared, four retained

According to Voice of Vietnam, Minister of Public Security Luong Tam Quang told the Assembly on August 20 that the draft aims to further differentiate criminal responsibility so that charges and penalties better match the nature and gravity of the act. The six crimes proposed for abolition include rebellion, rape of those under 16, drug trafficking, crimes against humanity and war crimes.

Retained under capital punishment are treason, murder, terrorism and illicit drug production. The draft also extends non-custodial penalties to ten categories of crime, sharply raises fine and sentencing thresholds, and removes capital penalties against convicted corporate entities.

## From abolition to judicial cooperation

A ministry statement this week said narrowing the scope of the death penalty "institutionalizes the party's policy, aligns with the trend of legislative progress, and enhances Vietnam's standing in criminal justice cooperation, extradition and mutual legal assistance." That framing ties penal reform to Vietnam's integration into the international judicial system.

The penal code that took effect last July already removed the death penalty for eight crimes, including corruption and acts aimed at overthrowing the government, sparing real-estate tycoon Truong My Lan, convicted in an alleged US$12 billion fraud. In Vietnam, death-penalty data are state secrets, and lethal injection — adopted in 2011 after abolishing firing squads — is the sole method of execution.

## Context and debate

The reform reflects Vietnam's long effort to edge closer to international human-rights standards. Critics note the question is less about abolishing the death penalty outright and more about how to shrink its reach without abandoning deterrence.

The draft is expected to be debated in the coming weeks and voted on at the October session. If passed, the revised code takes effect in March 2027. For a country whose rights record remains under Western scrutiny, the step is both an internal legal-technical adjustment and a managed external signal.

## Further reading

**Death Penalty (Capital Punishment)**: punishment by loss of life for the gravest crimes; Vietnam's sole method is lethal injection, and related data are classified as state secrets.

**Penal Code Amendment**: a partial revision of the criminal code; Vietnam has in recent years abolished the death penalty for some offenses under the banner of "differentiating criminal responsibility."

**Mutual Legal Assistance**: cross-border cooperation in investigation, evidence and extradition; narrowing the death penalty is often seen as a condition for deeper such cooperation.

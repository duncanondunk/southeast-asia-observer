# 日本银行收缩中国业务，东南亚为何成为资本避风港？

据《日经亚洲》报道，日本地方银行正悄然调整海外布局：过去五年里，它们在中国的分支机构从50处减至40处，北海道银行关闭了进驻19年的沈阳办事处，京都银行也撤出了大连。与此同时，千叶银行2025年1月在新加坡设点，业务覆盖泰国、越南和澳大利亚；佐贺银行则筹备在印尼开设子公司。日本三大巨型银行亦不例外——三井住友银行五年来在华贷款余额缩水约40%，瑞穗银行降幅超过30%，三菱日联银行也下降约20%。

这不是简单的资产负债表腾挪，而是一场跟随制造业客户的金融迁徙。日本车企在华销量承压、电动车本土竞争加剧，三菱与本田纷纷削减在华产能，零部件企业与地方银行赖以生存的"中国故事"随之褪色。劳动力与租金成本上升、香港国安法带来的合规风险，也促使金融机构重新评估敞口。当工厂与订单向南移动，银行网点、信贷与并购服务自然紧随其后。

新加坡与印尼正成为这场资本南下的主要受益者。新加坡凭借法治、外汇自由与区域总部生态，承接了日本银行的东南亚指挥中心功能；印尼则以人口红利和本地市场潜力，吸引佐贺银行等机构落地。印度的诱惑同样显著——京都金融集团计划开设日本地方银行首个印度代表处，三大巨型银行则通过入股印度金融机构扩大布局。不过，东南亚并非无风险天堂：东盟内部监管碎片化、基础设施瓶颈与汇率波动，仍是日本银行需要消化的成本。

日本金融资本从"押注中国"转向"多点下注"，映射的是整个东亚供应链的重组逻辑。"中国加一"不再只是制造业口号，它正在改写银行资产负债表的空间分布。东南亚国家若能借这股资金东移完善债券市场、改善监管确定性，就有望把短期的"避风港效应"转化为长期的资本粘性。

## 延伸阅读

- **脱钩（decoupling）**：指企业为降低单一市场依赖而进行的供应链多元化，常伴随生产基地与金融服务的重新布局。
- **中国加一（China Plus One）**：企业将投资分散到中国以外市场的策略，东南亚是主要承接地之一。
- **金融跟随（financial follow-the-client）**：银行随制造业客户迁移而调整网点、信贷与并购服务布局的现象。

# Why Japanese Banks Are Shrinking China and Betting on Southeast Asia

Japanese regional banks are quietly unwinding their China footprint. According to a Nikkei Asia report, their branch network on the mainland shrank from 50 locations in April 2021 to 40 by the end of March this year. Hokkaido Bank closed its Shenyang office after 19 years; Bank of Kyoto pulled out of Dalian. At the same time, Chiba Bank opened a Singapore branch in January 2025 covering Thailand, Vietnam and Australia, while Saikyo Bank is preparing an Indonesian subsidiary. Japan's three megabanks are also retrenching: Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation cut its China loan book by roughly 40% over five years, Mizuho Bank by more than 30%, and MUFG Bank by about 20%.

This is not a simple balance-sheet adjustment. It is a financial migration following manufacturing clients. Japanese carmakers are struggling in China as local electric-vehicle competition intensifies; Mitsubishi Motors and Honda Motor have scaled back production. As their suppliers and orders move south, the regional banks that helped them navigate Chinese tax and regulatory systems are losing their reason to stay. Rising labour and rental costs, together with tighter compliance concerns, have added to the case for redeployment.

Singapore and Indonesia are the clearest beneficiaries so far. Singapore offers the rule of law, currency convertibility and a regional-headquarter ecosystem that makes it a natural Southeast Asian command centre for Japanese lenders. Indonesia attracts institutions such as Saikyo Bank with its demographic heft and domestic-market potential. India is also drawing capital: Kyoto Financial Group plans the first Indian representative office by a Japanese regional bank, while the megabanks have taken stakes in Indian financial firms. Yet Southeast Asia is not a risk-free haven. Regulatory fragmentation across ASEAN, infrastructure bottlenecks and currency volatility remain costs that Japanese banks must absorb.

The shift from "all-in on China" to a multi-pivot strategy reflects the broader restructuring of East Asian supply chains. "China plus one" is no longer just a manufacturing slogan; it is reshaping the geography of bank balance sheets. If Southeast Asian states can use this capital inflow to deepen bond markets and improve regulatory clarity, they may turn a short-term safe-haven effect into long-term financial stickiness.

## Further reading

- **Decoupling**: the process by which firms reduce dependence on a single market, often relocating production and financial services.
- **China Plus One**: a strategy of diversifying investment beyond China, with Southeast Asia as a key recipient.
- **Financial follow-the-client**: the tendency of banks to move branches, credit and advisory services alongside relocating manufacturing clients.

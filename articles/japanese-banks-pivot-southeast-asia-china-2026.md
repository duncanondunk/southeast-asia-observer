# 日本银行收缩中国业务，东南亚与印度为何成为资本新腹地？

据《日经亚洲》报道，过去五年里，日本地方银行在中国的网点从 2021年4月的 50处缩减至 2025年3月底的 40处，降幅约 20%。北海道银行关闭了进驻 19年的沈阳办事处，京都银行撤出大连、将业务并入上海网点；千叶银行与七十七银行则先后在新加坡设立运营枢纽，佐贺银行筹备在印尼开设子公司。三菱日联、三井住友、瑞穗三大巨型银行的对华企业贷款，五年间最高缩水约 40%。与此同时，日本已是全球主要净债权国，庞大的海外资产正急切寻找更高回报的出口。

一句话点破：这不是一次简单的资产负债表腾挪，而是日本金融资本跟随制造业客户，把增长赌注从中国转向东南亚与印度的结构化重排——"中国加一"正从一句制造口号，改写银行资产负债表的空间分布。

## 车间往南，账房也往南

银行的海外布局，从来是跟着客户走的。过去二十年，日本制造业大举进入中国，金融机构紧随其后，为汽车零部件供应商提供跨境税务、合规与融资服务，帮它们度过市场进入期。如今这套"跟随客户"的逻辑正在反向运行。触发点之一，是中国电动车的崛起：比亚迪、蔚来等本土品牌在国内外市场快速抢占份额，使依托燃油车时代的日系车企及其配套供应链承压。当客户的产能与利润同步缩水，为其提供跨境金融服务的银行便失去了扩张的理由。这不是周期性波动，而是技术路线更迭带来的结构性退潮。日本地方银行协会对 61家区域金融机构的调研显示，中国虽仍占其海外网点近半数，但物理存在正在系统性收缩——这不是撤离，而是把资源从中国的边际收益里抽走。回望本世纪初，中国曾是日本银行海外扩张的明星目的地，制造业繁荣带动区域银行密集布点；如今潮水退去，同一套"跟随客户"逻辑把网点推向南方。资本并未消失，只是换了岸。

## 地方银行的"中国减法"

减法最直接地体现在关店上。北海道银行于 2025年5月终结了沈阳办事处 19年的运营；京都银行则在去年关闭大连网点，将相关商业活动并入既有上海架构。"维持分支的负担加重，而客户需求在下降，所以我们做了精简。"这类解释在业内并不罕见。成本是第一推力：中国的租金与工资逐年走高，而日本制造企业的边际收益却在收缩。当工厂与订单向南移动，信贷、并购与现金管理服务自然紧随其后——账房的位移，不过是车间位移的影子。

## 三巨头的账面收缩

三巨头的撤退更隐蔽，却更可观。据《日经亚洲》梳理，三井住友银行五年间对华贷款余额缩减约 40%；瑞穗银行降幅超过 30%；三菱日联银行也从约 3.5万亿日元（约 1.6万亿元人民币）的基准回落约 20%。值得注意的是，三巨头的利润并未因此受损——截至 2026年3月的财年，三菱日联净利润跃升 30% 至 2.4万亿日元（约 17万亿元人民币），瑞穗增长 41%。收缩中国，反而让它们把资本腾挪到了回报更高的印度与东南亚，账面反而更厚。

## 新加坡与印尼：承接南下的资本

新加坡与印尼是这场资本南下最直接的受益者。新加坡凭借法治、外汇自由与区域总部生态，承接了日本银行的东南亚"指挥中枢"功能；其缺陷在于运营成本居亚洲前列，但对财资与财富管理而言，监管稳定性足以抵消高成本。印尼则以人口红利与本土市场潜力，吸引佐贺银行等机构落地。东盟内部的劳动力成本优势与扩张中的年轻人口，使它成为日本制造企业"中国加一"的首选承接地之一——银行不过是跟在客户身后，把网点搬到了机会所在。

## 印度的诱惑：从代表处到真金白银

印度则是更长远的赌注。地方银行还没有像在中国那样大举设点，多通过代表处或战略合作切入；但巨型银行已砸下真金白银——三井住友斥约 16亿美元（约 115亿元人民币）收购 YES Bank 约 20% 股权，三菱日联以约 44.5亿美元（约 320亿元人民币）拿下 Shriram Finance 20% 股份，瑞穗则在 2025年底通过证券子公司控股 Avendus。日本对印直接投资在最新财年升至约 46亿美元（约 330亿元人民币）。瑞穗全球业务 CEO 加藤正彦的判断颇具代表性："印度已迅速成为日本企业最具前景的目的地。"

支撑这股热情的，是印度在半导体、数据中心与可再生能源上的制造野心。当全球企业寻求"去中国化"的供应链替代，日本在芯片材料、设备与工业技术上的积累恰好对接；而数字化、人工智能与云计算正推动印度数据中心投资激增，为银行参与长期基建融资打开窗口。人口结构的反差同样关键：日本暮气沉沉、国内信贷增长有限，印度却拥有年轻劳动力与扩张中的消费金融——对寻求未来数十年稳定增长的银行而言，吸引力不言而喻。

## 东南亚不是无风险天堂

但南下并非没有代价。东盟内部监管碎片化、基础设施瓶颈与汇率波动，仍是日本银行必须消化的成本。新加坡的高运营成本自不待言；印尼的司法与监管确定性、印度复杂的外资法律框架，都是陌生的考题。更深一层的变量是地缘政治：当"中国加一"在中美博弈的语境下被赋予战略含义，金融机构的选址也可能被卷入更大的摩擦。资本可以南移，风险却不会原地消失——它只是换了一个时区，重新计价。

这也是为何日本银行多选择与本地机构合资、而非独立扩张。东盟十国市场分割、会计准则与清结算系统各异，单打独斗的合规成本极高；能否借南移沉淀下真正的本地能力，而非仅仅搬动网点，将决定这场迁徙的成色。

对中国而言，日本资本的南移是一面镜子。当本国制造业外迁、金融跟随撤离，中国也在以熊猫债、本币结算等工具向南亚与东南亚输出人民币流动性——两股资本流在同一片海域相向而行，重塑的正是东亚"谁为谁融资"的旧秩序。

## 真正的棋局：东亚资本版图的重绘

放在更长的时间轴上看，这场迁徙折射的是整个东亚供应链与资本版图的重组。日本财务省数据显示，2025年日本对外总资产创下约 1,805万亿日元（约 11.6万亿美元，约 84.8万亿元人民币）的纪录，稳居全球主要净债权国。如此庞大的海外资产需要出路；当国内利率仍低、增长乏力，资本天然向外寻租。对东南亚与印度而言，能否借这股资金东移完善债券市场、改善监管确定性，决定了短期的"避风港效应"能否沉淀为长期的资本粘性。水道与网点会移动，真正的较量在制度——谁先把规则擦亮，谁才能把这波南下资本留在自家港口。这并非零和博弈——中日只是在同一片海域，选择了不同的下注方向。

## 延伸阅读

**中国加一（China Plus One）**：企业将投资与生产分散到中国以外市场的策略，东南亚与印度是主要承接地，近年从制造延伸到金融布局。

**金融跟随（Financial Follow-the-Client）**：银行随制造业客户迁移而调整网点、信贷与并购服务布局的现象，是日本银行此轮南下的核心逻辑。

**净债权国（Net Creditor）**：一国对外资产大于负债的状态；日本长期为全球主要净债权国，其资本外流深刻影响亚洲金融格局。

# Why Japanese Banks Are Shrinking China and Betting on Southeast Asia and India

According to a Nikkei Asia report, over the past five years the number of branches Japanese regional banks operate in China fell from 50 in April 2021 to 40 by the end of March 2025, a drop of about 20 per cent. Hokkaido Bank closed its 19-year-old Shenyang office; Bank of Kyoto shut its Dalian outlet and folded the business into its existing Shanghai network. At the same time, Chiba Bank and 77 Bank set up new operational hubs in Singapore, while Saikyo Bank is preparing an Indonesian subsidiary. The three megabanks — Mitsubishi UFJ, Sumitomo Mitsui and Mizuho — have cut their China corporate loan books by as much as 40 per cent over five years. Japan, meanwhile, is a leading global net creditor whose vast overseas assets are hunting for higher-yielding outlets.

One sentence lays bare the subtext: this is not a simple balance-sheet shuffle but a structural reallocation in which Japanese financial capital follows its manufacturing clients, shifting its growth bets from China to Southeast Asia and India — "China plus one" is rewriting the spatial distribution of bank balance sheets.

## From shop floor to ledger, the move is south

A bank's overseas footprint has always trailed its clients. For two decades Japanese manufacturers poured into China, and financial institutions followed, offering component suppliers cross-border tax, compliance and financing services through market entry. Today that "follow-the-client" logic is running in reverse. When Mitsubishi Motors exits and Honda curtails capacity, when local EV brands squeeze Japanese carmakers' margins, the regional banks that served those clients lose their reason to stay. A key trigger is the rise of Chinese electric vehicles. Brands such as BYD and Nio have rapidly taken share at home and abroad, squeezing the Japanese carmakers and supplier ecosystems built around the combustion-engine era. When clients' capacity and margins shrink together, the banks that served them cross-border lose their reason to expand. This is no cyclical dip but a structural ebb tied to a shift in technology paths. A survey by the Regional Banks Association of Japan covering 61 regional institutions shows China still accounts for nearly half of their overseas offices, yet the physical presence is contracting systematically — not an exit, but a drawdown of resources from China's diminishing marginal returns. Cast back to the early 2000s, and China was the star destination of Japanese banks' overseas expansion, its manufacturing boom drawing dense branch networks; today the tide has receded and the same follow-the-client logic pushes outlets southward. Capital has not vanished, only changed shore.

## The regional banks' "China subtraction"

The subtraction shows up most plainly in closures. Hokkaido Bank ended 19 years of operation in Shenyang in May 2025; Bank of Kyoto closed its Dalian outlet last year, consolidating the activity into its Shanghai structure. "As the burden of maintaining the branch grew while client demand fell, we downsized" — an explanation heard often in the industry. Cost is the first driver: rents and wages in China keep rising while the marginal returns of Japanese manufacturers shrink. When factories and orders move south, credit, M&A and cash-management services naturally follow — the ledger's displacement is merely the shadow of the shop floor's.

## The megabanks' shrinking ledgers

The megabanks' retreat is subtler but larger. Nikkei Asia's tally shows Sumitomo Mitsui trimmed its China loan book by roughly 40 per cent over five years; Mizuho fell more than 30 per cent; MUFG slipped about 20 per cent from a baseline of some 3.5 trillion yen (about US$21.9 billion). Notably, the megabanks' profits did not suffer — for the fiscal year ending March 2026, MUFG's net profit jumped 30 per cent to 2.4 trillion yen (about US$17 billion) and Mizuho rose 41 per cent. Shrinking China freed capital to deploy where returns are higher: India and Southeast Asia, leaving the books fatter rather than thinner.

## Singapore and Indonesia: catching the southward capital

Singapore and Indonesia are the most immediate beneficiaries of this southward drift. Singapore, with its rule of law, currency convertibility and regional-headquarter ecosystem, absorbs the role of Japanese banks' Southeast Asian "command centre"; its drawback is among Asia's highest operating costs, but for treasury and wealth management, regulatory stability offsets the price. Indonesia draws institutions such as Saikyo Bank with its demographic heft and domestic-market potential. Within ASEAN, competitive labour costs and an expanding young population make it a preferred landing spot for Japanese manufacturers' "China plus one" strategy — banks merely follow clients to where the opportunity is.

## India's pull: from representative offices to real money

India is the longer-horizon wager. Regional banks have not yet planted full branches as they did in China, entering instead through representative offices or strategic partnerships; the megabanks, however, have put real money on the table — SMBC took about a 20 per cent stake in YES Bank for roughly US$1.6 billion, MUFG acquired 20 per cent of Shriram Finance for about US$4.45 billion, and Mizuho took a majority stake in Avendus through its securities arm in late 2025. Japanese direct investment in India rose to about US$4.6 billion in the latest fiscal year. Mizuho Global CEO Masahiko Kato's line is telling: "India has quickly emerged as the most promising destination for Japanese businesses."

What underpins the enthusiasm is India's manufacturing ambition in semiconductors, data centres and renewable energy. As global firms seek "de-China" supply-chain alternatives, Japan's strength in chip materials, equipment and industrial technology fits neatly; digitalisation, AI and cloud computing are driving a surge in Indian data-centre investment, opening a window for banks to fund long-horizon infrastructure. The demographic contrast matters too: an ageing Japan with limited domestic credit growth versus a young workforce and expanding consumer finance in India — for banks seeking steady growth over coming decades, the pull is self-evident.

## Southeast Asia is no risk-free haven

But moving south is not costless. Regulatory fragmentation across ASEAN, infrastructure bottlenecks and currency volatility remain costs Japanese banks must absorb. Singapore's high operating cost needs no elaboration; Indonesia's judicial and regulatory predictability, and India's intricate foreign-investment legal framework, are unfamiliar exams. A deeper variable is geopolitics: as "China plus one" is invested with strategic meaning inside the US–China contest, the location choices of financial institutions can be drawn into larger friction. Capital can shift south; risk does not simply vanish in place — it merely changes time zone and is repriced.

## The real board: redrawing East Asia's capital map

On a longer timeline, this migration reflects the reordering of East Asia's supply chains and capital map. Japan's Ministry of Finance data show the country's gross external assets hit a record of about 1,805 trillion yen (roughly US$11.6 trillion) in 2025, keeping it among the world's top net creditors. Such a vast pool of overseas assets needs an outlet; with low domestic rates and weak growth, capital naturally seeks returns abroad. For China, the southward drift of Japanese capital is a mirror. As its own manufacturers relocate and finance follows them out, China is likewise exporting renminbi liquidity southward through Panda Bonds and local-currency settlement — two capital flows moving in opposite directions across the same waters, reshaping the old East Asian order of who finances whom. For Southeast Asia and India, whether they can use this capital inflow to deepen bond markets and improve regulatory certainty will decide whether the short-term "safe-haven effect" settles into durable financial stickiness. Waterways and branch networks move; the real contest is institutional — whoever polishes its rules first keeps this southward capital in its own harbour.

## Further reading

**China Plus One:** A strategy of diversifying investment and production beyond China, with Southeast Asia and India as main recipients — in recent years extending from manufacturing into financial positioning.

**Financial follow-the-client:** The tendency of banks to shift branches, credit and advisory services alongside relocating manufacturing clients; the core logic of Japanese banks' current southward turn.

**Net creditor:** A country whose external assets exceed its liabilities; Japan has long been a top global net creditor, and its capital outflows shape Asia's financial landscape.

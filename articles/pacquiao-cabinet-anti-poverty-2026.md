# 从拳台到内阁：帕奎奥的"脱贫攻坚战"与菲律宾的贫困顽疾

9月8日，菲律宾传奇拳王、前参议员曼尼·帕奎奥在马拉坎南宫宣誓就任国家反贫困委员会首席召集人。这是他16年政治生涯中首个内阁职位，也是他自2022年总统大选失利、2025年参议院选举落败后，时隔五年重回权力中心。一位净资产据报达2.2亿美元（约15.4亿元人民币）的拳坛偶像，如今要去打一场没有回合限制的"脱贫攻坚战"。

## 从街头纸板到马拉坎南宫

帕奎奥的贫寒出身几乎是菲律宾底层叙事的标本。他在自述中说，童年曾在货船偷渡，在街头打拳，每场仅赚2美元（约14元人民币），饿时"只能喝水饱腹"。47岁的他，19岁拿下第一个世界拳王头衔，职业生涯揽获八个量级、共12条金腰带，2025年入选国际拳击名人堂。从萨兰加尼众议员到参议员，再到如今的内阁职位，这条上升通道本身就写着"逆袭"二字。

## 一个"平等内阁部长"的职级

国家反贫困委员会是统筹政府社会改革议程的协调与咨询机构。按菲律宾法律，帕奎奥的首席召集人职级等同内阁秘书——虽无部会首长的名分，却坐进了内阁会议桌。对一个素来以"行动派"形象示人的拳王而言，这个职位既是象征性的安抚，也是实打实的治理入场券。问题在于，协调机构能调动的资源和强制力，远不及一个实权部门。

## 9.7%：贫困率的真实重量

帕奎奥要对垒的，不是某个对手，而是一组冷冰冰的数字。政府数据显示，2025年菲律宾贫困率为9.7%，相当于约1108万人生活在贫困线以下。小马可斯政府的目标是到2028年把贫困率压到8%至9%。拳台上的击倒只需数十秒，结构性贫困却由教育、就业、土地改革与地区失衡共同铸就，远非一记重拳能解决。

## 名人政治的红利与迷思

帕奎奥的号召力毋庸置疑——他是菲律宾人最引以为傲的体育符号之一。名人从政在菲律宾并不鲜见，从影视明星到运动员，人气常被直接兑换成选票。但光环也是迷思：当扶贫被包装成"拳王归来"的励志剧本，公众可能高估个人意志的作用，低估制度性梗阻。扶贫委员会要交付的，是政策而非票房。

## 扶贫委员会能做什么

作为协调机构，国家反贫困委员会的职责是把分散在各部门的减贫项目串起来、做评估、提建议。它能推动数据打通、瞄准最弱势群体、督促地方执行。但它的抓手有限：真正决定贫困率的，是农业部的大米补贴、教育部的人力资本投入、产业部的就业创造。帕奎奥若只做"呼吁者"，声音再大，也难改算式。

## 小马可斯的算盘

为何是现在、为何是帕奎奥？对小马可斯而言，任命一位国民级偶像主抓扶贫，既能在贫困议题上得分，又能把一位潜在的选举变量纳入体制内。帕奎奥曾深得杜特尔特信任，也一度是2022年总统热门；把他放进内阁，是安抚、也是收纳。政治吸纳明星，从来不只是出于公益。

## 拳王的对手不是人

帕奎奥说，他要帮助"正在经历艰难困苦的同胞"，因为他有过切身之痛。这份共情真实而珍贵。但贫困是一场没有对手擂台的比赛：它藏在台风后的农田、马尼拉郊区的简陋棚屋、外劳汇回却又被通胀吞噬的薪资里。拳王可以鼓舞士气，却无法替制度出拳。

## 一场注定艰难的对垒

帕奎奥接下来与贫困的对垒，注定比任何一场世纪之战都艰难。他能带来关注度、能推动协调、能在弱势议题上发声；但要把9.7%往下拉，靠的是跨部门改革而非个人魅力。这位曾把每一枚硬币攒起贴补家用的少年，如今握有的是内阁席位而非拳套——而菲律宾的贫困顽疾，恰恰最不惧怕名气。

## 延伸阅读

**国家反贫困委员会（National Anti-Poverty Commission）**：菲律宾统筹社会改革议程的协调机构，成立于1993年，负责连接政府部门、地方政府与基层扶贫组织。

**贫困率（Poverty incidence）**：以家庭人均支出是否低于官方贫困门槛衡量，2025年菲律宾为9.7%，约1108万人未达线。

**名人政治（Celebrity politics）**：凭借演艺或体育人气直接转化为选举资本的现象，在菲律宾、拉美等地尤为常见，红利与治理风险并存。

# From the Ring to the Cabinet: Pacquiao's Anti-Poverty Fight and the Philippines' Stubborn Poverty

On September 8, Manny Pacquiao — the eight-division boxing champion, former senator and 2022 presidential also-ran — was sworn in at the Malacañang as chief convenor of the National Anti-Poverty Commission. It is his first cabinet post in a 16-year political career, and his return to the centre of power five years after losing the presidency and, in 2025, a Senate bid. A man whose net worth was reported at US$220 million is now vowing to fight a poverty he knows firsthand.

## From cardboard on the street to the Malacañang

Pacquiao's rags-to-riches story is almost a template for the Filipino underclass. He has told of stowing away on cargo ships as a child, fighting in street bouts for US$2 a night, drinking water to quiet his hunger. At 47, he took his first world title at 19 and went on to win twelve belts across eight weight classes, entering the International Boxing Hall of Fame in 2025. From congressman for Sarangani to senator to cabinet, the ascent is real — but so is the structural poverty it overshadows.

## A cabinet secretary in rank, if not in name

The National Anti-Poverty Commission coordinates and advises on the government's social-reform agenda. By law, Pacquiao's post carries the rank of a cabinet secretary — a seat at the table, if not the title of a department head. For a politician built on the image of a doer, it is both a symbolic win and a real entry into governance. The catch: a coordinating body commands far less money and coercive power than a line ministry.

## 9.7%: the real weight of the poverty rate

The foe Pacquiao faces is not a person but a number. Government data put the Philippines' poverty rate at 9.7% in 2025 — roughly 11.08 million people below the line. The Marcos administration aims to pull that to 8–9% by 2028. A boxing knockout takes seconds; structural poverty is forged by education gaps, weak job creation, uneven land distribution and regional imbalance. No single punch dismantles it.

## The dividends and myths of celebrity politics

Pacquiao's drawing power is beyond dispute — he remains one of the sports figures Filipinos are proudest of. Celebrity-to-politics conversions are common here, with fame routinely converted into votes. But the halo is also a myth: when poverty relief is framed as a comeback story, the public may overrate individual will and underrate institutional blockages. The commission must deliver policy, not box-office.

## What the anti-poverty commission can actually do

As a coordinator, the commission's job is to link scattered poverty programmes, evaluate them and advise. It can push for better data, target the most vulnerable and prod local governments to execute. Its leverage is limited, though: what truly moves the poverty rate is the agriculture department's rice subsidy, the education department's human-capital spend and the industry department's job creation. Advocacy alone will not change the equation.

## Marcos's calculus

Why now, and why Pacquiao? For Marcos, appointing a national icon to lead poverty work scores points on a stubborn issue while absorbing a potential electoral variable into the system. Pacquiao was once close to Duterte and a 2022 presidential contender; bringing him into the cabinet is as much about accommodation as co-option. Co-opting a star is rarely purely altruistic.

## The opponent is not a man

Pacquiao says he wants to help compatriots "going through hardship" because he has felt it himself. The empathy is genuine. But poverty is a bout with no opponent in the ring — it hides in farms after a typhoon, in Manila's fringe shacks, in remittances eaten by inflation. The champion can lift morale; he cannot throw the punch for the system.

## A fight destined to be uphill

Pacquiao's contest with poverty will be far harder than any "fight of the century." He can bring attention, push coordination and speak for the vulnerable; pulling 9.7% down depends on cross-department reform, not charisma. The boy who once saved every coin to feed his family now holds a cabinet seat instead of gloves — and the Philippines' poverty, precisely, fears fame the least.

## Further reading

**National Anti-Poverty Commission (NAPC)**: The Philippine coordinating body for the social-reform agenda, established in 1993, linking government agencies, local governments and grassroots anti-poverty organisations.

**Poverty incidence**: Measured by whether household per-capita spending falls below the official threshold; 9.7% in 2025, leaving about 11.08 million Filipinos below the line.

**Celebrity politics**: The direct conversion of entertainment or sporting fame into electoral capital, common in the Philippines and parts of Latin America, carrying both mobilising dividends and governance risks.

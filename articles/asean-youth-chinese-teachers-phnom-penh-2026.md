# 近300名东盟青年中文教师金边集训：当中文教育遇上人工智能

10月20日，2026年东盟本土青年中文教师能力提升训练营在柬埔寨金边开营。这场由北京语言大学出版社与柬埔寨东盟外国语学院联合主办、为期两天的集训，吸引了近300名当地青年中文教师参加，主题直指「HSK3.0与人工智能驱动下的国际中文教育创新」。从副首相大臣助理到使馆秘书，从北京语言大学教授到中央财经大学学者，一个看似普通的师资培训班，折射出中文教育在东南亚正在发生的两重转向——本土化与数智化。

## 背景：中文人才需求随中国—东盟合作水涨船高

柬埔寨副首相兼教育、青年和体育部大臣助理黄子扬在开营致辞中说得很直白：随着中国与东盟国家合作不断深化，中文人才需求日益增长，本土中文教师在中文教育发展和人文交流中发挥着重要作用。这句话不是客套。中国与东盟已互为最大贸易伙伴，2025年双边贸易额突破1.3万亿美元；经贸往来越密，对懂中文、懂当地的复合型人才的需求就越刚性。中文不再只是「文化课」，而是实打实的就业与晋升筹码。

## 数字：近300名本土教师，两天密集培训

数字本身就能说明问题。近300名参训教师，全部是「当地青年中文教师」——注意这个定语，他们不是中国派去的志愿者，而是柬埔寨本国正在一线授课的师资。两天时间，围绕国际中文教育新标准、HSK3.0及人工智能在教学中的应用，北京语言大学郑艳群、王艳与中央财经大学李春玲三位教授带来数智化教学理念、教师数字素养提升与教学资源开发等专题。规模不大，却精准——它把资源直接投到了「最后一公里」的课堂执行者身上。

## HSK3.0与人工智能：中文教学的新坐标

本次训练营的主题把两个关键词并置：HSK3.0与人工智能。前者是中文水平等级标准的一次重构，更强调语言运用能力与跨文化交际；后者正在重塑「教」与「学」的形态。训练营专门设置数智化教学理念与实践、教师数字素养提升及教学资源开发等模块，目的很明确——让一线教师不只是被动接受新标准，而是主动把人工智能工具嵌进本土课堂。这背后是一个朴素判断：标准再好，落不到教师手里就是纸上谈兵；而人工智能的价值，恰恰在于降低优质教学资源的边际成本。

## 谁在教、谁在学：本土化与数字化的双重转向

值得注意的是主办方的组合：北京语言大学出版社提供《国际中文教育中文水平等级标准》配套资源与教师发展系列图书，并在开营当天举行共建书架落成仪式。这意味着资源下沉到了当地、沉淀到了机构，而非一次性的「送教上门」。更重要的是，参训主体是「本土」教师——国际中文教育的重心，正从「中国老师走出去」转向「培养当地老师留下来」。这与东南亚多国近年华文教育回暖、师资短缺的现实高度契合：与其长期依赖外派，不如把能力建在本地。

## 观察：软实力不必宏大，落地即是

中国驻柬埔寨大使馆一等秘书王辉把这场训练营称为「深化中柬教育交流、推动中文教学与数字教育合作的具体实践」。这个定性很准确。当下谈论「软实力」时，人们容易盯着孔子学院、大型文化节这类显眼工程；但真正可持续的软实力，往往藏在这些不起眼的师资培训里。一个被培训过的本地教师，会影响一届又一届学生；一座共建书架，会比一次访问留下更长的余温。中文教育的数智化转身，说到底是一场关于「谁能把知识留下」的竞争——而这次，金边给出了一个安静却扎实的答案。

## 延伸阅读

- **HSK（Hanyu Shuiping Kaoshi）**：汉语水平考试，中文作为第二语言的标准化能力测评体系；HSK3.0为2021年后逐步推行的等级标准修订框架，更强调实际语言运用。
- **国际中文教育（International Chinese Language Education）**：面向非母语者的中文教学事业，近年从「对外汉语」转向强调本土化、职业化与数字化。
- **本土化师资（Localised Teaching Force）**：指在对象国就地培养、长期使用的中文教师群体，被视为破解外派依赖与师资短缺的关键路径。

# Nearly 300 ASEAN youth Chinese teachers train in Phnom Penh as Chinese education meets AI

On 20 October 2026, the ASEAN Local Youth Chinese Language Teachers Capacity-Building Training Camp opened in Phnom Penh, Cambodia. Co-hosted by Beijing Language and Culture University Press and the Cambodia ASEAN Foreign Languages Institute, the two-day camp drew nearly 300 local young Chinese-language teachers under the theme "Innovation in International Chinese Language Education Driven by HSK 3.0 and Artificial Intelligence." From a deputy prime minister's aide to an embassy secretary, from a Beijing Language and Culture University professor to a Central University of Finance and Economics scholar, an otherwise ordinary teacher-training workshop reveals two shifts now underway in Chinese-language education across Southeast Asia — localization and digitalisation.

## The backdrop: Chinese-language talent rides the China–ASEAN wave

Huang Ziyang, assistant to Cambodia's Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Education, Youth and Sport, was blunt in his opening remarks: as cooperation between China and ASEAN deepens, demand for Chinese-language talent keeps rising, and local Chinese teachers play a vital role in language education and people-to-people exchange. This is no polite boilerplate. China and ASEAN are each other's largest trading partners, with two-way trade surpassing 1.3 trillion US dollars in 2025. The denser the economic ties, the more rigid the demand for bilingual, locally grounded talent. Chinese is no longer merely a "culture class" — it is a tangible credential for employment and advancement.

## The numbers: nearly 300 teachers, two intensive days

The numbers tell the story. Nearly 300 participants, all described as "local young Chinese-language teachers" — note the qualifier: they are not volunteers dispatched from China but Cambodian educators already teaching on the front line. Over two days, centred on the new international Chinese-education standards, HSK 3.0 and the use of AI in teaching, three professors — Zheng Yanqun and Wang Yan from Beijing Language and Culture University and Li Chunling from the Central University of Finance and Economics — delivered modules on digital teaching concepts, teachers' digital-literacy upgrading and instructional-resource development. The scale is modest but precise: it puts resources directly into the hands of the classroom practitioners at the "last mile."

## HSK 3.0 and AI: new coordinates for Chinese teaching

The camp's theme pairs two keywords: HSK 3.0 and artificial intelligence. The former reconfigures the Chinese proficiency grading standard, stressing practical language use and cross-cultural communication; the latter is reshaping the very shape of teaching and learning. The camp deliberately builds in modules on digital teaching concepts and practice, teachers' digital-literacy upgrading, and resource development — the aim is clear: to let front-line teachers not merely receive the new standard passively but actively embed AI tools into local classrooms. Behind this lies a simple judgment: the best standard is useless if it never reaches teachers' hands, and AI's real value lies in lowering the marginal cost of quality teaching resources.

## Who teaches, who learns: the twin turn to localization and digitalisation

Worth noting is the organising combination: Beijing Language and Culture University Press supplied supporting resources for the "International Chinese Language Education Chinese Proficiency Grading Standards" and a teacher-development book series, and on opening day held a ceremony for a co-built bookshelf. This means resources sink into the local context and settle within institutions, rather than a one-off "teaching delivered to the doorstep." More importantly, the participants are "local" teachers — the centre of gravity in international Chinese education is shifting from "Chinese teachers going out" to "training local teachers who stay." This fits squarely with the recent rebound in Chinese-medium education across Southeast Asia against a backdrop of teacher shortages: rather than long-term reliance on expatriates, build capacity on the ground.

## The takeaway: soft power need not be grand — grounded is enough

Wang Hui, first secretary at the Chinese Embassy in Cambodia, called the camp "a concrete practice for deepening China–Cambodia educational exchange and advancing cooperation in Chinese teaching and digital education." The characterisation is apt. When we discuss "soft power" today, attention easily fixes on the visible projects — Confucius Institutes, large cultural festivals. But the truly durable soft power often hides in these unremarkable teacher trainings. A trained local teacher influences cohort after cohort of students; a co-built bookshelf leaves a warmer afterglow than a single visit. The "digital" turn in Chinese-language education is, at bottom, a contest over who can leave knowledge behind — and this time, Phnom Penh has offered a quiet yet solid answer.

## Further reading

- **HSK (Hanyu Shuiping Kaoshi)**: the standardised proficiency test for Chinese as a second language; HSK 3.0 is the revised grading framework rolled out after 2021, stressing practical language use.
- **International Chinese Language Education**: the enterprise of teaching Chinese to non-native speakers, recently shifting from "teaching Chinese to foreigners" toward localization, professionalization and digitalisation.
- **Localised Teaching Force**: the cohort of Chinese-language teachers cultivated and retained within the host country, seen as the key to overcoming expatriate dependence and teacher shortages.

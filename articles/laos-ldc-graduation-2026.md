# 老挝“毕业”了，优惠开始退场：最不发达国家身份之后的三道题

## 日经亚洲的一周提醒：有人如期离场，有人申请延期

据《日经亚洲》（Nikkei Asia）11月22日报道，老挝于本周二正式脱离联合国最不发达国家（LDC）名单；原本同期“毕业”的尼泊尔与孟加拉国，则各自争取到三年延期。日经把这件事放进“亚洲一周”日历，语气平淡，含义却不轻：脱离名单意味着与身份挂钩的贸易与援助优惠逐步退场，而韧性、生产率这些老问题，不会因为一张证书自动解决。点评一句，有的国家把毕业写成庆典，有的国家把毕业写成缓期执行——差别不在面子，在于有没有准备好接住账。

## 三项指标达标，不等于脱困

老挝财政部与联合国开发计划署（UNDP）早前联合发布会确认，老挝连续三次三年期审查均达到毕业门槛：人均国民总收入（GNI）1996美元（约1.44万元人民币），高于1305美元（约0.94万元人民币）的门槛；人力资产指数72.8，高于66的底线；经济与环境脆弱性指数也过关，平均通胀率7.8%，汇率基本稳住。这是几十年的成绩，值得写在墙上。但门槛是静态的，脆弱性是动态的：矿价、电价、外部需求任何一项波动，都能把刚跨过去的国家推回原位。毕业证明的是“曾经达标”，不是“从此安全”。

## 优惠退坡：从免关税到靠竞争力

真正的账单在第一道海关。按照欧盟“除武器外全部免关税”（EBA）安排，老挝有三年过渡期，到2029年为止；2030年起，对欧出口与其他发展中国家同等待遇，免税免配额通道关闭。纺织服装与鞋类首当其冲——这些行业利润薄、订单敏感，关税差几个百分点，就足以让采购经理把订单转去别处。欧盟方面已把合作重点转向“全球门户”框架下的绿色、数字、能源与技能培训，并配套2021至2027年8.25亿欧元（约67亿元人民币）的团队欧洲计划。翻译成人话：优惠在收，项目在谈，但项目要拿竞争力换。

## 11.7亿美元的出口敞口落在哪里

联合国贸易和发展会议（UNCTAD）《2025年贸易优惠展望》做过测算：若失去最不发达国家专属优惠，老挝出口或减少约11.671亿美元（约84亿元人民币），相当于其出口总额的12.8%；其受惠市场的优惠覆盖率高达82.2%。更值得注意的是敞口方向：按模拟结果，出口下降约43.2%来自中国市场，36.2%来自泰国，17.4%来自欧盟。这说明老挝的风险不是抽象的“欧美关税”，而是区域供应链里的规则重排——它在中国的农产品准入、在泰国的加工与转运，一旦优惠身份消失、原产地与检验检疫规则收紧，成本会沿着最短的通道传导回来。

## 铁路把陆锁国变成通道，也加深了依赖

万象把中老铁路视为“陆锁国”转“陆联国”的关键抓手，第十个国家社会经济发展计划（2026—2030年）又把自主经济、人力资源与民生放在核心位置。通道确实改变了地理：香蕉、木薯、咖啡能更快抵达中国西南市场，物流时间从公路的六到八天压缩到四天以内。但通道同时是一种依赖——市场集中于一个邻国，铁路运价、口岸效率与检疫规则都成了新的“关税”。毕业之后，老挝需要的不只是更多班列，更是能在班列上卖得出价钱的产品：标准、品牌、加工深度。否则所谓陆联国，只是把出海口换成了别人的口岸。

## 融资缺口比荣誉更急迫

11月20日的年度圆桌会议讨论的是2027年发展融资，议题很实在：官方发展援助格局变化，老挝必须拓宽融资渠道、提高国内资源动员能力。此前国际货币基金组织已把老挝债务问题定性为不可持续，公共债务、中小企业融资与就业创造被列为必须处理的三件事。毕业的悖论就在这里：收入指标刚刚过线，融资成本却可能因为身份变化而上浮；援助方从赠款转向贷款，贷款又要求项目有现金流。一个内陆小国的升级，不能靠把“最不发达”四个字摘掉来完成，只能靠把财政的分子分母同时做实。

## 7.8%通胀与发育迟缓：被平均数遮住的账单

世界粮食计划署（WFP）已批准2027至2031年对老挝国别战略计划，预算9830万美元（约7.1亿元人民币），重点是粮食安全、营养、气候冲击与社会保障。五岁以下儿童发育迟缓比例从2000年的接近一半降到如今约三分之一，这是实打实的进步；但还有三分之一，且地区差异明显。7.8%的平均通胀同样是一层薄薄的安慰：城市物价、山区交通、湄公河流域旱涝，都会让同一个数字落在不同家庭身上给出完全不同的答案。平均数越漂亮，越要问它把谁藏了起来。毕业叙事里最容易被剪掉的，是那些没有出现在人均指标里的家庭。

## 华商与区域合作：桥梁，不是豁免

在老挝的华人商户与中资企业，是这条转型链上最直接的参与者。他们修铁路、做口岸、种香蕉、开加工厂，也在当地纳税、雇工。毕业之后，他们面对的规则不会更松：原产地证明、劳工标准、环保合规、融资凭证，都会比以前更细。把侨商当成“特殊通道”的想法既过时也危险；更现实的角色是桥梁——把中国的市场与标准、东盟的规则与老挝的产业需求接起来，同时让当地供应链真正留下增加值。中老铁路若只运货不育人，通道红利会停留在账面；若能带动本地技工、仓储与金融，毕业才有产业支撑。

## 三问决定“毕业”成色

第一问：三年过渡期里，纺织服装企业能不能完成从免税依赖到按规则竞争的转换？第二问：融资结构能不能从赠款、优惠贷款转向可偿债的项目现金流，同时不把公共债务推得更高？第三问：铁路与口岸带来的贸易增量，能不能变成就业、技能和本地增加值，而不是只把农产品更快运出去？三个问题都有答案，毕业才是终点线；没有答案，它只是优惠退坡的起跑线。老挝把2035年迈入中高收入国家行列写进规划，这是一条比“毕业”更长、也更难的路。面具可以摘，肌肉得练。

## 延伸阅读

**最不发达国家毕业（LDC graduation）**：联合国按人均国民总收入、人力资产指数与经济环境脆弱性指数三项标准定期审查，连续达标即可脱离名单，通常伴随三年过渡期。

**除武器外全部免关税（Everything But Arms, EBA）**：欧盟给予最不发达国家的免税免配额安排，毕业后经过渡期终止，纺织服装与鞋类最敏感。

**陆联国（Land-linked country）**：老挝对“陆锁国”的政策表述，强调以铁路、口岸与物流通道把内陆区位转化为区域互联互通优势。

# Laos graduates, preferences fade: the real test after LDC exit

## Nikkei Asia's weekly reminder: one leaves on schedule, two ask for more time

According to Nikkei Asia on 22 November, Laos formally left the United Nations' least developed country (LDC) list this Tuesday. Nepal and Bangladesh, due to graduate in the same round, each secured a three-year extension. Nikkei filed the item in its "week in Asia" calendar in matter-of-fact tones, but the substance is not light: graduation strips away trade and aid preferences tied to that status, while old problems — resilience, productivity — are not solved by a certificate. Some countries turn graduation into a celebration; others into a reprieve. The difference lies not in dignity but in whether anyone has prepared to catch the bill.

## Passing three thresholds is not the same as escaping vulnerability

A joint briefing by the Ministry of Finance and the UN Development Programme (UNDP) confirmed that Laos met the graduation criteria in three consecutive triennial reviews: gross national income (GNI) per head of US$1,996, above the US$1,305 threshold; a Human Assets Index of 72.8 against a floor of 66; and a passing score on economic and environmental vulnerability. Average inflation was 7.8% and the exchange rate broadly held. That is a decades-long achievement and should be recorded as such. Yet thresholds are static and vulnerability is not: a swing in mineral prices, electricity tariffs or external demand can push a freshly graduated economy back towards the edge. Graduation certifies that a country once qualified. It does not certify that it is safe.

## Preferences fade: from duty-free to competitiveness

The bill arrives at the first customs post. Under the EU's Everything But Arms (EBA) scheme, Laos has a three-year transition ending in 2029; from 2030 its exports to Europe face the same treatment as other developing economies, and duty-free, quota-free access disappears. Garments and footwear are exposed first: thin margins and mobile orders mean a few percentage points of tariff can move a purchase order elsewhere. Brussels has shifted cooperation towards green and digital transition, energy, skills and agriculture under Global Gateway, backed by €825 million (roughly US$900 million) under Team Europe for 2021-2027. Plain translation: the preferences are being withdrawn while the projects are being negotiated — and the projects must be earned through competitiveness.

## Where the US$1.17 billion export exposure lands

UNCTAD's Trade Preferences Outlook 2025 simulated the loss of LDC-specific preferences for Laos at about US$1.167 billion, equivalent to 12.8% of total exports, against a preferential coverage ratio of 82.2% in beneficiary markets. More telling is the direction: of the modelled export decline, 43.2% falls on China, 36.2% on Thailand and 17.4% on the European Union. The risk is therefore not an abstract "Western tariff" but a reordering of rules inside regional supply chains. Market access for Lao farm products in China, processing and transhipment in Thailand — once the preference status is gone and rules of origin and sanitary checks tighten, costs travel back along the shortest corridor.

## The railway turned a landlocked state into a corridor, and deepened dependence

Vientiane treats the China-Laos Railway as the instrument for moving from a landlocked to a land-linked country, and its tenth National Socio-Economic Development Plan (2026-2030) puts self-reliance, human resources and livelihoods at the centre. The corridor did change geography: bananas, cassava and coffee reach southwestern China faster, with transit down from six to eight days by road to under four. But a corridor is also a dependency. With one neighbour absorbing much of the market, freight tariffs, border efficiency and quarantine rules become the new tariffs. After graduation Laos needs more than trains; it needs goods that command a price aboard them — standards, brands, processing depth. Otherwise a land-linked country has merely swapped a seaport for somebody else's border post.

## A financing gap more urgent than the honour

The annual round table on 20 November was about 2027 development finance, and its agenda was blunt: as official aid patterns shift, Laos must broaden financing channels and mobilise domestic resources. The IMF has already judged Lao debt unsustainable; public debt, SME finance and job creation were named as tasks that cannot be deferred. Here is the paradox of graduation: income per head has just crossed the line, yet financing costs may rise with the new status; donors move from grants to loans, and loans demand projects with cash flow. A small inland economy cannot upgrade by deleting four words from its description. It has to make both sides of the fiscal ledger real.

## 7.8% inflation and stunting: the bill hidden by averages

The World Food Programme (WFP) has approved a US$98.3 million country strategic plan for Laos covering 2027-2031, focused on food security, nutrition, climate shocks and social protection. Stunting among children under five has fallen from nearly half in 2000 to about a third today — real progress, but a third remains, with sharp regional differences. Average inflation of 7.8% is an equally thin comfort: urban prices, upland transport and floods and droughts in the Mekong basin make the same number produce very different answers in different households. The more flattering the average, the more urgently one should ask whom it conceals. Graduation narratives tend to edit out the families who never appear in per-capita indicators.

## Chinese businesses and regional cooperation: bridges, not exemptions

Chinese merchants and firms in Laos are the most direct participants in this transition. They build railways, operate border facilities, grow bananas and run processing plants — and they pay local taxes and hire local workers. After graduation the rules they face will not loosen: proofs of origin, labour standards, environmental compliance and financing documentation all grow more detailed. Treating overseas Chinese business as a "special channel" is both dated and dangerous. Its realistic role is that of a bridge — connecting Chinese markets and standards, ASEAN rules and Lao industrial needs, while leaving value added inside local supply chains. If the railway only moves goods and never trains people, the corridor dividend stays on paper; if it builds local skills, warehousing and finance, graduation has an industrial base.

## Three questions that determine the quality of graduation

First, during the three-year transition, can garment and footwear firms shift from dependence on exemption to competition on rules? Second, can the financing mix move from grants and concessional loans towards debt-serviceable project cash flow without pushing public debt higher? Third, can trade growth through rail and border posts become jobs, skills and local value added rather than simply moving farm goods out faster? Only with answers to all three is graduation a finish line; without them it is a starting gun for preference withdrawal. Laos has written reaching upper-middle-income status by 2035 into its plan — a longer and harder road than graduation. The mask can come off; the muscle still has to be built.

## Further reading

**LDC graduation**: The UN reviews countries periodically against three criteria — GNI per head, the Human Assets Index and the Economic and Environmental Vulnerability Index. Sustained compliance permits exit from the list, usually with a three-year transition.

**Everything But Arms (EBA)**: The EU's duty-free, quota-free scheme for least developed countries. It ends after a transition following graduation, with garments and footwear the most sensitive sectors.

**Land-linked country**: Laos's policy reframing of "landlocked", using rail, border posts and logistics corridors to turn inland geography into a regional connectivity advantage.

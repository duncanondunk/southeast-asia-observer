# 从"落叶归根"到"落地生根"：印尼华裔总会换届背后的身份之变

8月7日，印度尼西亚华裔总会（INTI, Perhimpunan Indonesia Tionghoa）新一届理监事会在雅加达就职，黄印华（Huang Yinhua / Indra Wahidin）履新2026—2030年度总主席。他在受访时一句"过去讲落叶归根，现在更多讲落地生根"，被媒体反复引用。这句话背后，是印尼——这个全球华侨华人人口最多的国家——华人身份认同数十年的静默变迁，也折射出整个东南亚华人社会正在经历的代际转向。

## 一句被反复引用的话

黄印华说："华人还是华人，但随着中国的发展和国际影响力的提升，我们更有底气了。"这其中的"底气"二字，恰是理解新一代印尼华人心态变化的钥匙。它不再是一种防御性的、需要自证的身份，而是一种可以更坦然地同时持有"印尼国民"与"华裔"双重身份的底气。

## 历史底色：1998年之后

INTI成立于1999年，正值苏哈托（Suharto）威权统治结束、印尼步入民主化转型之际。其成立的现实背景，正是对1998年排华事件（1998 anti-Chinese riots）后社会需求的回应——推动华族作为印尼民族平等一员的合法地位与社会融合。在此之前，华人长期面临歧视性法律与强制同化政策，华文教育被禁、文化表达受限，"成为印尼人"与"保有华人身份"之间长期处于紧张状态。

## 从维权到融入：社团角色的演变

27年间，INTI的角色重心发生了位移：从最初争取平等权益、恢复华文教育与传承中华文化，逐步转向参与主流社会建设。它如今在印尼18个省、80余个市县设有分会。黄印华任内强调"对内加强团结，对外发挥桥梁纽带作用"。该会曾在2004年亚齐海啸（2004 Aceh tsunami）、2025年苏门答腊洪灾（2025 Sumatra floods）等灾害后积极参与赈济，并设立"彩虹奖学金"等公益项目，社会公信力稳步提升。一个耐人寻味的细节是：春节（Spring Festival）如今已成为印尼全民共庆的节日，今年印尼政府还首次在国家层面举办春节庆典——这正是华人融入主流社会的生动缩影。

## 中国崛起带来的"底气"

"底气"二字值得玩味。过去华人讲"落叶归根"，是把中国当作精神原乡、迟早要归去终老之乡；如今讲"落地生根"，是在承认并拥抱印尼国家认同的前提下，以更松弛的姿态保有自身的文化与族裔身份。中国数十年来的发展，让这种双重身份不再是一种需要隐藏的"负担"，而成为一种可被正向调用的资源——这恰恰是黄印华所说的"更有底气"。它既不是割裂的"两个祖国"，也不是非此即彼的取舍，而是华人得以在两者之间从容穿行的能力。

## 桥，而非单向道

INTI长期扮演中印尼（China–Indonesia）之间的民间桥梁：组织夏令营、冬令营，累计带领数千名印尼华裔及非华裔青年赴华参访；推动华裔青年寻根问祖；在经贸、人文与青年交流上牵线搭桥。中国驻印尼大使王鲁彤（Wang Lutong）也肯定其在促进两国民心相通中的作用。但需要强调的是，这座桥是双向的——它既让更多印尼民众亲眼看见真实的中国，也通过华人的社会参与，增进了印尼主流社会对华人的理解与认同。"桥梁"的真正价值，从来不在于单向输出，而在于双向互信的累积。

## 接棒的年轻人

年逾七旬的黄印华，走马上任后最牵挂的仍是青年接棒。"华人社团眼下青黄不接，必须鼓励年轻一代加入。"这不仅是INTI面临的难题，也是全球老牌侨社普遍遭遇的代际挑战。当"落叶归根"的旧叙事逐渐退场，"落地生根"的新认同要靠年轻人自己书写——他们既是印尼的建设者，也是连接中国与世界的重要节点。

## 延伸阅读

**印尼华裔总会（INTI / Perhimpunan Indonesia Tionghoa）**：1999年成立于雅加达的全国性华人社团，宗旨为维护华裔公民合法权益、推动华文教育与族群和谐，现于印尼18个省、80余个市县设有分会。

**1998年排华事件（1998 anti-Chinese riots）**：苏哈托政权末期印尼爆发的大规模反华暴力事件，成为华人争取平等地位、推动社会融合的重要历史转折点，INTI即在此后成立。

**落叶归根与落地生根（Luoye Guigen & Luodi Shenggen）**：华侨华人身份认同的两类隐喻——前者指心向故土、终老祖籍国，后者指在接受国安家立业、融入当地，常被用以概括战后东南亚华人的代际心态转变。

# From "returning to the roots" to "taking root locally": the quiet shift in Indonesia's ethnic-Chinese identity

On 7 August 2026, the new board of the Indonesian Chinese Association (INTI, Perhimpunan Indonesia Tionghoa) was inaugurated in Jakarta, with Huang Yinhua (Indra Wahidin) assuming the chairmanship for the 2026–2030 term. His remark in an interview — "In the past we spoke of falling leaves returning to their roots; now we speak more of taking root where we stand" — has been widely quoted. Behind that single sentence lies the slow, decades-long transformation of ethnic-Chinese identity in Indonesia, home to the world's largest overseas-Chinese population, and a generational turn now visible across Southeast Asian Chinese communities.

## A quote repeated for a reason

"Ethnic Chinese are still ethnic Chinese," Huang said, "but with China's development and its rising international stature, we feel more confident (youdiqi) today." That word — confidence — is the key to understanding the shifting mindset of a new generation of Indonesian Chinese. It is no longer a defensive identity that must be justified, but a confidence that allows one to hold, with greater ease, a dual identity as both Indonesian citizen and ethnic Chinese.

## The historical backdrop: after 1998

INTI was founded in 1999, at the dawn of Indonesia's democratic transition after the fall of Suharto. Its creation answered a concrete social need in the wake of the 1998 anti-Chinese riots: to advance the legal status of ethnic Chinese as equal citizens and to promote social integration. For decades before, Chinese Indonesians had lived under discriminatory laws and forced-assimilation policies that banned Chinese-language education and constrained cultural expression, leaving the relationship between "being Indonesian" and "being ethnically Chinese" in a state of chronic tension.

## From rights-defense to integration: the evolving role of the association

Over 27 years, INTI's center of gravity has shifted: from defending equal rights, restoring Chinese-language education and preserving heritage, toward active participation in mainstream nation-building. It now operates branches in 18 provinces and more than 80 regencies and cities. Huang has stressed "internal unity and an external bridging role." The association joined relief efforts after the 2004 Aceh tsunami and the 2025 Sumatra floods, and runs the "Rainbow Scholarship" and other public-welfare projects, steadily raising its credibility. A telling detail: the Spring Festival (Chunjie / Chinese New Year) has become a holiday celebrated across Indonesian society, and this year the government held its first-ever national-level Spring Festival celebration — a vivid emblem of integration.

## The "confidence" brought by China's rise

The word youdiqi deserves attention. Where earlier generations "returned to the roots," treating China as a spiritual homeland to retire to, today's "taking root locally" means holding onto cultural and ethnic identity with greater ease, on the premise of an accepted Indonesian national identity. China's development over recent decades has turned this dual identity from a liability to be hidden into a resource that can be drawn upon positively — precisely the "greater confidence" Huang describes. It is neither a split "two fatherlands" nor an either-or choice, but the capacity to move between the two with composure.

## A bridge, not a one-way street

INTI has long served as a people-to-people bridge between China and Indonesia (China–Indonesia). It organizes summer and winter camps that have taken thousands of Indonesian youth — ethnic Chinese and non-Chinese alike — to visit China, encourages young people to explore their ancestry, and brokers ties in trade, culture and youth exchange. China's ambassador to Indonesia, Wang Lutong, has praised its role in fostering mutual understanding. But the bridge runs both ways: it lets more Indonesians see the real China, and, through ethnic Chinese social participation, deepens mainstream Indonesian society's understanding of and identification with its own ethnic-Chinese citizens. The true value of a "bridge" has never lain in one-way transmission, but in the accretion of two-way trust.

## The young people who must take the baton

In his seventies, Huang's foremost concern upon taking office remains succession. "Our Chinese associations face a generational gap; we must encourage the younger generation to join." This is not only INTI's dilemma but a common challenge for established diaspora organizations worldwide. As the old "returning to the roots" narrative recedes, the new "taking root locally" identity must be written by the young themselves — who are at once builders of Indonesia and vital nodes connecting China with the wider world.

## Further reading

**Indonesian Chinese Association (INTI / Perhimpunan Indonesia Tionghoa)**: A national ethnic-Chinese organization founded in Jakarta in 1999 to defend citizens' rights, promote Chinese-language education and ethnic harmony; it now has branches in 18 provinces and over 80 regencies and cities.

**1998 anti-Chinese riots**: Widespread anti-Chinese violence at the end of the Suharto era, a historical turning point that galvanized ethnic Chinese to claim equal status and pursue social integration; INTI was founded in its aftermath.

**"Returning to the roots" and "taking root locally" (Luoye Guigen & Luodi Shenggen)**: Two metaphors for overseas-Chinese identity — the former looks homeward to an ancestral homeland, the latter to settling and integrating in the country of residence; together they capture the generational shift among Southeast Asian Chinese after World War II.

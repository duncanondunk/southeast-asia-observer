# 中国电动车改写东南亚车市版图：三季度销量重回80万辆

东南亚车市在2026年第三季度终于喘过一口气。据《日经亚洲》（Nikkei Asia）统计，印尼、泰国、越南、马来西亚、菲律宾五大核心市场7至9月合计售出828172辆，同比增长13%，是自2025年第四季度以来首个重回80万辆关口的季度。表面看是复苏，细看却更有意思：这是一场结构性洗牌——中国电动车在快车道上抢位，而日本巨头退守到它们仍占主导的混合动力"软垫"上。

## 数字：是复苏，但不是旧模样

回暖是真实的，但驱动力变了。这不是过去几十年那种传统燃油车的周期性反弹。电动化车型——纯电与混动——托起了这个季度。泰国前9个月乘用车纯电注册量大涨94%，接近16.8万辆；混动上涨24%，超过13万辆。马来西亚电动车注册量近乎翻倍，达53537辆。大盘整体疲软的菲律宾，其纯电、插电混动与混动合计销量仍激增163%至54372辆。就连体量小却富裕的新加坡，前9个月新注册车辆中纯电也已占63%。信号很直白：区域消费者用钱包投票，选的是更低的使用成本和更少的机械部件，而不是回归旧有的燃油秩序。

## 中国的抢位：价低与本地建厂

中国车企是显而易见的获益者。7月底至8月初雅加达附近的印尼国际车展（GIIAS）上，中国品牌拿下超过2.1万辆订单；比亚迪（BYD）一家就超过4000辆，仅次于丰田（Toyota）的6175辆。整个季度，丰田批发63877辆、大发（Daihatsu）38897辆，而比亚迪的20470辆让它成为这个东南亚最大市场里最畅销的中国品牌。比亚迪的打法很好读：把价格压到普通家庭够得着的区间。其本地生产的七座纯电多用途车（MPV）车型M6，起售价3.95亿印尼盾（约合2.2万美元，约15.8万元人民币），仅8月就批发7870辆。关键不在单一车型，而在策略——本地组装、激进定价，以及精准对准日本品牌姗姗来迟的电动化细分市场。

## 日本的反击：混动守住基本盘

东京没有投降，而是退守高地。在泰国，前三季度中国品牌约占乘用车纯电注册量的90%，但日本企业仍掌握近98%的混动市场。在GIIAS，丰田超过一半订单来自本地组装的混动车型——卡罗拉·茵诺瓦混动（Kijang Innova Hybrid）、维洛兹混动（Veloz Hybrid）与雅力士Cross混动（Yaris Cross Hybrid），混动订单同比近乎翻倍。这套逻辑是防守，却很精明：日本的服务网络和保值稳定性，对赌不起的买家依然重要。对网约车司机和车队运营者来说，丰田混动仍是稳妥之选。中国赢了电动化的展示台，日本守住了可靠的基本盘。

## 泰国样本：一个市场的两半

泰国把这种分裂暴露得最彻底。该国三季度销量同比增长约20%，但市场实则已一分为二。中国品牌占据纯电通道，日本占据混动通道。曼谷一位29岁的房产中介买长安深蓝L07（Changan Deepal L07），看重泰国设有生产基地、售后跟得上；一位47岁的网约车司机买丰田雅力士Cross，图的是维修网点与低风险。同一个国家，两类消费部落，两条供应链。这不是一方一夜取代另一方的故事，而是一种共存：增长的细分易主，而销量基座未动。

## 印尼：车展上的中日正面对决

印尼是这场较量最显眼也最决定性的战场。作为东南亚最大汽车市场，它三季度售出242411辆，同比增长31%——全年最佳季度，8月单月81756辆更创年内新高。GIIAS车展充当了上半年积压订单的释放阀。雅加达对纯电车辆豁免"单双号"交通管制，给了纯电额外的推力，这是单靠价格给不了的。丰田营销高管川井修司（Shuji Kawai）承认，拉动买家转向电动车的是这项豁免，而不只是成本。中国读出了政策信号并就地本地化，日本读出同一信号却加码混动。如今对决在展台与税法上展开，而不只在马路上。

## 这轮复苏能走多远

此处该给乐观降温。13%的跳升严重依赖一次性催化：车展订单释放、交通豁免红利，以及"电比油便宜"的成本账。它还不是普惠式的收入增长。同一季度仅增长2.1%、被通胀拖累的菲律宾经济，暴露出区域底盘有多薄。底层数字也值得打个问号：《日经亚洲》的汇总未必披露同比基数与各读数背后的统计机构，因此这些百分比宜作方向性参考，而非金科玉律。更深层的是战略问题。中国品牌用价格与本地工厂抢占电动化的未来，日本在位者（incumbent）守住混动的当下。复苏能否延续，与其说取决于展台盛况（spectacle），不如说取决于被挤压的东南亚消费者是否继续选择电力。眼下版图正在重绘，但旧日的制图师仍在屋内。

## 延伸阅读

**纯电动车（Battery Electric Vehicle, BEV）**：仅由可充电电池驱动、无汽油机的汽车，正是中国品牌在东南亚领先的那一细分。

**混合动力车（Hybrid Electric Vehicle, HEV）**：汽油机与电动机协同以提升燃油经济性的车型，是日本车企在泰国近乎垄断的堡垒。

**本地化生产（Local Assembly）**：车辆在目标市场当地制造的比例，也是比亚迪印尼工厂与日本泰国混动产线的政策杠杆与竞争战场。

# China's EVs redraw Southeast Asia's auto map as regional Q3 sales cross 800,000

Southeast Asia's car market finally caught its breath in the third quarter of 2026. According to Nikkei Asia, the five core markets — Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia and the Philippines — sold a combined 828,172 vehicles between July and September, up 13% from a year earlier and the first quarter above the 800,000 mark since the final three months of 2025. The headline looks like a recovery. Look closer, and it is something more interesting: a structural reshuffle in which Chinese electric vehicles are taking the fast lane while Japan's giants fall back on the hybrid cushion they still dominate.

## The numbers: a recovery, but not the old kind

The rebound is real, but its driver is not. This was not the traditional combustion-cycle upturn of past decades. Electrified models — battery electrics and hybrids — carried the quarter. Thailand's passenger battery-electric registrations jumped 94% in the first nine months to nearly 168,000 units; hybrids rose 24% to over 130,000. Malaysia's EV registrations nearly doubled to 53,537. The Philippines, whose overall market stayed soft, still saw its combined BEV, plug-in and hybrid sales surge 163% to 54,372. Even Singapore — a small, rich market — now registers battery EVs for 63% of new cars in the first nine months. The message is blunt: the region's consumers are voting with their wallets for cheaper running costs and fewer moving parts, not for a return to the old petrol order.

## China's land grab: price and local plants

China's carmakers are the obvious beneficiaries. At Indonesia's GIIAS motor show near Jakarta in late July and early August, Chinese brands booked more than 21,000 orders; BYD alone took over 4,000, behind only Toyota's 6,175. For the quarter, Toyota wholesaled 63,877 units and Daihatsu 38,897, but BYD's 20,470 made it the best-selling Chinese nameplate in the region's largest market. BYD's playbook is easy to read: bring the price within reach of ordinary families. Its locally built seven-seat electric MPV, the M6, starts at 395 million rupiah (about US$22,000), and the brand wholesaled 7,870 units in August alone. The point is not one model. It is a strategy — local assembly, aggressive pricing, and a product mix aimed squarely at the electrified segment where Japanese brands arrived late.

## Japan's counterpunch: hybrids hold the line

Tokyo has not surrendered; it has retreated to higher ground. In Thailand, Chinese brands account for roughly 90% of passenger battery-electric registrations through nine months, yet Japanese firms still command nearly 98% of the hybrid market. At GIIAS, more than half of Toyota's orders came from locally assembled hybrids — the Kijang Innova Hybrid, Veloz Hybrid and Yaris Cross Hybrid — with hybrid orders nearly doubling year on year. The logic is defensive but shrewd: Japanese service networks and resale stability still matter to buyers who cannot afford a wrong bet. For ride-hail drivers and fleet operators, a Toyota hybrid remains the safe choice. China wins the electric showcase; Japan keeps the dependable backbone.

## The Thailand split: one market, two halves

Thailand exposes the divide most starkly. The country's Q3 sales rose about 20% year on year, yet the market has effectively bifurcated. Chinese brands own the battery-electric aisle; Japan owns the hybrid one. A 29-year-old property agent in Bangkok buys a Changan Deepal L07 because Thailand has a production base and the after-sales follows; a 47-year-old ride-hail driver buys a Toyota Yaris Cross for the repair network and low risk. Same country, two consumer tribes, two supply chains. This is not a story of one side replacing the other overnight. It is a coexistence in which the growth segment has changed hands while the volume base has not.

## Indonesia: the head-to-head at the show

Indonesia is where the contest is most visible and most decisive. As Southeast Asia's largest market, it sold 242,411 vehicles in the third quarter, up 31% — its best quarter of the year, with August hitting 81,756, a yearly high. The GIIAS show acted as a release valve for orders that had been pent up through a sluggish first half. Jakarta's exemption of battery EVs from its odd-even traffic restrictions gave pure electrics an extra nudge that price alone did not provide. Toyota's marketing executive Shuji Kawai conceded that the exemption, not just cost, was pulling buyers toward EVs. China reads the policy signal and localises; Japan reads the same signal and doubles down on hybrids. The duel is now fought on the show floor and in the tax code, not only on the road.

## How far can this recovery run

Here the optimism should be tempered. The 13% surge leans heavily on one-off catalysts: a motor show's order release, a traffic-exemption perk, and running-cost math that favors electrons over petrol. It is not yet broad-based income growth. The Philippine economy, growing just 2.1% in the same quarter as inflation bit, shows how thin the regional floor remains. And the underlying figures deserve a caveat: Nikkei's aggregation does not always disclose the prior-year base or the statistical agency behind each reading, so treat the percentages as directional rather than gospel. The bigger question is strategic. Chinese brands have used price and local plants to seize the electrified future; Japanese incumbents have guarded the hybrid present. Whether the recovery compounds or fades will depend less on show-floor spectacle and more on whether Southeast Asia's consumers, many of them squeezed, keep choosing electrons. For now, the map is being redrawn — but the old cartographers are still in the room.

## Further reading

**Battery electric vehicle (BEV)**: A pure-electric car drawing power only from a rechargeable battery, with no petrol engine — the segment where Chinese brands now lead Southeast Asia.

**Hybrid electric vehicle (HEV)**: A car pairing a petrol engine with an electric motor for better fuel economy; the stronghold where Japanese makers retain near-total dominance in Thailand.

**Local assembly**: The share of a vehicle produced within the destination market — the policy lever and competitive battleground behind BYD's Indonesian plant and Japan's Thai hybrid lines.

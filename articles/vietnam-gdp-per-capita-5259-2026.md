# 人均GDP达5259美元：越南十年接近翻倍，悄然改写东盟座次

据《日经亚洲》测算，越南2026年名义人均国内生产总值（GDP）预计将达5259美元（约3.79万元人民币），同比增8.9%，较十年前接近翻倍。更耐人寻味的是座次：这一数字已高于印尼2026年预计的5153美元（约3.71万元人民币），使越南在东盟人均GDP排位中升至第五，紧追泰国。换句话说，在总量赶超泰国之后，越南在「人均」这条更贴近百姓腰包的赛道上也翻了身。

## 数字：十年接近翻倍，东盟座次悄然重排

一份《日经亚洲》的测算，把越南的人均账摆到了台面上。2026年名义人均GDP（国内生产总值）预计5259美元（约3.79万元人民币），同比增8.9%，十年间接近翻倍。真正让区域观察者侧目的，是排位的微妙位移：这一数字已压过印尼同年的5153美元（约3.71万元人民币），让越南在东盟人均序列里升到第五，仅落后泰国。总量超车之后，人均也翻了身——这比任何口号都更能说明问题。

## 反超印尼：总量之后，人均也翻了身

印尼长期被列为东盟最大经济体之一，但体量巨大的常住人口摊薄了人均。越南以不到印尼三成的人口，撬动了更高的人均产出，这本身说明两国增长逻辑不同：一个靠资源与内需的庞大体量，一个靠制造与出口的产业链密度。日经亚洲点出这一点，恰恰提醒我们——看东南亚不能只看总量，人均才是一面更诚实的镜子。

## 三驾马车：公共投资、外资与出口

越南把这轮增长归结为三股力量：公共投资、外商直接投资（FDI）与出口。配方不算新鲜，但2026年的力度不同寻常。公共投资前9个月同比猛增46%；实际到位的外商直接投资（FDI）前9个月增长12%，超过210亿美元（约1512亿元人民币）；新注册的外商直接投资（FDI）更是飙升76%，达503亿美元（约3622亿元人民币）。三股力量叠加，构成了人均收入跃升的底盘。

## 公共投资：一年砸出46%的增幅

越南计划投资部的数字显示，公共投资正以前所未有的节奏落地。前9个月46%的增幅，意味着公路、机场与工业园区等基建在加速铺开。日本贸易振兴机构（IDE-JETRO）研究员藤田麻衣指出，政府加码的公共投资正在托举整体增长动能。基建不只是钢筋水泥，它压低物流成本、疏通产业配套，最终都会反映到企业与居民的账面收入上。

## 外资赌注：503亿美元新注册，三星富士康扩产

新注册的外商直接投资（FDI）冲到503亿美元（约3622亿元人民币），背后是跨国企业用脚投票。三星电子继续在越南维持大规模的智能手机与电子产品制造；富士康在北部加码产能；英飞凌等半导体企业也宣布扩大在越研发与制造。外资看中的不只是廉价劳力，更是越南日益完整的电子与制造配套网络——这正是人均GDP能持续抬升的产业根基。

## 出口引擎：4343亿美元，25%的增速

前9个月，越南出口额达到4343亿美元（约3.13万亿元人民币），同比增长25%。智能手机、电子零件与纺织品撑起出口骨架，主要买家是美国与区域市场。出口旺，工厂就旺；工厂旺，工资与就业就旺。人均收入的增长，说到底要靠一个个真实下发的订单来兑现，而非统计口径的游戏。

## 2030的标尺：8500美元，对标今日的泰国

越南给2030年设了一根标尺：人均GDP（国内生产总值）达到8500美元（约6.12万元人民币），相当于泰国当前的水平。从5259美元迈向8500美元，六年要再上一个大台阶，这意味着年均实际增速须维持高位，且不能只靠外部订单。国际货币基金组织（IMF）与亚洲开发银行（ADB）预计越南2027年增速约7.5%——亮眼，但要填平与泰国的人均鸿沟，仍是一场硬仗。

## 光环之下：被平均数掩盖的短板

人均翻倍的故事很提气，但平均数会骗人。越南的增长高度依赖外向型制造，国内增加值率偏低，贸易顺差背后是巨大的逆差与对中间品的依赖；电力短缺、技能错配与配套率不足，仍是悬在头顶的软肋。更关键的是，人均GDP（国内生产总值）衡量的是产出而非分配——收入上去了，普通家庭的获得感能否同步，是另一本账。

## 结语：人均翻倍不是终点

越南用十年把人均GDP（国内生产总值）做到接近翻倍，的确给东南亚上了一课：小体量也能靠产业链密度跑出加速度。但东盟的座次从来不是静止的，印尼在改革、泰国在爬坡、马来西亚在卡位。5259美元是一个值得记住的数字，却远不是终点。对越南而言，真正的考题是——如何让这场增长，惠及每一个在工厂与田埂间奔忙的人。

## 延伸阅读

**人均国内生产总值（GDP per capita）**：一国总产出除以常住人口，衡量「平均每人创造多少财富」，比总量更能反映普通人的生活水准。

**外商直接投资（FDI）**：外国企业在本国设厂、持股或并购形成的长期投资，是越南制造崛起的核心外部动力。

**中越关系（China–Vietnam Relations）**：越南最大贸易伙伴与重要外资来源之一，中国元素深度嵌入其供应链，是理解越南经济不可绕开的一维。

# Vietnam's Per-Capita GDP Hits $5,259: A Decade of Near-Doubling That Reshuffles ASEAN's Ladder

Nikkei Asia projects Vietnam's nominal GDP per capita at $5,259 in 2026, up 8.9% year on year and nearly double the level a decade ago. The more intriguing shift is in the ranking: that figure now exceeds Indonesia's 2026 forecast of $5,153, lifting Vietnam to fifth place in ASEAN by per-capita output, just behind Thailand. In other words, after overtaking Thailand in total GDP, Vietnam has also turned the tables on the more citizen-facing metric of per-capita income.

## The numbers: nearly doubled in a decade, ASEAN's ladder quietly reordered

A Nikkei Asia projection puts Vietnam's per-capita account on the table. Nominal GDP per capita is expected at $5,259 in 2026, up 8.9% year on year and nearly double a decade ago. What truly catches regional watchers is the subtle move in the ranking: the number has edged past Indonesia's $5,153 for the year, lifting Vietnam to fifth in ASEAN's per-capita sequence, just behind Thailand. After overtaking in total size, it has turned the tables on per-capita income too — more telling than any slogan.

## Overtaking Indonesia: after total GDP, now per capita too

Indonesia has long been counted among ASEAN's largest economies, but its huge resident population dilutes the per-head figure. Vietnam, with less than a third of Indonesia's people, has levered a higher per-capita output — a sign of two different growth logics: one built on the sheer scale of resources and domestic demand, the other on the density of manufacturing and export supply chains. Nikkei Asia's framing is a timely reminder that Southeast Asia cannot be read by totals alone; per capita is the more honest mirror.

## The three engines: public investment, foreign capital, exports

Vietnam attributes this round of growth to three forces: public investment, foreign direct investment (FDI) and exports. The recipe is not new, but the 2026 intensity is. Public investment jumped 46% in the first nine months; realised FDI rose 12% to over $21 billion; and registered FDI surged 76% to $50.3 billion. Together they form the base on which per-capita income is climbing.

## Public investment: a 46% year-on-year surge

Data from Vietnam's Ministry of Planning and Investment shows public spending landing at an unprecedented pace. The 46% nine-month increase means roads, airports and industrial parks are being rolled out faster. Mai Fujita of the Institute for Developing Economies (IDE-JETRO) notes that the government's stepped-up public investment is propping up overall growth momentum. Infrastructure is not just concrete; it lowers logistics costs and unclogs industrial linkages, and eventually shows up in the books of firms and households.

## The FDI bet: $50.3bn registered, Samsung and Foxconn expand

Registered FDI reached $50.3 billion, the result of multinationals voting with their feet. Samsung Electronics keeps large-scale smartphone and electronics manufacturing in the country; Foxconn is scaling capacity in the north; Infineon and others have announced expanded R&D and production. Foreign capital is drawn not merely by cheap labour but by Vietnam's increasingly complete electronics and manufacturing network — the industrial foundation on which per-capita GDP can keep rising.

## The export engine: $434.3bn, a 25% climb

In the first nine months, Vietnam's exports reached $434.3 billion, up 25% year on year. Smartphones, electronic components and textiles hold up the export skeleton, with the United States and regional markets the main buyers. Strong exports mean busy factories; busy factories mean wages and jobs. The rise in per-capita income, ultimately, is delivered by real purchase orders, not by statistical sleight of hand.

## The 2030 yardstick: $8,500, today's Thailand

Vietnam has set a marker for 2030: per-capita GDP at $8,500, the level Thailand sits at today. Moving from $5,259 to $8,500 means clearing another large step in six years, requiring high real annual growth and not merely external orders. The IMF and ADB expect Vietnam's 2027 growth at around 7.5% — impressive, but closing the per-capita gap with Thailand remains a hard fight.

## Under the halo: the gaps the average hides

The doubling story is encouraging, but averages deceive. Vietnam's growth leans heavily on outward manufacturing, with a low domestic value-added rate; behind the trade surplus lie a large deficit and dependence on intermediate goods. Power shortages, skill mismatches and weak local content remain soft underbellies. More importantly, GDP per capita measures output, not distribution — whether households feel the gain is a separate ledger.

## Afterword: doubling per capita is not the finish line

Vietnam's decade-long near-doubling of per-capita GDP does teach Southeast Asia a lesson: a smaller economy can still accelerate through supply-chain density. But ASEAN's ladder is never static — Indonesia is reforming, Thailand is climbing, Malaysia is positioning. $5,259 is a number worth remembering, yet far from the endpoint. For Vietnam, the real test is making this growth reach every person hustling between factory floor and rice paddy.

## Further reading

**GDP per capita**: A country's total output divided by its resident population; it measures "how much wealth the average person creates" and reflects living standards better than totals.

**Foreign direct investment (FDI)**: Long-term investment by foreign firms through local plants, equity or acquisitions; the core external engine behind Vietnam's manufacturing rise.

**China–Vietnam relations**: Vietnam's largest trade partner and a major source of capital, with Chinese elements deep in its supply chain — a dimension impossible to skip when reading the economy.

# 逆差八连月：越南的"工业化过路费"，还是警报？

越南（Vietnam）政府 8 月 3 日公布的数据让外界一惊：7 月该国连续第八个月录得贸易逆差，前七个月累计逆差达 205 亿美元（约 1470 亿元人民币），已超过 2008 年全年 180 亿美元的纪录。出口仍在涨，但进口涨得更猛——这既可以被读作厂房与设备的投入，也可能是一声关于结构脆弱的警报。

## 一个数字，两种读法

7 月越南出口 531 亿美元（约 3810 亿元人民币），同比增长 25%；进口增速却达 41.4%，高于市场预期的 36.7%，当月逆差扩大至 35.9 亿美元（约 258 亿元人民币）。前七个月，出口总额 3200 亿美元、同比增 21.7%，进口 3400 亿美元、大增 34.8%。表面看，这是企业扩产、囤积设备与原料的结果，并不一定等于竞争力走弱。

## "中国供料、越南组装、美国销售"

结构里藏着关键线索。前七个月，美国仍是越南最大出口市场，对美顺差 914 亿美元（约 6560 亿元人民币）、同比增 22.6%；中国则是最大进口来源，自华进口约 1386 亿美元（约 9950 亿元人民币）、同比增 39.7%。一条"中国供料、越南组装、美国销售"的链条清晰可见：越南赚的是组装与转口的差价，上游物料高度依赖外部采购。成品油进口金额暴涨 67.6%，更凸显能源与中间品对进口的依赖。

## 关税阴影下的脆弱平衡

这条链条正被外部力量拧紧。美国对转口贸易加征惩罚性关税、启动 301 调查，并叠加所谓强迫劳动相关关税，实际关税成本被推高；越南上半年新设外资中资占比已降至约 5.6%、排第四，退出市场的企业近 2.52 万家、几乎翻倍。当"原产地红利"被关税削减，此前火热的建设可能迅速降温。

## 本币与储备的隐忧

持续逆差会压迫本币与外汇储备。前七个月，越南国内经济部门逆差 285 亿美元，外商投资部门（含原油）顺差 79.8 亿美元——顺差几乎全部来自外资工厂，本土产业的承压更值得警惕。所幸制造业 PMI 仍在扩张，CPI 同比上涨 4.45%、接近全年约 4.5% 的目标，物价尚在可控区间。

## 尾声：过路费该怎么交

把逆差看作"工业化的过路费"有其道理：设备还在运、厂子还在盖，产能尚未完全释放。但坡有多陡、风向变不变，是另一回事。越南若想从"接收方"变为真正的"承接方"，需要的不是更多组装线，而是把配套做起来、把效率提上去。否则，今天的热建厂，明天或许就成了难收的账。

## 延伸阅读

- **贸易逆差（Trade Deficit）**：进口额大于出口额的差额；持续逆差可能施压本币与外汇储备。
- **301 调查（Section 301 Investigation）**：美国依据《1974 年贸易法》第 301 条对他国贸易行为发起的调查与制裁工具。
- **PMI（采购经理人指数）**：衡量制造业景气的前瞻指标，高于 50 通常代表扩张。

# Eight Months in the Red: Vietnam's "Toll of Industrialisation," or a Warning?

Figures from Vietnam's government on 3 August startled observers: July marked the eighth straight month of trade deficit, with a cumulative shortfall of US$20.5 billion in the first seven months — already past the full-year record of US$18.0 billion set in 2008. Exports are still rising, but imports are rising faster. That can be read either as investment in plants and equipment or as an early warning about structural fragility.

## One Number, Two Readings

In July, Vietnam exported US$53.1 billion (up 25% year on year) but imported at 41.4% growth — above the 36.7% consensus — pushing the monthly deficit to US$3.59 billion. Over seven months, exports reached US$320 billion (+21.7%) while imports jumped 34.8% to US$340 billion. On the surface, this reflects firms expanding capacity and stocking equipment and materials, not necessarily a loss of competitiveness.

## "China Supplies, Vietnam Assembles, America Sells"

The structure tells the story. In seven months the United States remained Vietnam's top export market, with a surplus of US$91.4 billion (+22.6%); China was the largest source, with imports of about US$138.6 billion (+39.7%). A clear chain emerges — "China supplies, Vietnam assembles, America sells" — in which Vietnam earns the markup on assembly and transit while relying heavily on external sourcing for upstream inputs. A 67.6% surge in refined-oil import value underscores dependence on energy and intermediates.

## A Fragile Balance Under the Tariff Shadow

That chain is being tightened from outside. The United States has imposed penalty tariffs on transit trade, launched a Section 301 investigation and added so-called forced-labour tariffs, pushing real tariff costs higher; in the first half of the year, Chinese capital's share of new foreign-invested firms fell to about 5.6% (fourth place), and nearly 25,200 enterprises exited — almost double. As "rules-of-origin dividends" are cut by tariffs, the earlier construction boom could cool quickly.

## Worries Over Currency and Reserves

A persistent deficit pressures the dong and foreign-exchange reserves. In seven months the domestic sector ran a US$28.5 billion deficit, while the foreign-invested sector (including crude oil) posted a US$7.98 billion surplus — meaning almost all the surplus comes from foreign factories, and the strain on local industry deserves more attention. Fortunately the manufacturing PMI is still expanding, and CPI rose 4.45% year on year, close to the roughly 4.5% full-year target, so prices remain manageable.

## Coda: How to Pay the Toll

Seeing the deficit as the "toll of industrialisation" has merit: equipment is still arriving and plants still rising, capacity not yet fully released. But how steep the slope is, and whether the wind shifts, is another matter. If Vietnam is to move from "recipient" to true "taker," it needs not more assembly lines but stronger local supply chains and higher efficiency. Otherwise, today's construction fervour may become tomorrow's unrecoverable bill.

## Further reading

- **Trade Deficit (贸易逆差)**: The gap when imports exceed exports; a sustained deficit can pressure a currency and reserves.
- **Section 301 Investigation (301 调查)**: A US trade tool under the 1974 Trade Act to investigate and sanction foreign trade practices.
- **PMI (采购经理人指数)**: A forward-looking gauge of manufacturing health; above 50 usually signals expansion.

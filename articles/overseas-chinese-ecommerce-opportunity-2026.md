# 当"双11"走向世界：侨胞正在重筑贸易通道

11月11日，"双11"购物节又一次如期而至。据商务部有关负责人表示，中国已连续13年稳居全球最大网络零售市场，并正携手各国共享电商发展红利。跨境电商已成为国际贸易发展的重要趋势，也是人们生活中不可或缺的一部分。对于商业嗅觉敏锐、视野开阔的海内外侨胞而言，这其中，正如中国报道所言，"处处是机遇"。

但在节庆的热闹表象之下，还有一个更值得玩味的故事：侨胞正悄然从供应链末端的"买手"和"囤货者"，转变为中国出口机器的底层基础设施。所谓"从囤货到铺路搭台"，不只是一句口号，它折射的是中国触达世界方式的一次结构性变化。

## 双11的海外回声

"双11"早已不只是中国国内的热闹。俄罗斯"侨二代"蔡一航说，俄罗斯电商尚处起步阶段，当地平台上不少店家都是华人，许多营销打法都借鉴自中国电商，因此每年"双11"在当地也颇热闹。

常年穿梭于中国与老挝之间的邓良慧，也常参与"双11"促销：家里用的制冰机、电饭锅等小家电，就是趁折扣下单的，价格比平时优惠不少。据他观察，海外侨胞参与"双11"各类活动相当普遍，大家都会趁着便宜囤一些实用商品。

喜欢自称"拖鞋哥"的侨商徐磊，很早就尝试直播带货。他自幼随父母在意大利打拼，16岁创立了自己的鞋类品牌，如今已实现自建供应链，专注鞋服出口，市场覆盖意大利、西班牙、希腊、墨西哥等多个国家。2019年他就开始做短视频，彼时赛道人少、流量可观。他说，视频既能打造个人品牌，更关键的是，客户来谈生意时视频成了天然话题，生意更好谈。

## 从囤货到搭台：侨胞角色之变

徐磊的经历颇具代表性。如今他的公司运营总部设在浙江杭州，市场以欧洲为主；线下盘子更大，线上增速更快。"中国供应链发达，这是我们最大的底气，"他说，"中国电商发展快、人才多，人和货都强，这正是做跨境电商的基础。"他还点出一个侨胞独有的优势：多年线下贸易攒下的仓库，稍加改造便是海外仓。

这一点值得强调。数十年来，华侨华人的商贸网络本质上是一套分销与侨汇体系——在中国低价进货、在住国销售、再把钱寄回家。"双11"里的"囤货"，正是这一模式的消费端面孔。而真正的新意在于，侨胞正从被动的买手转向主动的"搭台者"：掌控物流节点（海外仓）、销售渠道（直播），乃至日益掌握平台本身。

## 铺路：物流才是真功夫

标题里的"铺路"，并不只是比喻。一辆悬挂TIR（国际公路运输）标识的国际公路运输车辆驶入温州海关监管场所，关员手持移动设备核验，几分钟后放行，满载跨境电商包裹沿国际公路一路向西，经霍尔果斯口岸出境，十余天后抵达俄罗斯——这样的场景，在温州几乎每日上演。

自2024年12月浙江首趟TIR车辆开行以来，"一次申报、一车直达、一箱到底"、全程无需换装倒装的模式，已成为继海运、空运、铁路之后的"第四物流通道"。据统计，今年前三季度杭州海关监管TIR进出口货物6544.9吨、货值10.1亿元，同比分别增长78.5%和19.02%；这也是浙江年度TIR货值首次突破10亿元，规模居全国前列。

这条"路"的畅通，离不开蔡一航父子的努力。其父上世纪90年代末赴俄经商，蔡一航2010年毕业后赴乌苏里斯克经贸合作区跑招商、办包装厂，2014年回国筹建鞋类研发生产基地，随后与俄罗斯电商平台合作拓展线上渠道。他还推动开通了"义新欧"班列温州鞋类号专列，将货运时间从45天压缩至20天左右。如今他的车队已有200多辆专用车，开通至俄罗斯、白俄罗斯、哈萨克斯坦等4国的20多条TIR线路。他眼下最着急的不是货源，而是常年跑北方线的成熟司机短缺。

## 搭台：把批发市场搬进手机

蔡经锋的故事，对应标题里"搭台"的另一半。在服装行业打拼多年后，他2019年从泰国回到家乡福建石狮创业；2020年东南亚采购集散中心揭牌；2021年12月，在首届RCEP（区域全面经济伙伴关系协定）青年侨商创新创业峰会上，福建省首个面向全球华侨华人的跨境电商服务平台"侨易邦"正式上线，把各地的产业馆、工厂与海外华商连成一线，选品、拼箱、报关、结汇、建仓都能在平台上完成。

"有了这个平台，动动手指就能看到家乡丰富的源头好货，下单、物流、支付一站式搞定，就像把泉州的批发市场'搬'进了海外华商的手机里。"有华商这样形容。蔡经锋介绍，泉州籍侨胞在东南亚根基深厚，平台最初便立足东南亚；随着市场变化，已开始深耕南美。截至目前，东南亚采购集散中心累计入驻企业超800家，为近3000家企业提供了选品、物流、仓储、人才孵化、政策指导等各类服务。

## 冷思考：红利之下有暗礁

官方叙事颇为乐观，数据也确有其事。但若冷静审视，仍有暗礁需要看清。其一，这一模式的长处也正是它的软肋：它高度依赖中国供应链的优势。一旦这一优势因成本上升或"友岸外包"而收窄，侨胞这座"桥"便没了货可载。其二，被寄予厚望的"第四物流通道"仍属新生事物，TIR线路集中、司机短缺皆是实情。其三，也最关乎政治——侨胞正被塑造成贸易壁垒日趋坚硬时代的一层"柔性缓冲"。这固然动听，却也把华侨华人社区暴露在大国博弈的反噬之下；而这一群体，历来是地缘风向转变时最易受损的。

"数字桥梁"的浪漫叙事，不应遮蔽其中的硬核经济学。跨境电商确实在重塑中国中小工厂触达海外消费者的路径，而侨胞的信任网络、语言能力与仓库，也都是实打实的资产。但若把每一位海外侨胞都称作"内置分销渠道"，则不免言过其实。机遇是真，热闹之中的水分，也该打个折扣。

## 延伸阅读

- **跨境电商（Cross-border e-commerce）**：通过线上平台跨境销售商品的模式，如今已成为中国外贸的重要支柱，也是侨胞重新切入出口链条的主要载体。
- **侨贸（Qiaomao）**：即"华侨华人贸易"，指连接侨乡与东南亚等地的历史性商贸网络，如今正被数字化重构。
- **侨商（Overseas Chinese Merchant）**：旅居海外的华商，是中国与住国之间充当中介、投资者与文化经纪人的桥梁角色。

# When "Double 11" Went Global: How the Chinese Diaspora is Rebuilding Trade Routes

On November 11, China's "Double 11" shopping festival arrived once again. According to the Ministry of Commerce, China has been the world's largest online retail market for 13 consecutive years, and it is now inviting other countries to share the dividends of e-commerce development. Cross-border e-commerce has become a vital trend in international trade and an indispensable part of daily life. For the overseas Chinese — a group with sharp commercial instincts and broad horizons — the festival is, as the Chinese piece puts it, "opportunity everywhere."

But behind the festive surface sits a more interesting story. The diaspora is quietly shifting from being mere shoppers and hoarders at the tail end of supply chains to becoming the very infrastructure of China's export machine. The phrase "from hoarding goods to building the stage" is not just a slogan; it captures a structural change in how China reaches the world.

## The overseas echo of Double 11

"Double 11" is no longer just a domestic Chinese spectacle. Cai Yihang, a second-generation overseas Chinese in Russia, notes that Russia's e-commerce is still in its infancy, that many of the merchants on local platforms are ethnic Chinese, and that a great deal of the marketing playbook has been borrowed from Chinese e-commerce — so every year Double 11 is, in his words, "quite lively" there too.

Deng Lianghui, who splits his time between China and Laos, regularly joins the promotions: the ice maker and rice cooker at home were bought during the discount window, he says, because prices are far lower than usual. According to him, it is common for overseas Chinese to take part in Double 11 activities and stock up on practical goods while they are cheap.

And Xu Lei — who likes to call himself "Slipper Brother" — began livestreaming sales years ago. He followed his parents to Italy as a child and built his own footwear brand at sixteen. Today he runs an integrated supply chain focused on apparel exports, with markets spanning Italy, Spain, Greece and Mexico. He started making short videos back in 2019, when the field was uncrowded and traffic was generous. The videos, he says, do double duty: they build a personal brand and, just as importantly, give clients a conversational opener when they come to talk business.

## From hoarding to hosting: a shift in the diaspora's role

Xu's arc is telling. His company's operational headquarters is now in Hangzhou, China, with Europe as its main market. Offline business remains larger, but online is growing faster. "China's supply chain is our greatest confidence," he says. "E-commerce developed fast, and there is no shortage of talent. With both people and goods strong, that is the foundation for cross-border e-commerce." And the diaspora has one more edge, he adds: the warehouses built up over years of offline trade can, with a little retrofitting, become overseas warehouses.

That last point deserves emphasis. For decades, the overseas Chinese commercial network was essentially a distribution and remittance system — buy cheap in China, sell abroad, send money home. The "hoarding" of Double 11 was the consumer face of that model. What is new is the move from passive buyer to active host: controlling the logistics node (the warehouse), the sales channel (the livestream), and increasingly the platform itself.

## Building the road: logistics is the real hard work

The "road" in the Chinese headline is not metaphorical. A TIR-marked international road transport vehicle pulls into Wenzhou's customs inspection yard; officers check it with handheld devices; within minutes it is cleared and rolls west toward Russia via the Khorgos port, arriving more than ten days later. This is now a daily scene in Wenzhou.

Since Zhejiang's first TIR run in December 2024, the "one declaration, one vehicle, one container to the end" model — no repacking, no transshipment — has become a "fourth logistics channel" alongside sea, air and rail. In the first three quarters of this year, Hangzhou Customs supervised 6,544.9 tonnes of TIR cargo worth 1.01 billion yuan (about US$140 million), up 78.5% and 19.02% year on year respectively. This is the first time Zhejiang's annual TIR value has crossed 1 billion yuan, ranking it among the national leaders.

Much of this "road" was built by Cai Yihang and his father. Cai's father went to Russia to do business in the late 1990s; Cai joined him after graduating in 2010 and worked in the Ussuriysk economic zone. In 2014 he returned to China to set up a footwear R&D and production base, then partnered with Russian e-commerce platforms to open online sales channels. He later pushed for a dedicated "Wenzhou shoes" freight train on the Yiwu–Europe (Yixinou) line, cutting transit time from 45 days to around 20. His fleet now runs more than 200 dedicated vehicles across more than 20 TIR routes to four countries. His biggest headache, he says, is no longer cargo but a shortage of experienced drivers willing to run the northern routes.

## Building the platform: moving the wholesale market into the phone

Cai Jingfeng's story is the "platform" half of the headline. After years in apparel, he returned from Thailand to Shishi, Fujian, in 2019. In 2020 the Southeast Asia procurement hub was inaugurated; in December 2021, at the first RCEP Young Overseas Chinese Innovation and Entrepreneurship Summit, Fujian's first cross-border e-commerce service platform aimed at global overseas Chinese — "Qiaoyibang" (侨易邦) — went live. It links Fujian's industrial halls, factories and overseas Chinese merchants on a single line, handling product selection, consolidation, customs declaration, settlement and warehousing.

"With this platform, a few taps show you the rich source goods from home; ordering, logistics and payment are all in one place — it's like moving Quanzhou's wholesale market into the phones of overseas Chinese," merchants say. Cai notes that Quanzhou-origin diaspora have deep roots in Southeast Asia, so the platform started there; as markets shifted, it began cultivating South America. To date, the Southeast Asia procurement hub has hosted more than 800 enterprises and provided services to nearly 3,000 companies.

## A reality check: reefs beneath the dividend

The official narrative is upbeat, and the numbers are real. But a clear-eyed reading should note the reefs. First, the model's strength is also its fragility: it leans heavily on China's supply-chain dominance. When that advantage narrows — through rising costs or friendshoring elsewhere — the diaspora's "bridge" loses its cargo. Second, the much-touted "fourth logistics channel" is still nascent; TIR routes are concentrated and driver shortages are real. Third, and most important politically, the diaspora is being cast as a soft buffer in an era of hardening trade barriers. That is flattering, but it also exposes overseas Chinese communities to the backlash of great-power competition — the same communities that have historically been most vulnerable when geopolitical winds shift.

The romance of the "digital bridge" should not obscure the hard economics. Cross-border e-commerce is genuinely reshaping how small Chinese factories reach foreign consumers, and the diaspora's trust networks, language skills and warehouses are real assets. But calling every overseas Chinese a "built-in distribution channel" risks overstating the case. The opportunity is real; the hype deserves a discount.

## Further reading

- **Cross-border e-commerce (跨境电商)**: The model of selling goods across borders via online platforms, now a core pillar of China's foreign trade and the main vehicle through which the diaspora re-enters the export chain.
- **Qiaomao (侨贸)**: "Overseas-Chinese trade" — the historically rooted commercial networks linking Qiaoxiang (emigrant hometowns) with Southeast Asia and beyond, now being digitised.
- **Overseas Chinese Merchant (侨商)**: Ethnic-Chinese business people abroad who act as intermediaries, investors and cultural brokers between China and their countries of residence.

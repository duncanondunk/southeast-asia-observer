# 安华重申一个中国政策：中国有权维护领土完整，反对党批"外交失误"

马来西亚首相安华（Anwar Ibrahim）8月20日重申，马来西亚坚持一个中国政策，并强调中国有权维护领土完整，类比美国独立战争后不容国家分裂的立场。相关涉台言论在马来西亚国内引发争议，反对党批评其表态是"严重且不必要的外交失误"，指马国不应为武力选项赋予正当性。与此同时，消息称安华拟于9月底应中国总理李强邀请访华三天。在一中原则与大国博弈之间，吉隆坡的平衡术正面临微妙考验。

## 一场风波：从"武力阻独"到"外交失误"指控

风波的起点在8月18日。安华接受半岛电视台访问时，重申对一个中国政策的立场，同时表明马来西亚政府将以武力阻止州属脱离联邦。这一表述被解读为表态支持中国大陆以武力解决台湾问题，随即引发舆论争议。到了8月20日，安华在公开活动后回应称，马来西亚自第二任首相阿都拉萨以来便奉行一个中国政策，今后也将继续维护这项立场；但他也主张"寻求和平解决之道"，试图为争议降温。

## 安华的辩白：以美国为镜，坚持一贯立场

面对质疑，安华的回应策略是"类比"。他指出，无论中国还是美国，都不允许领土分离；正如美国独立战争期间不容国家出现任何分裂，马来西亚只是采取一致的立场。他承认自己的表述方式可能引起负面反应，但仍坚持中美两国均有权维护领土完整的观点。这一辩护逻辑，把台海议题框定为"主权与领土完整"的普遍原则，而非针对特定情境的立场选边。

## 反对党的担忧：为何"选边站"在吉隆坡是敏感词

马来西亚伊斯兰党中委、吉打州行政议员海因希曼（Haim Hilman）发文告批评，安华的涉台表态是"严重且不必要的外交失误"。反对党的核心关切在于：台湾问题高度敏感，马来西亚长期奉行一个中国政策，但这仅停留在外交承认层面，从未对使用武力的合法或正当性质做出判断；马国更不应卷入大国博弈，损害与中国、台湾、美国多方经贸往来的战略空间。在依赖国际贸易的马来西亚，"选边站"向来是政治禁忌词。

## 更大的棋盘：9月访华与马来西亚的对冲外交

争议的背景，是安华政府正谨慎经营对华关系。据《南华早报》引述消息人士报道，安华拟于9月底应中国总理李强邀请访华三天，行程预计包括上海与杭州，但不包括北京，且仍未最终定案。对马来西亚而言，一方面要维持与中国的紧密经贸联系，另一方面又需在美国主导的安全架构中保持平衡。安华的涉台表述无论意图如何，都已牵动这条本就脆弱的对冲线索，使吉隆坡在两大国之间的腾挪空间更显逼仄。

## 延伸阅读

- **一个中国政策（One-China Policy）**：国际社会普遍承认中华人民共和国代表全中国的原则立场，马来西亚自1970年代起即奉行此政策。
- **安华·依布拉欣（Anwar Ibrahim）**：马来西亚第十任首相，政治生涯横跨改革派与执政阵营，外交上主张平衡中美。
- **对冲外交（Hedging Diplomacy）**：中小国家在大国竞争中同时维持多方关系、避免单一依赖的战略选择，常见于东盟国家。

# Anwar Reaffirms One-China Policy: China Has Right to Defend Territorial Integrity; Opposition Calls It a "Diplomatic Blunder"

Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim reaffirmed on August 20 that Malaysia adheres to the One-China policy and stressed that China has the right to defend its territorial integrity, likening it to the United States' post-independence stance against secession. His Taiwan-related remarks have stirred controversy at home; the opposition criticised the statement as a "serious and unnecessary diplomatic blunder," arguing that Malaysia should not lend legitimacy to a military option. Meanwhile, reports say Anwar is expected to visit China for three days in late September at the invitation of Chinese Premier Li Qiang. Between the One-China principle and great-power competition, Kuala Lumpur's balancing act faces a delicate test.

## The Storm: From "Force Against Secession" to Charges of a "Blunder"

The controversy began on August 18. In an Al Jazeera interview, Anwar reaffirmed the One-China stance while stating that the Malaysian government would use force to prevent any state from seceding from the federation. The remark was read as signalling support for mainland China's use of force over Taiwan and immediately sparked debate. On August 20, speaking after a public event, Anwar said Malaysia had followed the One-China policy since the tenure of second premier Abdul Razak and would continue to uphold it; but he also advocated "seeking a peaceful solution," attempting to cool the dispute.

## Anwar's Defence: Holding Up America as a Mirror

Facing pushback, Anwar's strategy was analogy. He noted that neither China nor the United States permits territorial separation; just as America tolerated no secession during its war of independence, Malaysia is simply taking a consistent position. He acknowledged that his phrasing might provoke a negative reaction, yet insisted on the view that both China and the US have the right to defend territorial integrity. This defence frames the Taiwan issue as a universal principle of "sovereignty and territorial integrity" rather than a choice of sides on a specific scenario.

## The Opposition's Worry: Why "Taking Sides" Is a Taboo in Kuala Lumpur

Haim Hilman, a central committee member of the Islamic Party of Malaysia and executive councillor of Kedah state, issued a statement criticising Anwar's Taiwan remarks as a "serious and unnecessary diplomatic blunder." The opposition's core concern is that the Taiwan question is highly sensitive: Malaysia has long practised the One-China policy, but only at the level of diplomatic recognition, never judging the legality or legitimacy of using force. Malaysia, it argues, should not be drawn into great-power rivalry, risking the strategic space for economic ties with China, Taiwan, and the United States alike. In trade-dependent Malaysia, "taking sides" has long been a political taboo.

## The Larger Board: The September China Visit and Malaysia's Hedging Diplomacy

The controversy unfolds as Anwar's government carefully manages ties with Beijing. The South China Morning Post, citing sources, reported that Anwar is expected to visit China for three days in late September at Premier Li Qiang's invitation, with the itinerary likely to include Shanghai and Hangzhou but not Beijing, and still undecided. For Malaysia, the task is to maintain close economic links with China while preserving balance within a US-led security architecture. Whatever Anwar's intent, his Taiwan remarks have tugged at this already fragile hedging thread, narrowing Kuala Lumpur's room to manoeuvre between the two powers.

## Further reading

- **One-China Policy**: the internationally prevalent principle recognising the People's Republic of China as representing all of China; Malaysia has practised it since the 1970s.
- **Anwar Ibrahim**: Malaysia's tenth prime minister, whose political career spans reformist and ruling coalitions and whose diplomacy favours balancing China and the United States.
- **Hedging Diplomacy**: a strategic choice by smaller states to maintain relations with multiple great powers and avoid single dependence, common among ASEAN countries.

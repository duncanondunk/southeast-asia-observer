# 三十八年，鼓声敲开了国家的门：二十四节令鼓入编马来西亚教育体系

2026年8月8日，马来西亚（Malaysia）霹雳州怡保。教育部第一次以主办方的身份，办了一场全国小学二十四节令鼓邀请赛，并把这项赛事命名为"徽崇杯"——纪念1988年与陈再藩共同创立这门鼓艺的已故文化人陈徽崇。据联合早报报道，教育部副部长黄家和在致辞时说，"当第一声鼓声响起时，我内心非常感触，因为经过38年努力，二十四节令鼓终于正式走入政府体制"。

16支小学代表队、417名小鼓手，规模不算大。但如果只把它读成一条文化活动新闻，就错过了这件事真正的重量。

## 一场小学生比赛，为什么值得认真对待

在马来西亚，族群文化的每一寸空间都是要争的。华文小学的拨款、独中统考的承认、教材里出现的爪夷文书法，任何一项拿出来都能引发数月的政治拉锯。就在这场鼓赛前后一周，同一版面上还挂着"伊党指民行党推动消灭回教议程"的新闻——这就是马来西亚族群政治的常态背景音。

在这样的背景下，教育部主动把一项由华人创造、以华人文化元素为骨架的表演艺术，纳入国家教育活动，并以一位华人文化工作者的名字命名年度赛事，这不是礼节性的姿态，而是一次制度性的接纳。差别在于：前者由社团自筹经费办，后者由部门列入预算和行事历；前者靠热心人一年一年撑着，后者写进制度就有了延续性。

所以真正的新闻不是"办了一场比赛"，而是"谁来办"。

## 从九面鼓到三百五十二支队伍

回看这条路径，会发现它几乎是"自下而上"的教科书样本。

1988年，第九届全国华人舞蹈节在柔佛新山举办。陈再藩作词、陈徽崇作曲，为舞蹈节写下主题曲《启舞》；配合演出设计了"九鼓雷鸣"，九面大鼓在音乐中被擂响，鼓手身影投在天幕上。原本只排一场，观众不肯散，只好天天加演。后来陈再藩偶然读到台湾诗人向阳以二十四节气写成的诗集《四季》，灵光一闪，把九面鼓扩成二十四面，二十四节令鼓由此定型：二十四节气为魂，书法为面，广东狮鼓为形。

世界上第一支节令鼓队，由新山宽柔中学的学生组成；诞生仪式选在柔佛古庙——一处承载着南洋华人集体记忆的地方。此后三十多年，鼓声顺着毕业生的脚步走出柔佛，走遍全马，也走到新加坡、泰国、英国、美国。

2009年，二十四节令鼓被列为马来西亚国家非物质文化遗产。两位创始人分别在2008年和2018年获颁国家文化人物奖。到今天，据教育部统计，全马各级学校共有352支节令鼓队，累计参与学生超过6万人。

看清这组数字，才能理解教育部这次的角色：国家不是发明者，而是追认者。它接纳的，是一个已经用三十八年时间把自己做大到无法忽视的存在。

## "非华族超过一成"这个数字的分量

主办方特意点出：417名参赛小鼓手中，非华族占比超过10%。

这个百分比看起来不高，却是整件事得以成立的关键。二十四节令鼓能被国家接纳，恰恰因为它在三个方面降低了政治成本。

第一，它是"本地生的"，不是"进口的"。陈再藩本人反复强调，这是马来西亚华人结合中华文化元素与本土创意的一次创造，而非从中国搬来的传统。这一点在马来西亚的语境里极其重要——它让任何"文化效忠对象在境外"的指控失去着力点。

第二，它是身体的，不是语言的。击鼓不需要中文能力，节拍与队形是通用语法。当一门艺术的入场门槛不含语言，它的族群边界就是软的。这与华文教育的处境形成了鲜明对照：华教之所以年年成为战场，正因为它同时是语言权利、资源分配与身份认同的三重议题。

第三，它已经有了群众基础。352支队伍、6万人次，意味着国家不需要投入资源去"培育"，只需要承认与规范。对任何一个财政紧张、族群议题敏感的政府来说，这都是成本最低的一种文化包容。

值得点破的是：这三条同时也构成了它的天花板。一面鼓解决不了一间华小的拨款问题，一场"徽崇杯"也不等于统考获得承认。制度接纳发生在成本最低的地方，恰恰说明高成本的地方仍未松动。

## 入编的代价：制度化会不会磨平创造力

对这门鼓艺自身而言，走进体制未必只有好处。

去年5月，教育部与二十四节令鼓协会及社会人士讨论过制度化路径：系统收集资料、培训人员、举办全国赛事，让节令鼓在国家教育体制内站稳，并推进申遗目标。这套动作是必要的，但每一项都指向同一个方向——标准化。

赛事需要评分，评分需要指标，指标反过来规定了什么算"好鼓"。当鼓从柔佛古庙的仪式空间搬进学校礼堂和比赛场地，从一群人为了一个节庆自己琢磨的表达，变成有教案、有考核、有名次的课外活动，它获得了延续性，也可能失去当年那种"想到什么就试什么"的松弛。二十四节令鼓最初的魅力，来自两个文化人为了一场舞蹈节临时起意的创造力；把创造力写进大纲，是一件本质上矛盾的事。

这不是反对制度化，而是提醒：制度能保存形式，不能保存冲动。真正的检验是十年之后——352支队伍会不会变成一种统一的、标准的、彼此高度相似的表演。

## 申遗：一场关于"谁的文化"的柔性博弈

二十四节令鼓已获马来西亚旅游、艺术及文化部提名，申请列入联合国教科文组织人类非物质文化遗产名录。而在2016年，中国的"二十四节气"已经进入该名录。

这两件事之间没有冲突——非遗名录允许同源文化在不同国家以不同形态各自申报，"节气"是知识体系，"节令鼓"是表演艺术，本就是两个条目。但这个组合确实提出了一个有意思的问题：当一种文化的基因来自中国、形态成型于马来西亚、传播扩散到英美，它到底属于谁？

比较稳妥的答案是：它属于创造它的那个社群，也属于承认它的那个国家。把二十四节令鼓简单描述成"中华文化在海外的延续"，既低估了马来西亚华人的原创性，也在马来西亚国内给它招来不必要的政治风险；反过来，把它完全切断与中华文化的关联，又不符合事实。

对研究华侨华人的人来说，这恰恰是最值得记录的一类样本：侨居地社群不是文化的搬运工，而是文化的再生产者。真正活下来的，从来不是原封不动被保存的那部分，而是被改造过、被本地人接受过、最终连隔壁邻居的孩子也愿意来敲一敲的那部分。

## 一个克制的判断

一场小学邀请赛不会改变马来西亚的族群政治结构，也不会让华教的老问题一夜化解。但它提供了一个可复制的经验：文化项目要进入主流，靠的不是强调差异，而是把参与门槛降到差异之下。

三十八年，从九面鼓到教育部的行事历。鼓声敲开的那扇门，不是文化的门，是制度的门——这道门一向比前者难开得多。接下来值得观察的是两件事：申遗能否推进，以及第二届"徽崇杯"里，非华族小鼓手的比例是升还是降。后一个数字，比任何官方表态都诚实。

## 延伸阅读

**二十四节令鼓（24 Festive Drums / Gendang 24 Perayaan）**：1988年由陈徽崇、陈再藩在马来西亚柔佛新山创立的鼓艺表演，以二十四节气为主题、书法为鼓面、广东狮鼓为形制，2009年列入马来西亚国家非物质文化遗产。

**国家文化人物奖（Anugerah Tokoh Budaya）**：马来西亚政府颁予对国家文化发展有重大贡献者的荣誉，陈徽崇、陈再藩分别于2008年、2018年获奖。

**在地化（localisation）**：移民社群把原乡文化元素与侨居地社会条件结合、生成新形态的过程；节令鼓被视为马来西亚华人在地化的代表性成果。

# Thirty-Eight Years to Enter the System: Malaysia's 24 Festive Drums Joins the National Education Fold

Ipoh, Perak, 8 August 2026. For the first time, Malaysia's Ministry of Education organised a national primary-school 24 Festive Drums invitational in its own name, and named the event the "Hooi Chong Cup" — after the late cultural figure Tan Hooi Chong, who co-created the drum art with Tan Chai Puan in 1988. According to Lianhe Zaobao, Deputy Education Minister Wong Kah Woh told the gathering that "when the first drumbeat sounded, I was deeply moved, because after 38 years of effort the 24 Festive Drums has finally entered the government system."

Sixteen school teams, 417 young drummers. Not a large event. But reading it as a routine cultural-activity story misses what actually happened.

## Why a primary-school competition deserves serious attention

In Malaysia, every inch of ethnic cultural space is contested. Funding for Chinese-medium primary schools, recognition of the independent-school UEC certificate, the appearance of Jawi calligraphy in textbooks — any one of these can generate months of political trench warfare. In the same week as the drum competition, the same news pages carried PAS accusing the DAP of pushing an agenda to eliminate Islam. That is the ambient noise of Malaysian ethnic politics.

Against that backdrop, a ministry voluntarily folding a performing art invented by Chinese Malaysians, and structurally built out of Chinese cultural elements, into national education activity — and naming its annual competition after a Chinese cultural worker — is not a ceremonial gesture. It is institutional acceptance. The difference is concrete: community associations fund events out of their own pockets; ministries put them in budget lines and annual calendars. The first depends on enthusiasts holding the line year after year; the second, once written into the system, has continuity.

The news, in other words, is not that a competition took place. It is who convened it.

## From nine drums to 352 school troupes

Traced backwards, the path is almost a textbook case of bottom-up cultural formation.

In 1988, the Ninth National Chinese Dance Festival was held in Johor Bahru. Tan Chai Puan wrote the lyrics and Tan Hooi Chong the music for the festival's theme song; to accompany it they designed a "nine drums of thunder" performance, nine large drums struck in unison with the drummers' silhouettes thrown against a backdrop. One performance had been scheduled; the audience refused to let it end, and it was repeated daily for the run of the festival. Tan Chai Puan later came across the Taiwanese poet Xiang Yang's collection *Four Seasons*, written around the twenty-four solar terms, and the idea clicked: expand nine drums to twenty-four. The form settled into what it is today — the solar terms as its soul, calligraphy on its drumheads, the Cantonese lion drum as its body.

The world's first festive-drum troupe was formed by students at Foon Yew High School in Johor Bahru, and its inaugural performance was staged at the Johor Ancient Temple, a site freighted with the collective memory of Nanyang Chinese. Over the following three decades the drumming travelled outward with each graduating cohort — across Malaysia, then to Singapore, Thailand, Britain and the United States.

In 2009 the 24 Festive Drums was listed as Malaysian national intangible cultural heritage. Its two founders received the National Cultural Figure Award in 2008 and 2018 respectively. Today, by the ministry's own count, there are 352 drum troupes across Malaysian schools, with more than 60,000 students having taken part over the years.

Only against those figures does the ministry's role come into focus. The state here is not the inventor but the ratifier. What it has accepted is something that spent thirty-eight years making itself too large to ignore.

## The weight of "more than ten per cent non-Chinese"

The organisers made a point of noting that of the 417 competing drummers, more than 10 per cent were non-Chinese.

The percentage looks modest. It is also the hinge on which the whole thing turns. The 24 Festive Drums became acceptable to the state precisely because it lowered political cost on three fronts.

First, it is homegrown, not imported. Tan Chai Puan has consistently framed it as a Malaysian Chinese creation that fused Chinese cultural elements with local invention, rather than a tradition shipped in from China. In the Malaysian context this matters enormously: it removes the purchase point for any accusation that a community's cultural loyalties reside abroad.

Second, it is bodily, not linguistic. Drumming requires no command of Chinese; rhythm and formation are a universal grammar. When an art form's entry requirement contains no language test, its ethnic boundary is soft. The contrast with Chinese-medium education is stark — Chinese education becomes a battlefield every year precisely because it is simultaneously a question of language rights, resource allocation and identity.

Third, it already had a mass base. With 352 troupes and 60,000 participants, the state does not need to spend money cultivating anything. It only needs to recognise and regularise. For a government facing fiscal constraints and combustible ethnic politics, this is the cheapest available form of cultural inclusion.

Worth saying plainly: those same three conditions also define the ceiling. A drum will not solve a Chinese primary school's funding problem, and a Hooi Chong Cup is not UEC recognition. That institutional acceptance arrived at the lowest-cost point is itself evidence that the high-cost points have not moved.

## The price of entering the system: will institutionalisation sand down invention?

For the art form itself, entering the system is not an unmixed gain.

In May 2025 the ministry held discussions with the 24 Festive Drums Association and community figures on an institutionalisation pathway: systematic documentation, trainer certification, national competitions — anchoring the drums inside the national education system and advancing the UNESCO bid. Each step is necessary. Each also points in one direction: standardisation.

Competitions require scoring; scoring requires criteria; criteria in turn define what counts as good drumming. When the drums move from the ritual space of an old temple into school halls and competition venues — from something a group of people worked out for themselves for a festival into an extracurricular activity with lesson plans, assessments and rankings — they gain continuity and may lose the looseness that let anyone try anything. The original charm of the 24 Festive Drums came from the improvisational energy of two cultural workers preparing a dance festival. Writing improvisation into a syllabus is an inherently contradictory act.

This is not an argument against institutionalisation. It is a reminder that institutions preserve form, not impulse. The real test comes a decade from now: whether 352 troupes converge into a single standardised, mutually indistinguishable performance.

## The UNESCO bid: a soft contest over "whose culture"

The 24 Festive Drums has been nominated by Malaysia's Ministry of Tourism, Arts and Culture for inscription on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. China's "twenty-four solar terms" was inscribed on the same list in 2016.

There is no conflict between the two. The lists accommodate cognate traditions submitted separately by different states in different forms; the solar terms are a knowledge system, the festive drums a performing art. But the pairing does raise a genuinely interesting question: when a cultural form's genes come from China, its shape was fixed in Malaysia, and its diffusion reaches Britain and the United States, whose culture is it?

The defensible answer is that it belongs to the community that created it and to the state that recognises it. Describing the 24 Festive Drums simply as "Chinese culture continuing overseas" both undersells the originality of Chinese Malaysians and invites unnecessary political risk for the form inside Malaysia. Severing it entirely from Chinese cultural lineage, conversely, is factually wrong.

For anyone studying overseas Chinese communities, this is the kind of case worth recording. Diaspora communities are not couriers of culture; they are its re-producers. What survives is never the part preserved unchanged, but the part that was reworked, accepted by local society, and eventually taken up by the neighbours' children too.

## A restrained judgment

A primary-school invitational will not restructure Malaysian ethnic politics, nor will it resolve the long-standing grievances of Chinese education overnight. But it offers a replicable lesson: a cultural project enters the mainstream not by emphasising difference, but by lowering the participation threshold beneath the level at which difference matters.

Thirty-eight years, from nine drums to a ministry calendar entry. The door the drumming opened is not a cultural door but an institutional one, and that door has always been the harder of the two. Two things are now worth watching: whether the UNESCO bid advances, and whether the share of non-Chinese drummers at the second Hooi Chong Cup rises or falls. The second number will be more honest than any official statement.

## Further reading

**24 Festive Drums (Gendang 24 Perayaan)** — A drum performance art created in 1988 by Tan Hooi Chong and Tan Chai Puan in Johor Bahru, Malaysia, built around the twenty-four solar terms, with calligraphy on the drumheads and the Cantonese lion drum as its physical form; listed as Malaysian national intangible cultural heritage in 2009.

**Anugerah Tokoh Budaya (National Cultural Figure Award)** — A Malaysian state honour for major contributions to national cultural development; Tan Hooi Chong and Tan Chai Puan received it in 2008 and 2018 respectively.

**Localisation** — The process by which migrant communities fuse elements of an ancestral culture with the social conditions of their country of settlement to generate new forms; the festive drums are treated as a signature product of Chinese Malaysian localisation.

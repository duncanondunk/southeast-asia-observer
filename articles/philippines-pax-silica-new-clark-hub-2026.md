# 两年的时钟：菲律宾能否在政局生变前把"硅晶计划"落地

## 两年的时钟

菲律宾按自己的政治算术，只剩下不到两年，要把"硅晶计划（Pax Silica）"这个由华盛顿支持的构想，变成真正"有钢筋落地"的实体。总统小马科斯定于2028年卸任；明年美国中期选举可能让国会易主，进而拖慢这一倡议的拨款节奏。菲律宾最大银行金融银行（BDO）前首席市场策略师乔纳森·拉韦拉斯说得很直白：马尼拉越快把协议制度化、把基建落地，政治风险就越低。美国已经接受马尼拉提议，租下新克拉克城（New Clark City）内一块1600公顷的土地、为期两年，用于设立"硅晶计划协调办公室"；一家企业也已签约租地，并计划从10月起招聘本地工程师。这种急迫本身就是信号：所有参与者都清楚，窗口是这两届政府的任期，而不是这类项目通常所需的十年。

## "硅晶计划"究竟是什么

"硅晶计划"是美国国务院于2025年12月推出的一项经济安全框架，其名取拉丁语"和平"与"二氧化硅（silica）"之意——二氧化硅正是提炼芯片核心材料硅的来源。它的底层逻辑，是把人工智能与半导体产业链"友岸外包"：关键矿产、芯片制造、先进制造业、数据中心与物流，分散到可信伙伴国，而非集中在少数国家。菲律宾于2026年4月作为第十三个成员加入；此后联盟已扩展至二十多个经济体，包括日本、韩国、新加坡、澳大利亚、印度与英国。华盛顿的目标说得明白——降低在数字经济时代对单一国家（即中国）物理投入的依赖。

## 菲律宾的契合点：矿产、电子与地利

这种契合并非偶然。菲律宾是全球矿产最丰富的国家之一，已探明未开采的铜、金、镍、锌、银等储量估值约1万亿美元（约7.1万亿元人民币）。它是全球第二大镍生产国，自印尼2020年禁止原矿出口后，又成为最大的镍矿出口国——而这正是电池、半导体与清洁能源技术最需要的金属。在制造一端，电子业已占菲律宾商品出口的一半以上，2024年达391亿美元（约2776亿元人民币），占总出口的53.4%。菲律宾真正的强项是芯片后端环节——封装、测试与组装。"硅晶计划"被包装成一把向上攀爬的梯子，通往更高附加值的环节，而非仅仅把既有产业做大。地利同样加分：吕宋岛横跨印太贸易航线，紧邻日本、韩国与台湾地区的成熟制造走廊。

## 真正的考验：电力与水

到了这里，浪漫就要撞上账本。规划中的园区位于吕宋经济走廊（Luzon Economic Corridor）之内，该走廊连接苏比克、克拉克、马尼拉与八打雁；全面建成后用电峰值可达3吉瓦，约等于吕宋岛当前电网负荷的16%。菲律宾的居民电价已是东南亚最高，截至2026年6月为每千瓦时12.43比索（约1.5元人民币）。用水需求估计每天1.3亿升，相当于60万户家庭的消耗。批评者警告，若没有清洁能源的及时跟上与严格保障，园区可能锁死对化石能源的依赖，并挤压中吕宋的农业与社区。基地转换与发展署（BCDA）称，园区全面开发可吸引400亿至700亿美元（约2840亿至4970亿元人民币）投资，并创造最多19万个就业岗位——但这些数字的前提，是电力与用水问题被解决，而不是被回避。

## 主权之争的反弹

并非所有人都相信这是一笔好买卖。左翼的"马卡巴扬（Makabayan）"政治阵营与"菲律宾农民运动（KMP）"称其为"大规模出卖"，认为菲律宾被锁死在从属位置——出口原材料、从事低附加值制造，而更高端的技术、控制权与利润留在海外。卡拉卡桑（Kalikasan）等环保组织则警告土地用途转换、生态退化与芯片厂的毒害排放。更尖锐的是主权层面：有报道称华盛顿寻求让园区适用美国法律、并赋予美方人员外交豁免权，这被国内舆论指责为在昔日美军空军基地内开辟现代"租界"。总统的姐姐、参议员伊梅·马科斯已公开要求，就农民被迫迁的投诉展开调查。

## "友岸外包"在东南亚落地

对中国—东盟关系的观察者而言，"硅晶计划"是华盛顿"友岸外包"首次如此实在地落在东南亚土地上的例证。它与菲美日三方吕宋经济走廊相互嵌套，也恰逢马尼拉对华盛顿的安全靠拢不断收紧。然而菲律宾的经济结构决定了"与中国脱钩"只是幻想：北京仍是其主要贸易伙伴，中国对菲基础设施、农业与能源的投资盘根错节。更可能的结局，是一个做中低端组装与矿产加工的园区——有用，却远不及700亿美元愿景所暗示的那种全栈人工智能制造中心。马来西亚已拿下东南亚芯片封装市场的一半，菲律宾要在高端环节分一杯羹，难度极大。

## 一个现实的判断

"硅晶计划"几乎肯定会作为理念存活下来——基地转换与发展署有跨政府更迭推进重大项目的记录，而供应链多元化的战略逻辑也具有韧性。但那个"黄金节点"落地时，恐怕比宣传册上更瘦小、更克制：一个由美日资本撑起的"半导体封装加矿产加工"园区，而非修辞中的主权级硅谷。因此，这两年冲刺的意义，不在于建出什么奇迹，而在于囤够协议、电力合同与锚定租户，让项目在2028年之后难以被逆转。马尼拉能否把一次地缘政治的窗口，转化为工业主权——而不是别人供应链里一个报酬尚可的中转站——这将是下一届政府接手的考题。

## 延伸阅读

- **友岸外包（Friend-shoring）**：华盛顿把关键供应链从单纯讲求成本，转向迁往政治立场一致伙伴国的策略。
- **吕宋经济走廊（Luzon Economic Corridor）**：菲美日三方基础设施倡议，串联苏比克、克拉克、马尼拉与八打雁，强化互联互通与清洁能源。
- **芯片后端封装（Back-end packaging）**：半导体制造中的封装、测试与组装环节，恰是菲律宾已有的区域强项。

# Pax Silica in the Philippines: a two-year sprint against the political clock

## The two-year clock

The Philippines has, by its own political arithmetic, less than two years to turn Pax Silica from a Washington-backed concept into something with steel in the ground. President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. leaves office in 2028; next year's US midterms could hand Congress to his rivals and slow the initiative's funding. Jonathan Ravelas, former chief market strategist at BDO — the country's largest bank — put it bluntly: the faster Manila institutionalises agreements and delivers infrastructure, the lower the political risk. The United States has already accepted Manila's offer to rent a 1,600-hectare plot for two years to house a "Pax Silica coordination office" inside New Clark City, and a company has signed on to lease land and begin hiring local engineers by October. The rush is the tell: everyone involved knows the window is the tenure of two administrations, not the decade a project of this scale normally needs.

## What Pax Silica actually is

Pax Silica is a US-led economic-security framework unveiled by the State Department in December 2025, named to fuse the Latin "peace" with "silica" — the compound refined into the silicon at the heart of every chip. Its premise is friend-shoring the AI and semiconductor stack: critical minerals, fabrication, advanced manufacturing, data centres and logistics, distributed across trusted partners rather than concentrated in one or two countries. The Philippines joined in April 2026 as the 13th member; the coalition has since expanded to more than two dozen economies, including Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Australia, India and the United Kingdom. Washington's aim is explicit — reduce dependence on China for the physical inputs of the digital age.

## The Philippine fit: minerals, electronics, geography

The fit is not accidental. The Philippines is among the world's most mineralised countries, with an estimated US$1 trillion in untapped copper, gold, nickel, zinc and silver. It is the world's second-largest nickel producer and, since Indonesia banned raw ore exports in 2020, its largest exporter of nickel ore — exactly the metals batteries, semiconductors and clean-energy tech demand. On the manufacturing side, electronics already account for more than half of Philippine merchandise exports, about US$39.1 billion in 2024, or 53.4 per cent of the total. The country's real strength is back-end chip work — assembly, testing and packaging. Pax Silica is pitched as a ladder toward higher-value segments, not merely a larger version of what already exists. Geography helps too: Luzon sits across the Indo-Pacific trade routes, close to the established manufacturing corridors of Japan, South Korea and Taiwan.

## The real test: power and water

Here the romance meets the spreadsheet. The proposed zone, planned within the Luzon Economic Corridor that links Subic, Clark, Manila and Batangas, could draw up to 3 gigawatts at full build-out — roughly 16 per cent of Luzon's current grid capacity. The Philippines already posts the highest residential power rate in Southeast Asia, at 12.43 pesos (about US$0.21) per kilowatt-hour as of June 2026. Water demand is estimated at 130 million litres a day, comparable to the consumption of 600,000 households. Without clean-energy ramp-up and strict safeguards, critics warn, the hub could lock in fossil-fuel dependence and strain Central Luzon's agriculture and communities. The Bases Conversion and Development Authority says the development could attract between US$40 billion and US$70 billion in investment and create up to 190,000 jobs — figures that presuppose the power and water questions are solved, not wished away.

## The sovereignty backlash

Not everyone is convinced the deal is a bargain. The left-leaning Makabayan bloc and peasant group Kilusang Magbubukid ng Pilipinas call it a "massive sellout" that locks the Philippines into a subordinate role — exporting raw materials and performing low-value manufacturing while higher-value technology, control and profits stay abroad. Environmental groups such as Kalikasan warn of land conversion, ecological degradation and toxic effluent from semiconductor plants. There is also a sharper sovereignty edge: reports that Washington sought to place the hub under US law, with diplomatic immunity for US personnel, have fed domestic accusations of a modern "concession" inside a former US air base. Senator Imee Marcos, the president's sister, has publicly called for an inquiry into complaints that farmers are being displaced.

## Friend-shoring lands in Southeast Asia

For observers of China–ASEAN relations, Pax Silica is the clearest instance yet of Washington's friend-shoring landing physically on Southeast Asian soil. It dovetails with the trilateral Luzon Economic Corridor (Philippines–US–Japan) and arrives as Manila's security alignment with Washington tightens. Yet the Philippines' economic structure means decoupling from China is a fantasy: Beijing remains a top trade partner, and Chinese investment in Philippine infrastructure, agriculture and energy is entrenched. The more likely outcome is a hub that does mid- and low-value assembly and minerals processing — useful, but short of the full-stack AI foundry the US$70 billion vision implies. Malaysia already commands half of Southeast Asia's chip-packaging market; the Philippines will have to fight for the premium slices.

## A realistic verdict

Pax Silica will almost certainly survive as an idea — the BCDA has a track record of carrying major projects across administrations, and the strategic logic of diversifying supply chains is durable. But the "Golden Node" may arrive smaller and more modest than the brochure: a semiconductor-packaging-and-minerals zone buttressed by US and Japanese capital, not the sovereign Silicon Valley of the rhetoric. The two-year sprint is therefore less about building a wonder than about banking enough agreements, power contracts and anchor tenants to make the project impossible to unwind after 2028. Whether Manila can convert a geopolitical opening into industrial sovereignty — rather than a well-paid way station in someone else's supply chain — is the question the next administration will inherit.

## Further reading

- **Friend-shoring**: Washington's strategy of relocating critical supply chains to politically aligned partners rather than optimising purely for cost.
- **Luzon Economic Corridor**: A Philippines–US–Japan infrastructure initiative linking Subic, Clark, Manila and Batangas to strengthen connectivity and clean energy.
- **Back-end chip packaging**: The assembly, testing and packaging stage of semiconductor manufacturing, where the Philippines already holds regional strength.

# 争议之下的能源算术：小马科斯为何在此时重提中菲联合勘探

菲律宾总统小马科斯（Ferdinand Marcos Jr.）8月14日在马尼拉出席菲律宾外国记者协会（Foreign Correspondents Association of the Philippines）论坛时说，尽管中菲在南海问题上的争议持续升温，两国仍有望在经济领域展开合作，其中包括联合进行能源勘探。据联合早报报道，他表示随着菲律宾寻求加强能源安全，与中国的谈判正在取得进展；近期的能源供应中断与价格飙升，使开发近海资源变得更为紧迫。

## 对峙未止，谈判先行

小马科斯的这番表态之所以引人注目，不在于它宣布了什么新协议，而在于时机。就在同一场活动上，他还回应了中菲在南海的海上摩擦，称马尼拉与北京之间不存在任何特殊安排，菲律宾无须在执行补给任务前征得中国同意，也没有放弃自身权利。一边强调立场不退，一边宣布经济议题"取得进展"，这种双轨表述本身就是当下中菲关系的准确写照。

他对联合勘探前景给出了相当乐观的措辞："我们必须找到新的途径……这一讨论已受到广泛关注。就目前而言，我认为我们已取得进展。"他还说："我的感觉是，所有相关方都希望这项合作成功，这是个非常好的迹象……我认为开展这类联合勘探的可能性很大。"按他的说法，菲方正与中国就未决问题进行磋商，包括任何最终安排的框架条款。

需要注意"框架条款"这个提法。它意味着谈判尚处于设计法律与商业外壳的阶段，而非分配区块与股权的阶段。历次中菲油气合作尝试卡住的地方，恰恰就是外壳。

## 礼乐滩：被法理分歧冻结的气田

争议的地理焦点是礼乐滩（Reed Bank，菲方称 Recto Bank）。中国对南海诸岛及其附近海域拥有主权，礼乐滩属于中国南沙群岛的组成部分；菲律宾方面则以其单方面主张的专属经济区（Exclusive Economic Zone）为依据要求开发权。多年来两国一直在探讨联合勘探该区域的天然气，但由于双方对主权归属存在根本分歧，谈判屡次破裂。

法理分歧带来的不是抽象的外交尴尬，而是具体的商业不可行。任何一家国际油气公司在投入数十亿美元的勘探与开采资本之前，都需要确认作业许可的法律来源、争议发生时的仲裁路径以及资产被冻结的风险。当两国对同一片海域的主权归属各持己见时，任何一份单方面颁发的许可证都可能被另一方视为无效——这就是为什么礼乐滩的天然气储量在纸面上存在了很多年，却始终没有变成产量。

## 供应中断与价格飙升：马尼拉的能源算术

小马科斯把紧迫性归因于能源供应中断和价格飙升，这不是外交辞令。菲律宾的电力结构长期依赖进口煤炭与本土天然气，而作为主力气源的马拉帕亚气田（Malampaya gas field）——长期为吕宋岛（Luzon）供应约五分之一的电力——储量已接近枯竭。一旦这块气源退出，缺口要么由更昂贵的液化天然气进口填补，要么由排放更高的煤电顶上。

这就构成了马尼拉的能源算术：新气田的开发周期通常以十年计，从签署框架到第一方气入网，中间要经过勘探、评价、最终投资决定与管道建设。换言之，如果现在不谈，缺口到来时也来不及。能源安全的时间表，正在压迫主权争议的时间表——这是理解菲方为何在对峙升温时仍愿意谈的关键。

## 宪法天花板与历次搁置

菲律宾国内还有一道更硬的约束。1987年宪法规定自然资源的勘探、开发与利用须由国家控制，外资参与需以菲方主导的合资形式进行。这意味着任何联合开发安排都必须被表述为"在菲律宾法律下作业"，而中方无法接受一份隐含承认菲方管辖权的文本。2018年双方曾签署油气开发合作谅解备忘录，试图以搁置主权争议的方式推进，但在2022年杜特尔特（Rodrigo Duterte）政府任期尾声，谈判被宣布终止。

这道天花板解释了为什么小马科斯的乐观需要打折扣。除非双方能设计出一种在法律上互不损害立场、在商业上又足以让投资者放心的结构，否则"取得进展"很容易停留在磋商层面。国际实践中此类安排并非没有先例，但成功案例通常需要双方在其他领域存在足够的战略互信作为担保——而这恰恰是当前中菲关系最稀缺的资源。

## 与东京谈边界、与北京谈开发：两条平行轨道

同一场活动上，小马科斯还说菲律宾计划继续与日本就海上边界问题进行谈判，并希望在今年内结束。把这条信息与联合勘探并读，可以看出马尼拉正在同时推进两条轨道：一条是通过与域外伙伴的制度化安排巩固自身立场，另一条是在争议海域寻求与中国的功能性合作。

小马科斯也为后一条轨道留下了余地。他强调对中菲经济合作持乐观态度，称没有任何政策或声明显示菲律宾要疏远中国，中国仍是菲律宾最大的投资国之一；他还说，如果察觉到紧张局势加剧，就必须考虑与中国的朋友沟通。这种表述透露出中等国家在大国竞争格局下的典型处境：安全上加固、经济上不脱钩，两者都要，且都不愿被对方绑定。

因此，真正值得观察的不是"可能性很大"这句表态，而是接下来是否会出现可核查的技术性动作——联合工作组的设立、勘探区块的界定、作业主体的法律形式。有了这些，联合勘探才算从政治意愿走向工程日程；没有这些，礼乐滩的天然气还会继续在纸面上待着。

## 延伸阅读

**礼乐滩（Reed Bank）**：位于南海的一处水下浅滩，蕴藏天然气资源。中国对南海诸岛及其附近海域拥有主权，礼乐滩属于中国南沙群岛的组成部分；菲律宾以其单方面主张的专属经济区为由要求开发权，法理分歧使该区域长期无法进入商业开采。

**联合开发（joint development）**：在主权归属争议未解决的前提下，相关方通过临时性安排共同勘探开发资源、暂不触动主权立场的合作模式。其核心难点在于设计一套既不损害各方法律主张、又能让商业投资者接受的税收与作业框架。

**马拉帕亚气田（Malampaya gas field）**：菲律宾最主要的天然气产地，长期为吕宋岛供应约五分之一电力，储量已接近枯竭，是马尼拉推动新气源开发的直接压力来源。

# Energy Arithmetic Under Dispute: Why Marcos Revived Joint Exploration Talks Now

Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. told a Foreign Correspondents Association of the Philippines forum in Manila on 14 August that despite escalating disputes with China in the South China Sea, the two countries could still cooperate economically — including on joint energy exploration. According to Lianhe Zaobao, he said negotiations with Beijing were making progress as the Philippines seeks to strengthen energy security, and that recent supply disruptions and price spikes had made developing offshore resources more urgent.

## Confrontation Persists, Talks Move First

What makes the remarks notable is not any new agreement but the timing. At the same event Marcos addressed maritime friction with China, saying there was no special arrangement between Manila and Beijing, that the Philippines need not seek Chinese consent before resupply missions, and that it had surrendered no rights. Holding a firm line on position while announcing "progress" on economics is itself an accurate portrait of the relationship as it now stands.

His language on exploration was notably upbeat: a new path had to be found, the discussion had attracted wide attention, and for now, he judged, progress had been made. He added that his sense was that all parties wanted the cooperation to succeed — a very good sign — and that the likelihood of such joint exploration was high. By his account, Manila is consulting Beijing on outstanding issues, including the framework terms of any eventual arrangement.

That phrase — framework terms — deserves attention. It indicates talks are still designing the legal and commercial shell rather than allocating blocks and equity. The shell is precisely where previous Chinese–Philippine oil and gas efforts have stalled.

## Reed Bank: A Gas Field Frozen by Legal Dispute

The geographic focus is Reed Bank, which the Philippine side calls Recto Bank. China holds sovereignty over the South China Sea islands and their adjacent waters, and Reed Bank forms part of China's Nansha Islands; the Philippine side claims development rights on the basis of an exclusive economic zone it asserts unilaterally. The two governments have discussed joint gas exploration there for years, but fundamental disagreement over sovereignty has repeatedly broken the talks.

The consequence of that disagreement is not abstract diplomatic awkwardness but concrete commercial impossibility. Before committing billions of dollars of exploration and production capital, any international oil company must establish the legal source of its operating licence, the arbitration route in the event of dispute, and the risk of asset seizure. When two states hold opposing views on sovereignty over the same waters, a licence issued by either may be treated as void by the other. That is why Reed Bank's gas has existed on paper for years without becoming output.

## Supply Disruption and Price Spikes: Manila's Energy Arithmetic

Marcos attributed the urgency to supply disruption and rising prices, and this is not diplomatic filler. Philippine power generation has long leaned on imported coal and domestic gas, and the Malampaya gas field — for years supplying roughly a fifth of Luzon's electricity — is approaching depletion. Once that source retires, the gap must be filled either by costlier liquefied natural gas imports or by higher-emitting coal.

Hence the arithmetic. Developing a new field is typically a decade-long undertaking: exploration, appraisal, final investment decision and pipeline construction all precede first gas. If talks do not begin now, no arrangement will arrive in time. The timetable of energy security is pressing on the timetable of the sovereignty dispute — the key to understanding why Manila remains willing to negotiate even as confrontation sharpens.

## The Constitutional Ceiling and Repeated Shelvings

A harder domestic constraint also applies. The 1987 Philippine constitution requires that exploration, development and utilisation of natural resources be under state control, with foreign participation structured through Filipino-majority arrangements. Any joint development must therefore be framed as operating under Philippine law — a text Beijing cannot accept insofar as it implies recognition of Philippine jurisdiction. The two sides signed a memorandum of understanding on oil and gas cooperation in 2018, attempting to proceed by setting sovereignty aside, but the talks were declared terminated late in the term of Rodrigo Duterte's government in 2022.

This ceiling is why Marcos's optimism warrants a discount. Unless the parties can design a structure that damages neither legal position while reassuring investors commercially, "progress" may remain at the level of consultation. Comparable arrangements exist internationally, but the successful ones generally rest on sufficient strategic trust elsewhere in the relationship to serve as collateral — which is exactly what Chinese–Philippine ties currently lack.

## Borders with Tokyo, Development with Beijing: Two Parallel Tracks

At the same event Marcos said the Philippines would continue maritime boundary negotiations with Japan and hoped to conclude them this year. Read alongside the exploration remarks, Manila is advancing two tracks at once: consolidating its position through institutionalised arrangements with partners outside the region, while seeking functional cooperation with China inside the disputed waters.

He also left room on the second track, stressing optimism about economic cooperation, noting that no policy or statement suggested the Philippines was distancing itself from China, that China remained one of its largest sources of investment, and that if tensions were seen to rise, Manila would have to talk to its friends in China. The formulation captures a familiar middle-power condition under great-power competition: harden on security, avoid decoupling on economics, want both, and resist being bound by either side.

What merits watching, then, is not the claim that the likelihood is high, but whether verifiable technical steps follow — a joint working group, defined exploration blocks, a legal form for the operating entity. With those, joint exploration moves from political intent to an engineering schedule. Without them, Reed Bank's gas stays on paper.

## Further reading

**Reed Bank**: A submerged bank in the South China Sea holding natural gas resources. China holds sovereignty over the South China Sea islands and adjacent waters, and Reed Bank forms part of China's Nansha Islands; the Philippines claims development rights on the basis of a unilaterally asserted exclusive economic zone, and the legal dispute has long kept the area out of commercial production.

**Joint development**: A cooperative model in which parties to an unresolved sovereignty dispute explore and develop resources under provisional arrangements that leave sovereign claims untouched. The central difficulty is designing tax and operating frameworks that neither prejudice legal positions nor deter commercial investors.

**Malampaya gas field**: The Philippines' principal domestic gas source, long supplying roughly a fifth of Luzon's electricity and now approaching depletion — the immediate pressure behind Manila's push for new supply.

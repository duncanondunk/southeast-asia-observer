# 2772公里铁路「画饼」：印尼再邀中国修路，雅万高铁的债还没还清

## 2772公里的「画饼」

印尼又来敲北京的门了。继东南亚首条高铁雅万高铁（Whoosh）通车之后，雅加达现在想把更大的一单交给中国资金和中国的工程队：一条横贯加里曼丹岛（婆罗洲的印尼部分）的跨岛铁路网，全长2772公里。线路西起坤甸，东至三马林达与巴厘巴板，并修支线直插深居雨林腹地的新首都努山塔拉。

交通部长杜迪·普尔瓦甘迪（Dudy Purwagandandi）称政府正在「探索」外资，安塔拉通讯社引述称中国和俄罗斯都表露了兴趣。普拉博沃总统7月30日已责令交通部与印尼国家铁路公司（KAI）尽快启动项目。纸面上，这像顺理成章的下一步；落到实算上，却是一笔还没算平的账。

## 雅万高铁的「前车之鉴」

最现成的参照就是雅万高铁本身。中国国家开发银行提供45.5亿美元贷款，利率2%、还款期40年；项目超支12亿美元，追加贷款利率涨到3.4%。通车后连年亏损——2024年净亏2.58亿美元，2025年上半年又亏近1亿美元。2026年9月，印尼做了一轮债务重组：还款期拉长到80年，主权财富基金接手运营公司60%股份，中方保留40%。

教训很直白：贷款方和建设方不兜运营的底；客流不够，缺口是印尼自己扛。洛伊研究所（Lowy Institute）说得更透：在雅万项目里，中国的角色是「贷款方加建设方」，不是「承担风险的投资者」。这条逻辑，搬到加里曼丹不会变。

## 印尼的「新玩法」：不掏钱，但要你接盘

这一回，雅加达说不动用国家预算。交通部长话说得很直：项目全交给私人投资者。思路是把沿线矿产运输权和港口出口通道打包，给一份长期货运协议——先锁定货源，再谈铺轨。

听起来在理。可矿还在规划阶段，把未来产量算进运输收入，账面好看，风险却全压在投资方身上。洛伊研究所按小型项目单价粗算，整条2772公里成本可能逼近400亿美元（约2880亿元人民币）；仅东加里曼丹段约501公里，初步预估造价就约133亿美元（约958亿元人民币）。全国层面，印尼规划把铁路总里程扩到1.4万公里，总投资约774亿美元（约5573亿元人民币）——但国库只覆盖四成，剩下六成要靠私资和外资。

## 中方的算盘：先重做可行性研究

这里才是风向变了的地方。8月部长说，中方已完成可行性研究，且「相当积极」推动谈判；但他补了一句要紧的话：中方做研究时，印尼的出口管理政策还没变。如今新规出台，原来的货运量预测可能不准，「可行性研究得重新看」。

翻译一下：北京要在落子前重定价。中国自己的海外基建放贷已经收紧，大开处方式贷款的时代让位于规模更小、商业可行性与风控更严的项目。加里曼丹规模是雅万高铁的近20倍。中方可以谈融资条件，却不会因为雅加达想赶进度，就一降再降门槛、多扛风险。镍矿风波是底色：今年4月印尼削减采矿配额、把计价系数从17%提到30%，部分中资企业成本暴涨近200%。镍矿之争余味未散，转头又请中企接下铁路大单，说好听点叫精巧，说难听点叫精明过头。

## 俄罗斯的「陪跑」与大国平衡

俄罗斯也在名单里，但多半是杠杆。部长说得很直白：苏门答腊方面，印尼国家铁路公司似乎更想做铁路物流；加里曼丹方面，中国和俄罗斯更有兴趣。可俄方铁路出口的实力不在一个量级。南部一条203公里加里曼丹铁路，原本由俄罗斯铁路公司出资，2022年俄方撤资，项目直接黄了。真正能把2772公里蓝图变成钢轨的，只有中国。

于是雅加达玩起典型的大国平衡：同时跟中国、俄罗斯、日本、韩国谈，用市场换资金。「以资源换资金」是得体外交，却改不了硬约束——总得有人相信货运真能落地。

## 新首都的「孤岛」焦虑

战略理由是真的。加里曼丹至今没有运营中的铁路。新首都努山塔拉深居雨林，不通铁路就等于地理上的「孤岛」。印尼喊了多年的「资源下游化」，恰恰卡在矿石出不了岛、运费降不下来。公路运大宗商品，成本是铁路的三到五倍。对资源出口国来说，物流成本就是国际竞争力。一条长线铁路，或许真能盘活外岛资源，纠正「爪哇太强、外岛太弱」的畸形格局。

但紧迫不等于可融资。山地纵横、湿度高、基础设施近乎为零，在货量还是假设的当下从零开建，正是当年那些冒险项目翻车的地方。

## 资源民族主义的悖论

更深的讽刺在后面。雅加达的资源民族主义政策——出口管制、修订计价、本地含量要求——本意是把更多价值留在国内；它们也吓退了如今被请来修铁路、把资源运出去的同一批投资者。镍矿之争余温未退，印尼转头就请中企接下380亿美元（约2736亿元人民币）以上的铁路账单。

西方舆论也值得看一眼。从签约到通车一路纠缠雅万高铁的「债务陷阱」叙事，如今轮到雅加达而非北京背着融资缺口，竟一下子安静了。这温差本身就说明问题：当年的审视，从来就不是真的冲着债务去。

## 延伸阅读

**一带一路（Belt and Road Initiative）：** 中国海外基建放贷框架，正从大型国资背书贷款，转向规模更小、商业尽调更严、风控更硬的项目。

**雅万高铁（Jakarta–Bandung HSR / Whoosh）：** 东南亚首条高铁，45.5亿美元中方贷款线路，其运营亏损与80年债务重组，成了印尼下一场铁路赌局的警示样本。

**资源民族主义（Resource Nationalism）：** 出口限制、修订计价、本地含量等旨在多留价值的政策，也可能吓退修建出口基建所需的外资。

# Indonesia's 2,772km Railway Gamble: Courting China Again Before the Whoosh Debt Is Settled

## A 2,772km pie in the sky

Indonesia is once again knocking on Beijing's door. After Southeast Asia's first high-speed line — the Jakarta–Bandung Whoosh — began running, Jakarta now wants Chinese money and Chinese builders for something far bigger: a 2,772km (1,722-mile) railway network across Kalimantan, the Indonesian share of Borneo. The line would run from Pontianak in the west to Samarinda and Balikpapan in the east, with branches feeding the new capital Nusantara deep in the rainforest interior.

Transportation Minister Dudy Purwagandhi says the government is "exploring" foreign investment, and Antara reports that both China and Russia have expressed interest. President Prabowo Subianto ordered the transport ministry and the state railway company KAI to fast-track the project on 30 July. On paper it looks like a natural next step. In practice, it is a bet whose arithmetic has not yet been made to add up.

## The Whoosh precedent

The obvious comparison is Whoosh itself. China Development Bank lent US$4.55 billion at 2 per cent over 40 years; the project overran by US$1.2 billion and the top-up loan carried 3.4 per cent. Once running, it lost money — a net US$258 million in 2024 and nearly US$100 million in the first half of 2025. In September 2026 Jakarta restructured the debt: the repayment period was stretched to 80 years and the sovereign wealth fund took 60 per cent of the operating company, leaving Chinese parties with 40 per cent.

The lesson is blunt. The lender and builder do not absorb the operating risk; when ridership falls short, the gap is Indonesia's to carry. Analysts at the Lowy Institute put it plainly: in Whoosh, China's role was "lender and builder," not "risk-bearing investor." That logic will not change for Kalimantan.

## Jakarta's new play: no public money, but you carry the risk

This time Jakarta says it will not touch the state budget. The transport minister has been explicit: the project will be left entirely to private investors. The idea is to bundle mineral transport rights and port export corridors along the line into long-term freight agreements — lock in the cargo first, then talk about laying track.

It sounds reasonable. But the mines are still at the planning stage, and booking their future output into transport revenue makes the spreadsheet look healthy while pushing the risk squarely onto the investor. The Lowy Institute estimates the full 2,772km could cost close to US$40 billion; the East Kalimantan segment alone, about 501km, was preliminarily priced at roughly US$13.3 billion. Nationally, Indonesia plans to expand its railway network to 14,000km with total investment near US$77.4 billion — but the treasury covers only 40 per cent, leaving 60 per cent to private and foreign capital.

## Beijing's calculus: redo the feasibility study first

Here is where the mood has shifted. In August the minister said Chinese parties had completed a feasibility study and were "quite positive" about pushing talks forward. But he added a telling caveat: when China did that study, Indonesia's export management policy had not yet changed. Now, with new export rules, the old freight-volume forecasts may no longer hold, and "the feasibility study needs to be looked at again."

Translate that: Beijing wants to reprice before committing. China's own outbound infrastructure lending has tightened; the era of blank-cheque loans is giving way to smaller projects and stricter commercial and risk checks. Kalimantan is nearly 20 times the scale of Whoosh. Beijing can negotiate financing terms, but it will not keep lowering the bar or absorbing more risk just because Jakarta wants speed. The nickel dispute is the backdrop: in April Indonesia cut mining quotas and raised the pricing coefficient from 17 to 30 per cent, pushing some Chinese operators' costs up by nearly 200 per cent. Courting Chinese capital again while the aftertaste of that fight lingers is, to put it mildly, a delicate ask.

## Russia as a foil, and the art of hedging

Russia is in the frame, but mostly as leverage. The minister noted that for Sumatra, KAI seems more interested in rail logistics, while for Kalimantan, China and Russia are keener. Yet Russia's railway export capacity is not in the same league. A 203km southern Kalimantan line was originally backed by Russian Railways — until Moscow pulled out in 2022 and the project collapsed. The only player that can realistically turn 2,772km of blueprint into steel is China.

So Jakarta is running a classic hedge: talk to China, Russia, Japan and South Korea at once, use the market to draw capital. The "resources-for-funding" formula is sound diplomacy, but it does not change the hard constraint — someone has to believe the freight will materialise.

## The 'island' anxiety over the new capital

The strategic case is real. Kalimantan has no operating railway. Nusantara, the new capital, sits deep in the rainforest; without modern rail it risks becoming a geographic island. Indonesia's long mantra of "resource downstreaming" stalls precisely because ore cannot leave the island cheaply. Hauling bulk commodities by road costs three to five times what rail would. For a resource-exporting country, logistics cost is competitive edge. A long-haul line could finally unlock the outer islands and correct the lopsided "Java too strong, outer islands too weak" structure.

But urgency is not the same as bankability. The terrain is mountainous, humidity is high, and the baseline infrastructure is close to zero. Building from scratch while the cargo is still hypothetical is exactly the kind of leap that sank earlier ventures.

## The paradox of resource nationalism

There is a deeper irony. Jakarta's resource-nationalist policies — export controls, revised pricing, local-content push — were meant to capture more value at home. They have also spooked the very investors now being asked to fund the railways those resources need to reach port. The nickel row had barely cooled when Indonesia turned around and asked Chinese firms to take on a US$38 billion-plus railway bill.

Western coverage is worth a glance, too. The "debt-trap" narrative that dogged Whoosh from signing to opening has gone conspicuously quiet now that Jakarta, not Beijing, owns the financing gap. The temperature difference tells its own story: the scrutiny was never really about debt.

## Further reading

**Belt and Road Initiative (一带一路):** China's overseas infrastructure lending framework, now shifting from large, state-backed loans toward smaller, commercially vetted projects with stricter risk control.

**Jakarta–Bandung HSR / Whoosh (雅万高铁):** Southeast Asia's first high-speed railway, a US$4.55 billion Chinese-funded line whose operating losses and 80-year debt restructuring became the cautionary tale for Indonesia's next railway bet.

**Resource nationalism (资源民族主义):** State policies — export curbs, pricing revisions, local-content rules — aimed at capturing more value domestically, which can also deter the foreign capital needed to build export infrastructure.

# 菲律宾暴雨成灾：数百村庄变泽国，邦板牙省十八地涝渍围城

受西南季风增强带来的持续暴雨影响，菲律宾洪灾灾情未见好转。首都马尼拉以北的邦板牙省（Pampanga）十九个地方政府单位中，有十八个发生洪涝，低洼城镇如阿帕利特（Apalit）因河水泛滥沦为水乡泽国。截至8月20日，全国已有二百七十二个村庄遭遇严重洪灾，多栋房屋与车辆被淹，近万名居民被迫转移。所幸当地暂未通报人员伤亡。这场季风涝渍，再次把菲律宾的排涝能力与灾害预警短板推到台前。

## 季风增压：邦板牙省十八地涝渍围城

本轮灾情的核心在邦板牙省。位于吕宋岛中央平原的邦板牙河三角洲地势低洼，历来是马尼拉都会区以北的"蓄水盆"。西南季风带来的强降雨使河水漫堤，省内十九个市镇有十八个报告洪涝，部分地段积水深约一点二米，底层住户与沿街商铺首当其冲。阿帕利特等城镇的主干道变成河道，居民出行只能依赖渔船或涉水通行，日常秩序被彻底打乱。

## 泽国实录：272村受困，近万人转移

据《马尼拉时报》援引路透社报道，截至8月20日（周四），菲律宾全国已有二百七十二个村庄遭遇严重洪灾，多栋房屋和车辆被淹。在邦板牙省，共有九十一个受灾村庄、九千八百三十九人被安置在各地一百六十七个疏散中心。转移规模虽大，但官方暂时未通报有人员伤亡，说明预警与疏散动员在一定程度上减轻了直接生命损失——然而"零伤亡"的背后，是数以万计家庭生计中断的现实。

## 无人伤亡背后的排涝与预警短板

菲律宾地处台风与西南季风的交汇带，季节性洪涝几乎年年上演，但城市排涝系统长期跟不上城市化速度。排水管网老化、河道被违建与垃圾占用、低洼地带无序扩张，使同等量级的降雨造成越来越严重的内涝。本轮邦板牙十八地齐涝，正说明单纯依靠"灾来即撤"的应急逻辑已难以为继；提升河道行洪能力、硬化预警"最后一公里"，才是降低反复受灾的根本之道。

## 季风常态下的菲律宾减灾考题

对菲律宾而言，季风与台风不是偶发意外，而是每年必须作答的常态考题。气候变化使极端降雨更频繁、更集中，传统经验式的防灾安排愈显吃力。如何从"灾后抢险"转向"事前减损"，涉及城市规划、财政支出与跨区域协同等多重维度。邦板牙省的泽国景象提醒人们：在季风常态下，真正的韧性不在于灾时反应多快，而在于平日把堤坝、管网与预警筑得有多牢。

## 延伸阅读

- **西南季风（Southwest Monsoon）**：每年夏季自西南方向吹向亚洲东南部的湿润气流，是菲律宾雨季与季风性暴雨的主要成因。
- **邦板牙省（Pampanga）**：菲律宾吕宋岛中部省份，地处中央平原河三角洲，地势低洼，历来易遭洪涝侵袭。
- **灾害风险管理（Disaster Risk Management）**：通过预警、疏散、基建与城市规划等手段降低自然灾害社会损失的系统性治理框架。

# Philippines Floods Submerge Hundreds of Villages as Monsoon Rains Trap Pampanga

Intensified by the southwest monsoon, persistent heavy rains have left the Philippines' flood crisis unresolved. North of the capital Manila, eighteen of Pampanga province's nineteen local government units are inundated, and low-lying towns such as Apalit have turned into a water landscape as rivers overflow. As of August 20, 272 villages across the country had suffered severe flooding, with numerous houses and vehicles submerged and nearly ten thousand residents displaced. Thankfully, no casualties have been reported so far. The monsoon deluge has once again exposed the weakness of the Philippines' drainage capacity and disaster early-warning system.

## Monsoon Under Pressure: Eighteen Towns of Pampanga Trapped

The epicentre of this disaster lies in Pampanga. Situated on the Central Luzon plain, the Pampanga River delta is naturally low-lying and has long served as a "catchment basin" north of the Manila metropolitan area. Heavy rains from the southwest monsoon sent rivers over their banks; eighteen of the province's nineteen municipalities reported flooding, with water in some areas reaching about 1.2 metres and ground-floor homes and roadside shops bearing the brunt. In towns like Apalit, main roads became waterways, and residents could move only by boat or on foot through the flood, upending daily order.

## On the Ground: 272 Villages Hit, Nearly 10,000 Displaced

According to the Manila Times citing Reuters, as of Thursday August 20, 272 villages nationwide had suffered severe flooding, with many houses and vehicles submerged. In Pampanga alone, 9,839 people from 91 affected villages were sheltered across 167 evacuation centres. Although the scale of displacement is large, authorities have reported no casualties so far—suggesting that early warning and evacuation efforts mitigated direct loss of life. Behind the "zero casualties" figure, however, lies the disrupted livelihood of tens of thousands of families.

## Drainage and Warning Gaps Behind the Absence of Casualties

The Philippines sits at the convergence of typhoons and the southwest monsoon, and seasonal floods recur almost every year—yet urban drainage systems have long lagged behind the pace of urbanisation. Ageing pipe networks, waterways choked by illegal structures and waste, and unplanned expansion in low-lying areas mean that equal amounts of rainfall now cause ever worse inundation. The simultaneous flooding of eighteen Pampanga towns shows that an emergency logic of "evacuate once the water comes" is no longer sustainable; raising river conveyance capacity and hardening the "last mile" of warning are the real remedies against repeated disaster.

## The Philippines' Disaster-Reduction Test Under a Monsoon Norm

For the Philippines, monsoons and typhoons are not occasional accidents but an annual, routine exam. Climate change makes extreme rainfall more frequent and concentrated, straining traditional, experience-based defences. Shifting from "post-disaster rescue" to "pre-disaster loss reduction" spans urban planning, fiscal spending, and cross-regional coordination. The flooded landscape of Pampanga is a reminder: under a monsoon norm, true resilience lies not in how fast one reacts during a disaster, but in how solidly dykes, pipes, and warnings are built in ordinary times.

## Further reading

- **Southwest Monsoon**: the moist airflow blowing from the southwest toward Southeast Asia each summer, the main driver of the Philippines' rainy season and monsoon floods.
- **Pampanga**: a province in Central Luzon, Philippines, located on a low-lying river delta and historically prone to flooding.
- **Disaster Risk Management**: a systematic governance framework that reduces the social cost of natural hazards through early warning, evacuation, infrastructure, and urban planning.

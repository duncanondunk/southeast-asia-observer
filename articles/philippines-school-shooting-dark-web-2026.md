# 当少年持枪走进教室：菲律宾校园枪击暴露的安全与网络之困

9月18日，菲律宾南部棉兰老岛南哥打巴托省邦加镇一所高中发生枪击，三死七伤，行凶的少年本人也在死者之列。联合早报（Lianhe Zaobao）的两篇连续报道勾勒出一个令人不安的轮廓：这名被班主任称为“品行良好”的优等生，早在8月便开始策划袭击，案发前几小时还提醒要好的同学午饭后回家、避开学案；而警方从其手机恢复的聊天记录与浏览痕迹显示，他深陷暗网（dark web）与名为“真实罪案”（True Crime）的极度暴力网络群组，疑似在“暗网力量的蛊惑”下撬开父亲保险箱取枪行凶。

## 事件：一名优等生的蓄谋

这不是一起冲动行凶。邦加镇镇长帕伦西亚转述警方初步调查称，这名16岁枪手原计划在9月3日动手，因故未实施，最终推迟到18日。更早前，他曾在8月便着手准备。案发当日，他提前数小时叮嘱同伴“午饭后回家”，一些同学因此逃过一劫——这说明同辈并非毫无察觉，只是没有人把玩笑般的警告当真。班主任的印象与监控般的策划之间形成刺眼反差：一个特长班里的好学生，如何一步步走向预先谋划的校园杀戮？

## 暗网与暴力群组：枪手被什么蛊惑

更值得警惕的是诱因的来源。菲律宾内政部与教育部确认，枪手生前极度沉迷暗网与“真实罪案”这类宣扬极度暴力的网络社群。教育部长安加拉表示，他见过枪手留下的文字，内容显示少年“知道自己即将做的事是错的，但似乎有人说服他这么做”。国家警察总长纳尔塔特斯也证实，枪手频繁出入暴力和极端内容。换言之，蛊惑他的不是现实中的同伙，而是屏幕另一端看不见的暗网与群组。这恰恰是当前校园安全最棘手的盲区：校门可以装探测器，却无法探测一个孩子手机里的极端化进程。

## 准备不足：教育部坦承的安保缺口

枪击发生后，教育部长安加拉走访事发学校时坦言：“对于此类枪击事件，我们确实准备不足。”这句话听来坦诚，却也沉重——因为这是菲律宾今年第三起造成多人死亡的校园枪击案。教育部表示已采购更多金属探测器，却未说明何时配发到校；同时计划在改善学生心理健康、增聘辅导人员上着手。镇长帕伦西亚则道出基层现实：学校保安有限，难以对学生逐一筛查，只能寄望班主任在教室门口、家长在孩子离家前做粗略检查。准备不足的背后，是资源与机制的双层缺位。

## 这是今年的第三起：校园枪击为何在菲律宾频发

把视线拉宽，校园枪击在菲律宾并非孤例，却也远未成为常态议题。枪支在菲律宾民间保有量大、管控相对宽松，加上学校安保长期投入不足，构成了脆弱底色；而棉兰老岛虽以历史冲突闻名，邦加镇所在的南哥打巴托并非战区，说明暴力未必只来自地缘冲突，也可能来自本土社会治理的缝隙。更深层的是青少年心理健康系统的薄弱：当一名“好学生”内心早已失衡，学校的辅导员与预警机制却未能接住他。三起案件叠加，拷问的是整个防护体系的迟钝。

## 治理方向：探测器、心理辅导与家长的角色

菲律宾教育部的应对思路已现雏形：硬件上补金属探测器，软件上补心理辅导与辅导员。但真正难的，是补上“事前”这一环。暗网极端化提示我们，校园安全的战场已延伸到网络空间，单靠校门安检远远不够；而心理健康筛查与同伴预警机制的缺位，让本可干预的信号滑过指缝。家长、班主任与网络平台须共同承担起“看见”的责任。否则，每一次事后的“准备不足”，都只是下一次悲剧的注脚。

## 延伸阅读

**暗网（dark web）**：互联网中需特殊软件才能访问的加密层，常游离于常规监管之外，极端与暴力内容在此更易隐匿传播。

**真实罪案（True Crime）**：一类以真实案件、血腥与暴力为卖点的网络社群或内容形态，对心智未熟的青少年存在诱导风险。

**金属探测器（metal detector）**：菲律宾教育部计划增配的校园安检设备，反映当局以硬件补短板的应急思路，但配发与常态化使用仍待落实。

# When a teenager walked into class with a gun: the security and cyber blind spots exposed by the Philippines school shooting

On 18 September, a shooting at a high school in Banga, South Cotabato, on the southern Philippine island of Mindanao, left three dead and seven wounded; the teenage gunman was among the dead. Two consecutive reports by Lianhe Zaobao sketch an unsettling picture. The shooter, described by his homeroom teacher as a well-behaved honors student, had been planning the attack since August. Hours before the shooting he warned close friends to go home after lunch to avoid the incident; recovered phone chats and browsing records showed he was deeply immersed in the dark web and in an extremely violent online group called "True Crime," and appears to have pried open his father's safe for the gun under the sway of dark-web influence.

## The incident: a premeditated attack by an honors student

This was no impulsive outburst. Banga Mayor Palencia, relaying the police's preliminary findings, said the 16-year-old gunman had originally planned to strike on 3 September but did not follow through, postponing to the 18th. Preparations dated back to August. On the day itself, he told companions hours ahead to "go home after lunch," and some classmates stayed away — meaning peers were not entirely unaware, yet nobody treated the joking warning as real. The contrast between the teacher's impression and the methodical planning is stark: how does a good student in a gifted track step toward a premeditated campus killing?

## The dark web and violent groups: what seduced the shooter

The more alarming question is the source of the inducement. The Philippine Department of the Interior and the Department of Education confirmed the shooter had been obsessively consumed by the dark web and by "True Crime," a network community that glorifies extreme violence. Education Secretary Angara said he had seen the shooter's writings, which suggested the boy "knew what he was about to do was wrong, but someone seemed to have convinced him to do it." National Police chief Nartates confirmed the shooter frequently consumed violent and extremist content. In other words, he was not radicalized by real-world accomplices but by invisible forums on the other side of a screen. This is precisely the hardest blind spot in campus security today: a school gate can be fitted with detectors, but no device can scan the radicalization unfolding inside a child's phone.

## "Underprepared": the security gap the education chief admitted

After the shooting, Education Secretary Angara visited the school and conceded: "We were indeed underprepared for this kind of shooting." The candor is striking — and heavy — because this is the third school shooting in the Philippines this year to cause multiple deaths. The department says it has purchased more metal detectors but has not said when they will reach schools, and it plans to improve student mental health and hire more counseling aides. Mayor Palencia laid out the grassroots reality: schools have limited security staff and cannot screen every student, so they must rely on homeroom teachers checking at classroom doors and parents checking belongings before children leave home. Behind the admission of unpreparedness lies a double gap in both resources and mechanism.

## The third this year: why school shootings keep happening in the Philippines

Zooming out, campus shootings are not isolated in the Philippines, yet they have not become a sustained public agenda either. A large, loosely regulated civilian firearms stock and chronically underfunded school security form a fragile baseline. Mindanao is known for historical conflict, but South Cotabato's Banga is not a war zone — which shows violence need not only stem from geopolitics but can also seep through the cracks of local social governance. Deeper still is the weakness of adolescent mental-health systems: when a "good student" is already unraveling inside, the school's counselors and early-warning mechanisms failed to catch him. Three cases stacked together indict the sluggishness of the entire protective system.

## The way forward: detectors, counseling, and the role of parents

The Philippine education department's response is taking shape: hardware in the form of metal detectors, software in the form of mental-health support and more counselors. The truly hard part, however, is closing the "before" gap. The dark-web radicalization vector tells us the battlefield of campus safety has extended into cyberspace; gate checks alone are far from enough. Meanwhile, the absence of mental-health screening and peer early-warning let signals that could have been intervened upon slip through fingers. Parents, homeroom teachers, and online platforms must jointly take up the responsibility to "see." Otherwise, every post-hoc admission of being "underprepared" is merely a footnote to the next tragedy.

## Further reading

**Dark web:** An encrypted layer of the internet reachable only through special software, often beyond ordinary regulation, where extreme and violent content can hide and spread more easily.

**True Crime:** A genre of online communities or content built around real cases, gore, and violence, carrying an indoctrination risk for mentally immature adolescents.

**Metal detector:** Campus security equipment the Philippine education department plans to procure more of, reflecting an emergency approach of using hardware to plug gaps — yet deployment and routine use remain to be implemented.

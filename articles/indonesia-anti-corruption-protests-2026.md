# 印尼（Indonesia）万人集会要求反腐：资产没收法案能否兑现承诺？

8月27日，印度尼西亚（Indonesia）首都雅加达（Jakarta）、日惹、望加锡等多地爆发反腐败集会，数千名示威者聚集在雅加达国会大厦外，要求国会加快通过"资产没收法案"，并以死刑严惩贪腐分子。这场集会恰逢去年（2025年）致死全国动乱的一周年，警方严阵以待，在雅加达部署1.85万警力。

国会副议长苏夫米当天作出罕见让步，向示威代表承诺，延宕多年的资产没收法案最迟将于12月15日通过。但现场气氛高度紧张：部分佩戴头盔、面罩的示威者试图冲撞国会大门、投掷杂物，并与驱赶街头小贩的警察发生推搡。

## 周年之忌：动乱记忆下的集会

选择8月27日，本身就是一种表态。示威组织者凯鲁说，去年此时爆发了骚乱与种种事件，如今他们要"表达诉求，而不是引发骚乱"。这话背后，是2025年8至9月那场因民生与政治矛盾升级的全国抗议——最终演变为流血暴乱，造成10人死亡、近7000人被捕。

正因如此，当局如临大敌。警方在国会大厦内外设下重重防线、封锁周边主干道，并在集会前拘留65名涉嫌携带汽油弹、气枪等危险物品的嫌犯。中爪哇巴蒂的"帕蒂团结民众联盟"等民间团体自上周起组织巴士，将支持者送往雅加达。

## 资产没收法案：延宕十七年的承诺

示威的核心诉求，是一部悬置十七年的法律。资产没收法案最早于2008年提出，允许政府在未定罪情况下，没收与腐败、洗钱相关者的资产。它被视为打击贪腐的利器，却也因可能被用于政治打压或敲诈勒索，在国会长期搁置。

此次副议长的承诺，是抗议压力下的罕见松动。但"最迟12月15日通过"仍是一个将来式——届时能否落地、落地后如何界定"与犯罪相关资产"，都将考验这部法律的成色。专家已警告，若缺乏程序护栏，利器亦可为刀。

## 愤怒所向：物价、免费餐与军事化

示威者的不满，并不止于一部法案。组织者凯鲁明确提出，政府应停止"免费营养餐计划"与军事化政策，并降低主食价格。免费营养餐是普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）政府的旗舰社会福利项目，却因成本与分配争议成为众矢之的；而物价高企、生计承压，才是把民众推上街头的底层逻辑。

反腐诉求与民生焦虑在此交汇：当民众既怀疑官员贪腐、又感受物价沉重，反腐败便从制度议题变成了情绪出口。

## 隐忧：利器亦可为刀

资产没收法案若顺利通过，确能为反腐提供实质工具；但若程序失守，亦可能沦为针对性武器。印尼的反腐叙事，从来不只是"严不严"的问题，更是"对谁严、由谁界定"的问题。

8月27日的火光与呼声，提醒人们：民众的耐心有年限，而制度的回应，必须跑在下一个周年之前。

## 延伸阅读

**资产没收法案（Asset Confiscation Bill）**：允许政府在未定罪情况下没收与腐败、洗钱相关资产的法律，2008年首提，因可能遭滥用于政治打压而长期搁置。

**免费营养餐计划（Free Nutritious Meals）**：普拉博沃政府旗舰社会福利项目，因成本与分配争议成为示威者诉求焦点之一。

**去年全国动乱（2025）**：2025年8–9月因民生与政治矛盾爆发的抗议，演变为流血暴乱，10死、近7000人被捕，构成本次集会的历史背景。

# Indonesia's Mass Anti-Corruption Protests: Will the Asset Confiscation Bill Deliver?

On 27 August, thousands rallied across Indonesia — in Jakarta, Yogyakarta and Makassar — gathering outside parliament in the capital to demand that lawmakers fast-track the Asset Confiscation Bill and impose the death penalty for corruption. The rally fell on the first anniversary of last year's (2025) deadly nationwide unrest, and police deployed 18,500 officers in Jakarta alone.

Deputy Speaker Sufmi made a rare concession that day, promising demonstrators' representatives that the long-stalled bill would pass by 15 December at the latest. But the scene was tense: some protesters in helmets and masks tried to storm the gates and hurl objects, and clashed with police clearing a street vendor.

## An anniversary of unrest: rallying under memory

The choice of 27 August was itself a statement. Organiser Keru said last year at this time riots and various events erupted; now they wanted "to express demands, not cause chaos." Behind that line lies the August–September 2025 protest wave — born of livelihood and political grievances, it spiralled into deadly riots that left 10 dead and nearly 7,000 arrested.

That history explains the heavy posture. Police ringed parliament with barriers, sealed off main roads, and detained 65 suspects before the rally for allegedly carrying petrol bombs and air guns. Civil groups such as the Pati Solidarity People's Alliance from Central Java had since last week organised buses to ferry supporters to Jakarta.

## The Asset Confiscation Bill: a 17-year-stalled promise

At the heart of the rally is a law suspended for seventeen years. First proposed in 2008, the bill would let the government confiscate assets linked to corruption and money-laundering without a conviction. Hailed as a potent anti-graft tool, it has languished in parliament for fear it could be turned against political foes or used for extortion.

The deputy speaker's pledge is a rare crack under pressure. But "pass by 15 December at the latest" remains future tense — whether it lands, and how "crime-linked assets" are then defined, will test the law's mettle. Experts already warn that without procedural guardrails, a sharp tool can cut both ways.

## Where the anger points: prices, free meals, militarisation

Protesters' grievance reaches beyond one bill. Keru explicitly demanded the government halt the Free Nutritious Meals programme and militarisation policy, and lower staple-food prices. The meal plan is President Prabowo's flagship welfare project, yet has become a target over cost and distribution disputes; but it is high prices and squeezed livelihoods that truly drove people onto the streets.

Anti-corruption demand and livelihood anxiety converge here: when citizens both suspect official graft and feel the pinch of prices, fighting corruption shifts from an institutional issue to an emotional outlet.

## The dark side: a sharp tool that can cut both ways

If the bill passes cleanly, it could hand anti-corruption real teeth; but if procedure slips, it may become a targeted weapon. Indonesia's corruption story has never been only about being "tough enough" — it is about whom it is tough on, and who defines that.

The flames and chants of 27 August are a reminder: public patience has a deadline, and institutional response must arrive before the next anniversary.

# 印尼（Indonesia）三季度GDP增长5.47%：看似稳健的答卷，究竟是真韧性还是「喂」出来的？

据印尼中央统计局11月5日公布的数据，2026年第三季度印尼国内生产总值（GDP）同比增长5.47%，不仅高于第二季度的5.29%，也高于去年同期的5.04%；前三季度累计增长5.45%。在林火烟霾未散、燃油短缺蔓延、本币近乎历史低位、资本市场还悬着被国际指数降级阴影的当口，这个增速乍看很「能打」——经济统筹部长艾尔朗加甚至把它拿去和二十国集团成员国比：高于中国的4.5%、韩国的3.4%、德国的1.2%、日本的0.7%，只是不及印度的7.8%。

可数据的体面背后，藏着一道绕不开的追问：这5.47%，到底是经济的真实韧性，还是财政「喂」出来的？

## 数字：第三季度增长5.47%，看似稳了

先把账摆清楚。按不变价格计算，三季度印尼GDP达到3633.2万亿印尼盾（约1447亿元人民币）；按现行价格计则达6741万亿印尼盾（约2685亿元人民币）。环比看，三季度较二季度增长1.59%。从需求端拆开，居民消费仍是压舱石：占GDP比重51.97%，单季同比增长5.04%，为整体增长贡献了2.62个百分点。投资（固定资本形成总额）同比增长6.06%，占GDP的30.81%。政府消费同比增长8.95%，占7.28%。

表面看，三驾马车各有贡献，外需之外靠内需顶着。可一旦往下细看，问题就来了。

## 结构：增长靠的是「吃」和「花」

印尼这轮增长，底色是内需，不是出口。从产业端看，制造业增加值同比增长5.87%，贸易业增长7.14%，建筑业增长6.42%——五大行业合计占GDP的65.31%，靠的都是国内消费与投资循环。这当然不算坏事，但在全球需求降温、大宗商品价格回落的背景下，一个资源型大国把增长押在内需上，意味着它对外生冲击的缓冲越来越薄。

更关键的是，内需里「政府花」的那一块，涨得格外猛。

## 政府消费：8.95%背后的免费餐与加薪

三季度政府消费同比增长8.95%，远高于去年同期的5.66%。驱动因素很直白：公务员薪资与津贴上调，以及「免费营养餐」（MBG）计划向民众发放物资的支出。换句话说，财政正在用「发钱」和「供餐」直接托住增长。普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）政府的MBG计划本意是改善儿童营养、拉动基层需求，但当它成为单季政府消费近9%涨幅的重要推手，就得问一句：这到底是在投资未来，还是在用当期支出买一季的漂亮数字？

财政系统稳定委员会（KSSK）显然心里有数。它在11月3日把2026年全年增长预测从8月时的5.6%—6.0%下调至5.4%—5.5%。一边是三季度「稳了」的表态，一边是全年目标悄悄往下调，这种「嘴上稳、手下调」的反差，本身就说明官方对成色并不全然放心。

## 家庭消费：阴霾与油荒中逆流而上

真正让人意外的是家庭消费。在林火烟霾困扰、燃油短缺扩散、资本市场承压的夹击下，居民消费仍同比增长5.04%，没有塌方。这既说明印尼庞大的人口基本面仍在，也说明MBG这类转移支付对底层购买力确有托底作用。可也要看到，这种「逆流而上」更多是防御性的——民众在不确定性里维持基本开支，而非信心扩张。

一旦把视野拉到区域，印尼的相对优势就显出来了：菲律宾（Philippines）三季度增速据日经亚洲（Nikkei Asia）报道仅2.1%，被通胀啃食；泰国（Thailand）靠中资与旅游业勉强撑场；越南（Vietnam）虽快但贸易逆差创纪录。对比之下，印尼5.47%确实「能打」。只是参照系放低了，不代表自身没有软肋。

## 投资与制造：内需撑起的底盘

投资端同比增长6.06%，投资统筹委员会（BKPM）公布的实际投资额增长7.59%，下游加工与基础设施项目是主力。制造业5.87%、建筑业6.42%的增速，说明国内投资循环仍在转。但问题是，这一底盘高度依赖政府优先项目与国企（国有企业）主导的下游化战略，民间与外资的边际拉动并不扎实。一旦财政赤字的腾挪空间收窄，投资的持续性就要打个问号。

## 暗面：烟霾、油荒与MSCI降级阴影

体面的增速下，三道阴影挥之不去。其一，林火与烟霾不仅损害健康，还导致大规模航班延误、扰乱生产，直接折损经济产出。其二，燃油短缺蔓延至巴厘岛等11个省，燃油补贴支出前三季度已达331.4万亿印尼盾（同比增长52.1%），逼近3%的赤字红线，能源安全被中东（the Middle East）冲突与群岛物流的脆弱双重绑架。其三，MSCI拟将印尼从新兴市场重分类为独立市场的咨询仍悬在头顶，资本外流与金融市场的不确定性远未消散，印尼盾近历史低位就是最直白的定价。

换句话说，5.47%是「顶着风」跑出来的，不是「顺风」跑出来的。

## 全年目标：5.4%—5.5%的窄走廊

KSSK把全年目标压到5.4%—5.5%，意味着四季度只要大体持平即可达标——这本身是个保守的信号。艾尔朗加说「目标与三季度表现大致相近」，可三季度的高基数里掺了相当分量的政府消费水分。若四季度财政补贴与薪资脉冲减弱，要守住5.4%，难度并不小。

更重要的是，5.5%附近会不会成为印尼增长的新天花板？当出口受外需拖累、投资靠财政续命、消费靠转移支付托底，结构性的增速平台或许正在悄悄下移。

## 启示：5.5%的韧性，究竟是谁的韧性？

日经亚洲在其「本周亚洲」栏目中将「印尼公布三季度GDP」列为当周焦点，足见外界对这片市场的关注。但数据告诉我们：印尼的韧性，更多是国家体量与财政手腕共同撑起的数字，而非结构优化的自然结果。免费餐喂出的消费、加薪撑起的政府支出、下游化战略托住的投资，三者叠加成一季的体面，却也把增长的「含财政量」越推越高。

对一个志在摆脱中等收入陷阱、又要在中美之间走钢丝的新兴经济体来说，真正的考题从来不是某一季的5.47%，而是当补贴退潮、外需转冷、资本重新定价时，还剩多少不被「喂」出来的增长。印尼交出的这份答卷，体面，却远未到可以松口气的时候。

## 延伸阅读

**免费营养餐计划（MBG / Free Nutritious Meals）**：普拉博沃政府旗舰民生工程，向学童与基层民众免费供餐，旨在改善营养并拉动内需，但单季已成政府消费近9%涨幅的重要推手，财政可持续性存疑。

**财政系统稳定委员会（KSSK / Financial System Stability Committee）**：由印尼财政部长牵头、央行与金融监管机构参与的跨部门委员会，11月3日将2026年全年增长预测下调至5.4%—5.5%，释放保守信号。

**独立市场重分类（Standalone Market）**：MSCI将某国从新兴市场移出、单列处理的机制，意味可投资性被全球指数机构否定，往往触发被动与主动资金外流，印尼正面临此咨询风险。

# Indonesia's Q3 GDP Grows 5.47%: Real Resilience or Growth Fed by Fiscal Handouts?

According to Statistics Indonesia, Southeast Asia's largest economy expanded 5.47% year on year in the third quarter of 2026 — above the 5.29% posted in Q2 and the 5.04% a year earlier, with year-to-date growth at 5.45%. The print looks reassuring on its face: Coordinating Economic Minister Airlangga Hartarto was quick to benchmark it against G20 peers, noting it beat China's 4.5%, South Korea's 3.4%, Germany's 1.2% and Japan's 0.7%, though still trailing India's 7.8%.

Yet behind the respectable headline lies an uncomfortable question: is this 5.47% genuine economic resilience, or growth effectively "fed" by the state?

## The numbers: 5.47% in Q3, steady on paper

Let the accounts speak. At constant prices, Q3 GDP reached Rp3,633.2 trillion (about US$201 billion); at current prices it hit Rp6,741.0 trillion (about US$372.9 billion). Quarter on quarter, the economy grew 1.59%. On the expenditure side, household consumption remained the anchor — 51.97% of GDP, up 5.04% year on year, contributing 2.62 percentage points to overall growth. Investment, or gross fixed capital formation, rose 6.06% and accounted for 30.81% of GDP. Government consumption grew 8.95%, making up 7.28%.

On the surface, all three engines contributed, with domestic demand carrying the load beyond exports. But look one layer deeper and the cracks appear.

## The composition: growth that runs on "eating" and "spending"

This round of expansion is fundamentally domestic-demand-driven, not export-led. By industry, manufacturing value added rose 5.87%, trade 7.14% and construction 6.42% — the five largest sectors together making up 65.31% of GDP, all powered by the domestic consumption–investment loop. That is not inherently bad, but for a resource-rich economy to stake its growth on domestic demand while global demand cools and commodity prices soften means its buffer against external shocks is getting thinner.

More tellingly, the "government spending" slice of domestic demand grew unusually fast.

## Government consumption: the 8.95% behind free meals and pay raises

Government consumption climbed 8.95% in Q3, far above the 5.66% a year earlier. The drivers are blunt: higher civil-service salaries and allowances, plus expenditure on goods and services distributed to the public through the Free Nutritious Meals (MBG) programme. In other words, the state is directly propping up growth by handing out pay and meals. President Prabowo's MBG scheme is meant to improve child nutrition and stimulate grassroots demand, but when it becomes a principal force behind a near-9% quarterly jump in government consumption, one must ask: is this investing in the future, or buying a pretty number for one quarter with current spending?

The Financial System Stability Committee (KSSK) evidently knows the answer. On November 3 it cut its 2026 full-year growth projection from 5.6%–6.0% (set in August) to 5.4%–5.5%. On one side, upbeat talk of a "steady" Q3; on the other, a quietly lowered annual target. That "steady mouth, lowered hand" contrast itself signals official unease about the quality of the print.

## Household consumption: defying haze and fuel shortages

The real surprise is household consumption. Besieged by forest-fire haze, spreading fuel shortages and a pressured capital market, household spending still grew 5.04% without collapsing. That speaks both to Indonesia's sheer demographic base and to the genuine floor that transfers like MBG provide to grassroots purchasing power. Yet this "swimming against the current" is largely defensive — households maintaining basic outlays amid uncertainty, not confidently expanding.

Set against the region, Indonesia's relative edge stands out: the Philippines grew just 2.1% in Q3 per Nikkei Asia, eaten away by inflation; Thailand scrapes by on Chinese capital and tourism; Vietnam is fast but runs record trade deficits. Next to those, Indonesia's 5.47% indeed "punches." But a lowered benchmark does not mean the soft spots are gone.

## Investment and manufacturing: a domestic-demand base

Investment rose 6.06%, with the Investment Coordinating Board (BKPM) reporting realized investment up 7.59%, led by downstream-processing and infrastructure projects. Manufacturing's 5.87% and construction's 6.42% show the domestic investment cycle still turning. The problem is that this base leans heavily on government-priority projects and the downstreaming strategy led by state-owned enterprises; the marginal pull from private and foreign capital is not solid. Once fiscal room to manoeuvre narrows, the durability of investment comes into question.

## The underside: haze, fuel shortages and the MSCI downgrade shadow

Beneath the tidy growth rate, three shadows loom. First, forest fires and haze not only harm health but cause mass flight delays and disrupt production, directly shaving economic output. Second, fuel shortages have spread to Bali and eleven other provinces; energy subsidies reached Rp331.4 trillion (about US$18 billion) in the first three quarters, up 52.1% year on year and approaching the 3% deficit red line, with energy security double-bound by the Middle East conflict and fragile archipelagic logistics. Third, MSCI's consultation to reclassify Indonesia from emerging to a standalone market still hangs overhead; capital outflows and financial-market uncertainty are far from dissipated, and the rupiah near record lows is the bluntest pricing of that risk.

In short, 5.47% was run "into the wind," not "with the wind."

## The full-year target: a narrow 5.4–5.5% corridor

KSSK's trimming of the annual target to 5.4%–5.5% means Q4 only needs to hold roughly steady to hit the mark — itself a conservative signal. Airlangga says the target is "roughly close to the Q3 performance," yet that high base is laced with a fair dose of government-consumption water. If fiscal subsidy and wage impulses fade in Q4, defending 5.4% will be no easy task.

More fundamentally, is 5.5% becoming Indonesia's new growth ceiling? With exports dragged by external demand, investment sustained by the state, and consumption propped by transfers, the structural growth platform may be quietly shifting lower.

## The takeaway: whose resilience is 5.5%?

Nikkei Asia flagged "Indonesia reports GDP for the July–September quarter" as a weekly focus in its "Your Week in Asia" column, a sign of how much outside attention this market draws. But the data tells us this: Indonesia's resilience is a number propped up by national scale and fiscal finesse, not the natural result of structural optimisation. Consumption fed by free meals, government spending buoyed by pay raises, investment held up by a downstreaming strategy — stacked together they produce one decent quarter, while pushing the "fiscal content" of growth ever higher.

For an emerging economy bent on escaping the middle-income trap while walking a tightrope between China and the United States, the real test was never a single quarter's 5.47%. It is how much growth remains once subsidies ebb, external demand cools and capital reprices. The report card Indonesia handed in is respectable — but far from a moment to exhale.

## Further reading

**Free Nutritious Meals (MBG)**: President Prabowo's flagship welfare programme providing free meals to schoolchildren and grassroots communities, intended to improve nutrition and stimulate demand; it has become a major driver of a near-9% quarterly jump in government consumption, raising questions over fiscal sustainability.

**Financial System Stability Committee (KSSK)**: An inter-agency committee led by Indonesia's finance minister with the central bank and financial regulators; on November 3 it cut the 2026 full-year growth projection to 5.4%–5.5%, a conservative signal.

**Standalone Market**: An MSCI mechanism that removes a country from emerging-market indices and lists it separately, signalling that global index providers judge it uninvestable and typically triggering passive and active capital outflows; Indonesia currently faces this consultation risk.

# 从抽签到志愿：泰国青年为何排队参军？

泰国每年四月的征兵抽签现场向来是媒体焦点——21岁男子在众目睽睽下抽取红黑卡片，红色意味着两年兵役，黑色则免役。但近年来，一个悄然变化正在改写这一百年传统：越来越多泰国青年主动报名参军，部分选区的征兵配额甚至完全由志愿者填满，抽签仪式沦为形式。在经济增速放缓、青年失业率约为全国平均五倍的背景下，军营正成为年轻人寻求稳定收入的避风港。阿努廷政府顺势推出“志愿兵”计划，将这一趋势制度化。

## 经济寒潮中的“铁饭碗”

泰国经济2026年增长明显放缓，第一季度GDP仅增1.9%，制造业PMI虽维持扩张但就业市场对年轻人尤其严酷。据彭博社数据，泰国青年失业率约为全国平均水平的五倍。对于刚步入职场的年轻人而言，私营部门的工作不仅稀缺且多为临时性——建筑日结、外卖配送、便利店兼职，收入不稳定且无社会保障。相比之下，军队提供每月约1.1万泰铢（约2400元人民币）的固定薪资，外加免费住宿、餐饮和医疗——这在当前就业市场中堪称“铁饭碗”。22岁的Manyum Chawanakorn在接受采访时直言：“没有真正被送上战场的风险，但有收入的保证。”他此前与父母一起打零工，如今期待通过服役获得士官培训机会。

## 志愿入伍率连年攀升

过去五年间，泰国志愿参军人数稳步上升。在大马尼拉郊区的一个选区，685名合格者中有52人主动报名，恰好填满该区配额——这意味着抽签环节已无必要。曼谷市中心部分区域，2026年的年轻人甚至在抽签前就完成了报名。全国范围内，在线申请人数已超过军方预期目标。军方预计，今年的最终志愿入伍人数将超过去年。一些新兵坦言，志愿报名可以避免抽签的不确定性——“我不愿冒被抽中后服更长期兵役的风险，不如自己申请，至少能掌握主动权。”22岁的数字媒体专业学生Purithat Thatvorchaiwat说。

## 阿努廷的“志愿兵”新政

2026年9月6日，总理府发言人拉差达·塔纳迪雷宣布，政府正式推进“志愿兵”（Volunteer Soldiers）计划，作为从1905年延续至今的抽签制向全志愿兵役制过渡的关键一步。根据该计划，2027财年陆军将招收2.5万名志愿兵，其中陆军占18,784名、海军3,496名、空军2,174名。志愿者月薪加补贴合计1.5万泰铢（约3300元人民币），合同期四年，可续签一次（总计不超过八年）。申请时间为2026年9月10日至2027年1月24日，可通过网站rcm.rta.mi.th在线申请或前往全国各征兵办公室现场报名。军官职位要求本科学历、年龄18–35岁；士官要求高中或职业教育学历、年龄18–30岁。

## 四大“诱人机会”

政府为志愿兵项目包装了四大卖点：其一，收入与福利——1.5万泰铢月薪加医疗、住房灾害补助和人寿保险；其二，自我提升——通过实际工作获得训练和经验，增强纪律与身心素质；其三，教育与职业拓展——可在服役期间继续学业或考取职业证书，退役后出路更宽；其四，未来职业延伸——陆军士官生考试为志愿兵保留80%配额，公务员录用考试享受额外学术加分。这套组合拳直击年轻人的痛点：既有眼前收入保障，又有长远职业路径。

## 兵役制改革的深层逻辑

泰国自1905年以来实行的抽签制长期以来饱受批评——随机性大、易滋生腐败（富裕家庭可通过关系免役）、且两年义务兵役对个人职业规划冲击严重。阿努廷政府将兵役改革写入国会施政承诺，目标是在2024至2027年间逐步淘汰抽签制，转向全志愿兵役制。国防部的逻辑很清晰：志愿兵更具积极性、训练效果更好，且能根据任务需求精准筛选人才，而非被动接受抽签结果。对国家而言，一支自愿入伍的军队在士气与专业性上天然优于强制征募的队伍。

## 区域比较中的泰国模式

放眼东南亚，各国的兵役制度差异显著。新加坡实行全民义务兵役（男性须服两年），但以高薪和精英化训练著称；越南保留义务兵役但正探索职业化改革；印尼则基本依赖职业军人。泰国的“志愿兵”模式介于新加坡的强制义务与印尼的职业化之间——它保留了兵役的普遍性（面向所有适龄男性），但用经济激励替代了强制力。这种“软性过渡”既避免了废除抽签可能引发的政治反弹，又顺应了劳动力市场的现实压力。

## 民意与代际：为何是此刻

泰国青年对兵役的态度正在经历代际转变。十年前，抽签现场的红卡落定往往伴随着当场落泪，兵役被视为打断学业与职业的“劫难”；如今，社交平台上“志愿入伍”的叙事逐渐被包装成“稳定出路”与“成人礼”。推动这一转变的，不只是经济寒潮，还有军方自身的形象工程——国防部在招募季投放的短视频以幽默口吻消解兵役的严肃性，将服役描绘为“加入大家庭”“保家卫国”的荣誉选择。与此同时，2025年泰柬边境冲突后民族情绪升温，也让部分年轻人将对国家的朴素情感转化为参军意愿。但批评者提醒，把结构性就业困境包装成“个人选择”，可能掩盖了劳动力市场失灵的真相：当参军成为低技能青年“没出路”时的默认选项，所谓“志愿”便掺杂了无奈。

## 风险与隐忧

然而，将军队变成“就业蓄水池”也引发担忧。首先，军队的核心职能是国防而非社会救济，过度强调经济吸引力可能稀释军事专业性。其次，1.5万泰铢的月薪虽高于传统义务兵（约8000–9000泰铢净到手），但仍低于制造业平均工资，吸引的主要是低技能劳动力——这可能加剧社会阶层固化，即“没出路的去当兵”。第三，若经济持续低迷导致志愿兵激增，国防预算能否支撑扩编后的薪酬与福利开支？国防部已为此预留14.5亿泰铢（约3.2亿元人民币）用于2027财年，但长期可持续性存疑。

## 延伸阅读

**征兵抽签制（Conscription lottery）**：泰国自1905年延续至今的兵役选拔制度，年满21岁男性须报到抽签，红卡约服两年、黑卡免役；阿努廷政府正推动以志愿兵制逐步取代之。

**志愿兵制（Volunteer Military Service）**：一种完全由公民自愿报名参军的兵役制度，区别于强制征兵或抽签制。泰国正从1905年延续的抽签制向此模式过渡，目标是2027年前基本实现全志愿兵役。

**阿努廷·参威拉恭（Anutin Charnvirakul）**：泰国现任总理兼内政部长，泰自豪党党魁，推动“志愿兵”计划是其核心施政承诺之一，同时主导大麻医疗合法化等争议政策。

# From Lottery to Volunteering: Why Young Thais Are Lining Up to Enlist

Thailand's annual April conscription lottery has long been a media spectacle: 21-year-old men draw red or black cards before crowds—red means two years of military service, black grants exemption. Yet a quiet shift is rewriting this century-old tradition: growing numbers of young Thais are volunteering for service, and in some districts the April quota is met entirely by volunteers, rendering the lottery ceremonial. With economic growth slowing and youth unemployment roughly five times the national average, the barracks are becoming a haven for young people seeking stable income. The Anutin government has seized the moment, institutionalising the trend through its "Volunteer Soldiers" programme.

## The "iron rice bowl" in an economic chill

Thailand's economy has slowed markedly in 2026, with first-quarter GDP growth at just 1.9 per cent. While manufacturing PMI remains in expansionary territory, the labour market is especially harsh on the young. According to Bloomberg, youth unemployment in Thailand is about five times the national average. For those entering the workforce now, private-sector jobs are not only scarce but often precarious—casual construction work, food delivery, convenience-store shifts, with unstable income and no social safety net. The military, by contrast, offers around 11,000 baht (about US$340) per month plus free accommodation, food and healthcare—a veritable "iron rice bowl" in the current job market. "There's no real certainty of being sent to war," said 22-year-old Manyum Chawanakorn after being measured for his uniform at a Bangkok draft centre. "But there's a guarantee of income." He had been taking on casual construction jobs alongside his parents; now he hopes to pursue non-commissioned officer training after completing initial service.

## Rising voluntary enlistment rates

Over the past five years, voluntary enlistment in Thailand has climbed steadily. In one suburban district outside Bangkok, 52 of 685 eligible men volunteered—exactly meeting the quota and eliminating the need for a lottery draw. In parts of central Bangkok, young men signed up in 2026 without waiting for the April lottery at all. Nationwide, online applications have already surpassed the military's targets. While final figures will not be finalised until mid-April, the armed forces expect numbers to exceed last year's. Some recruits admit that volunteering spares them the uncertainty of the draft. "I wouldn't want to risk getting drafted," said 22-year-old digital media student Purithat Thatvorchaiwat. "I'd rather apply and get it over with. At least it gives me something to do after graduation."

## Anutin's "Volunteer Soldiers" programme

On September 6, 2026, Prime Minister's Office spokesperson Ratchada Thanadirek announced that the government was formally advancing its "Volunteer Soldiers" (TOS) initiative—a key step in Thailand's transition from the conscription lottery system in place since 1905 towards an all-volunteer force. Under the plan, fiscal year 2027 will see 25,000 volunteer soldiers recruited across all branches: 18,784 for the Army, 3,496 for the Navy, 2,174 for the Air Force, plus smaller allocations for headquarters and defence ministry roles. Volunteers receive a combined salary and allowance of 15,000 baht (about US$430) per month on four-year contracts, renewable once (maximum eight years total). Applications run from 10 September 2026 to 24 January 2027 via the online portal rcm.rta.mi.th or at recruitment offices nationwide. Officer positions require a bachelor's degree and age 18–35; NCO positions require high school or vocational certification and age 18–30.

## Four "attractive opportunities"

The government has packaged four key selling points for the programme: first, income and benefits—15,000 baht monthly plus medical care, disaster relief assistance and life insurance; second, self-development and skill-building through real work experience that fosters discipline and physical fitness; third, educational and professional advancement, including opportunities to pursue secondary or vocational certifications during service; fourth, future career extension, with 80 per cent of Army NCO exam slots reserved for enlisted personnel and extra academic points in civil-service examinations. This combination directly addresses young people's pain points: immediate income security plus a credible long-term career pathway.

## The deeper logic of conscription reform

Thailand's lottery-based conscription, in place since 1905, has long drawn fire for its randomness, corruption risks (wealthy families can secure exemptions through connections) and the two-year service term's disruptive impact on individual career planning. The Anutin government made conscription reform a parliamentary commitment, aiming to phase out the lottery between 2024 and 2027 in favour of an all-volunteer force. The defence ministry's logic is clear: volunteers are more motivated, train better, and allow the military to select personnel matched to mission needs rather than passively accepting lottery results. For the nation, a voluntarily enlisted army carries inherently higher morale and professionalism than one built on compulsion.

## Thailand's model in regional comparison

Across Southeast Asia, military service systems vary sharply. Singapore mandates universal national service (two years for men) but compensates with elite-level training and high pay; Vietnam retains conscription while exploring professionalisation; Indonesia relies almost entirely on career soldiers. Thailand's "volunteer soldier" model falls between Singapore's compulsory service and Indonesia's professional force—it preserves universality (open to all eligible men) but substitutes economic incentives for coercion. This "soft transition" avoids the political backlash of abolishing the lottery outright while responding to labour-market realities.

## Public mood and the generational shift: why now

Thai youth attitudes toward military service are undergoing a generational shift. A decade ago, the moment a red card was drawn at the lottery often drew tears on the spot, conscription seen as a "calamity" interrupting studies and careers; today, social media narratives increasingly recast "volunteering" as a "stable path" and a "rite of passage." Driving this shift is not only the economic chill but also the military's own image management—in recruitment season the defence ministry releases short videos that use humour to soften the seriousness of service, portraying enlistment as joining a "big family" and a honourable choice to "protect the homeland." At the same time, nationalist sentiment warmed after the 2025 Thailand–Cambodia border clashes, converting some young people's plain patriotism into willingness to enlist. But critics warn that packaging structural unemployment as a "personal choice" may obscure the real failure of the labour market: when enlistment becomes the default option for low-skilled youth "with no other way out," the "voluntary" label carries a note of resignation.

## Risks and hidden concerns

Yet turning the military into an "employment reservoir" raises concerns. First, the military's core function is defence, not social welfare; over-emphasising economic attraction may dilute professional standards. Second, while 15,000 baht exceeds the roughly 8,000–9,000 baht net take-home of traditional conscripts, it remains below manufacturing average wages—risking that the programme primarily attracts low-skilled labour, potentially reinforcing class stratification ("those without options join the army"). Third, if a sluggish economy drives a surge in volunteers, can the defence budget sustain expanded payroll and benefits? The ministry has earmarked 1.45 billion baht (about US$41 million) for FY2027, but long-term sustainability is uncertain.

## Further reading

**Conscription lottery**: Thailand's century-old military draft system, in effect since 1905, requiring men to report for selection in the year they turn 21; participants draw coloured cards—red for approximately two years of service, black for exemption.

**Volunteer Soldiers (TOS) programme**: Thailand's new initiative to transition from conscription to an all-volunteer force, offering 15,000 baht/month, four-year contracts, and pathways to NCO roles and civil-service careers; applications open September 2026–January 2027.

**Anutin Charnvirakul**: Current Thai Prime Minister and Interior Minister, leader of the Bhumjaithai Party; the Volunteer Soldiers policy is among his key parliamentary pledges, alongside controversial moves such as medical cannabis legalisation.

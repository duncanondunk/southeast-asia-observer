# 第23届东博会签约活动落地南宁：一场例牌动作里的区域棋局

9月18日，第23届中国—东盟博览会（东博会）签约活动将在广西南宁举行。据南宁日报报道，本届签约由东博会组委会与自治区政府主办，商务部领导、自治区主要领导、国内外商协会与知名企业负责人及签约代表约300人出席。这看似是一场年度例牌的“投资促进”活动，却恰好卡在中国—东盟关系进入深水区的节点上——当外部不确定性上升、区域内产业链重构加速，这场签约的份量，远不止“签几个项目”那么简单。

## 一场“投资促进”的例牌动作

东博会签约仪式是东博会总体框架下的重大投资促进活动，已连续举办22届，本身是东博会投资促进活动的品牌。换句话说，它不是一个新事物，而是中国—东盟合作机器里一台运转了近二十年的“老设备”。但老设备年年转，转的却是新内容：今年的签约紧扣经贸合作主线，突出东盟开放合作，强化AI应用与创新，聚焦十大现代化支柱产业和向海经济。把“AI”和“向海经济”写进同一份签约说明，本身就是一种信号——广西想让这场会，既接得住数字经济的风口，也踩得中陆海联动的节拍。

## 从“展会招商”到“场景招商”

本届最值得玩味的设计，是《“投资广西”场景清单》的发布。过去招商靠的是展位、手册和领导站台，比的是“谁家摊位大”；现在广西把重点产业链的“场景机会”与“场景能力”打包成清单释放出去，意思很直白：不是请你来参展，而是请你来“入局”具体项目。这种从“展会招商”到“场景招商”的转向，本质是把招商从“广撒网”升级为“精准对接”——企业拿到清单，对得上能力的就能直接落子。对东盟企业而言，这意味着进入广西市场有了更明确的入口，也意味着中国地方层面的开放，正变得越来越精细、越来越可操作。

## AI 与向海经济：本届的两张新牌

AI应用与创新被单列强调，对应的是一个清晰的区域趋势：东盟多国正把AI与数字经济写进国家战略，而中国恰好在算力、模型和制造业应用上握有供给。东博会把这个议题摆到签约前台，等于为双方的AI合作开了个“对接口”。另一张牌是向海经济——广西背靠北部湾，向海经济是其从“内陆边陲”走向“陆海枢纽”的抓手。把向海经济与十大现代化支柱产业并列，释放出广西要把临港产业、跨境物流和海洋经济一起做大的意图。两张牌一虚一实，虚的是数字风口，实的是港口与产业。

## 华商与侨资：隐形的主场力量

这场签约的主场，远不止广西一地。中国—东盟经贸网络里，华侨华人资本长期是“隐形主角”：从东南亚的华商家族，到往返两地的侨资企业，他们既熟悉中国市场的规则，又深谙东盟本地的门道。东博会历届都不缺华商身影，本届的“国内外重要商协会机构”里，华侨华人商协会同样是绕不开的参与方。理解东博会，不能只看政府间签约，更要看见这条由乡谊、方言和网络织成的资本暗河——它在正式协议之外，默默把双边生意的摩擦成本降了下来。

## 东盟的算盘：在不确定中锁定中国需求

对东盟国家而言，东博会从来不是单向的“中国舞台”，而是锁定中国需求的窗口。当全球贸易保护主义抬头、主要市场需求波动，中国作为东盟第一大贸易伙伴的分量更加凸显。越南工业产出走强、泰国推进清洁电力、马来西亚对华贸易依存加深——这些趋势背后，都是东盟希望借中国动能对冲外部风险的算计。签约专场上设“东盟国家合作项目专场”，正是为各国把自身优势产业（农业、制造、资源、旅游）对接中国资本与市场的量身安排。东盟要的，是在不确定里握住一个确定的大买家。

## 广西的赌注：从边陲到枢纽

南宁能连续多年成为东博会永久举办地，背后是广西的一场豪赌：把地理上的“末梢”变成战略上的“枢纽”。中越边境、北部湾、西部陆海新通道，这三张牌让广西既是面向东盟的门户，也是中国西南出海的咽喉。本届签约强调“省际合作项目专场”，说明广西不只想当广西的广西，更想当中国腹地对接东盟的超级中转站。赌注不小——基础设施、产业配套、营商环境任何一环掉链子，枢纽梦就会卡在半路。但一旦跑通，广西将吃到整整一代的区域一体化红利。

## 一条暗线：规则与标准的软联通

比签约金额更耐嚼的，是签约背后的“软联通”。中国—东盟合作走到今天，硬基建（港口、铁路、园区）已不成问题，真正的瓶颈在规则、标准、认证、通关的对接。东博会的价值，恰恰在于它提供了一个把“软”问题摆上桌的机制：每年签约之外，更有大量标准互认、通关便利、检疫协定的磋商在会场外发生。这条暗线决定了一纸合同能否真正落地——它不喧哗，却才是区域一体化真正的深水区。

## 观察：签约只是开始

第23届东博会的签约活动，表面是约300人、几场专场、一份场景清单，内里却是中国—东盟关系在逆风中的一次“打卡式确认”：双方都还需要彼此。对广西，这是从边陲走向枢纽的关键一跃；对东盟，这是在不确定的世界里锁定确定需求的一道保险；对华商，这是资本暗河汇入主渠的又一弯。签约落笔只需一天，但真正的文章，写在签约之后的落地、联通与共生里。东博会这台老设备，今年转的，依旧是区域命运的新齿轮。

## 延伸阅读

**中国—东盟博览会（China-ASEAN Expo, CAEXPO）**：2004年起在南宁举办的国家级展会，是中国—东盟自贸区框架下最重要的投资与经贸促进平台，至2026年已办至第23届。

**向海经济（marine economy）**：以北部湾等沿海区域为依托，统筹港口、临港产业、海洋资源与跨境物流的经济发展思路，是广西从内陆边陲转向陆海枢纽的核心抓手。

**软联通（soft connectivity）**：相对于港口、铁路等“硬联通”，指规则、标准、认证、通关等制度层面的对接，被视为中国—东盟深度一体化的关键瓶颈与突破口。

# The 23rd CAEXPO Signing Lands in Nanning: A Routine Ritual in a Regional Chess Game

On September 18, the signing event of the 23rd China-ASEAN Expo (CAEXPO) will be held in Nanning, Guangxi. According to the Nanning Daily, this edition is organized by the CAEXPO Organizing Committee and the Guangxi government, drawing about 300 attendees including Ministry of Commerce leaders, senior provincial officials, heads of major business associations and enterprises, and signing representatives. On its face, this is an annual, formulaic "investment promotion" exercise. Yet it lands precisely as China-ASEAN relations enter deeper waters—with external uncertainty rising and regional supply chains being redrawn, the weight of this signing reaches well beyond "a few projects on paper."

## A routine move with outsized symbolism

The CAEXPO signing ceremony is a marquee investment-promotion event within the expo's framework and has been held for 22 consecutive editions—a brand-name fixture of the expo's investment arm. In other words, it is no novelty; it is an "old machine" in the China-ASEAN cooperation apparatus that has run for nearly two decades. But the old machine turns new content each year. This edition stays tight on economic and trade cooperation, foregrounds ASEAN openness, stresses AI application and innovation, and zeroes in on ten modern pillar industries and the marine economy. Writing both "AI" and "marine economy" into the same signing brief is itself a signal: Guangxi wants this event to catch the digital-economy wave while keeping pace with land-sea coordination.

## From "booth pitching" to "scenario pitching"

The most intriguing design this year is the release of the "Invest in Guangxi" scenario list. Traditional investment promotion ran on booths, brochures, and officials on stages—a contest of who had the bigger stand. Now Guangxi packages the "scenario opportunities" and "scenario capabilities" of its key industry chains into a list and pushes it outward. The message is blunt: you are not invited to exhibit, but to "enter" specific projects. This shift from exhibition pitching to scenario pitching upgrades promotion from casting a wide net to precise matchmaking. For ASEAN firms, it means a clearer entry point into the Guangxi market—and evidence that China's sub-national opening is becoming finer-grained and more operational.

## AI and the marine economy: two new cards

AI application and innovation are singled out, matching a clear regional trend: many ASEAN states are writing AI and the digital economy into national strategy, while China happens to hold supply-side strengths in computing power, models, and manufacturing applications. Putting this issue on the signing front stage opens an "interface" for bilateral AI cooperation. The other card is the marine economy. Backed by Beibu Gulf, the marine economy is Guangxi's lever to move from "inland frontier" to "land-sea hub." Lining it up beside the ten modern pillar industries signals an intent to grow port-adjacent industry, cross-border logistics, and ocean economy together. One card is virtual (the digital wind), the other tangible (ports and industry).

## Ethnic-Chinese capital: the invisible host

The home turf of this signing extends far beyond Guangxi. In the China-ASEAN economic web, overseas-Chinese capital has long been the "invisible lead": from ethnic-Chinese business families across Southeast Asia to diaspora-invested firms shuttling between the two sides, they know China's rules and ASEAN's local nuances alike. CAEXPO has never lacked ethnic-Chinese merchants, and among this edition's "domestic and foreign business associations," overseas-Chinese chambers are an unavoidable participant. To read CAEXPO, one must look past inter-governmental signatures to see the underground river of capital woven from kinship, dialect, and networks—quietly lowering the friction cost of bilateral business beyond the formal agreements.

## ASEAN's calculus: locking in Chinese demand

For ASEAN states, CAEXPO has never been a one-way "Chinese stage" but a window to lock in Chinese demand. As global protectionism rises and major-market demand wobbles, China's weight as ASEAN's largest trading partner grows. Vietnam's industrial output strengthens, Thailand pushes clean power, Malaysia deepens trade dependence on China—behind these trends lies ASEAN's calculation to hedge external risk with Chinese momentum. The dedicated "ASEAN country cooperation" session is a tailor-made arrangement for each state to match its strengths—agriculture, manufacturing, resources, tourism—with Chinese capital and market. What ASEAN wants is a sure large buyer in an uncertain world.

## Guangxi's bet: from frontier to hub

That Nanning has been CAEXPO's permanent home for years reflects Guangxi's bold wager: turning a geographic "dead end" into a strategic "hub." The Vietnam border, Beibu Gulf, and the New Western Land-Sea Corridor make Guangxi both a gateway to ASEAN and the throat of southwest China's sea access. This edition stresses an "inter-provincial cooperation" session, showing Guangxi wants not merely to be Guangxi but the super-transit point linking China's hinterland to ASEAN. The stakes are high—any weak link in infrastructure, industry support, or business environment stalls the hub dream. But if it clicks, Guangxi captures a full generation of regional-integration dividends.

## The undercurrent: soft connectivity of rules

More chewy than signing amounts is the "soft connectivity" behind the signatures. China-ASEAN cooperation today faces no shortage of hard infrastructure—ports, railways, parks. The real bottleneck is aligning rules, standards, certification, and customs. CAEXPO's value lies precisely in offering a mechanism to put "soft" issues on the table: beyond annual signings, vast consultations on standards mutual recognition, customs facilitation, and quarantine agreements happen off-stage. This undercurrent decides whether a contract truly lands. It is quiet, yet it is the true deep water of regional integration.

## Observation: signing is only the beginning

The 23rd CAEXPO signing, on its surface about 300 people, a few sessions, and one scenario list, is inwardly a "check-in" confirmation of China-ASEAN relations against headwinds: both sides still need each other. For Guangxi, it is a key leap from frontier to hub. For ASEAN, insurance locking in certain demand in an uncertain world. For ethnic-Chinese capital, another bend where the underground river meets the main channel. The pen lifts in a day, but the real story is written in the implementation, connectivity, and symbiosis after the signing. CAEXPO's old machine, this year, still turns the new gears of regional destiny.

## Further reading

**China-ASEAN Expo (CAEXPO)**: A national-level expo held in Nanning since 2004 and the premier investment and trade-promotion platform under the China-ASEAN Free Trade Area framework; the 23rd edition arrives in 2026.

**Marine economy**: A development approach anchored on coastal zones such as Beibu Gulf that integrates ports, port-adjacent industry, marine resources, and cross-border logistics—Guangxi's core lever to shift from inland frontier to land-sea hub.

**Soft connectivity**: Relative to "hard connectivity" (ports, railways), it refers to alignment of rules, standards, certification, and customs—viewed as the key bottleneck and breakthrough for deep China-ASEAN integration.

# 安华说中国更好打交道：马来西亚（Malaysia）的平衡术，为什么越来越难？

## 一句话点破了什么

据《南华早报》（South China Morning Post）报道，马来西亚总理安瓦尔·易卜拉欣在深圳接受专访时说，中国从未对马来西亚亲近美国表达过不满，也没有阻挠马方与美方达成任何技术安排；他觉得与北京（Beijing）打交道比与华盛顿容易，但政府并未倒向任何一方。

这句话值得停一下。多数东南亚（Southeast Asia）领导人在中美之间发言时，习惯把「不选边」说成原则；安华把它说成了体验——不是抽象的地缘政治立场，而是过去几年与两边打交道的直接感受。据他本人此前在马尼拉（Manila）东盟（ASEAN）峰会期间的说法，美国是重要的贸易伙伴与投资来源国，但马来西亚有权自主决定与谁合作；约一周半前他与美国国务卿鲁比奥通过电话，谈的是数据中心、芯片和人工智能。

点评：一个是「从未追问」，一个是「先问用途」，差别不在道德高低，而在双方把什么问题放在首位。

## 时点比措辞更重要

专访发生在11月20日习近平会见安华之前，地点是深圳——第33届亚太经合组织领导人非正式会议的举办地。两天前，习近平刚与美国总统特朗普在这里会面，并把中美关系描述为总体稳定、积极。

正是在这一周，美国商务部工业与安全局发布临时最终规则，把印尼（Indonesia）、马来西亚、新加坡、泰国（Thailand）和巴西纳入先进半导体出口许可范围，同时限制受禁地区企业经由海外数据中心远程获取先进算力。对马来西亚而言，这不是抽象的外部事件：柔佛等地快速扩张的数据中心产业，恰好落在规则的核心地带。

据中央社报道，马来西亚投资、贸易及工业部长佐哈里·阿卜杜勒加尼随后表示，马方正向美国询问许可细则，尤其是向第三方提供数据中心服务应如何界定；得到正式通知后，将把要求转达给全部117项已获批的数据中心项目。

点评：安华的话不是说给北京听的，而是说给那些担心马来西亚「选边」的国内外受众听的。

## 芯片新规把中立变成合规题

过去谈中立，谈的是军事同盟、外交表态与投票立场；现在谈中立，先要回答芯片运到哪里、服务器为谁提供算力、合同条款由谁签。美国的新规把「远程获取」也纳入管制，意味着一端在东南亚、一端在中国的算力租赁，可能被重新定义为受控交易。

这就解释了安华为何强调中国从未阻挠马来西亚与美国的技术安排。对吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）来说，真正的风险不是选边带来的外交代价，而是任何一边把商业往来重新定义为安全问题之后，本国企业要承担的合规成本。马来西亚并非先进芯片生产国，主要做封装测试与数据中心运营；新规影响的恰恰是这两项支柱。

点评：当中立从立场变成流程，外交部的声明就解决不了问题，得靠海关、许可证与审计。

## 马来西亚的两岸账本

安华的自信有数字支撑。中国长期是马来西亚最大贸易伙伴，美国则是主要投资来源与出口市场。两国在马来西亚的产业链上并非简单替代：来自美国的资本、来自中国的设备与订单，共同支撑了电子制造业与柔佛的数据中心集群。

据《海峡时报》等报道，马来西亚11月13日表示正与新加坡、美国加强防范人工智能芯片走私，并强调自2025年7月起，依《战略贸易法》出口、再出口或转运美国原产高性能芯片须申请许可，但进口本身不需要。佐哈里说，马来西亚不执行别国单边管制，但也不允许本国被用于规避管制。

这是一种典型的「双账本」：在贸易账上最大化来自两边的收益，在主权账上坚持最终解释权归自己。安华对美方提出的「投资项目不得损害马来西亚国家利益」表示理解，但补充说，向美方作出的说明并不剥夺马来西亚与中国合作的权利。

点评：这不是骑墙，而是把议价能力建立在两边都需要它之上。

## 东盟中心性不是自动到手

安华的表态还有一层区域含义。他在马尼拉说，均衡与各大国往来既是马来西亚方针，也体现东盟中心地位。问题在于，中心性从来不是声明出来的，而是靠区域组织能否在关键议题上提供共同规则。

当美国以国家安全名义设定技术门槛，当中国以完整产业链提供替代方案，东盟能做的是把两边的规则翻译成区域内可操作的共同标准：数据跨境流动、算力服务商准入、再出口尽职调查。若做不到，中心性就只剩会议照片。

点评：中小国家真正需要的不是两边都讨好，而是把两套规则翻译成同一套可执行的本地规则。

## 国内政治的边界

安华的余地也受制于国内。马来西亚是多族群社会，华人商界与中国联系密切，马来穆斯林社会对若干地区议题有自己的情绪，反对党则习惯把对华合作包装成「屈从」。安华在马尼拉晚宴上特意强调廉洁治理，要求外来投资遵守本国政策框架、严打贪腐，正是对这种质疑的回应。

这也是他发表看法时保持克制的原因。说北京「更好打交道」并不等于宣布转向；他同时重申一个中国政策，表示愿加强人工智能等新兴领域合作。既给北京面子，也给华盛顿台阶——话说到「体验」为止，不上升到「阵营」。

点评：对小国领导人而言，能说多直白，往往取决于国内能承受多少回声。

## 中小国家的议价空间

把安华的话放在更长的时间线看，马来西亚并非特例。从中南半岛的铁路到海岛国家的镍产业链，东南亚国家一直在利用大国竞争争取基础设施、投资与技术转移。区别在于，过去争取的是「谁修我的铁路」，现在争取的是「谁定义我的算力」。

安华给出的答案不是告别平衡术，而是给平衡术加一道程序：以本国法律为准绳，以本国利益为边界，两边的合作都欢迎，但解释权留给自己。这套做法能否持续，取决于马来西亚有没有足够透明的审批与足够强的执行能力——否则「不选边」很容易被两边同时解读为「对我有利」。

点评：真正考验不是安华说了什么，而是117个项目在许可新规下如何落地。

## 延伸阅读

**战略自主（Strategic Autonomy）**：中小国家不在大国之间选边，而是按议题保留独立决策权。

**长臂管辖（Long-arm Jurisdiction）**：一国以本国法规约束境外主体，出口管制是其最常见的工具。

**东盟中心性（Asean Centrality）**：东盟主张由区域组织设定合作议程，而不是由大国分配角色。

# Anwar says China is easier to deal with than the US: why Malaysia's balancing act is getting harder

## What the one-line remark reveals

According to the South China Morning Post, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said in a Shenzhen interview that China had never complained about Malaysia being close to the United States, nor stood in the way of any technology arrangements Kuala Lumpur made with Washington. He found Beijing easier to deal with, he said, while insisting his government was not leaning towards either side.

The line deserves a pause. Southeast Asian leaders usually describe non-alignment as a principle. Anwar described it as an experience — not an abstract geopolitical posture but the accumulated feel of dealing with both sides over several years. At an ASEAN summit dinner in Manila earlier this month, he said the United States remained an important trade partner and source of investment, but that Malaysia had the right to decide whom it worked with. He also disclosed a call with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio roughly a week and a half earlier, covering data centres, chips and artificial intelligence.

Comment: one side never asks a question; the other asks about end use first. The difference is not moral height. It is about which problem each party puts first.

## Timing matters more than wording

The interview took place ahead of Xi Jinping's November 20 meeting with Anwar in Shenzhen, host city of the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting. Two days earlier Xi had met US President Donald Trump in the same city and described China-US relations as generally stable and positive.

It was in that same week that the US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule bringing Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil within licensing requirements for advanced semiconductors, while restricting remote access to advanced compute by entities in embargoed destinations. For Malaysia this was not an abstract external event: the fast-growing data-centre industry in places such as Johor sits squarely inside the rule's core.

CNA reported that Malaysia's Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Johari Abdul Ghani subsequently said Kuala Lumpur was seeking clarification from Washington on licensing details, especially how services provided to third parties from Malaysian data centres would be defined. Once formally notified, he said, the ministry would pass the requirements to all 117 approved data-centre projects.

Comment: Anwar was not speaking to Beijing. He was speaking to domestic and foreign audiences worried that Malaysia might pick a side.

## Chip rules turn neutrality into a compliance question

Neutrality used to be discussed in terms of military alliances, diplomatic statements and voting positions. Now it begins with practical questions: where do the chips go, whose models run on the servers, who signs the contract. By pulling remote access into its rules, Washington made a computing lease with one end in Southeast Asia and the other in China liable to be redefined as a controlled transaction.

That explains why Anwar stressed that Beijing had never obstructed Malaysian-American technology arrangements. For Kuala Lumpur the real risk is not the diplomatic price of choosing a side; it is the compliance cost borne by local companies once either side reclassifies ordinary commerce as a security issue. Malaysia does not manufacture leading-edge chips. It specializes in packaging, testing and data-centre operations — precisely the two pillars the new rule touches.

Comment: when neutrality becomes a process rather than a position, foreign-ministry statements stop solving the problem. Customs officers, licences and audits do.

## Malaysia's two-ledger calculation

Anwar's confidence rests on numbers. China has long been Malaysia's largest trading partner; the United States is a major investment source and export market. In Malaysia's industrial chain the two are not simple substitutes. American capital and Chinese equipment and orders together sustain electronics manufacturing and the Johor data-centre cluster.

Malaysia said on November 13 that it was working with Singapore and the United States to curb AI-chip smuggling, according to the Straits Times and other reports. Kuala Lumpur noted that since July 2025 a permit under the Strategic Trade Act has been required to export, re-export, transship or transit high-performance US-origin chips, though imports themselves need no permit. Johari said Malaysia did not enforce other countries' unilateral controls, but would not allow its territory to be used to circumvent them.

This is a classic two-ledger operation: maximise gains from both sides in the trade ledger, keep final interpretive authority in the sovereignty ledger. Anwar said he understood the US request that investments in Malaysia should not harm Malaysian interests — while adding that explanations given to Washington did not deprive Malaysia of the right to cooperate with China.

Comment: this is not fence-sitting. It is bargaining power built on both sides needing you.

## ASEAN centrality is not automatic

Anwar's remark carries a regional meaning too. In Manila he said balancing relations with major powers was both Malaysia's policy and an expression of ASEAN centrality. The trouble is that centrality is never declared into existence. It depends on whether the regional organisation can supply common rules on the issues that matter.

When Washington sets technology thresholds in the name of national security, and Beijing offers an alternative through a complete industrial chain, what ASEAN can do is translate both rulebooks into workable regional standards: cross-border data flows, market access for compute providers, and re-export due diligence. Failing that, centrality reduces to conference photographs.

Comment: what smaller states need is not to please everyone but to translate two rulebooks into one enforceable local set.

## The domestic-political boundary

Anwar's room for manoeuvre is also bounded at home. Malaysia is a multi-ethnic society: the Chinese business community has dense links with China, Malay Muslim voters hold their own views on a range of regional issues, and opposition parties routinely frame China cooperation as submission. His emphasis in Manila on clean governance — foreign investment must observe Malaysian policy frameworks and corruption must be punished — answered those doubts directly.

It also explains the restraint in his choice of words. Saying Beijing is "easier to deal with" is not a declaration of realignment. In the same period he reaffirmed the one-China policy and signalled readiness to deepen cooperation in emerging fields such as artificial intelligence. He gave Beijing face and Washington a way down: the observation stops at experience and never becomes a camp.

Comment: for a leader of a smaller country, how blunt you can be depends on how much domestic echo you can absorb.

## Bargaining room for smaller states

Seen against a longer timeline, Malaysia is no exception. From mainland Southeast Asian railways to island-state nickel chains, the region has long used great-power competition to secure infrastructure, investment and technology transfer. What has changed is the object of the bargain: once it was who builds my railway; now it is who defines my compute.

Anwar's answer is not to abandon balancing but to add a procedure to it: domestic law as the standard, national interest as the boundary, cooperation welcome from both sides, interpretation retained at home. Whether that holds depends on transparent approval processes and credible enforcement. Otherwise non-alignment can be read by both capitals as being on their side.

Comment: the real test is not what Anwar said but how 117 projects fare under the new licensing rules.

## Further reading

**Strategic autonomy**: smaller states avoid choosing between great powers by retaining issue-by-issue decision-making.

**Long-arm jurisdiction**: a state applying its own laws to actors abroad; export controls are its most common instrument.

**Asean centrality**: the principle that regional organisations, not major powers, should set the cooperative agenda.

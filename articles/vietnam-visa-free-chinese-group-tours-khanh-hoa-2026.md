# 组团、直飞金兰、停留30天：越南对华免签试点的算盘

10月12日起，越南对中国游客打开了一扇此前紧闭的门——但只开了一条缝。据越南通讯社报道，越南政府决议规定，自本月12日起至2027年年底，对以至少两人组团形式赴庆和省旅游的中国公民试行免签，停留期限不超过30天；符合条件的游客须从中国内地直飞庆和省金兰国际机场，并预订由越南国际旅行社组织的包价旅游产品。这项政策被置于2026年至2027年“中越旅游合作年”的框架之下，适用于持有效中国普通护照的人员，不包括持中国香港、澳门特别行政区签发护照的人员。

## 政策：一条“有条件”的免签通道

把这串条件拆开看，会发现它远不是常规意义上的免签。真正的免签是“你来就行”，而越南这份试点给免签套上了四道锁：其一，必须组团，至少两人成行，单人自由行被排除在外；其二，必须直飞，且只能落在庆和省的金兰国际机场，从河内或胡志明市转机入境不在其列；其三，必须跟团，行程须由越南国际旅行社承接的包价产品构成；其四，必须守时，停留不超过30天。

换句话说，越南开放的是一条“点对点、团进团出”的通道，而非对普通中国游客的普遍开门。这更像一张定向邀请函，而不是一张通行证。

## 设计逻辑：为什么限定“组团”与“直飞金兰”

条件看似繁琐，却处处透着精算。组团的第一个好处是可度量：团客由旅行社统一承接，人数、行程、消费都留在台账里，政策效果能被清清楚楚地结算。第二个好处是可管理：跟团游客的行踪全程处于旅行社视野之内，逾期滞留、非法务工等风险被大幅压缩，这是自由行难以做到的。

至于“直飞金兰”这一条，则把政策的受益面精准锁定在庆和省一地。包机与直飞航线往往由旅行社包销，落地即意味着消费在地化——酒店、餐饮、购物、景点门票都在芽庄周边结算。越南要的是把中国游客的钱，尽量多地留在最能承接的目的地。

值得玩味的是，政策把中国香港、澳门特别行政区护照持有者排除在外。这既可能是出入境管理的技术性区分，也提示这项试点仍停留在“小范围试验”阶段，尚未上升为对全部中国公民的统一安排。

## 庆和省与芽庄：中国客源的旧账与新盘算

选择庆和省，而非河内或胡志明市，绝非偶然。庆和省首府芽庄是中国游客赴越的传统热门目的地，金兰国际机场长期承担着大量中国旅行团的进出。在中国出境游的版图里，芽庄曾是与普吉、巴厘岛并列的东南亚海滨选项之一。

疫情前的鼎盛时期，中国是越南最重要的客源市场之一，海滨城市对团客的依赖尤其明显。正因如此，当跨境旅游按下暂停键，受冲击最重的也正是这类高度依赖单一客源的目的地。此番以庆和省为试点，本质上是先把“最容易见效的地方”重新接通——用一个熟悉的招牌菜，撬动整体复苏。

## “中越旅游合作年”：政策背后的双边坐标

把免签试点放进“2026年至2027年中越旅游合作年”的框架里，政策的意图就更清楚了。旅游是双边关系中少有的低敏感领域：即便南海摩擦仍在，人文与旅游往来依然可以推进，并常常被双方用作稳定关系的缓冲带。

对越南而言，主动对接中国客源是一种务实选择。中国出境游市场体量巨大，谁能拿到份额，谁就能在服务业与就业上获得直接收益。而对双边关系来说，旅游合作提供的是一种“可以谈、不伤及根本”的互动方式——它不解决争议，但能让关系在争议之外仍有黏性。

## 打开的是门，还是阀门？

问题的关键在这里：这道门会越开越大，还是随时会被关上？从设计看，越南开的更像一个阀门，而非一扇门。它有明确的到期日（2027年年底），有明确的适用对象（组团、直飞、指定目的地），也有明确的排除项（自由行、香港澳门护照）。这意味着政策成本低、可撤销、可随时调参。

这是一种典型的“渐进式开放”：先在最可控的场景里试水，观察经济收益与社会管理成本，再决定是收紧、维持还是放宽。越南既想要中国游客带来的真金白银，又不愿承担人员大规模自由流动带来的不确定性。用团队游这条管道，恰好能同时满足“要钱”与“控人”两个目标。

## 值得观察的三点

第一，会不会扩容。若试点见效，最可能的下一步是放宽到自由行，并把适用范围从庆和省扩展到岘港、河内、胡志明市等其他目的地。门槛的松动幅度，将是判断越南开放诚意的关键指标。

第二，2027年年底之后怎么办。是到期即止，还是延续甚至升级为常态化安排，取决于试点期间的收益账本。若效果不彰，政策可能悄无声息地终止。

第三，会不会带出区域效应。泰国、马来西亚、新加坡近年来竞相放宽对华签证，越南此前在签证便利度上并不占优。这次定向试点，既是补课，也可能引发东南亚旅游目的地之间新一轮的“抢客”竞争。

归根结底，越南这次放的是一张有条件的邀请函。它读懂了中国游客的分量，却还在小心翼翼地试探水温。至于水有多热、能不能久留，答案要等这张邀请函发出去之后，才见分晓。

## 延伸阅读

**组团免签（Group Visa Exemption）**：指仅对以团队形式出行、由指定旅行社承接的游客给予免签待遇，而非对个人开放。其特点是可控性强、风险集中于旅行社，常用于目的地在开放初期“试水”。

**中越旅游合作年（China–Vietnam Tourism Cooperation Year）**：2026年至2027年，中越两国以旅游为主题开展的年度合作安排，为越南对华签证便利化措施提供了双边政策框架。

**金兰国际机场（Cam Ranh International Airport）**：位于越南庆和省、服务芽庄地区的国际机场。它长期是赴越中国旅行团的主要口岸之一，因此成为此次免签试点指定的唯一入境点。

# Group Tours, Direct Flights to Cam Ranh, 30 Days: Reading Vietnam's Visa-Free Pilot for Chinese Tourists

From 12 October, Vietnam opened a door to Chinese tourists that had long been shut — but only a crack. According to the Vietnam News Agency, a government resolution provides that, from the 12th of this month until the end of 2027, Chinese citizens travelling to Khanh Hoa province in groups of at least two will be granted visa-free entry on a trial basis, for stays of no more than 30 days. Eligible visitors must fly directly from mainland China to Cam Ranh International Airport in Khanh Hoa and book a package tour organised by a Vietnamese international travel agency. Framed under the 2026–2027 'China–Vietnam Tourism Cooperation Year', the scheme applies to holders of valid ordinary Chinese passports and excludes those holding passports issued by China's Hong Kong and Macao special administrative regions.

## The policy: a visa-free channel with conditions

Unpack the conditions and it becomes clear that this is far from visa-free entry in the ordinary sense. Real visa-free travel means 'just come'. Vietnam's pilot instead bolts four locks onto the concept. First, travellers must be in a group — at least two people — which rules out the solo independent traveller. Second, they must fly direct, and land only at Cam Ranh International Airport in Khanh Hoa; arriving via a transfer in Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City does not qualify. Third, they must join a tour, with the itinerary built from a package sold by a Vietnamese international travel agency. Fourth, they must keep to the clock, staying no longer than 30 days.

In other words, Vietnam has opened a point-to-point, group-in-group-out channel, not a general door for ordinary Chinese visitors. It reads more like a targeted invitation than a passport.

## The design logic: why 'groups' and 'direct flights to Cam Ranh'

The conditions look cumbersome, but each reflects careful arithmetic. The first benefit of group travel is that it is measurable: tour groups are handled by a single agency, so headcounts, itineraries and spending stay on the books, and the policy's effects can be tallied precisely. The second is that it is manageable: the movements of group tourists remain within an agency's line of sight throughout, sharply reducing the risks of overstaying or illegal work — something independent travel makes far harder to police.

As for the 'direct to Cam Ranh' rule, it pins the benefits of the policy precisely on Khanh Hoa. Charter and direct flights are typically block-booked by agencies, so landing means spending stays local — hotels, restaurants, shopping and attraction tickets are all settled around Nha Trang. What Vietnam wants is to keep as much of the Chinese tourist dollar as possible in the destination best equipped to absorb it.

Tellingly, the scheme excludes holders of Hong Kong and Macao SAR passports. That may be a technical distinction in immigration management, but it also signals that the pilot remains a small-scale experiment rather than a uniform arrangement for all Chinese citizens.

## Khanh Hoa and Nha Trang: an old ledger of Chinese arrivals and a new calculation

Choosing Khanh Hoa rather than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City was no accident. Nha Trang, the provincial capital, is a traditional magnet for Chinese visitors to Vietnam, and Cam Ranh International Airport has long handled large flows of Chinese tour groups. On the map of Chinese outbound travel, Nha Trang once stood alongside Phuket and Bali as a Southeast Asian seaside option.

At its pre-pandemic peak, China was among Vietnam's most important source markets, and seaside cities were especially dependent on group travellers. That is precisely why, when cross-border tourism hit pause, the hardest-hit were destinations built around a single source market. Choosing Khanh Hoa as the pilot is, in essence, reconnecting 'the place most likely to show results first' — using a familiar signature dish to spark a broader recovery.

## The 'China–Vietnam Tourism Cooperation Year': the bilateral coordinates behind the policy

Placing the pilot inside the 2026–2027 'China–Vietnam Tourism Cooperation Year' makes the intent clearer still. Tourism is one of the few low-sensitivity areas in the bilateral relationship: even with friction in the South China Sea continuing, cultural and tourist exchanges can still advance, and are often used by both sides as a cushion that stabilises ties.

For Vietnam, actively courting Chinese travellers is a pragmatic choice. China's outbound market is enormous, and whoever captures a share gains directly in services and employment. For the relationship at large, tourism cooperation offers a way of interacting that 'can be discussed without touching fundamentals' — it does not resolve disputes, but it keeps the relationship sticky beyond them.

## A door opened, or a valve?

The key question is this: will the door swing wider, or can it be shut at any time? By design, Vietnam has opened something closer to a valve than a door. It has a clear expiry date (the end of 2027), a clear set of beneficiaries (groups, direct flights, a designated destination) and clear exclusions (independent travel, Hong Kong and Macao passports). That makes the policy cheap, reversible and easy to recalibrate.

This is classic incremental opening: test the waters in the most controllable setting, observe the economic gains and the social-management costs, then decide whether to tighten, maintain or relax. Vietnam wants the hard currency that Chinese tourists bring, but not the uncertainty of large-scale, free-flowing arrivals. A group-tour pipeline lets it pursue 'the money' and 'control' at the same time.

## Three things to watch

First, will it expand? If the pilot works, the likeliest next step is to loosen it for independent travellers and extend it from Khanh Hoa to Da Nang, Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and beyond. How far the threshold shifts will be the key gauge of Vietnam's sincerity.

Second, what happens after the end of 2027? Whether the scheme lapses, continues or is upgraded to a standing arrangement will depend on the balance sheet compiled during the trial. If results disappoint, it may quietly expire.

Third, will it produce a regional effect? Thailand, Malaysia and Singapore have raced to ease visas for Chinese visitors in recent years, and Vietnam had not been ahead on visa convenience. This targeted pilot is both catch-up and a possible trigger for a new round of competition among Southeast Asian destinations for Chinese travellers.

In the end, Vietnam has issued a conditional invitation. It understands the weight of Chinese tourists, yet is still testing the water cautiously. How warm it proves, and whether they stay, will only be known once the invitation is out.

## Further reading

**Group Visa Exemption**: A visa waiver granted only to travellers moving in groups and handled by designated travel agencies, rather than open to individuals. Its strengths are controllability and the concentration of risk in agencies, which is why destinations often use it when opening up for the first time.

**China–Vietnam Tourism Cooperation Year**: An annual, tourism-themed cooperation arrangement between China and Vietnam for 2026–2027, providing the bilateral policy framework for Vietnam's visa-facilitation measures.

**Cam Ranh International Airport**: An international airport in Khanh Hoa province serving the Nha Trang area. Long one of the main gateways for Chinese tour groups to Vietnam, it became the sole designated entry point for this visa-free pilot.

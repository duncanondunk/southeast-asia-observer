# 马尼拉定调"人工智能合作年"：当1万亿美元贸易额遇上规则之争

据《南华早报》报道，中国国务院总理李强11月16日在马尼拉出席第29次中国—东盟领导人会议，宣布中国—东盟自贸区3.0版升级议定书将于12月1日生效，并把2027年定为"中国—东盟人工智能合作年"。消息本身不算意外，值得琢磨的是措辞的变化：五年前讲的是"一带一路"与互联互通，这一回的关键词换成了"人工智能+"、标准对接与产供链融合。贸易数字仍然漂亮，但真正被摆上桌面的，是谁来定义下一代区域规则。

## 数字：1万亿美元与29.6%

先看成绩单。李强在会上披露，双方去年贸易额已突破1万亿美元（约7.2万亿元人民币），比2021年增长20.1%；今年前10个月又增长29.6%。双向投资累计超过5000亿美元（约3.6万亿元人民币），比五年前增长五成以上。在单边主义、保护主义抬头的背景下，这组数字确实拿得出手。

不过要问一句：增长从哪来？答案很大程度上是产业转移与产供链重组，而非单纯的需求扩张。中国—东盟自贸区3.0版升级议定书的核心，恰恰是把货物贸易的降税，推进到原产地规则、数字经济、绿色经济和标准互认这些"边境后"议题。换句话说，关税红利接近见顶，接下来的增量要靠制度红利。这也是为什么北京把议定书生效与"人工智能合作年"绑在一起宣布——前者打开市场，后者争夺市场的操作系统。

## 自贸区3.0落地：从关税到规则

把3.0版仅仅读成"又一份自贸协定"，是低估了它。此前版本解决的是"能不能卖"，3.0版处理的是"按谁的规则卖"：数字证书互认、数据跨境流动、绿色产品标准，这些条款表面上技术性极强，实质上决定了未来十年区域产业链的利润分配权。

据《南华早报》报道，李强提出要探讨同《东盟数字经济框架协议》（DEFA）对接。这句话的分量，比一万亿美元的贸易额更重。谁的标准被写进互认清单，谁的认证机构就能收过路费，谁的数字基础设施就更容易变成默认选项。这不是阴谋论，而是区域一体化的常识：规则从来不是中立的。东盟强调"中心性"，恰恰是因为它清楚，在大国规则竞争中被迫选边，等于把谈判筹码拱手让人。

## 人工智能合作年：技术之外是标准之争

"人工智能合作年"听起来像一场展会日历上的活动，实际上是一整套政策信号的打包。中方提出的"人工智能+"合作，配套的还有中国—东盟数字学院明年提供的100个全额奖学金名额、妇幼健康合作中心三年培养1000名专业人才，以及正在推进的能源部长会议机制。这些安排有一个共同点：不止卖产品，还要培养用产品的人。

已经有落地样本。在广西凭祥，当地疾控部门与越南邻近省份合作部署了双语人工智能疫情早期监测平台；在老挝和柬埔寨，由广西企业与地方伙伴开发的系统用卫星影像、无人机和地面传感器优化热带作物种植。平心而论，这类项目的性价比，往往比动辄数十亿美元的"旗舰工程"更能打动东盟国家的财政部。人工智能合作能否做成，关键不在峰会公报写了什么，而在这些小而实的项目能否复制。

## 一个"家"字：命运共同体的叙事考题

李强这次反复用了"家"的隐喻：地理相近、人缘相亲，世代积淀的情感"不应也不会受一时一事影响"。这话是说给谁听的，不言自明。南海争议、个别国家的选边压力，都被归入"一时一事"的范畴。

这套叙事的强项，是把双边关系从单纯交易的逻辑里拉出来，强调长期性与社会基础。它的弱点也很明显：如果"家"只停留在修辞层面，而具体到项目招标、零部件本地化率和争端解决机制时仍是零和博弈，叙事就会被现实反噬。东盟国家欢迎中国的基建与市场，同时对投资审查、债务可持续性和技术依赖保持警惕，这种"经济上靠拢、安全上自主"的两面性，不会因为一句"命运共同体"就消失。叙事要成立，得靠可验证的公共产品来兑现。

## 南海与双轨：经济热、安全冷

值得注意的是会议的另一条线索。菲律宾外长拉扎罗此前表示，南海行为准则磋商已有约八成案文达成"临时一致"，进入最后阶段。李强则重申期待早日达成准则，同时提出争议应通过对话协商处理、反对外部干涉。

这里存在一个典型的"双轨"结构：经济合作高歌猛进，安全议题却始终在低空徘徊。80%的案文达成临时一致，剩下的20%恰恰是最难的部分——争端海域如何管理、域外因素如何定位。把经济上的成功自动外溢到安全领域，是最常见的误判。马尼拉峰会证明的是，即便在南海争议升温的年份，中国与东盟仍能保持贸易与投资的高速增长；它并没有证明，商业契约能够替代安全架构。承认这一点，比宣称"命运共同体"更接近事情的真相。

## 谁能让人工智能更便宜，谁就赢得东南亚

把镜头拉远，同一场马尼拉峰会还出现了另一套人工智能叙事：美国推动东南亚国家加入以供应链安全为名的"硅和平"（Pax Silica）框架，试图把人工智能合作安全化、阵营化。两套方案摆在一起，差异很清楚：一边强调"人工智能+"的产业应用与标准对接，一边强调出口管制与可信供应链。

对东南亚而言，这与其说是选边，不如说是议价。真正决定结果的，是谁能让人工智能算力、模型与人才变得更便宜、更容易获得，同时不附加过高的政治条件。中国在基建、制造和应用场景上有优势，东盟有数据、有青年人口、有增长需求；这种互补性是真实的，但信任赤字也是真实的。2027年被定为合作年，届时检验成效的标准只有一条：有多少东盟企业真的用上了负担得起的人工智能，而不是又有多少份联合声明被签署。

## 延伸阅读

**中国—东盟自贸区3.0（China–ASEAN FTA 3.0）**：从货物降税扩展到原产地规则、数字经济、绿色标准与竞争政策的升级议定书，2026年12月1日生效。

**东盟数字经济框架协议（ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement, DEFA）**：东盟推动的区域数字规则框架，涉及数据流动、数字身份与电子商务互认，是中国标准对接的主要对象。

**人工智能+（AI Plus）**：中方提出的产业融合政策概念，强调把人工智能嵌入制造、农业、医疗与公共服务，而非局限于模型研发本身。

# Manila sets the tone for an "AI Cooperation Year": what US$1 trillion in trade really buys

According to the South China Morning Post, Chinese Premier Li Qiang attended the 29th China-ASEAN Leaders' Meeting in Manila on November 16, where he announced that the China-ASEAN Free Trade Area 3.0 Upgrade Protocol will enter into force on December 1 and that 2027 will be designated the China-ASEAN Year of AI Cooperation. The announcement was hardly a surprise. What deserves scrutiny is the change in vocabulary: five years ago the watchwords were Belt and Road connectivity; this time they are "AI plus", standards alignment and supply-chain integration. The trade figures are still impressive, but what was really placed on the table is the question of who gets to define the region's next generation of rules.

## The numbers: US$1 trillion and 29.6 per cent

Start with the scorecard. Li disclosed that bilateral trade topped US$1 trillion last year, up 20.1 per cent from 2021, and grew a further 29.6 per cent in the first ten months of this year. Cumulative two-way investment has exceeded US$500 billion, more than 50 per cent higher than five years ago. Against a backdrop of rising unilateralism and protectionism, these are numbers Beijing is entitled to be proud of.

But ask where the growth comes from. Much of it reflects industrial relocation and supply-chain restructuring rather than a simple expansion of demand. The essence of the FTA 3.0 Upgrade Protocol is to move beyond tariff cuts on goods into "behind-the-border" issues: rules of origin, the digital economy, green standards and mutual recognition. In other words, the tariff dividend is nearly exhausted; future growth must come from institutional dividends. That is why Beijing bundled the protocol's entry into force with the AI Cooperation Year announcement — the former opens the market, the latter competes for the market's operating system.

## FTA 3.0: from tariffs to rules

Reading 3.0 as merely another free-trade agreement understates it. Earlier versions settled whether goods could be sold; version 3.0 settles whose rules govern the sale. Digital certificate recognition, cross-border data flows and green-product standards are technically dense clauses that in substance determine how profits across regional industrial chains will be divided over the next decade.

According to the South China Morning Post, Li called for exploring alignment with the ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA). That sentence carries more weight than a trillion dollars of trade. Whose standards enter the mutual-recognition lists, whose certification bodies can collect rents, and whose digital infrastructure becomes the default option — these are decided in the fine print. This is not conspiracy theory; it is the common sense of regional integration. Rules are never neutral. ASEAN's emphasis on "centrality" reflects a clear awareness that being forced to choose sides in a great-power rules contest means surrendering negotiating leverage.

## The AI Cooperation Year: beneath technology lies a standards contest

An "AI Cooperation Year" sounds like an item on an exhibition calendar; in practice it is a bundle of policy signals. Beijing's "AI plus" initiative comes with 100 full scholarships at the China-ASEAN Digital Academy next year, a plan to train 1,000 professionals in maternal and child health over three years, and an emerging energy-ministerial meeting mechanism. These arrangements share one feature: they do not merely sell products, they train the people who will use them.

There are already working examples. In Guangxi's Chongzuo, the local centre for disease control has partnered with neighbouring Vietnamese provinces to deploy a bilingual AI platform for early disease detection. In Laos and Cambodia, a platform developed by a Guangxi enterprise with local partners uses satellite imagery, drones and ground sensors to optimise tropical crop cultivation. Frankly, the cost-effectiveness of such projects is more likely to persuade ASEAN finance ministries than flagship schemes costing billions. Whether AI cooperation succeeds will depend less on what the summit communiqué says than on whether these small, practical projects can be replicated.

## One word, "home": the narrative test of a shared future

Li returned repeatedly to the metaphor of "home": geographical proximity, kinship among peoples, and bonds nurtured over generations that "shall not and will not be swayed by any temporary or isolated issue". The intended audience is not hard to identify. South China Sea disputes and pressure to pick sides are filed under the category of "temporary" matters.

The strength of this narrative is that it lifts bilateral relations out of a purely transactional logic and stresses longevity and social foundations. Its weakness is equally visible: if "home" remains rhetorical while project tenders, localisation requirements and dispute-settlement mechanisms stay zero-sum, the narrative will be consumed by reality. ASEAN countries welcome Chinese infrastructure and markets while remaining alert to investment screening, debt sustainability and technological dependence. This two-sided posture — economically drawing closer, strategically preserving autonomy — will not dissolve because of a phrase about a shared future. For the narrative to hold, it must be cashed out in verifiable public goods.

## The South China Sea and the dual track: warm economics, cold security

A second thread of the meeting deserves attention. Philippine Foreign Secretary Ma. Theresa Lazaro had said that about 80 per cent of the draft Code of Conduct in the South China Sea had reached "provisional agreement", with negotiations entering their final phase. Li reiterated Beijing's hope for an early code while insisting that differences be settled through dialogue and that foreign interference be opposed.

This reveals a classic dual-track structure: economic cooperation races ahead while security issues hover at a low altitude. The remaining 20 per cent of the code is precisely the hardest part — how disputed waters are managed and how external factors are positioned. The most common analytical error is to assume that economic success automatically spills over into security. What Manila demonstrated is that China and ASEAN can sustain rapid trade and investment growth even in a year of elevated maritime tension. It did not demonstrate that commercial contracts can substitute for a security architecture. Acknowledging that distinction is closer to the truth than declaring a community with a shared future.

## Whoever makes AI cheaper will win Southeast Asia

Widen the lens and the same Manila summit carried a competing AI narrative: Washington pressed Southeast Asian nations to join a supply-chain-security framework dubbed Pax Silica, seeking to securitise and bloc-ify AI cooperation. Set the two proposals side by side and the contrast is clear. One emphasises "AI plus" industrial applications and standards alignment; the other emphasises export controls and trusted supply chains.

For Southeast Asia this is less a choice of sides than an exercise in bargaining. What will decide the outcome is who can make AI compute, models and talent cheaper and more accessible without attaching excessive political conditions. China has advantages in infrastructure, manufacturing and application scenarios; ASEAN has data, a young population and growth demand. The complementarity is real, and so is the trust deficit. With 2027 designated a year of cooperation, the test will be a single one: how many ASEAN firms actually gained access to affordable AI — not how many joint statements were signed.

## Further reading

**China–ASEAN FTA 3.0**: an upgrade protocol extending beyond tariff cuts to rules of origin, the digital economy, green standards and competition policy; in force from December 1, 2026.

**ASEAN Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA)**: a regional rulebook covering data flows, digital identity and e-commerce recognition — the main object of Chinese standards-alignment efforts.

**AI Plus**: a Chinese policy concept promoting the embedding of artificial intelligence in manufacturing, agriculture, healthcare and public services rather than limiting it to model development.

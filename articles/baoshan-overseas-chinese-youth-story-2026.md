# 保山侨乡的一场展演：当"根"被年轻人自己讲出来

10月12日晚，云南保山。舞台上没有专业演员——登台的是缅甸的华文教师、泰国的华裔少年、老挝的留学生和侨团代表。他们用汉语、傣语和并不标准的普通话，讲述自己家族从哪里来。

这是"根脉延续·侨印永昌"2026年"讲好我们共同的故事"展演活动的现场，循着"寻根""铸魂""致远"三个篇章展开。台下的观众里，有从仰光赶来的侨领，也有本地中学的学生。

## 保山：云南第一大侨乡市

保山靠近中缅边境，是云南省第一大侨乡市。据保山海外联谊会会长冯逆光介绍，这里50余万侨胞遍布东南亚各国，从缅甸、泰国到马来西亚，几乎每个侨居国都能找到保山口音。

这个数字背后的通道是历史的。自明清以来的"走夷方"，到抗战时期的滇缅公路，再到今天的口岸与铁路，保山人从未停止过跨境谋生。侨乡的形成，往往不是因为某个宏大的规划，而是因为一条商道、一次灾荒、一场战争。

## "寻根"篇：把先辈的名字念出来

"寻根"篇章里，缅甸腊戍果文中学教师李家润带来《火种，从未熄灭》，讲述爱国侨领梁金山青年赴缅、实业兴邦、造福桑梓的故事。梁金山是保山人，上世纪初南下缅甸经商致富，抗战爆发后倾资助国。

华裔少年黄汉清以《异乡的远征》，回望80多年前中国远征军跨出国门、滇西军民共赴国难的历史。保山市侨商联合会则用音诗画《天那边的路》，在朗诵、音乐与实景影像的交织中，致敬先辈筚路蓝缕的开拓精神。

从崇山峻岭间的"走夷方"到今日山海相连的通途，变的是通达方式，不变的是回望来路的目光。这也是"寻根"篇最想传递的东西：先辈的名字不该只留在族谱里。

## "铸魂"篇：守在海外的华文教师

"铸魂"篇章的主角，是坚守在海外的华文教育者。

傣族华裔、缅甸云华师范学院华文教师冯相民记得，外公在夜灯下一笔一画教他写"家""福""春"。启蒙老师告诉他，中文不只是语言，更是打开中华文化大门的钥匙。学成后，他回到曼德勒云华师范学院，成为连接缅甸学子与中华文化的纽带。

泰国清迈教联高级中学的华裔学子尹国炜，在《南枝寄》里讲起自己的阿嬷。小时候，阿嬷牵着他的手写"人"字：一撇一捺，相互支撑，这就是中国人的样子。如今，尹国炜向同学们讲春节由来、教同学写"福"字，成了传播中华文化的小使者。

## "致远"篇：把故事交给下一代

"致远"篇章把目光投向青春。登台的讲述者里，有在华留学生，也有刚接手的年轻侨团成员。他们谈论的不再只是"祖辈有多苦"，而是"我为什么还要学中文""我要把这件事做到什么时候"。

提问方式变了，答案却指向同一个方向：文化传承不是背下来的，而是用出来的。

## 从一场活动到一个机制

据主办方介绍，2023年以来，保山紧扣"讲好我们共同的故事"主题持续开展文化交流，吸引海外60余所华校、侨团参与，累计征集作品220余部，实现了"一年打基础、两年挖内涵、三年拓渠道、四年树品牌"的阶梯式跨越。

本次展演由云南海外联谊会、保山海外联谊会、保山市总工会主办，保山市侨联协办。活动已经成为保山涵养侨务资源、促进中外人文交流的重要品牌。

## 侨务工作的新命题

把镜头拉远，保山的做法回答了一个正在浮现的问题：当侨务工作走出"捐资助学"的单一模式，它还能做什么？

老一代侨胞与故乡的联系，多靠汇款、修路、建校维持，情感是实在的，表达却是单向的。而新一代华裔青年生长在多元社会里，他们对祖籍地的认同，很少来自血缘的自动继承，更多来自一次次具体的接触——一次登台、一本中文书、一次被听懂的表达。

展演这种形式的价值，恰恰在于它把"认同"从一个抽象命题，变成了可参与的过程。讲述者不是被告知"应该认同什么"，而是在整理家族故事时，自己完成了确认。

## 华文教育：需求在升温

保山的这场展演之外，还有一个更硬的背景：东南亚的华文教育需求正在上升。

在泰国，中文课程从选修变成部分学校的必修；在印尼，越来越多家庭把中文视为“有用”的技能；在马来西亚，华小与独中的招生长期饱和。需求拉动之下，缺的往往不是热情，而是师资与教材。

把中文书送进泰国校园、把海外华校请回侨乡，指向的是同一个短板：教育资源的供给跟不上需求的增长。相比一次性的捐资助学，稳定的教师培养与图书流转机制，才是让华文教育真正“活下去”的关键。

## 一条被反复走通的路

保山的特殊性在于地理：它既是侨乡，又是通道。50余万侨胞分布在东南亚各国，意味着这里的每一次文化交流，天然带着跨境属性。

从这个意义上说，"讲好我们共同的故事"不只是保山的故事。它是一条被反复走通的路——从滇西出发，经缅甸、泰国、老挝，再绕回云南。路上的人会换，路本身不会。

## 延伸阅读

**侨乡（qiaoxiang）**：指华侨华人集中、侨眷众多、与海外联系密切的特定地区，如广东江门、福建泉州、云南保山等。侨乡的形成往往与历史上的商道、口岸或移民潮相关。

**华文教育（Chinese-language education）**：海外华侨华人社会自办的以汉语和中华文化为主要内容的教育体系，涵盖华文学校、补习班与大学中文系，是文化传承的主要载体。

**寻根（root-seeking）**：华裔青年回到祖籍地了解家族历史、学习语言与文化的活动。近年来从个体行为发展为由侨务部门与华校共同组织的常态项目。

# A Stage in Baoshan: When the Young Tell the Story of Their Own Roots

On the evening of October 12, in Baoshan, Yunnan. There were no professional actors on stage — those who stepped up were Chinese-language teachers from Myanmar, ethnic-Chinese teenagers from Thailand, students from Laos and delegates of overseas clan associations. They spoke in Mandarin, in Dai, and in accented Putonghua, telling where their families had come from.

It was the 2026 staging of "Telling Our Shared Story," an event titled "Roots Continued, Imprints Enduring," unfolding across three chapters: Seeking Roots, Forging Spirit, Reaching Out. In the audience were clan leaders who had travelled from Yangon, and local secondary-school students.

## Baoshan: Yunnan's largest hometown of emigrants

Baoshan sits close to the China-Myanmar border and is the largest qiaoxiang — an emigrant hometown — in Yunnan. According to Feng Niguang, head of the Baoshan Overseas Friendship Association, more than 500,000 people from this area are scattered across Southeast Asia, from Myanmar and Thailand to Malaysia; in almost every country of settlement you can find a Baoshan accent.

Behind that figure lies a history of movement. From the "walking to foreign lands" of the Ming and Qing eras, to the Burma Road of the war years, to today's border crossings and railways, the people of Baoshan have never stopped making a living across borders. A qiaoxiang is rarely the product of a grand plan; it grows out of a trade route, a famine, or a war.

## Seeking Roots: Saying the ancestors' names aloud

In the Seeking Roots chapter, Li Jiarun, a teacher at Guowen Middle School in Lashio, Myanmar, presented "The Fire Never Dies," telling the story of the patriotic clan leader Liang Jinshan, who went to Myanmar as a young man, built businesses and gave back to his home village. Liang was from Baoshan; he made his fortune in Myanmar in the early twentieth century and donated heavily to support China after the war broke out.

Huang Hanqing, a teenage ethnic Chinese, recalled in "An Expedition in a Foreign Land" the Chinese Expeditionary Force that crossed the border more than eighty years ago and the soldiers and civilians of western Yunnan who faced the national crisis together. The Baoshan Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese Chamber of Commerce offered a music-and-poetry piece, "The Road Beyond the Mountains," paying tribute to the pioneering spirit of earlier generations.

From the rugged "walking to foreign lands" to today's connected highways, what has changed is the means of travel; what has not is the backward glance. That is the message the chapter wants to convey: the names of the ancestors should not survive only in a genealogy.

## Forging Spirit: The teachers who stay abroad

The Forging Spirit chapter centred on the Chinese-language educators who remain overseas.

Feng Xiangmin, an ethnic-Dai Chinese-language teacher at Yunhua Normal College in Myanmar, remembers his grandfather teaching him, stroke by stroke by lamplight, to write 家, 福, 春 — home, blessing, spring. His first teacher told him that Chinese was not merely a language but a key to the door of Chinese culture. After finishing his studies he returned to Mandalay to teach, becoming a link between Myanmar students and that culture.

Yin Guowei, an ethnic-Chinese student at the Chiang Mai United Education High School in Thailand, spoke of his grandmother in "A Branch Sent South." As a boy, she held his hand and taught him to write the character 人 — person — two strokes leaning on each other, she said, and that is what a Chinese person is. Today Yin tells classmates about the origins of Spring Festival and teaches them to write 福, a small ambassador of culture.

## Reaching Out: Handing the story to the next generation

The Reaching Out chapter turned its gaze to youth. Among the speakers were students studying in China and young members who had just taken over clan associations. They no longer speak only of "how hard our grandparents had it," but of "why I still learn Chinese" and "how long I intend to keep this up."

The question has changed; the answers point the same way: cultural inheritance is not memorised, it is used.

## From an event to a mechanism

According to the organisers, since 2023 Baoshan has run cultural exchanges under the theme "Telling Our Shared Story," drawing participation from more than 60 overseas Chinese schools and clan associations and collecting over 220 works — a step-by-step progression described as laying a foundation, then deepening content, then widening channels, then building a brand.

This year's staging was hosted by the Yunnan Overseas Friendship Association, the Baoshan Overseas Friendship Association and the Baoshan Federation of Trade Unions, and co-organised by the Baoshan Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese. It has become a signature effort for Baoshan in cultivating diaspora resources and promoting people-to-people exchange.

## A new question for diaspora work

Zoom out, and Baoshan's approach answers a question that is only now coming into focus: when diaspora work moves beyond the single model of donations and scholarships, what else can it do?

The link between older emigrants and their hometowns was sustained largely by remittances, roads and school buildings — the feeling was real, but the expression ran one way. Younger ethnic-Chinese now grow up in plural societies; their sense of belonging to an ancestral place rarely comes automatically with blood, but rather from concrete encounters — a turn on stage, a Chinese book, a sentence that is understood.

The value of a performance like this is precisely that it turns "identity" from an abstraction into something one can take part in. The speakers are not told what to identify with; in arranging their family stories, they confirm it themselves.

## Chinese-language education: demand is rising

Beyond the Baoshan staging lies a harder backdrop: demand for Chinese-language education across Southeast Asia is climbing.

In Thailand, Chinese has moved from elective to compulsory in some schools; in Indonesia, more families see Mandarin as a useful skill; in Malaysia, enrolment in national-type and independent Chinese schools has long been saturated. Where demand pulls, what is missing is not enthusiasm but teachers and teaching materials.

Sending Chinese books into Thai schools and inviting overseas Chinese schools back to the qiaoxiang point to the same shortfall: the supply of educational resources lags demand. Compared with one-off donations, a stable mechanism for training teachers and circulating books is what keeps Chinese-language education alive.

## A road walked again and again

What makes Baoshan distinctive is geography: it is both a qiaoxiang and a corridor. With more than half a million emigrants spread across Southeast Asia, every cultural exchange here carries a cross-border nature by default.

In that sense, "Telling Our Shared Story" is not only Baoshan's story. It is a road that has been walked again and again — setting out from western Yunnan, through Myanmar, Thailand and Laos, and looping back. The people on it will change; the road itself will not.

## Further reading

**Qiaoxiang**: A locality marked by heavy emigration, a large population of returned overseas Chinese and dependents, and close ties abroad — such as Jiangmen in Guangdong, Quanzhou in Fujian, or Baoshan in Yunnan. Such places typically emerge around historical trade routes, ports or waves of migration.

**Chinese-language education**: The system of schools, supplementary classes and university departments run by overseas Chinese communities, teaching Chinese language and culture. It is the main vehicle of cultural transmission abroad.

**Root-seeking**: Travel by ethnic-Chinese youth to their ancestral hometowns to learn family history, language and culture — increasingly organised as a regular programme by diaspora affairs offices and Chinese schools rather than an individual pursuit.

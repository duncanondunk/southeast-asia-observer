# 孙中山诞辰160周年：雅加达的纪念与南洋侨胞的百年回响

9月22日，中国驻印度尼西亚使馆在雅加达举办座谈会，纪念孙中山先生诞辰160周年。与会嘉宾追忆这位“革命先行者”与海外华侨、尤其是南洋侨胞之间血脉相连的历史。表面看，这是一场逢十逢百的常规纪念；但放在印尼——这片土地上生活着世界上规模最大的华侨华人社群之一，又正与中国全面战略伙伴关系持续升温的国度——这场纪念本身就是北京面向东南亚华侨华人展开公共外交的一枚精致注脚。

## 一场在雅加达举行的纪念

据中国侨网报道，座谈会由中国驻印尼使馆主办，旅印尼侨界、中资机构与当地留学生代表等共同参与。发言者反复回到一个核心叙事：孙中山的一生，几乎是一部“以海外为根据地”的革命史。他十数次往返南洋筹措经费、办报鼓呼、联络同志；辛亥革命能成，南洋侨胞的捐款、舆论与人身掩护是关键变量。说直白些，维系反清运动命脉的现金，相当部分来自那些再也未必回乡的人汇回的侨资。

把这样的纪念放在驻外使馆、放在印尼，意味深长。它既是对历史人物的礼敬，也是一次面向在印尼华侨华人的“情感点名”——提醒这个庞大社群：你们的祖辈，并非中国近代民族国家建构的旁观者，而是出资人。对一个珍视侨胞忠诚的国家而言，这番话值得当面讲。毕竟，在异国落地生根的第三代、第四代华裔，与故土的联结已更多停留在符号与情感层面，纪念正是维系这层联结最轻巧也最体面的方式。

## 孙中山与南洋：革命之母的由来

孙中山那句“华侨是革命之母”，至今仍是理解近代中国民族主义与离散族群关系的一把钥匙。二十世纪初，清廷鞭长莫及的东南亚，反而成了革命党人最安全的募款场。从新加坡的晚晴园、槟城的会馆，到巴达维亚（今雅加达）的中华会馆，华人读书会、报馆与同乡组织构成了同盟会的海外神经。其运转机制简单而有效：读书会兼作联络站，海外报章刊登革命檄文，同乡会（会馆）则把分散的捐款汇集起来，经香港、新加坡流向一次次起义的前线。

一个常被引用的数字说明了规模：辛亥革命前后，海外华侨的捐款构成了革命经费的绝大部分。黄花岗七十二烈士中，有相当比例是南洋归国青年。孙中山本人多次坦言，若无南洋侨胞，革命难以为继。这段历史之所以被反复讲述，是因为它提供了一个极为干净的“国家—离散”叙事：离散族群不是边缘的流亡者，而是民族觉醒的母体。也正因如此，历届中国政府都乐于在海外重述这一叙事——它把离散族群从“离开者”重新定义为“贡献者”，为主权国家与移民后裔之间提供了一套体面的关系语言。

## 印尼华人：从捐助者到双向桥梁

今天印尼华裔人口普遍估计在千万上下，是这个国家经济生活中举足轻重的少数族群，却也长期处在微妙的政治与文化夹缝中。从苏哈托时代到独立之后的很长一段时期，华文教育与公开族裔表达曾被长期压制；1965至1966年的动荡及其余波，更把许多华人身份推入地下。直到世纪末的民主改革之后，华裔才逐步重获公开身份——在商业、在文化，以及小心翼翼地，在政治之中。正因如此，任何来自祖籍国、以“共同历史”为名的姿态，都可能被华社读作文化认同的滋养，而被印尼主流社会解读为外部渗透。

这恰好解释了为何北京的侨务话语格外讲究分寸：纪念孙中山，强调的是“反殖民、求复兴”的普遍价值，而非指向任何特定的现政权认同。换言之，北京要的，是让印尼华裔在情感上与中国“连得上”，却不至于在政治上“被绑定”。这是一种高度成熟的离散治理技巧，历经百年试错方才成形。它既不同于殖民时代的同化压力，也不同于冷战时期的敌意防范，而是在承认离散社群双重归属的前提下，维系一种灵活而低调的情感纽带。

## 纪念之外：侨务公共外交的逻辑

把镜头拉回当下。中国与印尼刚刚度过建交七十五周年，双边贸易与投资在东盟框架内持续走高，雅万高铁（Whoosh）已成为中国—印尼合作的标志性工程。在这一背景下，使馆层面的文化纪念并非为怀旧而怀旧，它本质上是在“硬合作”之外铺设一层“软连接”。

对北京而言，东南亚逾三千万华侨华人，是一张任何大国都艳羡的“民间关系网”。通过纪念孙中山这类兼具历史合法性与情感共鸣的符号，北京把宏大的双边关系，落到具体的社群记忆之上。它低成本、低政治敏感度，却能持续强化华裔对“文化中国”的归属感。这比生硬的宣传更有效，也更符合软实力运作的常识：身份一旦被激活，便能完成条约独自无法完成的黏合。但也要看到，软连接的效果取决于双边关系的大气候；一旦经贸或地缘出现波折，文化记忆的黏性也会随之松动。

## 结语：百年回响与现实镜鉴

孙中山若在今日回望，大概会惊讶于南洋的变迁：当年靠侨汇与街头演说维系的反清网络，如今已扩展为横跨供应链、资本与文化的庞大离散体系。雅加达这场座谈会，纪念的是一个世纪前的先行者，照见的却是当下中国经略东南亚的一条暗线——以侨为桥，以史为媒。对印尼华裔而言，孙中山既是中国的符号，也是他们自身家族史的一部分——这种双重可读性是纪念能够成立的前提。

当然，纪念终归是纪念。真正决定印尼华裔命运与中印尼关系走向的，不是一场座谈会的温度，而是两国能否在经济互利、族群包容与相互尊重中找到可持续的平衡。百年回响仍在，但答案要写在未来的每一天里。

## 延伸阅读

**辛亥革命（Xinhai Revolution）**：1911年推翻清王朝的民主革命，以孙中山领导的同盟会为核心，南洋侨胞提供了关键的资金与舆论支持。

**中华会馆（Tiong Hoa Hwe Koan）**：1900年创立于巴达维亚（今雅加达）的印尼首个现代华侨组织，推动了华文教育与族群启蒙。

**南洋（Nanyang）**：近代中国对东南亚的称谓；二十世纪初，这里是革命党人募款、办报与避祸的核心地带。

# When Jakarta commemorates Sun Yat-sen at 160

On 22 September, the Chinese embassy in Indonesia held a symposium in Jakarta to mark the 160th anniversary of the birth of Sun Yat-sen. The date is no accident: Sun was born on 12 November 1866, and a sexagenary-cycle milestone of this weight invites commemoration across the Chinese world. Participants — drawn from the Indonesian Chinese community, Chinese-invested enterprises and Chinese students in the country — recalled the bond between the "forerunner of the revolution" and overseas Chinese, especially those in Nanyang, the old term for Southeast Asia. On the surface this was a routine decennial event. Yet staged inside an embassy, in Indonesia, it reads as a finely drawn stroke in Beijing's public diplomacy toward Southeast Asian Chinese. The choice of venue matters: a symposium inside an embassy is a sovereign act, signalling that the diaspora falls within the ambit of the sending state's cultural diplomacy, not merely its consular care.

## A commemoration in Jakarta

According to China Qiao Wang, the symposium was organised by the embassy and brought together multiple generations of the local diaspora. The speakers returned, almost ritually, to a single core narrative: Sun's life was effectively a revolution "based overseas." He crossed the Nanyang more than a dozen times to raise funds, publish newspapers and rally comrades. That the 1911 Revolution ultimately succeeded owed much to the donations, the publicity and the personal shelter provided by Nanyang compatriots. To put it plainly, the cash that kept the anti-Qing movement alive came disproportionately from remittances sent home by people who would never again live there.

Holding such a commemoration inside a diplomatic mission, and in Indonesia of all places, is therefore telling. It is both a tribute to a historical figure and an "emotional roll-call" directed at the country's Chinese community. The subtext is unmistakable: your forebears were not bystanders to China's modern founding — they were its financiers. For a state that prizes diaspora loyalty, that is a message worth delivering in person.

## Sun Yat-sen and the Nanyang: the "mother of the revolution"

Sun's famous line — that overseas Chinese are the "mother of the revolution" — remains the key to understanding modern Chinese nationalism and its diaspora. In the early 1900s, Southeast Asia, beyond the reach of the Qing court, became the safest fundraising ground for the revolutionaries. From the Sun Yat-sen Villa in Singapore to the clan halls of Penang and the Tiong Hoa Hwe Koan in Batavia (now Jakarta), Chinese study societies, newspapers and native-place associations formed the overseas nervous system of the Tongmenghui. The mechanics were simple and effective: reading clubs doubled as cell meetings, overseas newspapers carried the movement's manifestos, and the native-place associations pooled contributions that flowed, often via Hong Kong and Singapore, to the front lines of uprising. What made the Nanyang so useful was not only its distance from Qing officials but its wealth: the tin of Bangka, the rubber of Sumatra and the port commerce of the Straits Settlements had produced a merchant class with both surplus capital and a grievance against imperial decay.

A figure often cited captures the scale: in the years around the 1911 Revolution, overseas Chinese donations made up the great majority of revolutionary funds. A notable share of the seventy-two martyrs of Huanghuagang were young men who had returned from Nanyang. Sun himself admitted, more than once, that without the Nanyang compatriots the revolution could not have continued. This history is retold so often precisely because it supplies a supremely clean "state–diaspora" narrative: the diaspora were not marginal exiles but the womb of national awakening.

## Indonesian Chinese: from benefactors to a two-way bridge

Today Indonesia's ethnic Chinese, commonly estimated at around ten million, are a minority of outsized economic weight — yet one long caught in a delicate political and cultural cleft. Under Suharto, Chinese-language education and open ethnic expression were suppressed for decades; the 1965–66 upheaval and its aftermath pushed much of Chinese identity underground. Only after the democratic reforms at the century's turn did ethnic Chinese gradually recover a public identity — in business, in culture, and, cautiously, in politics. For that very reason, any gesture from the ancestral country framed around "shared history" can be read two ways at once: by the community as nourishment for cultural identity, and by Indonesia's majority society as a form of outside interference. The memory of that suppression is long, and it has shaped how the community weighs gestures from Beijing: welcome as cultural affirmation, scrutinised as political claim.

This duality explains why Beijing's diaspora rhetoric is so carefully calibrated. In commemorating Sun, it stresses the universal values of "anti-colonialism and renewal" rather than any allegiance to a particular present-day regime. In other words, Beijing wants Indonesian Chinese to feel emotionally "connected" to China without being politically "bound" to it. It is a highly mature technique of diaspora governance — one that has been refined across a century of trial and error.

## Beyond memory: the logic of diaspora public diplomacy

Step back to the present. China and Indonesia have just passed the 75th anniversary of diplomatic ties; bilateral trade and investment keep rising within the ASEAN framework, and the Jakarta–Bandung high-speed railway (Whoosh) has become a flagship of China–Indonesia cooperation. Against that backdrop, a cultural commemoration at the embassy level is not nostalgia for its own sake. It is, in effect, laying a layer of "soft connection" atop the "hard cooperation" of infrastructure and trade. None of this is unique to Indonesia: from Bangkok's Chinatown associations to the clan halls of Manila, the same repertoire of commemorations, language classes and homeland tours forms the connective tissue of China's diaspora outreach across the region.

For Beijing, the more than 30 million overseas Chinese in Southeast Asia are a "people-to-people network" that any great power would envy. By commemorating symbols like Sun Yat-sen — which carry both historical legitimacy and emotional resonance — Beijing translates grand bilateral relations into the concrete memory of a community. It is low-cost, low in political sensitivity, and yet steadily reinforces the ethnic Chinese attachment to a "cultural China." That is more effective than blunt propaganda, and it fits the common sense of how soft power actually operates: identity, once activated, does the work that treaties alone cannot.

## Closing: a century's echo and the present lens

Were Sun to look back today, he might be astonished at how the Nanyang has changed. The anti-Qing network once sustained by remittances and stump speeches has grown into a vast diasporic system spanning supply chains, capital and culture. The Jakarta symposium honours a forerunner of a century ago, yet it also illuminates a quiet thread in China's current management of Southeast Asia — using the diaspora as a bridge and history as a medium. The risk, for both Jakarta and Beijing, is that symbolism outruns substance. A commemorative symposium costs little and offends fewer people than a port concession or a debt deal. But the loyalties it courts are slow to form and quick to fade if the underlying relationship sours.

Commemoration, of course, is only commemoration. What truly decides the fate of Indonesian Chinese and the trajectory of China–Indonesia relations is not the warmth of one symposium, but whether the two countries can find a sustainable balance in economic mutual benefit, ethnic inclusion and mutual respect. The century's echo still sounds — but the answer must be written in the days still to come.

## Further reading

**Xinhai Revolution:** The 1911 democratic revolution that overthrew the Qing dynasty, led by Sun's Tongmenghui, with Nanyang overseas Chinese providing critical funds and publicity.

**Tiong Hoa Hwe Koan:** The first modern Chinese organisation in Indonesia, founded in Batavia (now Jakarta) in 1900, which pioneered Chinese-language education and communal awakening.

**Nanyang:** The late-imperial Chinese term for Southeast Asia; in the early 1900s it was the core zone where revolutionaries raised funds, ran newspapers and found refuge.

# 越南半导体战略崛起：从"世界工厂"到"芯片玩家"的赌注

## 从"世界工厂"到"芯片玩家"的野心

当全球供应链讨论"中国+1"时，越南正试图把叙事再往前推一步：不只承接组装，还要切入半导体。2026年，越南政府更新的国家半导体战略提出，到2030年前建立至少10个芯片封装与测试设施，到2050年成为全球主要供应国之一。这一目标的底气，来自多年电子制造积累——三星、苹果供应商已在越南北部形成庞杂的配套网络。但"组装强国"与"芯片强国"之间，隔着设计、材料与设备的深沟。河内要的，是一场从劳动密集型向技术密集型的身份跃迁。

## 三星与安靠的封测押注

外资是这场跃迁的第一块基石。三星电子2026年宣布投资约40亿美元（约合人民币290亿元）在越南北部太原省建设芯片封装测试工厂，是其自2008年进入越南以来最大单笔投资；封测巨头安靠（Amkor）亦在北宁省累计投入16亿美元布局先进封装。封装测试处在产业链中后段，技术门槛低于晶圆制造，却足以让越南嵌入全球芯片分工。对河内而言，这些项目不只是就业与税收，更是"我们也能做高科技"的政治证明——用外资的资本，换本土产业升级的门票。

## Viettel 自研芯片厂的象征意义

更具象征意义的，是军方背景的电信国企 Viettel。2026年1月，其在河内和乐高科技园区动工建设越南首座芯片制造厂，占地27公顷，采用32纳米制程，目标2027年底试产。国有企业下场造芯，凸显河内的战略焦虑：关键元器件不能永远依赖进口。32纳米并非尖端节点，却足以覆盖通信、安防与工业控制等刚需场景。Viettel 的案例说明，越南的半导体路径并非纯市场驱动，而带有鲜明的国家安全与自主可控色彩。

## 稀土出口禁令的战略算计

2025年底，越南投票决定停止稀土元素出口——这个拥有全球第二大稀土储量的国家，把资源优势直接转化为产业链筹码。逻辑很清晰：与其低价卖矿，不如把稀土留在境内，喂养本土的磁材与芯片材料环节。这一决策被解读为对标中国的"资源换产业"思路。但现实骨感：越南提炼与分离技术薄弱，储量优势短期内难以转化为制造优势。禁令更像一张远期期权，能否兑现，取决于本土加工能力能否在数年之内补课。

## 人才与生态的短板

野心越大，短板越显。越南每年工程类毕业生数量可观，但半导体高端人才——尤其是制程工艺、EDA工具与良率管理——极度稀缺。高校课程与产业需求脱节，企业普遍反映"招得到人、找不到将"。此外，越南缺乏晶圆厂设备、材料与IP生态，连封测环节也高度依赖进口设备。没有完整的上下游，单一工厂只是孤岛。河内寄望通过税收优惠与外资研发中心引流，但这套打法见效以十年计，远慢于政治议程的节拍。

## 在美中之间走钢丝

越南的半导体崛起，注定被放在大国博弈的显微镜下。美国推动"友岸外包"，乐见越南分担部分中国产能；中国则是越南最大贸易伙伴与关键设备来源。河内奉行"竹子外交"，既接入美方补贴与技术标准，又不愿对华脱钩伤及根本。这种平衡术在芯片领域尤其敏感：先进设备受出口管制约束，而中资又深度嵌入越南制造链。越南要的"战略自主"，恰是在两套体系夹缝中争取最大回旋余地。

## 日经视角：东南亚半导体版图重排

从区域视角看，越南的进击正在重排东南亚半导体版图。新加坡守住高端封装与设计，马来西亚槟城是OSAT重镇，泰国押注车规芯片，如今越南以成本与政策野心切入封测。产业转移不再是"一国通吃"，而是按环节分层落子。对日经亚洲观察到的跨国企业而言，这意味着供应链组合更灵活，也更易受地缘冲击。越南能否从"成本洼地"升级为"能力高地"，将决定它在新版图里的坐标。

## 越南模式的悬念

越南半导体战略最吊诡之处，在于用国家意志去追赶一个由市场与专利筑墙的赛道。它的优势是政局稳定、外资友好、劳动力年轻；劣势是技术底子薄、生态残缺、地缘敏感。10座封测厂的目标能否兑现，不只取决于招商数字，更取决于人才培养与设计能力的真实沉淀。河内押下的是时间赌注：若十年内未能形成闭环，今日的战略宏图，恐沦为又一轮"产业升级叙事"的未竟之篇。

## 延伸阅读

**封装与测试（OSAT, Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test）**：半导体产业链中后端环节，负责芯片封装保护与功能测试，技术门槛低于晶圆制造，是东南亚多国切入全球分工的主要入口。

**竹子外交（Bamboo diplomacy）**：越南外交战略比喻，指根基深扎本土、枝干灵活转向，在大国之间保持平衡、不选边站队的务实取向。

# Vietnam's Semiconductor Ambition: From Factory Floor to Fab Edge

## From "world's factory" to "chip player"

As supply chains debate "China+1," Vietnam is pushing the narrative one step further: not merely absorbing assembly but entering semiconductors. In 2026 Hanoi refreshed its national semiconductor strategy, targeting at least ten packaging and testing facilities by 2030 and a place among the world's major suppliers by 2050. The confidence rests on years of electronics manufacturing — Samsung and Apple suppliers have built dense supplier networks in northern Vietnam. But a chasm of design, materials and equipment separates "assembly power" from "chip power." What Hanoi wants is an identity leap from labour-intensive to technology-intensive.

## Samsung and Amkor bet on packaging

Foreign capital is the first building block. Samsung Electronics announced in 2026 a roughly US$4 billion (~RMB 29 billion) packaging-and-test plant in Thai Nguyen province — its largest single investment since entering Vietnam in 2008; OSAT giant Amkor has sunk US$1.6 billion into advanced packaging in Bac Ninh. Packaging and test sit in the mid-to-back end, lower-risk than wafer fabrication yet enough to embed Vietnam in global chip division of labour. For Hanoi these projects are more than jobs and tax — they are political proof that "we too can do high tech," buying a ticket to upgrading on the back of foreign capital.

## What Viettel's home-grown fab symbolises

More symbolic is Viettel, the military-linked telecom SOE. In January 2026 it broke ground on Vietnam's first chip fab in Hoa Lac, 27 hectares, a 32-nm process, aiming for trial production by late 2027. A state firm building chips betrays Hanoi's strategic anxiety: critical components cannot depend forever on imports. Thirty-two nanometres is no cutting edge, yet sufficient for communications, security and industrial-control needs. Viettel shows Vietnam's semiconductor path is not purely market-driven but carries a clear national-security and self-reliance hue.

## The calculus behind the rare-earth export ban

Late in 2025 Vietnam voted to halt rare-earth exports — a country with the world's second-largest reserves turning resource advantage directly into industrial-chain leverage. The logic is plain: sell ore cheap, or keep rare earths home to feed domestic magnet and chip-material loops. Read as Hanoi's answer to China's "resource-for-industry" playbook. But reality bites: Vietnam's refining and separation tech is weak, so the reserve edge cannot quickly become manufacturing edge. The ban looks more like a long-dated option whose payoff hinges on domestic processing catching up within years.

## The talent and ecosystem gap

The bigger the ambition, the barer the gap. Vietnam produces plenty of engineering graduates yet severely lacks high-end semiconductor talent — process engineering, EDA tools, yield management. University curricula lag industry need; firms say they can hire hands but not generals. Worse, Vietnam lacks the equipment, materials and IP ecosystem of fabs; even packaging leans on imported gear. Without an intact upstream-downstream chain, a lone fab is an island. Hanoi bets on tax breaks and foreign R&D centres, but that playbook matures in decades, far slower than political calendars tick.

## Walking the tightrope between the US and China

Vietnam's rise is watched through great-power lenses. Washington pushes "friend-shoring" and welcomes Hanoi sharing China's load; Beijing is Vietnam's top trade partner and key equipment source. Hanoi's "bamboo diplomacy" plugs into US subsidies and standards while refusing a decoupling that would wound its core. The balancing act is acute in chips: advanced gear is export-controlled, yet Chinese capital is deep in Vietnam's manufacturing chain. The "strategic autonomy" Hanoi seeks is precisely maximum manoeuvre room in the cleft of two systems.

## Nikkei view: Southeast Asia's chip map redrawn

Regionally, Vietnam's charge is redrawing the Southeast Asian semiconductor map. Singapore holds high-end packaging and design, Malaysia's Penang is an OSAT stronghold, Thailand bets on automotive chips, and now Vietnam enters packaging on cost and policy ambition. Relocation no longer means one country wins all, but layers by segment. For multinationals Nikkei Asia tracks, that means more flexible supply combinations and more geopolitical exposure. Whether Vietnam climbs from "cost lowland" to "capability highland" decides its coordinate in the new map.

## The suspense of the Vietnam model

The most paradoxical part of Vietnam's strategy is using state will to chase a track walled by market and patents. Its strengths are stable politics, foreign-investor friendliness and a young workforce; its weaknesses are thin technological foundations, a broken ecosystem and geopolitical sensitivity. Whether the ten-fab target lands depends less on investment tallies than on real accumulation of talent and design capability. Hanoi is betting on time: if a closed loop is not formed within a decade, today's strategic vision risks becoming another unfinished "upgrading narrative."

## Further reading

**OSAT (Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test)**: the mid-to-back-end of the chip value chain, handling packaging protection and functional testing; lower-risk than wafer fabrication and the main entry for many Southeast Asian countries into global division of labour.

**Bamboo diplomacy**: a metaphor for Vietnam's foreign strategy — deep roots at home, flexible branches abroad, keeping balance among great powers without taking sides.

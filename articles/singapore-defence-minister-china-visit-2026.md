# 新加坡防长访华出席香山论坛：小国大外交里的一处平衡支点

## 一次"例牌"之外的访华

2026年9月15日至17日，新加坡国防部长陈振声应中国国防部长董军邀请访华，并出席第13届北京香山论坛。对新加坡而言，防长级访华并非稀罕事，但此行的节点与语境使其超出"例行交流"：中美战略竞争 intensifying、区域安全议题频发的当口，一个以"平衡外交"立国的小国，如何既维持与华盛顿的深厚防务合作，又向北京释放"关系温热"的信号，本身就值得细读。陈振声的北京之行，是一次精心校准的再确认——确认的不只是双边关系，更是新加坡立国以来那条"不选边"的底层逻辑。

## 香山论坛的角色

香山论坛是中国主办的国际安全与防务对话平台，被视为中方版"香格里拉对话"的 Track 1.5 机制。本届主题定为"凝聚稳定共识，共促安全治理"，与中方近期强调的"全球南方代表权"叙事相互呼应。新加坡连续第二年派防长出席并致辞，传递的意味是：即便不认同中方所有安全主张，仍愿留在同一张桌子前对话。对一个高度依赖规则与沟通渠道的小国而言，"在场"本身就是一种资产——缺席才意味着被排除在议程之外。

## 新加坡的平衡术为何重要

新加坡的地缘处境，决定了它的平衡不是姿态而是生存术。扼守马六甲海峡要冲，经济命脉系于全球贸易与航行自由，任何大国对抗的溢出都会直接冲击其繁荣。因此新加坡长期把"与所有大国保持建设性关系"写入国家安全教条：与美国有紧密的防务合作与轮换部署，与中国则有常年防务政策对话、军演与人员往来。陈振声此访，正是对这条"双轨"的再次演示——见董军、晤解放军高层，同时不讳言与华盛顿的同盟式协作。平衡不是等距，而是让每条线都不断。

## 中新防务交流的实质

中新防务关系常被概括为"低调而密集"。两国设有防务政策对话、开展"中新合作"海上联合演习，并在反恐、维和、人道救援等领域有务实合作。这些交流的意义不在于达成重大突破，而在于降低误判：当双方军方有固定沟通管道，海上偶遇或误会升级的概率便下降。对中国而言，与新加坡这样的区域枢纽保持防务沟通，有助于在南海等敏感议题上保留"软接触"空间；对新加坡而言，则是把"被卷入"的风险，通过透明化降至最低。

## 在大国之间不选边的逻辑

"不选边"常被误解为骑墙。对新加坡，它是一套清晰的利益算法：选边意味着把自身安全绑定的变量交给他人决策，违背小国主权逻辑。更务实的做法，是在每个议题上依据规则与国际法独立表态，而非预设阵营。陈振声在论坛演讲中延续其一贯风格，以东西方典故说明"权力与原则"的取舍，正是把这套算法公开化。这种"原则导向"的自主，恰是新加坡能在中美之间都保住对话资格的原因。

## 对区域安全架构的意味

新加坡防长访华，放在区域尺度看，是东南亚"对冲战略"的一个微缩样本。东盟多国普遍奉行类似逻辑——既不希望美国撤出，也不愿激怒中国，于是用多边机制、双边对话与经贸纽带织成缓冲网。当区域安全架构面临"阵营化"压力，这类小国的中间态反成稳定器：它们用持续对话抵消硬对抗的引力。香山论坛上的新加坡身影，提醒外界——"选边"并非区域国家的默认选项，缓冲仍有可能。

## 陈振声此行的信号

回到个人层面，这是陈振声出任防长后一次重要的对华亮相。他在社交平台以"赶红眼航班落地一座以鸟巢闻名的城市"的轻松口吻开场，既贴合其亲民风格，也软化了高层防务交往的硬壳。信号层面，此行至少传递三层：其一，中新防务关系"长期、友好、稳定"的定性不变；其二，新加坡重视与北京的沟通渠道；其三，在区域不确定性上升时，小国仍愿以对话而非疏远应对。轻描淡写之下，是精算过的分寸。

## 小国大外交的恒常命题

新加坡的案例，折射出所有中等与小国的恒常命题：在一个被大国引力拉扯的世界里，如何保住自主。答案不在口号，而在日复一日的制度性经营——固定的对话、透明的规则、可预期的立场。陈振声的北京之行不会登上头条，却正是这类"不 headline"的往来，构成了区域稳定的毛细血管。当动荡成为常态，小国的价值往往不在体量，而在它能否把平衡活成一种可复制的艺术。

## 延伸阅读

**香山论坛（Xiangshan Forum）**：由中国军事科学学会与中国国际战略学会共同主办的国际安全与防务对话平台，汇集各国防务、军方与外交代表，被视为中方主导的 Track 1.5 安全机制。

**对冲战略（Hedging）**：中小国家在大国竞争中同时与多方保持合作、以降低单一依赖风险的策略；东南亚多国普遍以多边机制与双边对话编织缓冲。

# Singapore's Defence Minister Visits China for Xiangshan Forum: A Balancing Pivot in Small-State Diplomacy

## A visit beyond the "routine"

From 15 to 17 September 2026, Singapore's Defence Minister Chan Chun Sing visited China at the invitation of Chinese Defence Minister Admiral Dong Jun and attended the 13th Beijing Xiangshan Forum. For Singapore, ministerial visits to China are not rare, but the timing and context pushed this one past "routine exchange": amid intensifying US–China strategic competition and frequent regional security flashpoints, how a small state founded on "balancing diplomacy" maintains deep defence ties with Washington while signalling "warm relations" to Beijing deserves close reading. Chan's Beijing trip is a carefully calibrated re-confirmation — not only of bilateral ties but of Singapore's founding logic of "not choosing sides."

## The role of the Xiangshan Forum

The Xiangshan Forum is China's flagship international security and defence dialogue platform, often read as Beijing's Track 1.5 answer to the Shangri-La Dialogue. This edition's theme, "Build Consensus on Stability, Advance Security Governance Together," echoes Beijing's recent "Global South representation" narrative. Singapore's second consecutive year sending its defence minister to speak signals this: even without endorsing every Chinese security proposition, it stays at the same table to talk. For a small state reliant on rules and communication channels, "being present" is itself an asset — absence means exclusion from the agenda.

## Why Singapore's balancing matters

Singapore's geostrategic position makes its balance a survival art, not a posture. Holding the Malacca Strait chokepoint, its prosperity hinges on global trade and freedom of navigation; any great-power spillover hits directly. Hence Singapore has long written "constructive relations with all powers" into its national-security creed: deep defence cooperation and rotational deployments with the US, plus常年 defence-policy dialogue, exercises and personnel exchanges with China. Chan's visit re-demonstrates this "dual track" — meeting Dong and PLA leaders while not hiding close collaboration with Washington. Balance is not equidistance but keeping every line alive.

## The substance of China–Singapore defence ties

The China–Singapore defence relationship is often summarised as "low-key but dense." The two hold defence-policy dialogues, run the "Cooperation" maritime joint exercises, and cooperate pragmatically on counter-terrorism, peacekeeping and humanitarian relief. The point is not breakthroughs but miscalculation reduction: fixed military-to-military channels lower the odds of escalation from maritime encounters or misunderstandings. For China, keeping defence communication with a regional hub preserves "soft-contact" space on sensitive issues like the South China Sea; for Singapore, it minimises the risk of being "drawn in" through transparency.

## The logic of not choosing sides

"Not choosing sides" is often misread as fence-sitting. For Singapore it is a clear interest algorithm: choosing a side means handing the variable of its own security to others' decisions, violating small-state sovereignty logic. The pragmatic alternative is independent stance-taking on each issue per rules and international law, not preset camps. In his forum speech Chan延续 his signature style, using Eastern and Western aphorisms to illustrate the trade-off between power and principle — making that algorithm public. This "principle-oriented" autonomy is precisely why Singapore retains dialogue eligibility with both Washington and Beijing.

## What it means for the regional security architecture

Chan's China visit, at regional scale, is a miniature of Southeast Asia's "hedging strategy." Many ASEAN states share the logic — neither wanting US withdrawal nor provoking China, they weave buffer nets from multilateral mechanisms, bilateral dialogues and economic ties. As the regional architecture faces "bloc-ification" pressure, such small-state middle positions become stabilisers: they offset the gravity of hard confrontation through sustained dialogue. Singapore's figure at Xiangshan reminds outsiders that "choosing sides" is not the default option for regional states — buffers remain possible.

## The signals from Chan's trip

At the personal level, this is an important China debut for Chan as defence minister. He opened on social media with a light touch — "caught a red-eye after meeting voters, landed in a city famous for its Bird's Nest" — fitting his approachable style while softening the hard shell of high-level defence engagement. At the signal level the trip sends at least three layers: first, the定性 of China–Singapore defence ties as "long-term, friendly, stable" holds; second, Singapore values its communication channel with Beijing; third, when regional uncertainty rises, small states still prefer dialogue over distance. Beneath the understatement lies calculated proportion.

## The perennial question of small-state diplomacy

Singapore's case reflects the perennial question for all middle and small states: in a world pulled by great-power gravity, how to preserve autonomy. The answer lies not in slogans but in day-to-day institutional cultivation — fixed dialogues, transparent rules, predictable stances. Chan's Beijing trip will not make headlines, yet it is precisely such "non-headline" exchanges that form the capillaries of regional stability. When turbulence becomes normal, a small state's value lies less in size than in whether it can live balance as a replicable art.

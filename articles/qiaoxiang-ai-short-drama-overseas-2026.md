# 从卖货到卖故事：侨乡如何用AI短剧，把文化"新三样"交给侨胞带出海

2026年秋天，中国的侨乡——那些海外华侨华人的祖籍地——出现了一处不易察觉的变化。曾经装满服装、家具、家电的出口篮子，如今装进了一些更轻、也更难定价的东西：网络文学、网络影视剧、网络游戏，也就是所谓的文化"新三样"。这一轮与以往不同的地方，不只在人工智能——它大幅压低了视频制作与多语种本地化的成本，更在于一种老资产的重新被发现：那些横跨东南亚（Southeast Asia）、欧洲与美洲的华侨商界与社群网络。当短剧遇上侨乡的"朋友圈"，讲出的与其说是一段国家软实力的故事，不如说是一段关于商业渠道与文化亲近性的故事。

## 汕头：算力打底，短剧出海添动力

广东潮汕侨乡汕头，靠基础设施打头阵。2026年9月，汕头华侨经济文化合作试验区——全国少数获准试点"来数加工"业务的区域之一——推出"词元十条"，发放"算力券"与"词元券"。针对AI微短剧，政策按不超过实际算力成本的40%给予补贴，单个企业每年最高可得100万元（约14万美元）。这套逻辑站得住脚：画面生成与多语种译制都要消耗算力与电力，一家小工作室独自扛不住。汕头的优势是结构性的——它拥有规模可观的国际海缆登陆站、区域性国际通信业务出入口局、海上风电，以及合规开展跨境数据处理的空间。目前试验区已落地文化传媒类企业263家，其中重点微短剧企业5家，签约及合作创作者超5000人。2020年由一位英国留学归国人员创办的稻谷文化，如今已打通从剧本到全球发行的全链条；其一部本土化短剧在北美3个月累计播放量突破10亿次。经验很清楚：文化出海，如今始于服务器，而非摄影棚。

## 泉州：侨韵入戏，AI演绎侨乡故事

福建泉州，靠的是故事。一部以王文教为主人公的人物微短剧正在筹拍——这位羽毛球名宿生于印度尼西亚（Indonesia），祖籍泉州南安，该剧由中方机构与印尼（Indonesia）国家电视台合作，采用中印尼（China–Indonesia）双语、实景与AIGC结合的方式制作。2025年10月开园的百崎两岸微短剧产业园，配有79个实景拍摄场景、AI内容创作中心与OPC（一人公司）社区。泉州的卖点是"不只好看，更有故事"：一封侨批、一栋番客楼，背后可能藏着几代人下南洋的记忆。园区已举办6期AI漫剧培训班，学员接受为期7天的培训，学习AI工具应用与剪辑发布。关键一步是，泉州把发行交给了侨胞——它已与印尼短剧平台Folaplay合作，并对接菲律宾（Philippines）、越南（Vietnam）、新加坡、马来西亚（Malaysia）等地的发行渠道。正如园区一位负责人所说，长期旅居海外的侨胞熟悉当地口味，既能充当渠道联系人，也能成为内容共创者；很多好素材可能就在老照片、家书或长辈的口述里。

## 瑞安：创意扎堆，漫剧出海有门道

浙江瑞安，示范的是"扎堆"模式。2026年夏天，一场AIGC训练营吸引了来自10多个省份的80余名创作者；短短数天，学员完成63部AIGC视频，而当地网络文学村此前已孵化48个影视项目，其中18部实现商业化落地。瑞安的独到之处，是把创作者与一家本就有海外触达的运营方配对：瑛麒动漫旗下漫画阅读平台"漫画人"成长于瑞安，长期深耕东南亚华人市场并拓展至欧美，目前月活跃用户约2000万。这家企业按地区口味做本地化——欧美偏奇幻冒险，东南亚更多推国风玄幻、逆袭类——再相应调整人物、剧情与配音。2026年10月，瑞安与中华版权服务有限公司合作，落地"创意中国AIGC权益存证平台"首个县域试点，这释放出一个信号：瓶颈已从"做内容"转移到"护内容、卖内容"。截至目前，瑞安累计孵化短剧作品近500部，未来还计划借遍布世界的侨商、侨团网络拓展海外发行。

## 侨网即渠道：离散网络为何是文化出海的"最后一公里"

剥开技术外壳，真正的故事是"分发"。如今AI几天就能产出一部配好音、调好风格的短剧，但触达外国观众，仍取决于你知不知道他们看什么、怎么付费、忌讳什么。而这道缺口，恰好由侨胞网络补齐——他们不是被动的消费者，而是"文化亲近性"的最后一公里。华侨商人握有渠道、平台关系与语言能力；华侨社群提供的文化桥梁，是任何算法都伪造不出的。侨乡政府凭直觉抓住了这一点：它们的补贴不只投向生产，更投向更难的一环——把成品送进海外的信息流。不过也须克制。这里扩张的是商业性的文化贸易，而非自动变现的影响力。这些短剧能否建立真正的共鸣，取决于故事本身，而非管道；而瑞安已现端的AIGC版权问题，将决定创作者是被保护还是被替代。侨乡的实践提醒我们：把内容交给侨胞带出海，关键不在渠道多广，而在内容是否值得被带。

## 延伸阅读

- **侨乡（Qiaoxiang）**：中国南方侨民祖籍地（以广东、福建、浙江最为集中），与海外华侨保持紧密纽带；长期是侨汇节点，如今也成为文化出海的节点。
- **侨批（Qiaopi）**：兼具家书功能的华侨汇款凭证，已被联合国教科文组织列入"世界记忆名录"，是侨乡叙事反复取用的素材。
- **漫画（Manhua）**：华语漫画；"漫画人"平台显示，既有漫画IP库如何成为AI漫剧的素材来源。

# From Goods to Stories: How China's Qiaoxiang Are Turning AI Short Dramas into a Diaspora-Led Cultural Export

In the autumn of 2026, a quiet shift is visible in China's qiaoxiang — the hometowns of overseas Chinese. The export basket that once carried clothing, furniture and home appliances now carries something lighter and harder to price: web novels, online dramas and games, the so-called "new three items" of culture. What makes this round different is not only artificial intelligence, which has slashed the cost of producing and localising video, but the rediscovery of an old asset — the overseas Chinese business and community networks that stretch across Southeast Asia, Europe and the Americas. When short dramas meet the qiaoxiang "circle of friends," the result is less a story of state soft power than a story of commercial channels and cultural proximity.

## Shantou: computing power as the base

Shantou, a Teochew-origin qiaoxiang in Guangdong, leads with infrastructure. In September 2026 the Shantou Overseas Chinese Economic and Cultural Cooperation Zone — one of the few areas authorised to pilot "incoming data processing" — rolled out the "Ciyuan Ten" package, offering "computing vouchers" and "token vouchers." For AI micro-dramas, it covers up to 40% of actual computing costs, capped at 1 million yuan (about US$140,000) per firm per year. The logic is sound: generating images and dubbing into many languages consumes tokens and electricity, and a small studio cannot absorb that alone. Shantou's edge is structural — a major international submarine-cable landing station, a regional international communications gateway, offshore wind power, and the legal room to process cross-border data. The zone now hosts 263 cultural and media enterprises, including 5 key micro-drama firms, with over 5,000 signed or collaborating creators. Daogu Culture, founded in 2020 by a returnee from Britain, runs an end-to-end chain from scripting to global distribution; one of its localised dramas drew over 1 billion views in three months in North America. The lesson is plain: cultural export now begins with servers, not studios.

## Quanzhou: diaspora stories re-enacted by AI

Quanzhou in Fujian leans on narrative. A biographical short drama about Wang Wenjiao — a badminton pioneer born in Indonesia with ancestral roots in Quanzhou's Nan'an — is being co-produced with Indonesia's state television, in Chinese-Indonesian bilingual form, mixing real footage with AIGC. The Baiqi cross-strait micro-drama industrial park, opened in October 2025, offers 79 shooting sets, an AI creation centre and an OPC (one-person company) community. Quanzhou's pitch is that it is "not just photogenic but story-rich": a single qiaopi (remittance letter) or an old overseas Chinese mansion can hold generations of Nanyang migration memory. The park has run six AI manhua-drama training cohorts, each giving participants seven days of hands-on instruction in AI tools and editing. Crucially, Quanzhou turns to its diaspora for distribution — it has partnered with Indonesia's Folaplay platform and connected with channels in the Philippines, Vietnam, Singapore and Malaysia. As one park manager put it, overseas Chinese who live abroad know local taste and can act not only as contact points but as co-creators; many good stories may sit in old photographs, family letters or an elder's oral accounts.

## Rui'an: where creativity clusters for manhua-drama export

Zhejiang's Rui'an shows the clustering model. In summer 2026 an AIGC training camp drew more than 80 creators from over ten provinces; in a few days they produced 63 AIGC videos, and the local literary village has already incubated 48 film projects, 18 of them commercialised. Rui'an's distinctive move is pairing creators with an operator that already has overseas reach: Yingqi Animation, whose comic platform "Manhuaren" grew in Rui'an and now serves about 20 million monthly active users across Southeast Asia's Chinese communities and beyond. The firm localises by analysing regional taste — fantasy and adventure for the West, gufeng (ancient-style) and "counterattack" tropes for Southeast Asia — then adjusts characters, plots and dubbing accordingly. In October 2026 Rui'an launched the first county-level pilot of an AIGC rights platform with the China Copyright Service, a telling sign that the bottleneck has moved from making content to protecting and monetising it. To date the city has incubated nearly 500 short-drama works and plans to use its worldwide qiao merchant and association networks to widen overseas distribution.

## The qiao network as channel: why diaspora networks are the "last mile"

Strip away the technology and the real story is distribution. AI can now generate a dubbed, styled drama in days, but reaching a foreign audience still depends on knowing what they watch, how they pay, and what offends them. This is precisely the gap that diaspora networks fill — not as passive consumers but as the "last mile" of cultural proximity. Overseas Chinese merchants hold shelf space, platform ties and linguistic fluency; overseas Chinese communities supply the cultural bridge that no algorithm can fake. The qiaoxiang governments have grasped this intuitively: their subsidies target not just production but the harder link — getting a finished product into overseas feeds. Yet restraint is warranted. What is expanding here is commercial cultural trade, not automatic influence. Whether these dramas build genuine resonance depends on the stories themselves, not the pipes; and the AIGC copyright question already visible in Rui'an will decide whether creators are protected or displaced. The qiaoxiang experiment is a reminder that handing content to the diaspora to carry abroad hinges not on how wide the channel is, but on whether the content is worth carrying.

## Further reading

- **Qiaoxiang (侨乡)**: Hometowns of emigrants in southern China (notably Guangdong, Fujian and Zhejiang) that maintain dense ties with their diaspora; long a node for remittances and, now, for cultural export.
- **Qiaopi (侨批)**: Overseas Chinese remittance letters that doubled as family correspondence; recognised by UNESCO as a Memory of the World, they are a recurring source of qiaoxiang narratives.
- **Manhua (漫画)**: Chinese-language comics; the "Manhuaren" platform shows how an existing comic IP base becomes feedstock for AI manhua-dramas.

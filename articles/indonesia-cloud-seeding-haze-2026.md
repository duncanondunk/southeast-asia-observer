# 撒盐催雨，难掩焦土：印尼扩大人工增雨抗林火，专家指治标不治本

印尼（Indonesia）林业部8月18日公布的数据触目惊心：今年1月至7月，全国共有20万2004公顷土地被烧毁；仅7月一个月，林火就吞噬近9万5000公顷，几乎相当于前六个月过火面积的两倍。受强厄尔尼诺（El Niño）现象影响，今年的林火很可能成为近年来最严重之一。

## 一场被厄尔尼诺点燃的旱季

为缓解持续高温、多日无雨与林火肆虐带来的烟霾（haze），印尼政府正扩大人工增雨（cloud seeding）作业规模。数十架直升机与固定翼飞机在全国待命，向云中播撒碘化银、干冰与盐粉等催化剂。数据显示，2021年至今年8月，印尼共实施252次人工增雨，远高于此前十年的85次。

## 技术能唤来雨，却唤不来云

专家质疑，在野火最猛的旱季作业，效果存疑。印尼茂物农业大学（IPB University）水文气候学讲师桑蒂卡亚萨指出："由于缺乏云层，人工增雨能取得的效果将非常有限。"人们也对碘化银的潜在危害与高成本提出疑问。印尼气象、气候和地球物理局天气改良主任布迪承认，人工增雨只是缓解措施，而非永久方案。

## 根子在土地，不在天空

环保组织印尼环境论坛（WALHI）指出，森林与泥炭地（peatland）被大规模开发转作他用，加上部分小农户仍以焚烧方式清理土地，这才是林火频发的根源。环境部长朱慕已指示易受灾地区加强预防性巡逻，并对非法焚烧施以罚款乃至刑事处罚。

## 城市的肺也要修

持续山火与无雨天气令雅加达（Jakarta）空气质量恶化，居民反映呼吸不适。这座城市的绿地仅占5%，90%的土地被混凝土覆盖；环境论坛认为，旱季极端高温正源于这种环境失衡。协调员穆罕默德说，人工增雨不能成为唯一方案，"我们需要的是城市结构的重组"。

## 跨境烟霾的老问题

林火烟霾常年外溢至新加坡（Singapore）、马来西亚（Malaysia）等邻国，触及《东盟跨境烟霾污染协定》（ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution）。短期靠催雨治标，长期仍须泥炭地修复、土地执法与区域协作——天空的盐，填不满土地的沟。

## 延伸阅读

- **人工增雨（Cloud Seeding）**：向云中播撒碘化银、干冰、盐粉等催化剂以提高降水效率的技术，印尼用于缓解林火旱情。
- **泥炭地（Peatland）**：富含碳的湿地土壤，一旦排干焚烧会释放巨量温室气体，是东南亚跨境烟霾的主要来源。
- **东盟跨境烟霾污染协定（ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution）**：2002年签署的 regional 协定，旨在遏制林火烟霾越境，但执行长期受诟病。

# Salting the Skies, Not the Soil: Indonesia Scales Up Cloud-Seeding Against Fires, but Experts Call It a Band-Aid

Figures released by Indonesia's Ministry of Forestry on 18 August are sobering: from January to July this year, 202,004 hectares of land were burned nationwide; in July alone, fires consumed nearly 95,000 hectares — almost twice the total for the first six months. Driven by a strong El Niño, this year's fires could rank among the worst in recent memory.

## A Dry Season Lit by El Niño

To ease the haze from prolonged heat, rainlessness and wildfires, the government is expanding its cloud-seeding operations. Dozens of helicopters and fixed-wing aircraft stand ready nationwide to scatter silver iodide, dry ice and salt powder into the clouds. Data show that from 2021 to August this year Indonesia conducted 252 seeding sorties — far above the 85 logged in the prior decade.

## Technology Can Summon Rain, Not Clouds

Experts question the efficacy at the peak of the dry season. Santika Yasa, a hydrometeorology lecturer at IPB University, notes: "Because of the lack of clouds, the effect cloud seeding can achieve will be very limited." Concerns have also been raised about the potential harm of silver iodide and its cost. Budi, head of weather modification at the meteorology agency, concedes seeding is a palliative, not a permanent fix.

## The Root Is in the Land, Not the Sky

The environmental group WALHI points out that forests and peatlands have been extensively converted to other uses, and that some smallholders still clear land by burning — the real source of recurrent fires. Environment Minister Sri Supit lists preventive patrols in vulnerable areas and penalties, including fines and criminal sanctions, for illegal burning.

## The City's Lungs Need Repair Too

Persistent fires and rainless weather have worsened air quality in Jakarta, with residents reporting breathing trouble. The city's green space is just 5% of its area, 90% covered in concrete; WALHI argues the extreme dry-season heat stems from precisely this environmental imbalance. Coordinator Muhammad says seeding cannot be the only answer — "what we need is a restructuring of the urban fabric."

## The Old Problem of Cross-Border Haze

Fire haze routinely spills into Singapore, Malaysia and other neighbours, implicating the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution. Short-term relief comes from salting the skies; the long-term cure still demands peatland restoration, land enforcement and regional cooperation — the salt in the sky cannot fill the trenches in the soil.

## Further reading

- **Cloud Seeding**: A technique that scatters silver iodide, dry ice or salt powder into clouds to raise precipitation efficiency; Indonesia uses it to ease fire-driven drought.
- **Peatland**: Carbon-rich wetland soil that, once drained and burned, releases vast greenhouse-gas emissions and is the main source of transboundary haze in Southeast Asia.
- **ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution**: A 2002 regional pact aimed at curbing cross-border fire haze, whose enforcement has long been criticised as weak.

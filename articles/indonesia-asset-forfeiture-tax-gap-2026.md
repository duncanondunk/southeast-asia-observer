# 十八年等来的资产没收法，为什么把税务犯罪排除在外

印尼国会11月19日通过《与腐败及其他刑事犯罪相关资产没收法》。据《日经亚洲》报道，这部历经18年才落槌的法律，本该是追缴赃款的利剑，却在最后关头把税务犯罪排除在适用范围之外。反腐监督团体对此不满，认为这是明显的弱化。一把迟到的法槌砍向腐败，却绕开了最常见的资金藏匿通道之一。问题来了：这是立法的技术性取舍，还是信心工程压过了追赃决心？

## 十八年才落槌，双轨机制终于进门

这部法律的构想始于2008年，草案2012年提交国会，此后几度搁置，直到2026年11月19日才在全体会议上一锤定音。国会议长普安强调，法案比原定12月15日的目标提前完成；法律事务委员会主席哈比博罗赫曼则把它概括为两套机制并行：传统的定罪后没收，以及特定情形下的无罪没收。

所谓无罪没收，并非警察可以直接抄家。嫌疑人或被告死亡、潜逃、永久患病，或者身份不明、下落不明，刑事案件无法正常推进时，检方才能向法院申请没收涉案资产，门槛是资产取得时价值至少10亿印尼盾（约5.5万美元，约合40万元人民币）。法院审查、异议举证和善意第三方保护都被写进条文。印尼官僚改革部长里妮·维迪扬蒂说，过去追赃高度依赖刑事定罪，新法补上了程序缺口。这个缺口确实存在，但补得够不够，要看后面几刀砍在哪里。

## 十三类缩成六类，被砍掉的不只是税务

最终文本共74条，适用犯罪从讨论中的13类压缩到腐败加6类其他犯罪：毒品与精神药物、恐怖主义及恐怖主义融资、人口走私、武器弹药及危险品走私、人口贩运，以及利用信息技术实施且影响广泛公众经济的欺诈或侵占。税务、银行、保险、林业、矿业和环境犯罪，全部被挡在门外。

哈比博罗赫曼给出的理由是防止范围过宽、避免执法机关滥用权力。这个理由并非没有道理：没收权一旦泛化，确实可能伤及无辜。但把税务犯罪整体剔除，等于在腐败与洗钱之间人为切开一道口子。税务犯罪不是小偷小摸；虚开发票、转移定价、隐匿收入，往往就是把贪腐所得洗成“合法商业利润”的管道。只追行贿受贿的赃款，却不追洗白赃款的那条河，追赃效率能高到哪里去？

## 不明财富条款消失，反腐工具箱少了一件

据印尼商业媒体与反腐观察团体披露，11月16日版本草案中原有一项“不明财富”条款：公职人员的财产若无法用合法收入解释，或未如实列入财产申报，检方即可追查。最终文本把这项条款删除了。印尼腐败观察组织指出，这一删除背离了印尼2006年批准的《联合国反腐败公约》第20条所体现的方向；该条虽属建议性条款，但正是各国据以设计本国工具的依据。

委员会方面的解释是，不明财富条款与人权保护存在冲突，美国和英国没有类似规定，德国的同类条款也被宪法法院推翻。这套辩解经不起细看：英国有不明财富令，美国有民事没收，比较法引用选择性地避开了不利例子。更需要追问的是，既然保留无罪没收这个更有争议的工具，为什么偏偏丢掉不明财富这个相对成熟的配套？答案恐怕不在法条技术，而在政治意愿。

## “爱国债券”与金融中心，信心工程压过追赃

《日经亚洲》的报道把法案缩水与另一条线索放在一起：政府正在推销“爱国债券”，并力图把印尼推上国际金融中心的台阶。两件事看似不相干，逻辑却相通——当国家需要向市场证明“资金是安全的”，对经济犯罪的追缴力度就容易被放进次要位置。

这不是说政府有意保护犯罪所得，而是说政策排序露了底：吸引资本、稳住信心、做大市场，优先于把每一条资金链查个水落石出。对一座想成为区域金融枢纽的城市而言，清洁的名声和宽松的执法之间，本来就该有清晰边界。现在的问题是，边界被画在了税务犯罪之外。投资者要的是可预期的规则，不是给灰色资金留后门；后者短期像优惠，长期是信用折价。

## 无罪没收不是免罪，门槛与救济都要落地

支持者担心，无罪没收会被当成“先没收、后定罪”的捷径。法律文本其实设置了缓冲：潜逃者须被警方列入通缉名单满60天，检方才能提出申请；审理期间嫌疑人到案，没收程序必须中止；案件以无罪或免予起诉告终，没收申请也要终止。资产追查可以延伸到配偶、子女、亲属、同事和朋友，但原犯罪所得无法追回时，才能申请没收等值的其他资产。

这些设计说明立法者并非没有想过权力边界。可问题在于，条文写得细，不等于执行会照着读。尤其是“影响广泛公众经济的欺诈”这类表述，弹性很大；如果监管机关把网络赌博、虚拟货币骗局和一般商业纠纷都往里装，门槛就会从制度护栏变成可伸缩的橡皮筋。反腐不能靠扩大自由裁量来证明决心，那是用另一个风险换一个风险。

## 总统任命的监督机构，谁来看守看门人

法律要求设立独立的管理与监督机构，采用数字化的资产登记与透明程序。哈比博罗赫曼强调，这个机构由总统任命，举证责任平衡，目的就是让法律难以被滥用。然而，由总统任命的监督机构，能否对行政体系保持真正的距离，恰恰是反腐观察团体最不放心的地方。印尼腐败观察组织直言，缺失的是法定保障的透明度、独立性和资产登记公开；还有团体质疑草案和配套学术文件未充分公开，公众参与流于形式。

国会方面则列举了87场公众听证、15个省和46个选区的走访，认为程序已经足够开放。双方各执一词，反映出同一个症结：程序正义不是用场次来计量的。会议开了多少场，不如草案全文何时公开、意见如何被采纳、反对理由有没有写进立法说明。资产没收制度一旦运转，被没收者面对的是国家机器；监督机构若只是另一架机器的一部分，天平就不会自己回正。

## 华商不是嫌疑人，但资产来源要说得清

对印尼华商和华侨华人社区来说，这部法律的实际影响不亚于任何一项投资优惠。华人商业网络遍布贸易、制造、矿业和金融，跨境持股、家族代持、亲友账户都是常见安排。新法允许追查转移给配偶、子女、亲友和同事的资产，这意味着过去的“分散持有”不再是天然的防火墙。法律不追溯既往犯罪，正式颁布满12个月且相关机构成立后才会实施，企业有一段合规窗口期，但窗口不是豁免期。

必须说清楚：反华商不是这部法律的主题，也不该被解读成针对某个族群。真正的风险是合规能力差的企业容易被卷进程序，尤其是在政商关系密集的行业。华商应当把股权链条、资金来源、税务申报和关联交易整理成可出示的证据，而不是等到资产被冻结才找律师。清清楚楚的账本既是护身符，也是对法治最实在的支持。

## 三问决定成色：透明、追溯、救济

判断这部法律的成色，不必看它通过了多久、条文有多少，只要盯住三个问题。第一，税务犯罪和不明财富条款会不会在实施细则或后续修法中补回来？如果长期不补，追赃就只追得到被抓的那一环，追不到洗白的那条链。第二，监督机构的人选、预算和资产登记数据能否公开？如果答案是否定的，独立监督就只是名义上的装饰。第三，被错误冻结的资产能否快速救济？没有及时、可执行的返还机制，善意第三方的保护条款就只是一纸空文。

十八年磨一剑，最怕磨出来的是一把只砍别人、不砍自己的刀。印尼需要的不是又一项写在纸上的反腐口号，而是一套让贪腐者藏不住钱、让守法者睡得着觉的规则。法槌已经落下，接下来要看它落在哪里。

## 延伸阅读

**无罪没收（Non-conviction-based forfeiture）**：在未取得刑事定罪判决的特定情形下，由法院审查后对涉案资产实施没收的机制，核心争议在于追赃效率与财产权保障的平衡。

**不明财富（Unexplained wealth）**：公职人员或特定主体名下财产明显超出合法收入且无法合理解释来源时，可被追查的法律概念；支持者视其为反腐工具，批评者担忧举证责任倒置。

**善意第三方（Good-faith third party）**：未参与犯罪、以合法交易取得资产的权利人；资产没收制度通常须为其设置异议和返还程序，以免追赃伤及无辜。

# Indonesia's long-awaited forfeiture law: the tax crime hole at the centre

Indonesia's parliament passed the Asset Forfeiture Law on November 19. According to Nikkei Asia, a bill that took 18 years to reach the statute book was meant to become a scalpel for recovering illicit wealth — yet at the last stage it excluded tax crimes from its scope. Anti-corruption watchdogs called the omission a significant weakening. A gavel that finally fell on corruption stopped short of one of the most common routes for hiding money. Was this a technical legislative compromise, or a confidence project taking priority over the will to recover stolen assets?

## Eighteen years to a gavel, and a dual track at last

The idea dates to 2008; a draft reached parliament in 2012 and then stalled for years until the plenary vote on November 19, 2026. House Speaker Puan Maharani stressed that the bill cleared the chamber ahead of the original December 15 target. Habiburokhman, chair of Commission III, described two parallel tracks: conventional conviction-based forfeiture and non-conviction-based forfeiture in specified circumstances.

Non-conviction forfeiture does not let police simply raid a house. It applies when a suspect or defendant has died, fled, become permanently incapacitated, or cannot be identified or located, so that ordinary criminal proceedings cannot move forward. Prosecutors must apply to a district court, and the asset must have been worth at least 1 billion rupiah (about US$55,000) when acquired. Court review, a balanced burden of proof and protection for good-faith third parties are written into the text. bureaucratic reform minister Rini Widyantini said the law fills a gap in a system that had long depended on a conviction before assets could be recovered. The gap was real. Whether it has been closed properly depends on where the next cuts landed.

## Thirteen categories cut to six, and not only tax was dropped

The final statute contains 74 articles. The scope was narrowed from 13 proposed categories to corruption plus six others: narcotics and psychotropic drugs; terrorism and terrorism financing; people smuggling; smuggling of weapons, ammunition and hazardous materials; human trafficking; and fraud or embezzlement using information technology or affecting the wider public economy. Tax, banking, insurance, forestry, mining and environmental crimes were all left outside.

Habiburokhman's rationale was to prevent overreach and curb abuse by law enforcement. That concern is not imaginary: a forfeiture power without limits can harm innocents. Yet removing tax crimes wholesale drives an arbitrary wedge between corruption and the washing of its proceeds. Tax crimes are not petty offences. Fake invoices, transfer pricing and concealed income often convert bribe money into apparently legitimate commercial profit. Chasing the bribe while ignoring the river that cleans it is unlikely to produce impressive recovery rates.

## The unexplained-wealth provision disappears

According to Indonesian business media and watchdog assessments, the November 16 draft contained an unexplained-wealth provision: assets held by public officials that could not be reasonably explained by lawful income, or that were omitted from mandatory wealth declarations, could be investigated. The final text dropped it. Indonesia Corruption Watch argued that the deletion moved away from the direction implied by Article 20 of the UN Convention against Corruption, which Indonesia ratified in 2006. Article 20 is recommendatory rather than mandatory, but it is precisely the benchmark from which domestic tools are designed.

The commission's defence was that unexplained-wealth rules conflict with human rights protections, that the United States and United Kingdom lack equivalent provisions, and that Germany's version was struck down by its constitutional court. That comparison is selective: the UK has unexplained wealth orders, and the US has civil forfeiture. More importantly, why keep the more contentious tool — non-conviction forfeiture — while discarding the more established complementary one? The answer lies less in legal technique than in political will.

## Patriot bonds and a financial hub: confidence outranks recovery

Nikkei Asia connects the watered-down bill with another policy thread: the government's campaign to sell "patriot bonds" and its push to establish Indonesia as an international financial hub. The two stories appear unrelated but share a logic. When a state needs to convince markets that money is safe, aggressive pursuit of economic crime tends to slip down the priority list.

This is not to say officials set out to protect criminal proceeds. It is to say that the ordering is visible: attracting capital, stabilising confidence and deepening markets come before following every money trail to its end. A city that wants to be a regional financial hub needs a clear boundary between a clean reputation and lax enforcement. The boundary has now been drawn outside tax crimes. Investors want predictable rules, not a back door for grey money. What looks like a short-term inducement becomes a long-term discount on credibility.

## Non-conviction is not no-process: thresholds and remedies must hold

Critics fear non-conviction forfeiture will become seize first, convict later. The text does contain buffers. A fugitive must have been on a police wanted list for at least 60 days before an application can be filed. If the suspect is arrested while proceedings are under way, the forfeiture case must stop. If the criminal case ends in acquittal or discharge, the application must also terminate. Assets transferred to spouses, children, relatives, colleagues or friends can be traced, but substitutes may be seized only when the original proceeds cannot be recovered.

These details show that legislators thought about the limits of power. But careful wording is not careful enforcement. The phrase "fraud affecting the wider public economy" is elastic; if regulators fold online gambling, crypto fraud and ordinary commercial disputes into it, the threshold changes from a guardrail into a rubber band. Anti-corruption cannot be proved by expanding discretion. That merely trades one risk for another.

## A presidentially appointed watchdog: who guards the guardians?

The law provides for an independent management and supervisory body, together with digital asset registers and transparent procedures. Habiburokhman says the body is appointed by the president, with a balanced burden of proof, precisely so the law cannot be easily abused. Yet whether a presidentially appointed watchdog can keep genuine distance from the executive is exactly what civil society groups distrust. Indonesia Corruption Watch says what is missing is legally guaranteed independence, transparent criteria and a registry whose openness is protected by statute. Others question whether drafts and supporting documents were sufficiently published, making public participation formal rather than substantive.

Parliament counters with 87 public hearings and visits to 15 provinces and 46 electoral districts. Both sides describe the same problem from different angles: procedural legitimacy is not measured in event counts. What matters is when the full draft was published, how objections were handled and whether reasons for rejection entered the legislative record. Once forfeiture machinery starts running, an owner faces the state. If supervision is just another arm of the state, the scales will not right themselves.

## Overseas Chinese are not suspects, but sources of wealth must be explainable

For Indonesian Chinese businesses and the wider overseas Chinese community, the practical impact of this law is no smaller than any investment incentive. Chinese commercial networks span trade, manufacturing, mining and finance, where cross-border shareholding, family nominees and relatives' accounts are common. Because the law allows tracing assets transferred to spouses, children, relatives, colleagues and friends, dispersed ownership is no longer an automatic firewall. The law is non-retroactive and takes effect only 12 months after promulgation and once the institutions are in place. Companies have a compliance window, not an exemption.

This is not an anti-Chinese measure and should not be read as one. The real risk is that firms with weak compliance become easy targets, especially in sectors dense with political-business ties. Overseas Chinese entrepreneurs should put shareholding chains, funding sources, tax filings and related-party transactions into documentary order before assets are frozen. Transparent books are both a defence and the most practical support for the rule of law.

## Three questions will decide its quality

The law's quality will not be determined by how long it took or how many articles it contains. Watch three questions. First, will tax crimes and unexplained wealth return through implementing rules or later amendments? If not, the state can recover only the visible bribe, not the chain that cleans it. Second, can the supervisor's appointments, budget and asset registry be made public? If not, independent oversight is decorative. Third, can wrongly frozen assets be returned quickly? Without prompt, enforceable remedies, protection for good-faith third parties remains words on paper.

Eighteen years to sharpen a blade, only to risk producing a knife that cuts others but not the powerful. Indonesia needs not another anti-corruption slogan but a system in which the corrupt cannot hide money and the law-abiding can sleep. The gavel has fallen. Where it lands next is the test.

## Further reading

**Non-conviction-based forfeiture**: A mechanism allowing a court to forfeit assets linked to crime without a prior criminal conviction in specified cases. Its central tension is between recovery efficiency and protection of property rights.

**Unexplained wealth**: A legal concept applied when assets held by public officials or other covered persons greatly exceed known lawful income and cannot be reasonably explained. Supporters treat it as an anti-corruption tool; critics worry about a reversed burden of proof.

**Good-faith third party**: An uninvolved person who acquired assets through a lawful transaction. Forfeiture regimes are expected to provide objection and restitution procedures so that recovery does not punish the innocent.

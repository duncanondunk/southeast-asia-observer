# 美菲“海上合作”升温，南海博弈再添变量

2026年，美菲同盟在“海上合作”的名义下明显升温：联合巡航频次增加、基地接入扩大、海岸警卫队协作深化。对马尼拉而言，这是对冲南海风险的杠杆；对华盛顿而言，这是“印太战略”的前沿支点。但对整个南海而言，升温的合作正在把博弈推入更敏感的区间。

## 合作升温：从演习到常态化存在

区别于以往偶发的联合军演，今年的美菲合作更强调“常态化”。其一是扩大《加强防务合作协定》（EDCA）基地的接入与使用，使美军在吕宋岛北部的存在更接近关键水道。其二是海岸警卫队层面的联合巡逻与能力建设，把“准军事”维度纳入协作。其三是情报与态势共享的机制化，提升危机响应的速度。

## 马尼拉的算盘：用同盟换空间

小马科斯政府把美菲同盟当作南海政策的底盘。其逻辑是：越深的美国承诺，越能抬高争端中的谈判筹码与威慑可信度。对菲律宾而言，海上合作既是安全投资，也是对内政治资产——展现“不示弱”的姿态。但风险在于，过度绑定可能压缩其与北京周旋的外交弹性。

## 华盛顿的逻辑：前沿与同盟网络化

对美国而言，菲律宾是“第一岛链”的关键齿牙。通过海上合作把菲律宾锚定为前沿节点，既服务“印太战略”的对华竞争，也把同盟关系从双边扩展为含日本、澳大利亚的“网络化”部署。这种网络化，使单次合作具备更强的信号效应与威慑叠加。

## 区域回响：紧张与对冲并存

合作升温引发连锁反应。北京视之为域外势力激化争议的佐证，反制与外交施压并行。东盟内部则心态分裂：多数成员不愿被卷入大国对抗，呼吁“东盟中心性”与行为准则磋商。与此同时，部分国家也在加大对美安全协作，形成“选边螺旋”的隐忧。

## 值得观察的几点

其一，EDCA 基地的实际使用强度，是否越过北京所划的“红线”。其二，菲律宾是否保留与北京的双边沟通渠道，避免危机失控。其三，东盟行为准则（COC）磋商能否在紧张中保持节奏，防止争议机制化。

归根结底，美菲海上合作的升温，给南海增添了确定性，也增添了不确定性。它给马尼拉以底气，给华盛顿以前沿，却也给整个区域出了一道更难的题：当合作常态化，如何避免把博弈推向不可逆的轨道。

## 延伸阅读

**EDCA（Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement）**：美菲《加强防务合作协定》，允许美军在菲律宾指定基地预置装备与轮驻，2014年签署、近年扩大。

**第一岛链（First Island Chain）**：从日本列岛经台湾、菲律宾至南海的岛弧链，被视为美国西太平洋防务的前沿屏障。

**东盟中心性（ASEAN Centrality）**：东盟在区域安全架构中居于核心协调地位的原则，旨在避免大国主导、维护东盟话语权。

# US–Philippines 'Maritime Cooperation' Heats Up, Adding a Variable to the South China Sea

In 2026, the US–Philippines alliance is visibly warming under the banner of 'maritime cooperation': more joint patrols, expanded base access, deeper coast-guard coordination. For Manila, it is leverage to hedge South China Sea risk; for Washington, a forward pivot of its Indo-Pacific strategy. But for the whole sea, the heating cooperation is pushing the game into a more sensitive zone.

## Cooperation heats up: from exercises to a permanent presence

Unlike past occasional joint drills, this year's US–Philippines cooperation stresses 'normalisation'. First, expanded access and use of bases under the Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement (EDCA), bringing US presence in northern Luzon closer to key waterways. Second, coast-guard-level joint patrols and capacity-building, pulling the 'paramilitary' dimension into collaboration. Third, institutionalised intelligence and situational-sharing, speeding crisis response.

## Manila's calculus: trading alliance for space

The Marcos Jr. government treats the US alliance as the bedrock of its South China Sea policy. The logic: the deeper the US commitment, the higher the negotiating chip and the credibility of deterrence in disputes. For the Philippines, maritime cooperation is both a security investment and a domestic political asset — signalling 'no backing down'. The risk: over-binding may squeeze the diplomatic flexibility to manoeuvre with Beijing.

## Washington's logic: forward posture and alliance networking

For the US, the Philippines is a key tooth of the 'first island chain'. Anchoring it as a forward node via maritime cooperation serves both the Indo-Pacific strategy's China competition and expands the bilateral alliance into a 'networked' layout with Japan and Australia. This networking gives single cooperation stronger signalling and stacked deterrence.

## Regional reverberations: tension and hedging coexist

The heating cooperation triggers chain reactions. Beijing reads it as proof of extraterritorial powers inflaming disputes, pairing counter-measures with diplomatic pressure. Within ASEAN, minds are split: most members refuse to be dragged into great-power rivalry, calling for 'ASEAN centrality' and code-of-conduct talks. Meanwhile some countries also deepen US security ties, raising fears of a 'bandwagoning spiral'.

## A few things to watch

First, the actual intensity of EDCA base use — whether it crosses the 'red line' Beijing draws. Second, whether the Philippines keeps bilateral channels with Beijing to prevent crisis runaway. Third, whether COC talks keep pace amid tension, preventing the dispute's institutionalisation.

In the end, the warming US–Philippines maritime cooperation adds both certainty and uncertainty to the South China Sea. It gives Manila backbone and Washington a forward post, yet poses the region a harder question: as cooperation normalises, how to avoid pushing the game onto an irreversible track.

## Further reading

**EDCA (Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement)**: A US–Philippines pact allowing US forces to preposition equipment and rotate through designated Philippine bases; signed in 2014 and expanded in recent years.

**First Island Chain**: The arc from the Japanese islands through Taiwan and the Philippines to the South China Sea, seen as the forward barrier of US western-Pacific defence.

**ASEAN Centrality**: The principle that ASEAN sits at the core of regional security architecture, aimed at avoiding great-power dominance and preserving ASEAN's voice.

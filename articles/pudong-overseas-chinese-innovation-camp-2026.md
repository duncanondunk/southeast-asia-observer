# 张江开营：浦东把「华侨华人」请进科创主舞台

8月26日，由上海市浦东新区侨办主办的「2026华侨华人创新创业研修营」在张江开营，为期3天。从海内外遴选产生的42名华侨华人科创精英，在张江接受一场紧贴产业一线的「沉浸式辅导」。主办方用三个词概括学员的画像——「高学历、国际化、硬科技」。这并不是一场普通的联谊活动。在上海打造具有全球影响力的科技创新中心、各地竞相加码「人才第一资源」的当口，浦东把最被高看一眼的「华侨华人」群体，径直引向了产业竞争最激烈的张江主战场。

## 从「迎进来」到「赋能落地」：侨务逻辑的范式切换

把时间轴拉开看，过去十余年中国地方侨务工作的重心，是接待联谊、牵线搭桥、维系情感——它本质上是一种「人脉维护型」工作。浦东此次研修营的课程体系——政策引航、创业课堂、技术赋能、参观考察、资本对接——却把侨务逻辑直接推向「成果转化」一端：学员需要在三天之内走完从政策到资本、从课堂到园区的全流程。这并非简单的提级，而是一种范式切换：侨的资产，被重新理解为可被科创资本市场定价的人力资本。问题在于，这种切换究竟是阶段性活动，还是会被固化为可复制的工作机制？尤其当苏州工业园、深圳河套、北京海淀都在用类似口径争夺同一批「高质量回流人才」时，浦东必须把单次活动升级为可比较、可追踪、可考核的产品——而不再是「办了就是成绩」。至少在浦东层面，答案正在由这一营42人的去向给出。

## 学员画像：「高学历、国际化、硬科技」三重身份

研修营的42名学员，绝大多数有英国、美国、新加坡、澳大利亚等多国学习或工作经历；硕博士占比80%，平均年龄约35岁。换句话说，这是一群刚刚完成海外学术训练、进入职业黄金期，又主动把目光投向中国市场的「跨境人才」。他们中26%来自人工智能领域，21%来自生物医药与大健康领域，剩下半数分布于电子信息、新能源、新材料等硬科技赛道——基本与浦东「智能造」「创新药」「未来车」「蓝天梦」等主导产业同频。「高学历、国际化、硬科技」不是宣传话术，而是一组可以被快速对应到产业地图上的结构变量。更值得注意的是，这批学员大多数具备双语甚至多语工作能力，理解两套监管口径与两套融资逻辑——这是单纯国内培养的同类创业者所不具备的「跨境中介优势」。从结构上看，这批人也已经显著有别于以中餐馆业、商业零售为主的传统侨团群体，他们是新侨画像的典型代表。这一点，比任何仪式感都更具信号意义。

## 三大赛道：AI、生物医药与硬科技的中国机会

在26%的AI学员中，具身智能机器人（Embodied Intelligence Robotics）、AI教育硬件是两个被反复提及的细分方向——前者在特斯拉擎天柱（Tesla Optimus）、宇树科技等头部产品的带动下进入产业大众视野，后者则把大模型的能力转化为可规模化的消费品。21%的生物医药与大健康学员中，多癌早筛试剂盒、合成生物抗衰等创新项目特别值得注意：它们都属于「长周期、高投入、合规密集」赛道，最需要制度性支持。把这样两类项目的创业者同时放进一个营，本身就透露了浦东选人的偏好——它更愿意为「重资产、合规密集、未来可贡献产业链关键节点」的项目背书，而不只是「容易出新闻」的项目。换句话说，浦东不是在挑「明星创业者」，而是在挑「未来五年能嵌入张江产业链关键节点的创业者」——这是一种偏供给侧、偏供应链的挑选逻辑。

## 张江作为「侨务—科创」界面：空间、链条与制度

研修营的「参观考察」一节，之所以被安排在张江，而不是陆家嘴或前滩，并非偶然。张江科学城是全国集成电路、生物医药、人工智能三大先导产业最密集的空间之一，集聚了全国约1/3的AI芯片设计企业、1/5的创新药管线。把研修营嵌入张江，等于把侨务工作的物理界面直接接入了中国硬科技最完整的产业链条：学员在这里既能看到真实可触的上下游企业，也能更高效地完成与本地研发机构、临床医院、政府部门的「多边对齐」。从这个意义上看，浦东正在把张江从一个「地理名词」，升级为一个「侨务—科创」的制度性接口。换言之，张江对研修营的意义，已经从「会址」升级为「接口」——它的角色不再是「借个场地」，而是「承担起把侨务流量转化为科创变量的全部制度成本」。

## 「短期集训 + 长期结对」能否转化为落地项目？

研修营最具操作性的安排，是同步举行的创业导师、人才服务专员与学员结对仪式，提供长期跟进的配套支持。换句话说，三天集训不是终点，而是更长产业链的入口。短期集训的优势在于密度——42人、三天、五大模块——能把原本分散的产业资源、政策资源、资本资源一次性「打包给」学员；长期结对的优势则在于连续性——把一次性的活动转化为可问责的服务关系。真正的考验在于：结对之后，学员的项目是否真的在上海注册？是否拿到了第一笔本地融资？是否进入了张江的孵化器或加速器？是否对接到了本地临床医院或代工厂？这些都是「事后六个月」才能被验证的指标。换言之，浦东把侨务工作「产品化」的诚意，最终要由「事后六个月的转化率」来检验，而不是由「现场的热烈程度」来背书。如果首批42人中有三成以上能在六个月内完成本地注册或拿到本地首轮融资，那么这个模型就有可能成为可被各地侨务部门「采购」的标准产品。

## 学员视角：王磊的「产业一线」逻辑与上海算盘

学员代表、宁波三石工业技术有限公司执行董事王磊的发言很具代表性。他曾在美国、澳大利亚深耕能源、供应链领域，此行重点关注电子化学品、生物医药赛道，并明确表示看好具身智能前沿应用在上海的发展。他的原话是：「本次研修营跳出传统课堂授课模式，深入产业园区、科创企业一线，可以近距离考察产业现状、对接本土资源，对个人创业和项目落地帮助极大。」王磊的路径——海外多年产业积累、回国寻找落地场景、以上海为应用市场——基本代表了「成熟型新侨」的标准路线。他们不是从零开始的留学生，而是带着行业人脉和资本认知回流的「产业老兵」。这意味着，浦东研修营真正的目标客户，并不是海外青年学者，而是这类「有产业资源、缺落地通道」的成熟回流者——这一定位相当精准。

## 数字之外：规模有限，但结构具有样板意义

42人、3天、26%、21%、80%——这些数字本身并不算大，但它们背后的结构，却具有样板意义。浦东用一种「小而精」的研修营，把侨务工作从「面上铺开」收拢为「点上突破」：以高学历过滤掉非目标群体，以国际化筛选掉国内可替代的人才，以硬科技限定在能嵌入张江产业链的方向上。当传统的联谊接待式侨务，已经难以在区域产业竞争中证明自己的不可替代性时，这种「精准化、产品化、长期化」的转型，或许正是各地侨务部门都要回答的同一道题。更进一步说，浦东的真正赌注，是把「侨」从一个文化身份，重新编码为一种可被考核、可被比较、可被资本跟进的产业接口——这或许比任何一个「项目落地」都更具长远的样板意义。

## 延伸阅读

- **新侨（Xinqiao / New-Generation Overseas Chinese）**：指改革开放后出国的留学人员、研发人员及在海外完成职业积累的高技术移民，与传统华侨华人社团意义上的「老侨」相对；近年来是中国地方侨务工作的主要服务对象。
- **创业导师结对（Mentor Pairing for Entrepreneurs）**：研修营在三天集训之外，配套安排的「一位创业导师 + 一位人才服务专员 + 一位学员」长期对接机制，目标是让一次性活动转化为可问责的持续服务。
- **张江科学城（Zhangjiang Science City）**：位于上海浦东的核心科创集聚区，全国集成电路、生物医药、人工智能三大先导产业最密集的空间之一，本次研修营被嵌入其中，意味着侨务工作正式与张江的产业链条对接。

# Zhangjiang Opens Its Doors: Pudong Places Overseas Chinese Center-Stage of Its Innovation Play

On 26 August, the 2026 Overseas Chinese Innovation and Entrepreneurship Training Camp, organised by the Overseas Chinese Affairs Office of Shanghai's Pudong New Area, opened in Zhangjiang for a three-day run. Forty-two overseas Chinese science and innovation talents, selected from candidates at home and abroad, are taking part in an immersive, industry-oriented programme on the ground. The organisers describe the cohort in three words: "highly educated, international, hard-tech." This is no ordinary networking gathering. At a moment when Shanghai is consolidating its bid to become a globally influential science and innovation hub, and cities across China are doubling down on "talent as the first resource," Pudong has channelled one of its most-coveted demographic assets — overseas Chinese — directly onto the most contested industrial stage in the country: Zhangjiang.

## From "Welcome Them In" to "Make Them Land": A Shift in the Logic of Overseas Chinese Affairs

Step back and the longer arc is clear. For more than a decade, local governments' work on overseas Chinese affairs centred on hospitality, introductions, and the upkeep of personal ties — a fundamentally "relationship-maintenance" function. The Pudong camp's curriculum — policy orientation, an entrepreneurship classroom, technology enablement, on-site visits, and capital matchmaking — pushes that logic explicitly toward "commercial landing": participants are expected to walk, in three days, the full path from policy to capital, from classroom to industrial park. This is not a routine upgrade but a paradigm shift: overseas Chinese capital is being reframed as human capital that can be priced by the science-and-innovation market. The open question is whether this shift is a one-off exercise or a replicable operating model. Especially when the Suzhou Industrial Park, Shenzhen's Hetao, and Beijing's Haidian are all using a similar pitch to compete for the same pool of "high-quality returnees," Pudong has to convert a single event into a product that is comparable, trackable, and auditable — not an event whose "success" is measured by whether it was held at all. In Pudong, at least, the answer will be written in where the forty-two end up.

## Cohort Profile: "Highly Educated, International, Hard-Tech"

Almost every one of the 42 participants has studied or worked in multiple countries — the United Kingdom, the United States, Singapore, Australia among them; 80% hold master's or doctoral degrees, and the average age is around 35. In other words, this is a group that has just completed overseas academic training, is entering its professional prime, and has actively turned its gaze back to the Chinese market. Twenty-six percent work in artificial intelligence, 21% in biomedicine and broader healthcare, with the remaining half spread across electronic information, new energy, and advanced materials — tracks that closely mirror Pudong's flagship industries of "AI Made in Shanghai," "Innovative Drugs," "Future Vehicles," and "Blue Sky Dreams." "Highly educated, international, hard-tech" is not promotional language but a set of structural variables that can be plotted directly onto an industrial map. More importantly, most members of this cohort are bilingual or multilingual, fluent in two regulatory regimes and two capital-markets logics — a "cross-border intermediation advantage" that domestic-only peers do not share. That, more than ceremony, is what makes the cohort a signal.

## Three Tracks: AI, Biomedicine, and the China Opportunity in Hard-Tech

Among the 26% working in AI, two sub-tracks are repeatedly named: embodied intelligence robotics and AI-enabled educational hardware. The first has been pushed into mainstream industrial view by flagship products such as Tesla's Optimus ("Qing Tian Zhu") and Unitree, while the second translates large-model capabilities into mass-market consumer goods. Within the 21% in biomedicine and broader health, the standout projects are multi-cancer early-screening kits and synthetic-biology anti-ageing — both "long-cycle, capital-intensive, compliance-heavy" tracks, and precisely the ones most in need of institutional support. Putting these two classes of founder into the same camp betrays Pudong's preference: it wants to back projects that are "asset-heavy, compliance-intensive, and able to occupy key nodes of an industrial chain" — not merely projects that generate headlines. In other words, Pudong is not picking "celebrity founders"; it is picking "founders who, five years out, can plug into the key nodes of Zhangjiang's supply chain." That is a supply-side, chain-oriented selection logic, not a consumer-marketing one.

## Zhangjiang as the Overseas-Chinese / Innovation Interface: Space, Chains, and Institutions

The "on-site visits" module was placed in Zhangjiang rather than in Lujiazui or Qiantan, and not by accident. Zhangjiang Science City is one of China's most concentrated hubs for the three leading industries of integrated circuits, biomedicine, and artificial intelligence, hosting roughly one-third of the country's AI chip design firms and one-fifth of its innovative-drug pipelines. Embedding the training camp in Zhangjiang effectively welds the physical interface of overseas-Chinese work onto China's most complete hard-tech supply chain: participants can see real upstream and downstream firms on the ground, and align in a single sweep with local R&D institutes, clinical hospitals, and government departments. Read this way, Pudong is upgrading Zhangjiang from a place-name into an institutional interface between diaspora policy and frontier industry. Put differently, Zhangjiang's role for the camp has shifted from "venue" to "interface" — it is no longer "lending a hall," but "absorbing the full institutional cost of converting diaspora flows into science-and-innovation variables."

## Can "Short-Form Training + Long-Form Pairing" Actually Translate into Landed Projects?

The camp's most operational feature is the synchronised mentor-and-staff pairing ceremony, in which each participant is matched with an entrepreneurship mentor and a dedicated talent-services officer, providing a long-tail support framework. In other words, the three-day programme is not the endpoint but the entry point of a longer chain. The short-form bootcamp offers density — 42 people, three days, five modules — packaging policy, industrial, and capital resources into a single window for participants; the long-form pairing offers continuity, converting a one-off event into an accountable service relationship. The real test is whether, after pairing, participants actually register companies in Shanghai, secure their first local round of funding, enter a Zhangjiang incubator or accelerator, and connect with a local clinical hospital or contract manufacturer. These are metrics that can only be verified "six months out." In other words, Pudong's sincerity in "productising" its overseas-Chinese work will ultimately be audited by the six-month conversion rate, not by the warmth of the on-site atmosphere.

## The Participant's View: Wang Lei's "First-Line Industry" Logic and the Shanghai Calculus

The remarks of participant representative Wang Lei, executive director of Ningbo Sanshi Industrial Technology, are representative. Having previously worked in the energy and supply-chain sectors in the United States and Australia, he travelled to the camp focused on electronic chemicals and biomedicine, and made clear that he sees strong prospects for embodied-intelligence applications in Shanghai. In his words: "This training camp stepped beyond the traditional classroom model, going deep into industrial parks and frontline science-and-innovation firms. It let me observe the industry's current state at close range and connect with local resources, which is hugely helpful for both my own entrepreneurship and project landing." Wang's trajectory — years of overseas industrial experience, returning to look for application scenarios, and choosing Shanghai as the end market — is the standard path of the "mature new-generation overseas Chinese." These are not students starting from zero but "industry veterans" returning with sectoral networks and capital literacy. This means the camp's real target customer is not the young overseas scholar but the mature returnee who has industry resources but lacks a domestic landing channel — a remarkably precise positioning.

## Beyond the Numbers: A Small Cohort, but a Template-Worthy Structure

Forty-two people, three days, 26%, 21%, 80% — the numbers are not large in themselves, but the structure behind them is template-worthy. Pudong is using a "small but precise" camp to re-shape overseas-Chinese work from a "wide-net" model into a "point-of-breakthrough" one: graduate-level education filters out non-target groups, international experience filters out domestically substitutable talent, and hard-tech confines the cohort to directions that can plug into Zhangjiang's supply chain. At a moment when traditional hospitality-style overseas-Chinese work can no longer easily prove its indispensability in regional industrial competition, this kind of "precise, productised, long-term" transformation is, in effect, the same question every local diaspora-affairs office is now being asked to answer. More than that, Pudong's real wager is to re-encode "diaspora Chinese" from a cultural identity into an industrial interface that can be audited, compared, and followed by capital — a wager whose template value may well outlast any single project landing.

## Further reading

- **Xinqiao (New-Generation Overseas Chinese)**: refers to students, researchers, and high-skill migrants who went abroad after reform and opening-up, and to overseas Chinese who have completed their professional accumulation abroad; distinguished from the "old diaspora" tied to traditional hometown associations, and now the principal client group of local diaspora work in China.
- **Mentor Pairing for Entrepreneurs**: the camp's companion scheme, in which each participant is matched with one entrepreneurship mentor and one talent-services officer, intended to convert a one-off event into an accountable, long-running service relationship.
- **Zhangjiang Science City**: a core innovation cluster in Pudong, Shanghai, and one of the densest concentrations in China of integrated circuits, biomedicine, and AI; the camp is deliberately embedded within it, signalling that diaspora work has been formally tied to Zhangjiang's industrial chain.

# 马来西亚放眼2030年量产本土晶片：从封装测试迈向设计与制造

马来西亚政府把"本土造芯"写进了时间表。经济部长阿克马纳斯鲁拉8月21日表示，借助与英国晶片设计巨头安谋控股（Arm Holdings）的技术授权，马国力争最迟在2030年量产"本土设计与制造"的晶片，用于国内半导体供应链。从长期扮演全球封测角色，到尝试掌握设计与制造的两端，马来西亚的半导体野心，正踩在全球供应链重构与地缘博弈的双重节拍上。

## 从"封测王国"到"晶片设计国"

马来西亚是全球第六大半导体出口国，占全球半导体组装、测试和封装市场约13%，并贡献全球7%的半导体产量，美国约23%的晶片在马国生产。但这一优势长期集中在产业链中下游的封测环节，设计与制造的高附加值部分仍掌握在美欧与东亚手中。阿克马纳斯鲁拉口中的目标，正是让马来西亚从"测试和组装"升级为"更高档次和价值的半导体设计与生产国"——这一定位转变，才是2030计划真正的野心所在。

## 安谋授权：三年设计、七年量产的时间表

按既定路径，获得安谋技术授权的马国企业约需三年开发设计晶片，再进入制造阶段。由于首项授权于去年底发出，晶片设计预计2028年完成，随后投产目标定在2030年。目前已有四家马国企业处于晶片设计与知识产权评估阶段。政府希望先在2028年完成设计，再给企业时间打磨产品竞争力、争取客户认可，因此把量产节点审慎地放在约两年之后。

## 十年2.5亿美元：买来的技术主权

为换取这一系列能力，马国将在未来十年向安谋支付2亿5000万美元（约18亿元人民币），获得半导体相关设备与技术的知识产权，并提供给本国企业使用；费用涵盖七个高端晶片设计蓝图，以及培训约1万名工程师。马国还计划在未来五至十年自行生产图形处理器（GPU）晶片，搭建本土晶片设计与制造的生态系统。可以说，这是一笔以真金白银换"技术主权"的长期下注。

## 地缘政治下的筹码：马来西亚的半导体野心

马国把这项投资与出口目标绑定：力争2030年实现1.2万亿令吉（约1.9万亿元人民币）的半导体出口。在美中科技脱钩、产能分散化的背景下，马来西亚凭借既有封测基础、英语人才与中立地缘位置，正成为供应链"中国+1"与"友岸外包"的受惠者。但若要从授权走向自主，仍须跨过生态薄弱、人才缺口与"客户黏性"等门槛。2030能否如期点亮第一颗"马来西亚设计、马来西亚制造"的晶片，将检验这场野心成色。

## 延伸阅读

- **半导体（Semiconductor）**：现代电子产业的核心；马国长期主导封测环节，正试图向设计与制造两端上移。
- **友岸外包（Friend-shoring）**：美中脱钩下产能分散化趋势，马来西亚凭中立位置与封测基础成为受惠节点。

# Malaysia Aims to Produce Homegrown Chips by 2030: From Assembly-Test toward Design and Fabrication

The Malaysian government has put "homegrown chips" on a timetable. On 21 August, Economy Minister Akmal Nasrullah said that, leveraging technology licensing from British chip-design giant Arm Holdings, Malaysia aims to mass-produce "domestically designed and manufactured" chips for its domestic semiconductor supply chain by 2030 at the latest. Moving from its long-standing global role in assembly and testing toward mastering both design and fabrication, Malaysia's chip ambition rides the dual beat of supply-chain reconfiguration and geopolitical contest.

## From "Packaging Kingdom" to "Chip-Design Nation"

Malaysia is the world's sixth-largest semiconductor exporter, holding about 13% of the global assembly, test and packaging market and contributing 7% of global semiconductor output; roughly 23% of U.S. chips are produced there. Yet this strength has long sat in the mid-to-lower stream of packaging and testing, while the high-value design and fabrication remain with the U.S., Europe and East Asia. The minister's stated goal is to upgrade Malaysia from "testing and assembly" to a "higher-tier, higher-value semiconductor design and production nation" — that repositioning, not the headline year, is the real ambition of the 2030 plan.

## The Arm License: A Timeline of Three Years to Design, Seven to Volume

Under the planned path, Malaysian firms with Arm licenses need about three years to develop and design chips before entering fabrication. Since the first license was issued at the end of last year, design is expected to complete in 2028, with volume production targeted for 2030. Four Malaysian firms are already in the chip-design and IP-evaluation stage. The government hopes to finish design in 2028, then give firms time to sharpen competitiveness and win client approval, hence the cautious two-year buffer before mass production.

## US$250 Million Over a Decade: Bought Technological Sovereignty

To acquire this capability, Malaysia will pay Arm US$250 million over ten years for IP in semiconductor equipment and technology, made available to domestic firms; the fee covers seven high-end chip-design blueprints and training for roughly 10,000 engineers. The country also plans to produce its own graphics-processing-unit (GPU) chips within five to ten years, building a local ecosystem of chip design and fabrication. In effect, this is a long-term bet of real money for "technological sovereignty."

## Chips as Geopolitical Leverage: Malaysia's Semiconductor Ambition

Malaysia ties the investment to an export goal: reaching 1.2 trillion ringgit (about US$264 billion) in semiconductor exports by 2030. Amid U.S.–China tech decoupling and capacity dispersion, Malaysia — with its existing packaging base, English-speaking talent and neutral geography — is a beneficiary of "China+1" and friend-shoring. But moving from licensing to autonomy still requires crossing thin ecosystems, talent gaps and client lock-in. Whether 2030 lights up the first "Malaysia-designed, Malaysia-made" chip will test the mettle of this ambition.

## Further reading

- **Arm Holdings**: a British chip-design giant known for its instruction-set licensing model; Malaysia uses its license to enter high-end design, bypassing the long haul of building its own architecture.
- **Semiconductor**: the core of modern electronics; Malaysia has long dominated packaging and is now pushing upstream into design and fabrication.
- **Friend-shoring**: the capacity-dispersion trend under U.S.–China decoupling; Malaysia's neutral position and packaging base make it a beneficiary node.

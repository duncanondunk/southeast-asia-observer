# 赫格塞思的"美—东盟防务框架"：在一个偏爱灵活性的地区，这是一场难卖的推销

2026年10月28日，美国国防部长赫格塞思在菲律宾宿务抛出一项新的"美—东盟防务框架"，声称要为未来"至关重要的几年"指引合作方向。这一提议出现在美—东盟防长非正式会议的开幕环节，而2027年恰逢美国与东盟建立正式关系五十周年。话说得漂亮，但据赫格塞思自己承认，框架具体要做什么，他给的细节少得可怜。

据《南华早报》报道，这一提议立刻引来质疑：分析人士预计，东盟成员国更可能偏爱那些能保留自身在中美之间腾挪空间的双边安排，而不是整队签下一份美国主导的集团框架。这种反应算不上敌意，它只是这个地区早已定型的本能。

## 模糊的"框架"：壳子多于内容

赫格塞思几乎没有说明这个框架究竟要落实什么。他大谈要建立在地区"务实、开放对话、尊重对话方、自豪的民族主义"之上——这套表述宽泛到几乎能为任何政策背书。没有具体机制、没有资金、没有义务，缺位本身就已经说明问题。一个不指名机制、不列明出资、不定下责任的框架，与其说是战略，不如说是战略的占位符。

华盛顿真正想要的是什么，其实很清楚：制度化的存在感，以及最重要的是"责任分担"——一个体面说法，让前线国家自己掏钱搞威慑，而美国只提供架构。框架是照片，账单才是潜台词。

## 东盟为何更爱双边？

东盟最拿手的本事就是对冲。成员国几十年来拒绝成为任何大国的代理集团，而东盟的协商一致规则，恰恰就是为了不让域外强权替这个地区写好安全剧本。一个强调"海上态势感知"和"海军互操作"、却对已经在进行的中國—东盟"南海行为准则"磋商只字不提的美—东盟框架，在许多地区国家眼里，更像一条绕开包容性程序的平行轨道。

双边安排给各国首都的，恰恰是一个集团框架给不了的：否认的空间与灵活的余地。一个国家可以深化与华盛顿的情报共享或联合演习，却不必绑定自己的邻国，也不必显得在它从未主动挑起的较量中选边。对既与中国做大生意、又同美国做安全伙伴的国家而言，这种模糊不是缺陷，它本身就是全部要点。

## 小圈子先行：Squad 与"责任分担"

含糊框架背后那台真正的机器，其实已经开动。在宿务会议的边线，赫格塞思分别与日本、菲律宾防长单独会谈，讨论"第一岛链（First Island Chain）"的有效拒止防御、弹药生产协调，以及美日澳菲这个被称为 Squad 的小圈子安排。这些小多边链接无需东盟签字，三四个首都点头就能运转。

这正是华盛顿最熟稔的套路：对着镜头宣布一个宏大的多边框架，再把硬货——基地、演习、情报共享——填进绕开协商一致要求的小圈子。在这样的设计里，东盟扮演的是背景板，不是执笔人。而已经深陷多年海上对峙的菲律宾，正面临被改写命运的风险：它本是有自身外交选项的主权声索方，却可能被改造成一场它无法掌控的较量里的前哨节点。

## 北京的冷处理与东盟的算盘

北京的回应，克制本身就是信号。中国只派了低级别官员赴宿务，国防部长没有到场。这不是疏忽，而是经过算计的冷遇——它表明，在缺少切实交付物的情况下，这套框架不值得中国郑重入座。与此同时，中国继续推动与东盟尽早达成"南海行为准则"，别人给姿态，它给文本。

东南亚各国政府也在掂量华盛顿自己的掣肘。美国与伊朗之间的战争已牵扯其军事资源、搅动能源市场，而高度依赖进口燃料的东南亚国家直接承受着冲击波。地区观察人士公开质疑：一个被中东牵制的五角大楼，还能否持续聚焦亚洲？美国越是要求别人分担责任，越暴露出自身之薄。

## 照片好看，架构难成

赫格塞思的框架并非一文不值，但它的价值主要停留在象征层面：为临近的五十周年立个牌坊，重申一下意图，给盟友一句可引用的话。它不会凭空产出这个地区真正缺的东西——一个把中国纳入其中而非绕开它、由东盟自己书写而非仅仅栖身其间的秩序。

更难听却更实在的真相是：美国卖的是壳，地区买的是期权。东盟会拍下这张照片、收下这份象征，然后把自己的双边对冲原样保留。对于一个花了五十年拒绝当任何人棋子的集团来说，这不叫忘恩，而是它唯一能做的理性回应——也是华盛顿这套"框架"从来就没打算改变的回应。

# Hegseth's US–ASEAN Defence Framework: A Hard Sell in a Region That Prefers Flexibility

On 28 October 2026, in Cebu, US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth proposed a new US–ASEAN defence framework meant to guide cooperation through what he called the "critically important years" ahead. The pitch came at the opening of the US–ASEAN Defence Ministers' Informal Meeting, with 2027 marking the fiftieth anniversary of formal US–ASEAN relations. It sounded grand. It was also, by Hegseth's own admission, short on specifics.

The South China Morning Post reported that the proposal drew immediate scepticism: analysts expect ASEAN members to favour bilateral arrangements that preserve their freedom to manoeuvre between Washington and Beijing, rather than signing up to a bloc-wide American framework. That reaction is not hostility. It is the region's settled instinct.

## A vague framework: shell over substance

Hegseth offered few details on what the framework would actually entail. He spoke of building on the region's "pragmatism, openness to dialogue, respect for counterparts, and proud nationalism" — a formulation so broad it could endorse almost any policy. The absence of concrete commitments is itself the tell. A framework that names no mechanism, no funding, and no obligation is less a strategy than a placeholder for one.

What Washington clearly wants is institutionalised presence and, above all, "burden-sharing" — the polite term for getting frontline states to fund their own deterrence while the United States supplies the architecture. The framework is the photograph; the bill is the subtext.

## Why ASEAN prefers bilateral pacts

ASEAN's defining habit is hedging. Its members have spent decades refusing to become anyone's proxy bloc, and the association's consensus rule exists precisely to stop great powers from writing the region's security script. A US–ASEAN framework that emphasises maritime domain awareness and naval interoperability, while saying nothing about the China–ASEAN Code of Conduct talks already underway, reads to many in the region as a parallel track designed to bypass inclusive processes.

Bilateral pacts give capitals exactly what a bloc framework cannot: deniability and flexibility. A country can deepen intelligence sharing or joint exercises with Washington without committing its neighbours, and without appearing to choose sides in a contest it did not start. For states that trade heavily with China and security-partner with the US, that ambiguity is not a bug. It is the whole point.

## Minilateral first: the Squad and 'burden-sharing'

The real machine behind the vague framework is already running. On the sidelines of the Cebu meeting, Hegseth sat down separately with his Japanese and Philippine counterparts to discuss "effective denial defence in the First Island Chain," munitions production coordination, and the US–Japan–Australia–Philippines arrangement known as the Squad. These minilateral links need no ASEAN signature; three or four capitals can activate them at will.

This is the pattern Washington knows works: announce a grand multilateral frame for the cameras, then fill it with the hard content — bases, exercises, intelligence sharing — through small circles that exclude the consensus requirement. ASEAN's role in such a design is backdrop, not authorship. The Philippines, already locked in years of maritime confrontation, risks being converted from a sovereign claimant with its own diplomatic options into a forward node in a competition it cannot control.

## Beijing's cool response and ASEAN's calculus

China's answer was telling in its restraint. Beijing sent only low-level officials to Cebu; its defence minister stayed away. That was not an oversight. It was a calculated cold shoulder — a signal that the framework, absent real deliverables, did not deserve a seat at the table. Meanwhile, China continues to push for an early conclusion of the Code of Conduct with ASEAN, offering text where Washington offers posture.

Southeast Asian governments are also watching Washington's own constraints. The US war with Iran has strained military resources and unsettled energy markets, and dependent on imported fuel, regional states feel the shock directly. Observers in the region openly ask whether a Pentagon stretched across the Middle East can sustain its focus on Asia. The more the United States asks others to share the burden, the more it exposes how thin its own is.

## A good photo, a hard architecture

Hegseth's framework is not worthless, but its value is largely symbolic. It frames the upcoming anniversary, restates intent, and gives allies a line to quote. It will not, by itself, produce the one thing the region actually lacks: a security order that includes rather than circumscribes China, and that ASEAN writes rather than merely inhabits.

The harder truth is that the United States is selling a shell while the region is buying options. ASEAN will take the photo, pocket the symbolism, and keep its bilateral hedges intact. For a bloc that has spent fifty years refusing to be anyone's pawn, that is not ingratitude. It is the most rational response available — and the one Washington's "framework" was never built to change.

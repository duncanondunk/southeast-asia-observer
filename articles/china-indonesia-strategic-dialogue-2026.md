# 中国印尼首次全面战略对话：从"五大支柱"到矿能与防务的实线

8月21日，中国外长王毅在雅加达同印尼外长苏吉奥诺举行中国印尼全面战略对话机制首次会议。这场被双方称为"双边关系重要里程碑"的对话，把政治、经济、海事、民间与安保"五大支柱"正式框架化，并明确要在矿物、能源、科技与防务领域深化合作。与此同时，中国印尼"2+2"外长防长对话机制第二次部长级会议同日举行。表面看是一场例行外交，实则把两国关系从"领导人共识"推向"常态化、多层次战略沟通架构"。

## 一场"里程碑式"的对话，落在雅加达

苏吉奥诺在开场致辞中说，此次会谈是双边关系的重要里程碑，全面战略对话机制为两国提供了"前瞻性的战略平台"。王毅则把印尼定义为"中国区域外交的优先方向"，并称当前世界"错综复杂"，不点名批评单边主义与霸权主义冲击全球合作与稳定。

把一次外长级会议抬到"里程碑"的高度，折射出雅加达峰会的分量。去年4月苏吉奥诺访华时，两国同意在外长层级设立全面战略对话机制，用以检讨合作进展、制定战略重点。如今首次落地，意味着这一机制从纸面设计进入年度运行轨道，也为后续经贸、海事、安保议题提供了固定协调口。

## 五大支柱之外，矿与能是硬核

对话最务实的部分，落在矿物与能源。双方同意探讨扩大印尼主要大宗商品的市场准入，并在人工智能、通信卫星、能源与矿产领域深化合作。这并非空话：印尼拥有镍、锡、铜、金与铝土矿等大量资源，中国企业在印尼镍加工领域占据主导地位；但普拉博沃政府上台后收紧出口、削减开采配额，直接影响中企商业利益。今年5月，在印尼中企曾公开致函普拉博沃，指政策"严重干扰正常业务运作，直接削弱长期投资信心"。

换言之，矿产与能源合作既是经济议题，也是政治摩擦的减震器。对话把这一块摆到台前，说明双方都意识到：若不把资源合作的可预期性稳住，高层友好的叙事会持续被基层政策反复拉扯。

## 营商环境的承诺，回应了中企关切

王毅会后指出，印尼承诺为中企提供"公平、透明、友好的营商环境"。这句话的听众，正是那些因矿业配额、税费与执法不确定性而焦虑的投资人。复旦大学副研究员薛松认为，这一承诺回应了中企最大的关切，有助于稳定投资预期与信心，但下一步要观察印尼能否在矿业配额、税费、执法与政策沟通上拿出实质举措。

承诺易，兑现难。印尼战略与国际研究中心研究员哈比直言，中企提出的关切未必已经"翻篇"，印尼须进行更彻底的内政与经济改革、提高决策透明度，否则同样的问题未来可能再次浮现。这也是对话机制真正的考题：它能否把高层表态转化为可核查的国内改革。

## 2+2与南海：安全架构的两条线

安全维度同样醒目。中国防长董军同期访印尼，中印尼"2+2"对话机制第二次部长级会议举行——这一机制2023年佐科访华时确立，是中国在全球建立的第一个部长级"2+2"。印尼防长夏弗里表示，两国将展开更多联合演习、人员交流与军工合作。

南海议题则走"软"线：双方重启渔业合作，推动海上联合开发，并朝促成《南海行为准则》努力。中方不点名的"反对外部势力干涉"，被解读为较强的地缘政治信号——既指向区域安全合作加速调整，也暗含对印尼近期加强与美日澳防务合作的回应。两条安全线一硬一软，恰好勾勒出雅加达在大国之间"经济靠中、安全多元"的现实姿态。

## 平衡术的难题：学者的冷静提醒

印尼战略与国际研究中心研究员彼得提醒，印尼虽同意对华军工合作，但与美国也签有重要防务伙伴关系，一旦涉敏感信息与科技交换，可能给印尼带来麻烦。薛松则指出，中方把印尼视为"全球南方重要大国和地区安全伙伴"，这一表态与地区安全合作加速调整有关。

对印尼而言，真正的艺术不在选边，而在保持两边都能获利的空间。全面战略对话机制的设立，给了这套平衡术一个制度化的托盘；但托盘能否盛住不断加码的大国竞争，仍要看雅加达自身的改革定力与战略清醒。

## 延伸阅读

**中印尼关系（China–Indonesia Relations）**：中国与印尼自2013年建立全面战略伙伴关系，是东盟最大经济体的双边关系，涵盖经贸、海事、安全与全球治理等多层次合作。

**全面战略伙伴（Comprehensive Strategic Partnership）**：国家间最高层级合作框架之一，超越一般经贸协定，强调战略协调与机制化对话，中国与多个东南亚国家建有此类关系。

**矿物能源（Minerals & Energy）**：印尼以镍、锡、铜、铝土矿等资源著称，其矿产出口政策与中企冶炼投资深度绑定，是双边经济关系中敏感度最高的议题之一。

# China–Indonesia's First Comprehensive Strategic Dialogue: From "Five Pillars" to Hard Lines in Minerals, Energy and Defence

On 21 August, Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi held the inaugural meeting of the China–Indonesia Comprehensive Strategic Dialogue mechanism with his Indonesian counterpart Sugiono in Jakarta. Hailed by both sides as an "important milestone," the dialogue institutionalised the "five pillars" — political, economic, maritime, people-to-people and security — and explicitly pledged deeper cooperation in minerals, energy, technology and defence. On the same day, the second ministerial meeting of the China–Indonesia "2+2" foreign- and defence-minister dialogue was held. What looks like routine diplomacy is in fact pushing the relationship from "leaders' consensus" toward a "normalised, multi-level strategic communication architecture."

## A "milestone" dialogue, landed in Jakarta

In his opening remarks, Sugiono called the talks a major milestone, describing the Comprehensive Strategic Dialogue as a "forward-looking strategic platform." Wang Yi framed Indonesia as "a priority direction of China's regional diplomacy" and, noting a "complex and intricate" world, criticised — without naming names — unilateralism and hegemonism for undermining global cooperation and stability.

Elevating a foreign-minister-level meeting to "milestone" status signals Jakarta's weight. The two sides had agreed in April last year, during Sugiono's visit to China, to establish the dialogue at ministerial level to review progress and set strategic priorities. Its first convening moves the mechanism from paper design to an annual operating track, and gives trade, maritime and security issues a fixed coordination channel.

## Beyond the five pillars, minerals and energy are the hard core

The most concrete part of the dialogue sat in minerals and energy. The two sides agreed to explore broader market access for Indonesia's key commodities and deepen cooperation in AI, communications satellites, energy and mining. This is no abstraction: Indonesia holds vast nickel, tin, copper, gold and bauxite resources, and Chinese firms dominate its nickel-processing sector; but since Prabowo took office, export curbs and quota cuts have directly hit Chinese commercial interests. In May this year, Chinese enterprises in Indonesia publicly wrote to Prabowo, complaining that the policies "seriously disrupted normal operations and directly weakened long-term investment confidence."

In other words, mineral and energy cooperation is both an economic issue and a shock-absorber for political friction. Putting it front and centre shows both sides understand that without stabilising the predictability of resource cooperation, the friendly high-level narrative will keep being pulled apart by grassroots policy swings.

## The business-environment pledge answers Chinese firms' concerns

After the meeting, Wang Yi noted that Indonesia had promised a "fair, transparent and friendly business environment" for Chinese enterprises. The audience for that line is precisely the investors anxious about quota, tax and enforcement uncertainty. Xue Song, a Fudan University associate researcher, argues the pledge answers Chinese firms' biggest concern and helps stabilise expectations, but the next step is whether Indonesia delivers on quotas, taxes, enforcement and policy communication.

Pledges are easy; delivery is hard. Muhammad Habib Abiyan Dzakwan of CSIS flatly warns the firms' concerns may not be "closed," and that Indonesia must pursue deeper domestic reform and transparency, or the same problems will resurface. That is the mechanism's real test: can it turn high-level rhetoric into verifiable domestic reform?

## The 2+2 and the South China Sea: two lines of security architecture

The security dimension is equally striking. Chinese Defence Minister Dong Jun visited Indonesia concurrently, and the second ministerial "2+2" meeting was held — a mechanism established in 2023 under Jokowi and China's first ministerial-level "2+2" anywhere. Indonesian Defence Minister Sjafrie said the two sides would expand joint exercises, personnel exchanges and defence-industry cooperation.

The South China Sea runs a "soft" line: the two sides restarted fisheries cooperation, pushed maritime joint development, and worked toward a Code of Conduct. China's unnamed "opposition to external interference" reads as a strong geopolitical signal — pointing both to accelerating regional security realignment and, implicitly, to Indonesia's recent deepening of US–Japan–Australia defence ties. One hard line, one soft line together sketch Jakarta's real posture: "economy with China, security diversified."

## The balancing act's hard part: scholars' sober reminders

CSIS researcher Pieter Pandie cautions that while Indonesia agrees to defence-industry cooperation with China, it also holds a major defence partnership with the United States, and any sensitive information or technology exchange could land Jakarta in trouble. Xue Song notes Beijing now treats Indonesia as a "major Global South power and regional security partner," a stance tied to the region's accelerating security reordering.

For Indonesia, the real art is not choosing sides but preserving room to profit from both. The Comprehensive Strategic Dialogue gives that balancing act an institutional tray; whether the tray can hold intensifying great-power competition depends on Jakarta's reform resolve and strategic clarity.

**Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**: one of the highest-tier cooperation frameworks between states, going beyond trade accords to stress strategic coordination and institutionalised dialogue; China holds such ties with several Southeast Asian countries.

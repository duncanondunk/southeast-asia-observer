# 停办半世纪后，柬埔寨白马省土城新民学校复校开学

8月24日，柬埔寨白马省（Kep）土城市（Tho Thanh）的新民学校正式复校开学。这所创办于上世纪60年代的华文学校，在因战乱停办约半个世纪后，重新响起读书声。200多名学生家长和地方政府官员共同见证了这场迟来的开学典礼。

## 半个世纪的断层

新民学校曾是土城地区华裔子弟学习中文、传承中华文化的重要场所。上世纪60年代创办后，它陪伴了当地至少两代华侨华人成长。然而，随后的长期战乱让学校被迫停办，旧校舍在动荡中损毁，校园一度被夷为平地。此后的近五十年里，土城地区始终缺少一所正规的华文学校，当地华裔家庭若想让孩子系统学习中文，往往只能送到更远的城市，或依赖零散的塾馆式教学。

这种"断层"并非孤例。柬埔寨的华文教育在上世纪中后期普遍经历过低谷，许多华校在战乱与社会动荡中关闭。当和平重建来临，华社最先想到的，往往是把"学校"重新立起来——因为语言与文化的传承，需要一间真正的教室，而不是口耳相传。

## 侨商接力：从废墟到课堂

推动新民学校复校的关键力量，是侨商。柬埔寨港澳侨商总会会长黄瑞华去年初发动香港热心侨商捐资，筹集善款兴建两栋教学楼；土城理事会则负责协调与筹备办学事宜。在各方支持下，新校舍与教学设施陆续落成，学校于8月24日恢复招生授课。

开学典礼上，黄开杨会长宣布新民学校正式恢复教学，并感谢柬华理事总会、黄瑞华及社会各界热心人士的支持。他表示，学校将认真抓好教学与校园管理，为当地学生营造良好的中文学习环境，"让更多孩子有机会学习华文、了解中华文化"。校方还表示，未来将逐步完善课程与教学管理，持续推动华文基础教育，为当地培养更多通晓中柬双语的年轻一代。

## 华文教育为何在柬埔寨扎根

新民学校的复校，折射出柬埔寨华文教育一种独特的韧性。与东南亚一些国家将华文教育视为"族裔内部事务"不同，柬埔寨的华校在和平年代获得了相对宽松的生存空间，华文能力也逐渐从"文化认同的象征"转化为实实在在的"经济资本"——中柬经贸往来深化、中国游客与投资者大量进入，使得懂中文的年轻人在就业、经商上更具优势。

这也解释了为何复校消息能引来200多名家长自发到场。对当地华裔家庭而言，送孩子进华校，既是接续祖辈的语言记忆，也是在为下一代铺设一条更宽的人生路径。

## 从一所学校看中柬民间纽带

一所乡村华校的复活，看似是侨社内部的"小事"，却也是中柬民间纽带的一环。华人移民在柬埔寨已历数代，他们既扎根当地，又与故土保持着文化与血缘的联系；而华文学校的复办，正是这种"双重认同"最朴素的物质载体。当两百多名家长站在新校舍前，他们见证的不只是一栋楼，更是一段被战乱打断、如今又被重新接上的文化传承。

## 延伸阅读

**柬华理事总会（Cambodian Chinese Association）**：柬埔寨最具代表性的华社组织之一，长期统筹全柬华校网络、办学资助与文化交流，是本次新民学校复校的重要协调方。

**华文教育（Chinese-medium education）**：以中文为教学语言的学校教育体系，在东南亚华侨华人社会中承担着语言传承与文化认同的双重功能，常与所在国国民教育体系并行。

**白马省（Kep）**：柬埔寨南部临海省份，以海滨与休闲旅游闻名，华人聚落历史悠久，是该国华文教育网络中的地方节点之一。

# After a Half-Century Hiatus, Cambodia's Tho Thanh Xinmin School Reopens

On 24 August, the Xinmin School in Tho Thanh, Kep province, Cambodia, officially resumed classes. Founded in the 1960s as a Chinese-medium school, it had fallen silent for roughly half a century after being forced to close by war. More than 200 students' parents and local government officials attended the long-delayed opening ceremony, where the sound of lessons rang out once again.

## A half-century rift

Xinmin School was once the place where ethnic-Chinese children in Tho Thanh learned Chinese and inherited their culture. After its founding in the 1960s it served at least two generations of local overseas Chinese. Yet prolonged conflict forced its closure; the old campus was damaged in the turmoil and eventually razed to the ground. For nearly five decades thereafter, Tho Thanh lacked a proper Chinese school. Families who wanted their children to study Chinese systematically had to send them to more distant cities or rely on scattered, tutorial-style instruction.

This rupture was far from unique. Chinese-medium education across Cambodia broadly hit a low point in the mid-to-late twentieth century, with many schools shuttered amid war and social upheaval. When peace returned, the first thing Overseas-Chinese communities often rebuilt was the school itself — because the transmission of language and culture needs a real classroom, not just word of mouth.

## Diaspora merchants relay: from ruins to classrooms

The drive to reopen Xinmin came from the business diaspora. Wong Sui-wah (Huang Ruihua), chairman of the Cambodia Hong Kong–Macau Overseas Chinese Chamber of Commerce, launched an appeal in early last year among Hong Kong donors, raising funds to build two teaching blocks. The Tho Thanh council coordinated and prepared the schooling. With broad support, the new campus and facilities were completed, and classes resumed on 24 August.

At the ceremony, Chairman Wong Hoi-yeung (Huang Kaiyang) declared the school reopened and thanked the Cambodian Chinese Association (柬华理事总会), Wong Sui-wah, and other benefactors. He pledged that the school would manage teaching and campus life well, "so that more children have the chance to learn Chinese and understand Chinese culture." The school added that it would gradually refine its curriculum and administration, sustaining basic Chinese education and nurturing a young generation fluent in both Chinese and Khmer.

## Why Chinese-medium education takes root in Cambodia

Xinmin's revival reflects a distinctive resilience in Cambodia's Chinese education. Unlike some Southeast Asian states that treat it as an internal ethnic affair, Cambodian Chinese schools have enjoyed relatively permissive space in peacetime; proficiency in Chinese has also shifted from a "symbol of cultural identity" into tangible "economic capital" — deepening China–Cambodia trade and a flood of Chinese tourists and investors make Mandarin-speaking youth more employable and better positioned in business.

That helps explain why more than 200 parents showed up unprompted. For local ethnic-Chinese families, sending a child to a Chinese school is both reconnecting with grandparents' linguistic memory and paving a wider path for the next generation.

## One school as a window on China–Cambodia ties

The resurrection of a village school looks like an internal diaspora matter, yet it is also a thread in the China–Cambodia people-to-people fabric. Chinese migrants in Cambodia span several generations: rooted locally yet bound to their homeland by culture and kinship. The school's reopening is the most material expression of that dual identity. As those 200-plus parents stood before the new buildings, they witnessed not just a structure but a cultural continuity interrupted by war and now rejoined.

## Further reading

**Cambodian Chinese Association (柬华理事总会)**: Among the most representative overseas-Chinese organizations in Cambodia, it has long coordinated the national Chinese-school network, funded schooling, and promoted cultural exchange — a key coordinator in Xinmin's reopening.

**Chinese-medium education**: Schooling conducted in Chinese as the language of instruction; across Southeast Asian overseas-Chinese communities it carries the dual function of language transmission and cultural identity, usually running parallel to the host country's national system.

**Kep province**: A coastal province in southern Cambodia known for its seashore and resort tourism, with a long-established ethnic-Chinese community and a local node in the country's Chinese-education network.

# AI与内需加持 马来西亚次季经济增速6%超出预期

马来西亚今年第二季经济表现亮眼。据联合早报报道，国家银行（Bank Negara Malaysia，中央银行）8月14日公布数据，次季国内生产总值（GDP）按年增长6%，优于首季的5.4%，也超出统计局与市场预测的5.8%。这是继去年第四季6.3%之后，马来西亚经济增速再度站上6%关口。

## 6%背后：出口与内需双轮驱动

马来西亚次季增长，由更强的出口表现与稳定的家庭开销共同拉动。在全球环境充满不确定、中东战事与能源成本高涨的背景下，马国经济仍保持势头。其中，整体净出口（net exports）按年大增168.5%，成为本轮增长最抢眼的引擎；投资增长亦提供支撑。

## AI浪潮：电子电气出口的“放大器”

真正的亮点在结构。全球AI科技周期持续升温，带动半导体与电子电气设备需求急升。作为全球半导体供应链的重要一环，马来西亚次季电子电气（E&E）产品出口按年上涨57.1%，远高于首季的23%。制造业因此增长7.3%（首季5.9%），主要由出口导向产业、尤其是AI相关需求拉动。一条由“AI投资—芯片需求—马国代工与封测”串起的价值链，正在重塑东南亚制造版图。

## 隐忧：农业回落与通胀暗流

亮眼数据下仍有裂缝。农业次季按年萎缩3.7%，主因棕榈油（palm oil）产量在去年大增后回落；央行提示，若厄尔尼诺（El Niño）现象加剧，农产品产量将进一步受压。通胀方面，次季整体通胀微升至1.9%，核心通胀则放缓至1.9%；央行称中东局势推高外部成本，但针对性燃料补贴有助限制全球成本上升对物价的传导。

## 全年5%：在不确定中保持定力

综合上半年表现，央行预计马来西亚今年全年经济增长率可达5%。总裁阿都拉昔（Abdul Rasheed）表示，尽管前景仍受外部环境影响，但马国凭借强劲的基本面与政策“可从容应对”。对一个高度依赖贸易与外资的小型开放经济体而言，6%的季度增速固然提振信心，但能否把AI红利转化为持续动能，仍要看全球周期与区域供应链的下一步走向。

## 延伸阅读

**电子电气产业（Electrical & Electronics, E&E）**：马来西亚支柱出口产业，涵盖半导体封测、电子元件与设备制造，是其在全球供应链中的核心位置所在。

**净出口（Net Exports）**：出口额减进口额之差，为正时拉动GDP增长；马国次季净出口按年大增168.5%，是增长主引擎。

**针对性燃料补贴（Targeted Fuel Subsidy）**：将补贴精准面向特定群体而非全民，以缓释财政压力并抑制通胀传导的财政政策。

# AI Boom and Domestic Demand Lift Malaysia’s Q2 Growth to 6%, Beating Forecasts

Malaysia’s second-quarter economy outperformed. According to Lianhe Zaobao, Bank Negara Malaysia (the central bank) reported on 14 August that Q2 GDP grew 6% year on year — above Q1’s 5.4% and ahead of the 5.8% expected by the statistics department and the market. It is the first time since Q4 last year’s 6.3% that growth has returned to the 6% mark.

## Behind the 6%: Exports and Domestic Demand

The quarter’s expansion was driven by stronger exports and stable household spending. Even amid global uncertainty, Middle East conflict and high energy costs, Malaysia held its momentum. Overall net exports surged 168.5% year on year, the most striking engine of growth, while investment provided further support.

## The AI Wave: An “Amplifier” for E&E Exports

The real highlight is structural. The global AI cycle keeps heating up, lifting demand for semiconductors and electrical equipment. As a key link in the world’s semiconductor supply chain, Malaysia saw its electrical and electronics (E&E) exports rise 57.1% year on year in Q2, far above Q1’s 23%. Manufacturing thus grew 7.3% (vs 5.9% in Q1), led by export-oriented industries and especially AI-related demand. A value chain running from “AI investment → chip demand → Malaysian foundry and packaging” is reshaping Southeast Asia’s manufacturing map.

## Lurking Risks: Agriculture’s Slide and Inflation

Cracks remain beneath the bright numbers. Agriculture contracted 3.7% year on year, mainly because palm oil output fell back after last year’s surge; the central bank warned that a stronger El Niño could further pressure farm output. On inflation, headline inflation edged up to 1.9% while core inflation eased to 1.9%; the bank said the Middle East situation raised external costs, but targeted fuel subsidies helped limit the pass-through of global cost increases to prices.

## A 5% Full-Year Target: Steadiness Amid Uncertainty

On the strength of the first half, the central bank projects full-year growth of around 5%. Governor Abdul Rasheed said that despite external headwinds, Malaysia’s strong fundamentals and policies let it “respond with composure.” For a small, open economy heavily reliant on trade and foreign capital, a 6% quarterly print boosts confidence — but whether AI dividends become durable momentum depends on where the global cycle and regional supply chains turn next.

## Further reading

**Electrical & Electronics (E&E)**: Malaysia’s pillar export industry — semiconductor assembly, testing, components and equipment — that anchors its position in global supply chains.

**Net Exports**: Exports minus imports; when positive, they add to GDP. Malaysia’s net exports jumped 168.5% year on year in Q2, the main growth engine.

**Targeted Fuel Subsidy**: A fiscal policy directing subsidies to specific groups rather than the whole population, easing fiscal pressure and curbing inflation pass-through.

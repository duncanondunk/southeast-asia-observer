# 东盟青年华商走进惠州：当"侨"成为出海的桥

9月20日至23日，第三届"侨兴南粤·惠贸东盟"东盟青年华商惠州行在广东惠州举行。来自东盟九国的43名青年华商代表与惠州本地企业家展开为期四天的实地考察与对接洽谈。主办方披露的不完全统计显示，现场达成初步合作意向9项。代表团先后走进名博智能家居、恩雅乐器、TCL液晶产业园、乐亿通科技等企业，实地了解智能家居、乐器、半导体显示与新能源产品。单看数字并不惊人，但恰恰是这类小规模、靠关系的务实订单，历来是华侨华人商网最擅长的领域。

## 数字：43人、9国、9项意向

9月20日至23日，第三届"侨兴南粤·惠贸东盟"东盟青年华商惠州行在广东惠州举行。来自东盟九国的43名青年华商代表与惠州本地企业家展开为期四天的实地考察与对接洽谈。据主办方不完全统计，现场达成初步合作意向9项。代表团先后走进名博智能家居、恩雅乐器、TCL液晶产业园、乐亿通科技等企业，实地了解智能家居、乐器、半导体显示与新能源等产业的发展情况。纸面上的数字并不惊人，但恰恰是这类小规模、靠关系的务实订单，历来是华侨华人商网最擅长的领域。

## "伴商出海"：侨务话语里的贸易新逻辑

这次活动的提法耐人寻味。一场交流会直接命名为"伴商出海·以侨助贸"，供需对接会则冠以"伴商出海·惠贸拓东盟"。两句话合在一起，意思很清楚：惠州不再只把海外侨胞看作捐助者或返乡投资者，而是把他们当作渠道——既能进货、又能本地化的买手，把惠州制造带进中国企业单打独斗难以进入的市场。这是侨务工作从早年"引智引资"向更硬核的"以侨助贸"贸易逻辑的一次转向。放在中国"企业出海"的大盘子里，华侨华人正被重新定位为介于工厂与外国消费者之间那一层柔软而可信的缓冲。

## 惠州凭什么：制造底盘与侨乡底子

惠州绝非随便选的东道主。它既是粤港澳大湾区里电子与新能源集群厚实的制造城市，TCL的显示基地只是最显眼的一块；同时惠州又有"侨乡"的身份，海外乡亲大量集中在东南亚。这个组合正是关键：一个既会造东西、又在海外有亲缘纽带的地方，在结构上最适合扮演活动想要它扮演的"桥"。市委统战部与市侨联已连续三年举办这一行，把一次性的接待办成了制度化的平台。连续性很重要——反复的低调聚会比热闹的投资洽谈会更能积累信任。

## 青年华商的三重身份：纽带、买手、合伙人

这一轮真正新鲜之处，是对"青年"的强调。代表团被称为"青年华商"，而非早年间那种德高望重的宗亲会领袖。他们的角色有三重。第一是纽带：既懂中国的供给文化，也懂家乡市场的监管与消费脾性。第二是买手：在供需对接会上，他们与近30家本地制造企业一对一配对，扮演第一道采购侦察兵。第三是合伙人：9项初步意向意味着共同本地化，而非简单转售。换言之，年轻一代华侨华人创业者被要求承接"出海"中最难的一段——最后一公里的信任，而中国工厂只管供货。这是一种清爽的分工，也契合侨务系统一贯偏爱借力"侨力"、而非花国家钱的习惯。

## 冷静看：光有乡情不够

不过，评论不该盲目乐观。9项"初步意向"不是9份签约合同；对接会产出情谊，远比产出营收更可靠。更深的疑问在于，这座侨桥能否经得起市场现实的碰撞。年轻华侨华人往往与祖籍县相距甚远——母语流利、根植于住在国经济，对"家乡"的忠诚更多是情绪性的，而非操作性的。一句潮汕或客家姓氏的渊源或许能换来一次见面，却替代不了价格、质保、售后，也替代不了对东盟产品标准的合规。更何况东盟从来不是一个统一市场：卖得动新加坡的东西，与卖得动印尼或越南的东西天差地别。若把"以侨助贸"简化成宴请式外交，惠州出口商迟早会发现，情绪绝非分销协议的替代品。聪明的做法是让侨网的信任与硬商业条款相配，并让青年创业者从利差中获利，使这座桥自我维持，而非依赖官方补贴。

## 延伸阅读

- **华商（Overseas Chinese Business）**：长期居中协调中国—东盟贸易与投资的东南亚族群企业。
- **侨贸（Qiaomao）**：借华侨华人与侨乡纽带开展的跨境商贸实践。
- **企业出海（Chinese Firms Going Global）**：中国推动本土企业拓展海外市场的整体布局，华侨华人被视作可信的中间人。

# Young ASEAN Chinese Entrepreneurs in Huizhou: When the Diaspora Becomes the Bridge for Going Global

From September 20 to 23, the third "Qiaoxing Nanyue · Huizhou Trade with ASEAN" youth overseas-Chinese business tour was held in Huizhou, Guangdong. Forty-three young ethnic-Chinese business representatives from nine ASEAN countries joined local entrepreneurs for four days of site visits and matching sessions. According to incomplete statistics cited by the organisers, nine preliminary cooperation intentions were reached on the spot. The participants toured companies such as Mingbo Smart Home, Enya Musical Instruments, the TCL liquid-crystal industrial park and Leyiton Technology, examining smart-home devices, musical instruments, semiconductor displays and new-energy products. On paper the numbers look modest; in practice they are precisely the kind of small, relationship-based deals that the diaspora business network has always been good at.

## The numbers: 43 delegates, 9 countries, 9 intentions

From September 20 to 23, the third "Qiaoxing Nanyue · Huizhou Trade with ASEAN" youth overseas-Chinese business tour was held in Huizhou, Guangdong. Forty-three young ethnic-Chinese business representatives from nine ASEAN countries joined local entrepreneurs for four days of site visits and matching sessions. According to incomplete statistics cited by the organisers, nine preliminary cooperation intentions were reached on the spot. The delegation visited firms including Mingbo Smart Home, Enya Musical Instruments, the TCL liquid-crystal industrial park and Leyiton Technology, looking closely at smart-home devices, musical instruments, semiconductor displays and new-energy industries. The headline figures are unremarkable, yet it is exactly this kind of small-scale, relationship-driven, practical order that the overseas-Chinese business network has historically excelled at.

## "Accompanying businesses abroad": a new trade logic in diaspora affairs

The framing of the event is telling. One session was literally titled "Accompanying Businesses Abroad · Helping Trade Through the Qiao" (伴商出海·以侨助贸), and a supply-demand meeting carried the label "Accompanying Businesses Abroad · Huizhou Trade Expanding into ASEAN" (伴商出海·惠贸拓东盟). Read together, the message is clear: Huizhou no longer sees its overseas Chinese merely as donors or returning investors. It now treats them as distribution channels—local buyers and localisers who can take Huizhou-made goods into markets that a Chinese exporter would otherwise struggle to enter. This is a shift from the old diaspora-affairs script of "attracting talent and capital" (引智引资) toward a harder, trade-oriented logic of "using the diaspora to help trade" (以侨助贸). Within Beijing's wider "Chinese firms going global" push, the overseas Chinese are being repositioned as a soft, trusted layer sitting between the factory floor and the foreign consumer.

## Why Huizhou: a manufacturing base and a qiaoxiang foundation

Huizhou is no random host. It is a manufacturing city in the Greater Bay Area with a substantial electronics and new-energy cluster—TCL's display base is only the most visible piece. At the same time, Huizhou carries a qiaoxiang (侨乡, ancestral hometown of emigrants) identity, with overseas kin concentrated across Southeast Asia. That combination is the whole point: a place that both makes things and has family ties abroad is structurally suited to the "bridge" role the event wants to perform. The municipal united-front and overseas-Chinese federation organs have now run this tour three years running, turning what might have been a one-off reception into an institutionalised platform. Continuity matters—repeated, low-spectacle gatherings build the trust that flashy investment forums rarely do.

## The triple role of young overseas Chinese: connector, buyer, partner

What is genuinely new this round is the emphasis on youth. The delegates are described as "young overseas Chinese business people" (青年华商), not the senior clan-association leaders of an earlier era. Their role is threefold. First, as connectors: they know both the supplier culture in China and the regulatory and consumer quirks of their home markets. Second, as buyers: several were paired one-on-one with nearly 30 local manufacturers at the supply-demand meeting, functioning as first-stage procurement scouts. Third, as partners: the nine preliminary intentions imply joint localisation rather than simple reselling. In other words, the young diaspora entrepreneur is being asked to absorb the hardest part of "going global"—last-mile trust—while Chinese factories supply the product. It is a neat division of labour, and one that sits comfortably with the Overseas Chinese Affairs system's long-standing preference for leveraging "qiaoli" (侨力, overseas-Chinese capacity) rather than spending state money.

## A sober view: hometown sentiment alone is not enough

Still, the commentary should not be starry-eyed. Nine "preliminary intentions" is not nine signed contracts; matching sessions produce goodwill more reliably than they produce revenue. The deeper question is whether the diaspora bridge survives contact with market reality. Younger overseas Chinese are often far removed from the ancestral county—fluent in the local language, rooted in the host economy, and loyalty to "hometown" is sentimental rather than operational. A Teochew or Hakka surname tie may open a first meeting, but it will not substitute for price, warranty, after-sales service or compliance with ASEAN product standards. Moreover, ASEAN is not one market: what sells in Singapore differs sharply from what sells in Indonesia or Vietnam. If "using the diaspora to help trade" is reduced to banquet diplomacy, Huizhou's exporters will discover that sentiment is a poor substitute for a distribution agreement. The smart play is to pair the qiao network's trust with hard commercial terms—and to let the young entrepreneurs profit from the margin, so the bridge is self-sustaining rather than dependent on official sponsorship.

## Further reading

- **Overseas Chinese business (华商)**: Ethnic-Chinese enterprises across Southeast Asia that have long mediated China–ASEAN trade and investment.
- **Qiaomao (侨贸)**: The practice of channelling cross-border commerce through overseas-Chinese networks and hometown ties.
- **Chinese firms going global (企业出海)**: Beijing's broad push for domestic companies to expand into overseas markets, with the diaspora cast as a trusted intermediary.

世界银行（World Bank）最新一期《东亚与太平洋地区经济更新》给出了一组令区域警觉的数字：受外部冲击叠加，东亚与太平洋地区二零二六年经济增长预计放缓至百分之四点二，低于上年的百分之五点零。对高度依赖外贸的东南亚而言，这声减速的刹车，来自两股外力——中东能源冲击与美国关税不确定性。

## 从5.0%到4.2%

这不是个别国家的偶发波动，而是区域的普遍回撤。报告将放缓主要归因于中国增速回落，以及中东冲突导致的能源价格走高。世界银行研究部主任马图指出，关税不确定性与霍尔木兹海峡（Hormuz）供应中断的叠加，对最依赖出口的亚洲经济体伤害最深。报告甚至给出情景：若燃料价格持续上涨五成，区域家庭收入可能损失百分之三至四，并波及相关国家的粮食安全。

## 分化中的经济体

放缓并非整齐划一。越南受创最重，增速料从逾百分之八回落至百分之六点三，但仍居区域前列；泰国在债务与政局不稳中挣扎，增速或仅百分之一点三；印尼预计增百分之四点七、马来西亚增百分之四点四，落差相对温和；柬埔寨、老挝亦同步走低。唯一逆势改善的是饱经战火的缅甸，预计小幅回升至约百分之二——低基数下的微弱亮色。

## 人工智能成少数亮点

灰暗中仍有微光。报告点名马来西亚、泰国与越南，是人工智能相关出口与投资激增的主要受益者。但世界银行也提醒，受连接、技能与初创生态短板制约，区域对人工智能的生产率红利远未充分释放——中国与泰国仅百分之十三至十七的跨国企业子公司使用人工智能，约为工业国家三分之一。技术红利近在咫尺，却隔着一道能力鸿沟。

## 政策空间决定韧性

冲击的最终烈度，取决于各国的"暴露度、脆弱性与政策空间"。能源进口依赖高、财政余量薄的经济体最易受伤。报告预计多数国家增速将于二零二七年回升，但马图强调，中东能源扰动"并非暂时冲击"，当下的两周停火难消不确定性。对东南亚而言，真正的护城河不在外部顺风，而在国内的结构改革与数码时代的生产率投资。

## 一场给区域的压力测试

东南亚的减速提醒我们：增长可以借外部周期上行，却难抵结构性逆风。当关税与能源两股外力同时收紧，决定走向的，是各国把外部冲击转化为改革动能的本领。风能助势，亦能逆袭；唯韧性可穿越周期。

## 延伸阅读

**东亚与太平洋地区经济更新（East Asia and Pacific Economic Update）**：世界银行每年发布的区域宏观经济展望，评估增长、贫困与结构性挑战。

**霍尔木兹海峡（Strait of Hormuz）**：连接波斯湾与印度洋的关键能源通道，全球相当比例的油气运输经此，其安全直接影响能源价格。

**"中国+1"（China Plus One）**：企业将部分产能从中国分散至其他国家的供应链策略，东南亚是主要承接方之一。

The World Bank's latest East Asia and Pacific Economic Update delivers a set of numbers that should jolt the region: hit by stacked external shocks, regional growth is projected to slow to 4.2% in 2026, down from 5.0% in 2025. For Southeast Asia—deeply trade-dependent—the brakes come from two external forces: the Middle East energy shock and US tariff uncertainty.

## From 5.0% to 4.2%

This is no isolated wobble but a broad regional pullback. The Bank pins the slowdown chiefly on China's decelerating growth and higher energy prices from the Middle East conflict. Aaditya Mattoo, the Bank's director of development research, noted that the combination of tariff uncertainty and disruptions to Hormuz Strait supplies would hurt the most export-dependent Asian economies hardest. The report even models a scenario: a sustained 50% fuel-price rise could cost regional households 3–4% of income and spill into food security.

## Economies on Divergent Paths

The slowdown is far from uniform. Vietnam suffers the steepest drop, its growth seen easing from just over 8% to 6.3%—still near the regional front. Thailand, struggling with household debt and chronic political instability, may manage only 1.3%. Indonesia is projected at 4.7% and Malaysia at 4.4%, with gentler gaps; Cambodia and Laos also slip. The lone upside is conflict-scarred Myanmar, forecast to tick up to around 2%—a faint bright spot on a low base.

## AI as a Rare Bright Spot

Amid the grey, a sliver of light remains. The report names Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam as the chief beneficiaries of the AI-related export and investment surge. But the Bank cautions that, held back by gaps in connectivity, skills and startup ecosystems, the region has barely tapped AI's productivity dividend—only 13–17% of multinational subsidiaries in China and Thailand use AI, about a third of industrialised economies' rate. The technological payoff is within reach yet separated by a capability gap.

## Policy Space Defines Resilience

The final intensity of the shock hinges on each country's "exposure, vulnerability, and policy space." Economies with high energy-import reliance and thin fiscal room are most exposed. The Bank expects most to regain momentum in 2027, yet Mattoo stressed the Middle East energy disruption is "not a temporary shock" and a two-week ceasefire does little to dispel uncertainty. For Southeast Asia, the real moat lies not in external tailwinds but in domestic structural reform and productivity investment for the digital age.

## A Stress Test for the Region

Southeast Asia's slowdown is a reminder: growth can ride external cycles up, yet bends under structural headwinds. When tariffs and energy tighten together, what decides the trajectory is each country's ability to convert external shocks into reform momentum. Tailwinds can lift; they can also reverse. Only resilience crosses cycles.

## Further reading

**East Asia and Pacific Economic Update**: The World Bank's periodic regional macroeconomic outlook, assessing growth, poverty and structural challenges.

**Strait of Hormuz**: The critical energy channel linking the Persian Gulf and the Indian Ocean; a large share of global oil and gas transit passes through it, so its security directly moves energy prices.

**"China Plus One"**: A supply-chain strategy spreading some capacity from China to other countries; Southeast Asia is a prime recipient.

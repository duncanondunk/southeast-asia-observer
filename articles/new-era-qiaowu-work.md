# 从"引资引智"到"服务到人"：新时代侨务工作的范式转移，与东南亚（Southeast Asia）的试金石

在北京（Beijing）召开全国侨务工作会议一周之后，中国侨网（China Qiao Wang）以"向实、向新、向未来"为题推出了一组专家笔谈。这个提法并不只是场面话。仔细读来，它透露出北京对其六千万海外侨胞的治理思路正在发生一次安静却深刻的重新排序：侨务不再只把侨胞视为可供汲取的资本与人才蓄水池，而越来越把他们当作需要被服务的对象；而这套思路，在东南亚——尤其是东南亚华人社会——将遭遇它自身无法掌控的族裔政治考验。

## 转向：从"引资引智"到"服务到人"

过去三十年，中国侨务政策（diaspora policy）的主导话语是功利主义的。"引资引智"是核心口号，衡量标准是能把多少海外资本与专才引回国内。侨海（中国）创新发展研究院执行院长邢菁华把这种转变说得很直白：工作重心已从"重联谊、重活动"转向"重需求、重落地、重效能"。"最后一公里"的服务语言——"线上数字侨务平台""侨胞之家""新侨驿站"——不再是为归国巨贾摆宴，而是把政策变成侨胞真正用得上的办事路径：企业注册、领事协助、资质互认。

这是一种更成熟的姿态，专家们称许它是对的。但请留意它意味着什么。"服务到人"的转向，也意味着国家正在争夺一群人的忠诚——而这群人按定义生活在它的管辖之外，效忠于别国政府。服务，是留人的柔软外包。当北京构建起"信息收集—需求识别—资源匹配—服务交付—效果反馈"的闭环，它同时也绘制了一张精细得多的侨情地图：谁是中国移民、他们想要什么。在这套设计里，务实与监测是一枚硬币的两面。

## 数字背后的重新布局

这次重新布局并非空谈。据报道列举的数据，2025年12月在福州举行的第三届中国侨智发展大会，促成科技合作项目（意向）一百余项，推介高新技术成果四百五十项。2026年"亲情中华·中国寻根之旅"夏令营由全国侨联主办，把来自丹麦、荷兰、西班牙、德国、越南（Vietnam）、法国、阿曼的五十名华裔青少年带到广州暨南大学（Jinan University）华文学院，展开十天研学。8月4日，泰国（Thailand）潮州会馆为当地社群举办了一场电影专场放映。

每一个数字都讲着不同的故事。那一百个项目，是旧逻辑穿了件新衣——人才即管道。那五十名青少年，是新逻辑：身份即传承，是被缓慢涵养而非被抽取的东西。而曼谷的那场放映，才是真正的提示——文化是一种无需使馆的联结组织。三者合起来，描绘出一个从"汲取"走向"涵养"的治理模型。至于"涵养"能否在不滑向同化压力的前提下放大，是专家们没有提出的问题。

## 为什么东南亚是试金石

到这里，分析必须离开舒适的国内框架。在六千万海外侨胞中，绝大多数生活在东南亚；在那里，"华人"不是一句侨务标签，而是一种与殖民史、公民身份、以及对"双重忠诚"的反复猜疑纠缠在一起的真实族裔身份。同一份文化自信，在广州的醒狮节上显得无害，一旦被东南亚的国防部长援引为安全关切，味道就完全不同了。

我们最近就见过这套机制。7月29日，菲律宾（Philippines）国防部长特奥多罗把华文教师培训重新框定为安全议题——他借一份 SeaLight 报告，把镜头从巴丹群岛的一场研讨会，拉宽到整个华校网络。他不是在发现什么阴谋，而是在重新打开一个东南亚各国大多在几十年前就已正式封存的问题：一个华人公民的首要忠诚到底在哪里？印尼（Indonesia）在1966年以限制令作答；马来西亚（Malaysia）另建了一套华文独立中学体系；越南在1978年驱逐了华人。把华侨华人"安全化"，从来不能解决忠诚之问，它只是把身份重新拖回政治竞技场。

这正是新版侨务话语所回避的矛盾。"服务侨胞"与"发挥融通中外的桥梁作用"，听起来对称而善意。但在雅加达（Jakarta）或马尼拉（Manila），一个明显更擅长服务同族的中国，反而可能印证、而非消解这样一种猜疑：那些同族人是第五纵队。北京越是努力当侨胞的靠山，就越是给当地的民族主义者递上口号。在这里，软实力是有硬天花板的。

## 润物无声的软实力：文化成为新前沿

专家们感觉到了这一点，所以文化被赋予了最谨慎的框架。中国华侨历史学会副会长张春旺主张，工作应从"重情感动员"转向"尊重现实身份、厚植文化认同"，从"重短期活动"转向"长期涵养资源"。暨南大学（我所在的单位）华侨华人研究院教授陈奕平，把它拆解为"硬联通"与"软联通"之分：经贸与科创打通的是硬联通，文化与公益实现的是软联通。邢菁华说得更尖锐：今后的关键，是把侨胞天然的跨文化、跨区域优势，转化为推动高质量发展与中外友好的长久力量。

请注意其中的克制。词汇不再是"团结统一祖国"，而是"共同发展""文明交流互鉴""亲和力"。这是润物无声的软实力——不带旗帜的影响力。对东南亚的东道主社会而言，这种框架比公开的政治动员更容易容忍。一场潮汕会馆的电影放映、社区节庆上的一头醒狮、一所教写汉字的华校：这些都不要求政治忠诚，却都在悄悄让一种文化在异乡存活。

然而数字化的转向自带风险。张春旺正确地提醒，大数据与人工智能不能加剧老年归侨侨眷的"数字鸿沟"。而同样这些能让北京实时绘制侨情地图的工具，在住在国政府眼中，也可能浮现为"外国干涉基础设施"的幽灵。被承诺为"服务"的技术，到了海外，也可能被读成"触角"。

## 未竟之问

这组专家笔谈对方向很有信心，对限度却保持沉默。有三个问题，应当随政策一同上路。

第一，双向之问。陈奕平预言未来五到十年将"更加注重为侨服务和侨益维护，实现国家发展与侨胞自身发展的相互支撑"。说得好——但"相互支撑"易于宣示、难于证明。当某个东南亚国家怀疑一名华人公民忠贞有亏时，北京的"服务"究竟服务于哪一边？侨胞利益与派出国利益重合的频率，远低于会议修辞所假设的那样。

第二，制度之问。"大侨务"工作格局承诺国内侨务与国外侨务衔接更紧。在国内把衔接做密，是好事。但在海外——海外侨胞是别国公民——把衔接做密，恰恰是触发东道国焦虑的那一下。同一套架构，既交付服务，也广播"触角"。

第三，代际之赌。整套战略压在"华裔新生代"身上。张春旺指出，华裔新生代"逐渐成为海外侨社重要力量"。但这些青年首先是泰国、印尼、马来西亚、菲律宾的公民。他们的"文化认同"真实却有界；他们的政治认同是当地的。涵养前者而尊重后者，是一条窄路；新话语尚未证明自己能走好它，而不滑向"代理同化"或"弃养"两端。

这一切，都不是反对服务侨胞。恰恰相反：一套认真对待海外侨胞真实生活——他们的安全、他们的教育、他们"既是中国人又是泰国人、既是马来人又是华人"的权利——的侨务政策，早就该来了。风险不在"转向服务"。风险在那个未经检验的假定：被放大、被数字化、与软实力雄心熔为一炉的"服务"，对悉尼的移民与对泗水的华人公民，是同一件事。它不是。东南亚，将是检验这套新版侨务工作是被印证、还是被戳穿的地方。

## 延伸阅读

- **侨务工作（Qiaowu / overseas Chinese affairs）**：中国面向海外侨胞的综合性工作体系，涵盖联谊、服务、权益保护与文化传播，近年从"引资引智"向"服务到人"转型。
- **华裔新生代（Ethnic-Chinese youth / 华裔新生代）**：在住在国出生或成长的年轻华侨华人，身份认同兼具所在国公民与族裔文化双重属性，是新时代侨务涵养的重点对象。
- **硬联通与软联通（Hard and soft connectivity）**：经贸科创层面的"硬联通"与文化交流、公益层面的"软联通"，常被用来区分侨务工作中可量化合作与柔性影响力两种路径。

# From "wooing capital" to "serving people": the paradigm shift in China's diaspora governance — and Southeast Asia as the stress test

A week after Beijing convened its national qiaowu (overseas Chinese affairs) work conference, China Qiao Wang ran an expert roundtable under the banner "向实、向新、向未来" — pragmatic, new, forward-looking. The framing is not merely ceremonial. Read closely, the commentary signals a quiet but consequential reordering of how Beijing thinks about its 60-million-strong diaspora: less as a reservoir of capital and talent to be tapped, more as a constituency to be served, and — in Southeast Asia above all — a bridge whose value is tested by ethnic politics it cannot control.

## The pivot: from "wooing capital" to "serving people"

For three decades, the dominant idiom of Chinese diaspora policy was utilitarianism. The slogans were 引资引智 — "bring in investment, bring in talent" — and the metric was how much overseas capital and expertise could be channelled home. Xing Jinghua, executive director of the Qiaohai (China) Innovation Development Research Institute, captured the shift plainly: the work has moved "from emphasising ties and activities toward emphasising needs, implementation, and effectiveness." The language of "last-mile service" — digital qiaowu platforms, "侨胞之家" (overseas Chinese homes), "新侨驿站" (new immigrant stations) — is no longer about staging banquets for returning tycoons. It is about turning policy into procedures a migrant can actually use: business registration, consular help, recognition of credentials.

That is a more mature posture, and the experts are right to praise it. But note what it implies. The pivot to "serving people" also means the state is now competing for the loyalty of people who, by definition, live outside its jurisdiction and answer to other governments. Service is the soft edge of retention. When Beijing builds a closed loop of "information collection — need identification — resource matching — service delivery — feedback," it is also building a far finer map of who its diaspora is and what it wants. Pragmatism and surveillance are, in this design, two sides of the same coin.

## The numbers behind the reshuffle

The reshuffle is not abstract. According to figures cited in the report, the third China Overseas Chinese Talent Development Conference in Fuzhou (December 2025) yielded more than 100 intended science-and-technology cooperation projects and promoted 450 high-tech achievements. The 2026 "Root-Seeking Journey" summer camp — run by the All-China Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese — brought 50 ethnic-Chinese teenagers from Denmark, the Netherlands, Spain, Germany, Vietnam, France and Oman to Jinan University's College of Chinese Language and Culture in Guangzhou for ten days. And on 4 August, the Bangkok Teochew Association hosted a film screening for the local community.

Each number tells a different story. The 100 projects are the old logic wearing new clothes — talent as pipeline. The 50 teenagers are the new logic: identity as inheritance, cultivated slowly, not extracted. The Bangkok screening is the Tell — culture as the connective tissue that needs no embassy. Put together, they suggest a governance model moving from extraction to cultivation. Whether cultivation scales without tipping into assimilation pressure is the question the experts did not ask.

## Why Southeast Asia is the stress test

Here the analysis must leave the comfortable domestic frame. Of the 60 million overseas Chinese, the overwhelming majority live in Southeast Asia, where "Chinese" is not a diaspora label but a lived ethnicity entangled with colonialism, citizenship, and recurrent suspicion of dual loyalty. The same cultural confidence that reads as benign at a lion-dance festival in Guangzhou reads very differently when a Southeast Asian defence minister invokes it as a security concern.

We have seen the mechanism recently. When Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro, on 29 July, recast Chinese-language teacher training as a security file — leaning on a SeaLight report to widen the lens from a Batanes workshop to the entire network of huaqiao schools — he was not discovering a plot. He was re-opening a question Southeast Asia had, in most countries, formally closed decades ago: where does a Chinese citizen's primary loyalty lie? Indonesia answered with restrictions in 1966; Malaysia built a separate Chinese-school system; Vietnam expelled ethnic Chinese in 1978. The securitisation of overseas Chinese does not resolve the loyalty question; it merely drags identity back into the political arena.

This is the contradiction the new qiaowu discourse papers over. "Serving the diaspora" and "leveraging its bridge role" sound symmetrical and benign. But in Jakarta or Manila, a Chinese state that is visibly better at serving its co-ethnics can confirm, rather than dispel, the suspicion that those co-ethnics are a fifth column. The harder Beijing tries to be the diaspora's patron, the more it hands local nationalists a rallying cry. Soft power, here, has a hard ceiling.

## Soft power by stealth: culture as the new frontier

The experts sense this, which is why culture gets the most careful framing. Zhang Chunwang of the China Society for Overseas Chinese History argues the work should shift "from emotional mobilisation to respecting real identities and deepening cultural identity," and "from short-term activities to long-term resource cultivation." Chen Yiping of Jinan University (my own institution) frames it as a distinction between 硬联通 — hard connectivity through trade and technology — and 软联通 — soft connectivity through culture and philanthropy. Xing Jinghua puts the point sharply: the task ahead is to convert the diaspora's natural cross-cultural, cross-regional advantage into a durable force for mutual understanding.

Note the restraint. The vocabulary is no longer "unite the motherland" but "mutual development," "civilisational exchange," "affinity." This is soft power by stealth — influence without the flag. For Southeast Asian host societies, that framing is easier to tolerate than overt political mobilisation. A Teochew association screening a film, a lion dance at a community festival, a Chinese school teaching characters: none of these demands political loyalty, and all of them quietly keep a culture alive abroad.

Yet the digital turn carries its own risk. Zhang correctly warns that big data and AI must not deepen a "digital divide" for elderly returned overseas Chinese. The same tools that let Beijing map its diaspora in real time also raise, for host governments, the spectre of foreign-interference infrastructure. Technology promised as service can be read, abroad, as reach.

## The open questions

The expert roundtable is confident about direction but silent on limits. Three questions deserve to travel with the policy.

First, the two-way test. Chen Yiping predicts the next five to ten years will "pay more attention to serving overseas Chinese and protecting their interests, achieving mutual support between national development and the diaspora's own development." Fair enough — but "mutual support" is easy to declare and hard to prove. When a Southeast Asian state suspects an ethnic-Chinese citizen of divided loyalty, which side does Beijing's "service" actually serve? The diaspora's interest and the sending state's interest coincide far less often than the conference rhetoric assumes.

Second, the institutional question. The "大侨务" (grand qiaowu) framework promises tighter linkage between domestic and external qiaowu work. Tighter linkage at home is welcome. Tighter linkage abroad, where overseas Chinese live as citizens of other states, is precisely what triggers host-country anxiety. The same architecture that delivers service also broadcasts reach.

Third, the generational bet. The entire strategy rests on 华裔新生代 — ethnic-Chinese youth who, Zhang notes, are "gradually becoming an important force" in overseas communities. But these youths are, first, citizens of Thailand, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines. Their "cultural identity" is real but bounded; their political identity is local. Cultivating the former while respecting the latter is a narrow path, and the new discourse has not yet shown it can walk it without slipping into either assimilation-by-proxy or abandonment.

None of this is an argument against serving the diaspora. On the contrary: a qiaowu policy that takes the actual lives of overseas Chinese seriously — their safety, their schooling, their right to be both Chinese and Thai, both Malaysian and ethnic Chinese — is long overdue. The risk is not the turn toward service. It is the unexamined assumption that service, scaled and digitised and fused with soft-power ambition, means the same thing to a migrant in Sydney and an ethnic-Chinese citizen in Surabaya. It does not. Southeast Asia will be the place where the new qiaowu work is either vindicated or exposed.

## Further reading

- **Qiaowu (overseas Chinese affairs)**: China's comprehensive framework for its diaspora, spanning outreach, service, rights protection and cultural transmission, now shifting from "bringing in capital and talent" toward "serving people."
- **Ethnic-Chinese youth (华裔新生代)**: Younger overseas Chinese born or raised in host countries, whose identity blends local citizenship with ethnic-cultural belonging — the prime target of new-era diaspora cultivation.
- **Hard and soft connectivity**: The distinction between "hard connectivity" through trade and technology and "soft connectivity" through culture and philanthropy, used to separate measurable cooperation from flexible influence.

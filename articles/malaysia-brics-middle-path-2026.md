# 中间道路与特别机制：马来西亚在金砖框架下的多边对冲

2026年9月11日，马来西亚总理安华在印度新德里出席第18届金砖国家（BRICS）领导人峰会期间，与俄罗斯总统普京举行场边会谈，提议设立马俄特别机制以加快落实双边协议，普京表示同意。马来西亚官方通讯机构（J-KOM）同时强调，马国的BRICS伙伴关系在拓宽贸易之余坚持“中间道路”（middle path）。此行中，马俄同意扩大棕榈油出口，印度总理与安华亦就贸易、国防与半导体合作交换意见。在美中博弈加剧的当下，吉隆坡的对冲姿态愈发清晰，也愈发需要技巧。

## 加入金砖后的对冲逻辑

马来西亚于近年正式成为金砖伙伴国，其动机并非选边，而是扩宽选项。作为高度依赖外贸的中等规模经济体，吉隆坡长期受大国周期摆布：美中博弈令其左右为难，加入多边机制则能提供缓冲与议价空间。金砖平台给了马来西亚一个不必依附单一阵营、却仍能参与全球南方议程的入口。安华借新德里峰会密集会见俄印领导人，正是把“伙伴国”身份转化为具体经贸与外交收益的操作，而非意识形态靠拢。

## “中间道路”：不结盟传统的当代表述

“中间道路”并非新词，而是马来西亚不结盟外交的当代表述。自独立以来，吉隆坡便在不结盟运动框架下游走于东西阵营之间，既与美国保持安全合作，又与苏联、后来的俄罗斯及中国维系经贸纽带。J-KOM在峰会期间特意强调这一措辞，意在向国内外释放信号：参与金砖不等于倒向中俄集团，而是延续“各方都交朋友、谁也不依附”的均衡传统。话语的重复使用，本身就是一种战略定调。在金砖内部，成员国对“不结盟”的理解并不一致，马来西亚把传统话语搬出来，也意在与其他伙伴国划清姿态差异，避免被笼统归入“反西方”阵营。

## 棕榈油：对俄经济抓手的地缘含义

马俄会谈中最具实质含量的，是扩大棕榈油出口、做大双边贸易。据马方披露，俄罗斯是马来西亚在欧洲的第九大贸易伙伴，去年双边贸易总额达87.2亿令吉（约27.4亿美元，折合约195亿元人民币），呈稳健增长。棕榈油既是马来西亚的支柱出口品，也是撬动对俄关系的经济杠杆——在西方制裁削弱俄欧传统农产品通道后，东南亚油脂填补空间显现。以单一大宗农产品加深绑定，既是生意，也是地缘筹码。

## 战略自主的代价：在美中之间

“中间道路”有收益，也有成本。马来西亚越是在金砖框架内靠近俄罗斯、强化与中国的经贸，越易招致华盛顿的疑虑，尤其在半导体与防务合作等敏感领域。美国虽不会轻易惩罚一个中等伙伴，但其《芯片与科学法案》与出口管制逻辑，注定对“两头下注”者保留约束力。吉隆坡要维持自主，须在被审视的红线内腾挪：扩大俄油贸易可以，越界触及受管制技术则代价高昂。战略自主从来不是免费午餐。这也是为何吉隆坡在对俄示好时仍刻意用“经济抓手”而非“战略结盟”来定调——既拿到实利，又避免触碰华盛顿的安全红线，措辞本身便是风险管理的工具。

## 东盟内部分歧：扩员怎么看

马来西亚的进取姿态，在东盟内部并非共识。东盟对BRICS扩员态度分化：印尼、泰国等持开放或参与态度，而越南、菲律宾等对与中俄走得太近更为谨慎。这种分歧折射出成员国地缘处境与历史记忆的差异——曾在南海与中国有摩擦者，天然对“向东看”保持距离。马来西亚敢于高调，部分因其与中美均无直接安全冲突。但集团内步调不一，也削弱了其以“东盟整体”议价的分量。

## 半导体合作能否兑现产业转移

会谈提及的半导体合作方向，是马来西亚最在意的结构性议题。马来西亚坐拥全球封测重镇地位，槟城、吉隆坡一带集聚大量后端产能，正借AI周期承接溢出。与印度探讨半导体协作，潜在逻辑是互补：印度有设计与市场野心，马方有制造与封测生态。但“合作方向”距真实产能转移尚远，受人才、设备管制与产业链成熟度制约。若只停留在谅解备忘录层面，便难以转化为就业与增加值，兑现考验后续执行。

## 国内政治牵制：巫统与希盟的博弈

外交姿态背后是内政天平。安华领导的希盟（Pakatan Harapan）需与巫统（UMNO）共组联合政府，后者代表马来民族主义与既得利益，对外部结盟与少数族裔议题高度敏感。过于高调地贴近中俄，可能在巫统内部激起“背离西方”的反弹，威胁执政稳定；过于审慎，又难满足“全球南方领袖”的自我叙事。外交的回旋余地，实受国会席位与盟友容忍度框定，并非总理一人可定。安华需在国会微弱多数下维系平衡，任何外交过冲都可能被在野阵营转化为执政危机，这让“中间道路”在实操中更偏向谨慎而非进取。

## 多边对冲不是选边，而是购买选项

收束而言，马来西亚的金砖叙事揭示一种中小国生存逻辑：多边对冲的本质不是站队，而是购买选项。特别机制、棕榈油杠杆、半导体意向，都是为不确定时代预置的“保险”。当美中竞争令单边依附风险陡增，手握多个平台的马来西亚反而获得议价弹性。真正的考验不在宣言，而在能否把话语转化为可兑现的合作，同时不被任一方的红线反噬——这恰是“中间道路”最难走的一段。

## 延伸阅读

**金砖国家（BRICS）**：新兴市场与发展中国家合作机制，马来西亚于近年正式成为伙伴国，借多边平台拓宽贸易与外交空间，而不绑定单一阵营。

**中间道路（Middle Path）**：马来西亚官方对BRICS伙伴关系的定位表述，强调在拓宽经贸的同时不依附阵营，是独立以来不结盟外交传统的当代表述。

**马俄特别机制（Special Mechanism）**：安华向普京提议、普京同意设立的双边安排，旨在加速落实直航、免签及既有合作协议，缩短从共识到落地的时延。

# The Middle Path and a Special Mechanism: Malaysia's Multilateral Hedging Within BRICS

On 11 September 2026, on the sidelines of the 18th BRICS Leaders' Summit in New Delhi, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim met Russian President Vladimir Putin and proposed a special Malaysia–Russia mechanism to speed up implementation of bilateral agreements; Putin agreed. Malaysia's communication agency (J-KOM) stressed that the country's BRICS partnership, while widening trade, adheres to a "middle path." During the trip, the two sides agreed to expand palm-oil exports, and Anwar also exchanged views with the Indian premier on trade, defence and semiconductor cooperation. As US–China rivalry sharpens, Kuala Lumpur's hedging posture grows both clearer and more delicate.

## The hedging logic after joining BRICS

Malaysia's recent accession as a BRICS partner is less about taking sides than about widening options. As a trade-dependent, mid-sized economy, Kuala Lumpur has long been swayed by great-power cycles; the US–China contest leaves it squeezed, and multilateral platforms offer buffer and bargaining room. BRICS gives Malaysia an entry point into Global South agendas without binding it to one bloc. Anwar's numerous back-to-back meetings with Russian and Indian leaders in New Delhi turn the "partner" label into concrete economic and diplomatic gains—not ideological alignment.

## The "middle path": a modern reading of non-alignment## The "middle path": a modern reading of non-alignment

The "middle path" is not new; it is the contemporary phrasing of Malaysia's non-aligned diplomacy. Since independence, Kuala Lumpur has navigated between East and West under the Non-Aligned Movement—security ties with the United States, yet economic bonds with the Soviet Union, later Russia, and China. J-KOM's deliberate use of the phrase during the summit signals both home and abroad that joining BRICS is not a tilt toward the Russia–China bloc, but a continuation of "befriend all, attach to none." Repeating the wording is itself a strategic fix.

## Palm oil: the geopolitical meaning of an economic lever

The most substantive item in the Putin talks was expanding palm-oil exports and growing bilateral trade. Malaysian figures show Russia is its ninth-largest trading partner in Europe, with two-way trade of RM8.72bn (about US$2.74bn) last year, on a steady rise. Palm oil is both a pillar export and a lever on Russia: as Western sanctions thin traditional EU agri-channels, Southeast Asian edible oil finds room. Deepening ties through a single bulk commodity is at once business and a geopolitical bargaining chip.

## The cost of strategic autonomy between Washington and Beijing

The "middle path" yields gains and carries costs. The more Malaysia draws close to Russia within BRICS and deepens trade with China, the more it invites Washington's wariness—especially in sensitive areas like semiconductors and defence. The US is unlikely to punish a mid-sized partner outright, yet its CHIPS Act and export-control logic keep constraints on those who "play both sides." To preserve autonomy, Kuala Lumpur must manoeuvre within scrutinised red lines: more Russian oil trade is tolerable, crossing into controlled technology is expensive. Strategic autonomy is never a free lunch. This is why Kuala Lumpur frames its Russia outreach as an "economic lever" rather than a "strategic alliance"—capturing the gain while avoiding Washington's security red lines, with wording itself a risk-management tool.

## ASEAN's divide over BRICS expansion

Malaysia's assertiveness is no consensus within ASEAN. The bloc splits on BRICS enlargement: Indonesia and Thailand take open or participatory stances, while Vietnam and the Philippines stay cautious about getting too close to Russia and China. The split reflects differing geopolitical positions and historical memory—those with South China Sea friction with China naturally keep their distance. Malaysia can afford to be vocal partly because it has no direct security clash with either Washington or Beijing. Yet the lack of a common stride also weakens its weight when bargaining as "ASEAN."

## Can semiconductor cooperation deliver a transfer?

The semiconductor cooperation direction raised in the talks is Malaysia's most structural concern. Malaysia hosts a global test-and-packaging hub, with Penang and Kuala Lumpur clustering back-end capacity that rides the AI cycle to absorb spillovers. Exploring semiconductor collaboration with India makes sense as complementarity: India has design ambition and a market, Malaysia has manufacturing and AT&P ecosystem. But a "direction" is far from real transfer, held back by talent, equipment controls and ecosystem maturity. If it stays at the memorandum level, it will not convert into jobs or value-added—delivery is the test.

## Domestic politics: the UMNO–PH balancing act

Behind the diplomatic posture sits the domestic scale. Anwar's Pakatan Harapan (PH) shares a coalition with UMNO, which represents Malay nationalism and entrenched interests and is sensitive to external alignment and minority issues. Leaning too visibly toward Russia and China could stir "abandoning the West" backlash inside UMNO, threatening the government's stability; being too cautious frustrates the "Global South leader" self-narrative. The room to manoeuvre is framed by parliamentary seats and allies' tolerance, not the premier alone.

## Multilateral hedging buys options, not sides

In sum, Malaysia's BRICS narrative reveals a survival logic for smaller states: multilateral hedging is not about picking camps but about buying options. The special mechanism, the palm-oil lever and the semiconductor intent are all "insurance" pre-positioned for an uncertain age. As US–China competition makes single-sided attachment riskier, holding several platforms gives Malaysia bargaining elasticity. The real test lies not in declarations but in converting words into deliverable cooperation without being bitten by any side's red lines—precisely the hardest stretch of the "middle path."

## Further reading

**BRICS**: A cooperation mechanism of emerging and developing economies; Malaysia joined as a partner in recent years to widen trade and diplomatic space through a multilateral platform without binding to one bloc.

**Middle Path**: Malaysia's official framing of its BRICS partnership, stressing trade expansion without bloc attachment—a contemporary expression of its post-independence non-aligned tradition.

**Malaysia–Russia Special Mechanism**: A bilateral arrangement proposed by Anwar and agreed by Putin to accelerate direct flights, visa-free travel and existing agreements, shortening the gap from consensus to delivery.

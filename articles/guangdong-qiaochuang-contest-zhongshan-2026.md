# 从"捐资捐物"到"带项目落地"：广东侨创赛与侨务引才的常态化

2026年9月10日，2026年广东国际侨界创新创业大赛（人工智能+）颁奖仪式在中山市翠亨新区举行。大赛以3个"揭榜挂帅"项目现场签约、15个参赛优质项目向中山提交落地意向需求的成绩，刷新历届国际侨创赛之最。当"以赛引才"成为广东侨务工作的固定动作，这场赛事背后，是一套将海外华侨华人智力资本导入地方产业的成熟机制。

## 侨创赛：广东"以赛引才"的常态化抓手

广东并非首次举办侨创赛。自赛事创办以来已历六届，而2026年这一届在规模与机制上都有明显升级。据中国侨网报道，本届大赛由广东省侨联、广东粤科金融集团、中山市政府联合主办，中山市委组织部、中山市侨联等承办，首次采用"常规赛+揭榜挂帅"双轨赛制，并首次设置海外分赛区。一头吸引全球侨界项目参赛，一头为本土企业匹配技术解决方案——赛事被设计成一个双向撮合平台，而非单纯的项目评比。

## 从"捐资捐物"到"带项目落地"

华侨华人与祖（籍）国的连接方式，正在发生结构性变化。上世纪改革开放初期，侨力回归以捐资兴学、兴办实业的"资本型"投入为主；而今天，随着新一代侨界专业人士与科技创业者涌现，回归形式转向"智力型"——带技术、带团队、带项目落地。本届赛事自3月31日启动以来，吸引28个国家和地区的742个项目报名，汇聚侨界高层次人才848人，超60%项目配备博士以上核心研发团队，累计拥有各类知识产权近900项。这些数字背后，是华侨华人从"出钱"到"出脑"的角色迁移。

## 中山与大湾区：产业承接的底盘

15个项目选择意向落地中山，并非偶然。中山地处粤港澳大湾区西岸，装备制造、健康医药、光电信息等领域有较长积累；翠亨新区作为省级新区，承载着对接深中通道、承接东岸创新溢出的功能。更关键的是，"揭榜挂帅"机制让企业直接抛出技术需求——本届10家头部企业发布12项技术需求，侨界人才"揭榜攻坚"，把实验室创新变成生产线上的生产力。这种"需求侧驱动"的对接，比泛泛的招商推介更能命中产业痛点。

## 与传统侨乡引资模式的差别

传统侨乡引资，往往依赖宗亲网络、同乡会馆的情感纽带与熟人信任，具有强关系、弱制度的特点。侨创赛则呈现出另一副面貌：它用赛事规则、评审标准、知识产权登记等制度化手段，把散落在全球的侨界科创资源"标准化"地集聚起来。从情感动员到制度撮合，侨务工作的方法论正在专业化。这对潮州、青田、福建等传统侨乡亦有示范意义——引才不必只靠乡谊，也能靠平台。

## "意向"与"落地"之间的落差

然而，"意向落地"不等于"已经落地"。15个项目提交的是落地意向需求，3个"揭榜挂帅"项目完成的是现场签约，二者都只是合作的起点。过往类似赛事中，不乏签约热闹、后续沉寂的案例。项目真正落地，要跨过注册选址、政策兑现、融资到位、团队搬迁等多道门槛。中山市侨联负责人表示要做好大赛"后半篇文章"，这正点出了关键：赛事的价值，最终要看落地转化率，而非仪式上的签约数。

## 政策可持续性与地方竞争

"以赛引才"要可持续，离不开稳定的政策供给。中山市委常委、统战部部长刘美新表示，将拿出最大诚意、最优政策、最好服务。但地方竞争同样激烈——粤港澳大湾区内的深圳、广州，以及江门、汕头等侨乡都在争夺侨界科创资源。赛事带来的关注度是流量，能否转化为留量，取决于持续的营商环境与产业配套，而非一次性补贴。一旦政策退坡或同质化竞争加剧，项目的"意向"可能转向别处。

## 对东南亚华商网络的外溢意义

这场赛事的参赛者来自28个国家和地区，其中相当部分来自东南亚——新加坡、马来西亚、泰国等地的华商与专业人才，正成为技术跨境流动的重要节点。广东以赛事为接口，实质是在编织一张连接东南亚华商网络的创新协作网。对东南亚华人而言，这既是回乡创业的路径，也是借大湾区市场放大自身技术价值的通道。侨务政策由此获得超越"引资"的区域经济整合意涵。

## 收束：侨务政策现代化的普遍命题

侨创赛的意义，不止于15个项目或一场颁奖礼。它折射出一个更大的命题：在人才全球流动的时代，侨务政策正从"联络感情、争取支持"的统战逻辑，转向"配置全球智力、服务创新发展"的治理逻辑。当"以赛引才"在广东常态化，它实际上为其他地区提供了一个样本——如何用市场化、制度化的方式，把离散在外的智力资本重新组织起来。这，或许才是侨务工作现代化的真正注脚。

需要清醒看到，赛事只是引才链条的前端。从签约到量产，中间隔着研发中试、市场拓展与资本衔接的长路，任何一环掉队都会让"意向"止步于纸面。广东的探索能否沉淀为可复制的制度，而非一阵风的政绩工程，仍待时间检验。

## 延伸阅读

**以赛引才（Talent recruitment through competitions）**：以创新创业赛事为抓手，吸引海外高层次人才携项目、团队落地的地方引才模式，近年在中国多地的侨务与科技工作中常态化。

**揭榜挂帅（Open call for solutions）**：一种需求导向的产学研对接机制，由企业发布技术难题（"榜"），科研团队"揭榜"攻关，政府或平台提供撮合与配套支持。

**侨界高层次人才（High-calibre overseas Chinese talent）**：指具备博士及以上学历或突出专业能力的华侨华人科技、管理与创业人才，是当前侨务引才工作的重点对象。

# From "Donations" to "Projects on the Ground": Guangdong's Institutionalised Talent Drive via Startup Competitions

On 10 September 2026, the awards ceremony of the 2026 Guangdong International Overseas Chinese Innovation and Entrepreneurship Competition (AI+) was held at the Cuiheng New District in Zhongshan. The event closed with three "open-call" projects signed on site and 15 quality contest entries submitting letters of intent to settle in Zhongshan—a record across the competition's six editions. As "talent recruitment through competitions" becomes a fixed instrument of Guangdong's diaspora policy, the contest reveals a mature mechanism channelling overseas Chinese intellectual capital into local industry.

## A normalised instrument: the Overseas Chinese Innovation Contest

Guangdong is no stranger to such contests. Six editions have been held, but the 2026 iteration marked a clear upgrade in scale and design. According to ChinaQW, the event was jointly hosted by the Guangdong Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese, the Guangdong Yueke Financial Group and the Zhongshan municipal government, with the Zhongshan Municipal Party Organisation Department and the Zhongshan Returned Overseas Chinese Federation among the organisers. For the first time it adopted a dual-track format—a regular contest plus an "open-call" track—and for the first time set up overseas sub-venues. One end attracts global overseas Chinese projects; the other matches local firms with technical solutions. The contest is designed as a two-way matchmaking platform, not a mere pitch contest.

## From "donations" to "projects on the ground"

The way overseas Chinese connect with the homeland is structurally shifting. In the early reform era, the return of "qiao" resources meant capital—endowing schools, funding enterprises. Today, with a new generation of overseas Chinese professionals and tech founders, the return takes an "intellectual" form: bringing technology, teams and projects to land. Since its 31 March launch, the contest drew 742 projects from 28 countries and regions, gathered 848 high-calibre overseas Chinese talents, saw over 60 per cent of projects field core R&D teams led by PhD holders, and amassed nearly 900 pieces of intellectual property. Behind these figures lies a migration of the overseas Chinese role—from "money" to "mind."

## Zhongshan and the Greater Bay Area: the industrial base

That 15 projects chose Zhongshan as their preferred landing site is no accident. Zhongshan sits on the western shore of the Greater Bay Area, with long accumulations in equipment manufacturing, health and biomedicine, and optoelectronics. The Cuiheng New District, a provincial-level zone, is built to capture innovation spillovers from the eastern shore via the Shenzhen–Zhongshan Bridge. More decisively, the "open-call" mechanism lets firms post needs directly—ten leading enterprises released 12 technical demands this year, and overseas Chinese talent "answered the call," turning lab innovation into production-line productivity. Demand-driven matching hits industrial pain points better than generic investment pitches.

## How it differs from traditional qiaoxiang fundraising

Traditional qiaoxiang (emigrant homeland) fundraising relied on kinship networks and hometown associations—strong ties, weak institutions. The innovation contest looks different: it uses contest rules, evaluation criteria and IP registration to "standardise" the gathering of scattered overseas Chinese science-and-tech resources. From emotional mobilisation to institutional matchmaking, diaspora work is professionalising. This carries demonstrative value for traditional qiaoxiang like Chaozhou, Qingtian and Fujian—talent attraction need not rest only on native-place sentiment; it can also rest on platforms.

## The gap between "intent" and "landing"

Yet "intent to land" is not "landed." The 15 projects submitted letters of intent; the three open-call projects signed on site—both are starting points, not outcomes. Past contests have seen signing fanfare followed by silence. Real landing must clear many hurdles: registration, site selection, policy fulfilment, financing and team relocation. The Zhongshan Overseas Chinese Federation pledged to write the contest's "second half"—precisely the point: a contest's value lies in its conversion rate, not its ceremony signing count.

## Policy sustainability amid local competition

Sustainable "talent-by-contest" needs steady policy supply. Liu Meixin, Zhongshan's standing committee member and head of the United Front Work Department, promised the "utmost sincerity, best policies and best service." But local competition is fierce—Shenzhen, Guangzhou, and qiaoxiang like Jiangmen and Shantou all court overseas Chinese sci-tech resources. The contest brings attention, a flow of visibility; whether it converts to retained projects depends on a durable business environment and a supporting industrial ecosystem, not one-off subsidies. Should policy retreat or homogenised competition intensify, "intent" may drift elsewhere.

## Spillover for Southeast Asian Chinese business networks

Contestants came from 28 countries and regions, a substantial share from Southeast Asia—Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand—where Chinese business and professional communities are increasingly nodes of cross-border technology flow. By using the contest as an interface, Guangdong is in effect weaving an innovation collaboration web linking Southeast Asian Chinese business networks. For Southeast Asian Chinese, this is both a path homeward to entrepreneurship and a channel to amplify their own technological value through the Greater Bay Area market. Diaspora policy thus acquires a regional economic-integration meaning beyond mere investment attraction.

## Coda: the modernisation of diaspora policy

The contest's significance exceeds 15 projects or one awards ceremony. It points to a larger proposition: in an age of globally mobile talent, diaspora policy is shifting from a united-front logic of "maintaining ties and securing support" to a governance logic of "allocating global intellect to serve innovation." As "talent-by-contest" normalises in Guangdong, it offers other regions a template—how to reorganise dispersed intellectual capital through market-based, institutional means. That, perhaps, is the true footnote of modernised diaspora work.

## Further reading

**Talent recruitment through competitions**: A local talent-attraction model that uses innovation and entrepreneurship contests to draw overseas high-calibre talent with projects and teams to settle—now routine in diaspora and science-tech work across many parts of China.

**Open call for solutions (揭榜挂帅)**: A demand-oriented industry–research matchmaking mechanism in which enterprises post technical problems (the "call") and research teams "answer" to tackle them, with government or platforms providing matchmaking and support.

**High-calibre overseas Chinese talent (侨界高层次人才)**: Overseas Chinese science, management and entrepreneurial talent with PhD-level or otherwise outstanding professional capacity, the current focus of diaspora talent work.

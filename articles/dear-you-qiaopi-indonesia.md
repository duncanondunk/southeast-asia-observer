# 当"银信"在雅加达响起：一部潮汕方言片为何牵动印尼华人

8月7日，潮汕方言电影《给阿嬷的情书》（Dear You）在印度尼西亚正式公映。印尼多家华社、商会、侨团与中资企业组织公益包场，华侨华人、留学生与中资员工在熟悉的乡音乡情中，重温祖辈"下南洋（going to Nanyang）"的往事。一部讲述1940年代潮汕人闯荡泰国的片子，为什么能让今天的印尼华人集体动容？答案，藏在"侨批（qiaopi / 银信）"这四个字里。

## 一封迟到数十年的"情书"

《给阿嬷的情书》是导演蓝鸿春（Lan Hongchun）"潮汕三部曲"的收官之作，2026年五一档在中国内地上映，票房突破17亿元人民币，豆瓣开分高达9.0以上，成为近十年口碑最好的国产剧情片之一。影片的故事开始于上世纪四十年代：潮汕（Teochew / Chaoshan）青年郑木生为谋生远赴南洋（泰国），把妻子叶淑柔与三个孩子留在故土。六十年后，孙子晓伟赴泰国寻找传说中"暴富"的阿公，却揭开令人心碎的真相——木生早已意外离世，而阿嬷珍藏半生、视若珍宝的那些"情书"，其实是一位陌生的泰国华人女子谢南枝，以木生之名代笔续写了十八年。影片以世界记忆遗产"侨批"为叙事线索，串联起潮汕与南洋两代人跨越半个多世纪的情感旅程。

## 侨批：连接南洋与故土的生命线

侨批，在潮汕地区多称"银信"，是信款合一的载体——既是家书，也是汇款凭证，是海外游子与故土之间最实在的生命线。2013年，侨批档案被联合国教科文组织（UNESCO）正式列入《世界记忆名录》。它不只是一种经济纽带，更承载着亲情、责任与守护：当年不识字的华人靠同乡代笔写信寄钱，一封封泛黄的银信，是无数家庭跨越山海的全部指望。影片里那句"江海有岸，团圆可盼"，写的正是这种刻进骨子里的牵挂。

## 为什么是印尼？为什么是现在？

印度尼西亚是全球华侨华人人口最多的国家，早期移民多来自闽南、潮汕与客家。从这个意义上说，"下南洋"的记忆并非潮汕独有，而是整个东南亚华人的共同史。影片先在吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）于6月举办特别展映，再于8月落地雅加达，说明"侨批叙事"能够跨越方言与国界，唤起的是整个南洋华人的集体记忆。对印尼华人而言，银信、乡音与红头船，同样是父辈口中耳熟能详的故事。

## 银幕之外的"包场"：侨团在做什么

印尼华社、商会、侨团与中资企业组织公益包场，本质上是一场社区记忆的集体再生产。在熟悉的方言与旋律里，老一辈重温往昔，年轻一代则补上缺席的一课。马来西亚旅游、艺术和文化部副部长在吉隆坡展映致辞时曾说，对年轻一代而言，这部电影"不仅是一部感人的作品，更是一堂生动的人文历史课"。当侨团把整场电影包下来，他们包下的不只是座位，而是一个让族群记忆得以传递的空间。

## 当记忆成为桥梁

这与昨日印尼华裔总会（INTI）换届所揭示的"落地生根"形成了有趣的互文：今天印尼华人早已深植当地社会，但《给阿嬷的情书》证明，共同的"下南洋"记忆，依旧是连接不同世代、连接中国与海外华人社会的柔软纽带。侨批不再只是家书，而成为一种可被正向调用的文化资本——它让华人在保有自身族裔身份的同时，也成为中外人文交流天然的桥梁。

## 延伸阅读

**侨批（qiaopi / 银信）**：19至20世纪海外华侨"信款合一"的家书与汇款凭证，是南洋华人与故土相连的生命线，2013年入选联合国教科文组织《世界记忆名录》。

**下南洋（going to Nanyang）**：近代中国南方民众（尤以闽南、潮汕、客家为主）赴东南亚谋生的历史浪潮，构成了今日东南亚华侨华人的主体来源。

**蓝鸿春（Lan Hongchun）**：中国潮汕籍导演，《给阿嬷的情书》（Dear You）为其"潮汕三部曲"收官之作，影片以真实侨批故事为蓝本，2026年上映后成为现象级华语电影。

# When "silver letters" echo in Jakarta: why a Teochew-dialect film moves Indonesia's Chinese

On 7 August 2026, the Teochew-dialect film Dear You (《给阿嬷的情书》) officially premiered in Indonesia. Multiple Indonesian Chinese associations, chambers of commerce, overseas-Chinese organizations and Chinese-invested enterprises organized charitable group screenings; ethnic Chinese, students and Chinese-company staff revisited the "going to Nanyang" (down-south-sea) past in familiar dialect and hometown sentiment. Why would a film about 1940s Teochew migrants in Thailand move today's Indonesian Chinese to tears? The answer lies in two characters: qiaopi.

## A "love letter" delayed by decades

Dear You is the finale of director Lan Hongchun's "Teochew trilogy," released during the May 2026 holiday slot in mainland China. It grossed over 1.7 billion RMB (about US$240 million) and opened on Douban at above 9.0, becoming one of the best-reviewed domestic dramas in a decade. The story begins in the 1940s: a Teochew (Chaoshan) youth, Zheng Musheng, leaves for Nanyang (Thailand) to make a living, leaving his wife Ye Shurou and three children behind. Sixty years later, his grandson Xiaowei travels to Thailand to find the supposedly wealthy grandfather, only to uncover a heartbreaking truth — Musheng had long since died, and the "love letters" his grandmother treasured for half a lifetime were in fact written for eighteen years by a stranger, a kind Thai-Chinese woman named Xie Nanzhi, in Musheng's name. The film uses qiaopi, a Memory-of-the-World heritage, as its narrative thread, linking two generations across the Teochew homeland and Nanyang in an emotional journey of more than half a century.

## Qiaopi: the lifeline binding Nanyang to the homeland

Qiaopi, known as yinxin or "silver letters" in Teochew areas, were remittance-cum-letter instruments — at once family mail and proof of remittance, the most tangible lifeline between overseas sojourners and their native soil. In 2013, qiaopi archives were inscribed by UNESCO on the Memory of the World Register. They were not merely an economic tie but a carrier of kinship, duty and protection: illiterate migrants relied on fellow villagers to write and send money home, and each yellowing silver letter was a family's sole hope across the seas. The film's line "rivers and seas have shores; reunion can be hoped for" captures precisely that bone-deep longing.

## Why Indonesia, and why now?

Indonesia is home to the world's largest overseas-Chinese population, with early migrants largely from Fujian, Teochew and Hakka communities. In this sense, the memory of "going to Nanyang" is not unique to Teochew but shared across all Southeast Asian Chinese. The film held a special screening in Kuala Lumpur in June before landing in Jakarta in August, showing that the "qiaopi narrative" can cross dialect and border to awaken a pan-Nanyang collective memory. For Indonesian Chinese, silver letters, native dialects and the red-sail boats are equally stories told by their parents and grandparents.

## The "block bookings" beyond the screen: what the associations do

The charitable group screenings organized by Indonesian Chinese associations, chambers and enterprises are, in essence, a collective reproduction of community memory. In familiar dialect and melody, the older generation reminisces while the young catch up on a missed lesson. At the Kuala Lumpur screening, Malaysia's deputy minister of tourism, arts and culture remarked that, for the younger generation, the film is "not only a moving work but a vivid lesson in cultural history." When an association books an entire theater, what it reserves is not merely seats but a space in which ethnic memory can be passed on.

## When memory becomes a bridge

This resonates intriguingly with the "taking root locally" identity revealed in the Indonesian Chinese Association's (INTI) recent leadership transition: today's Indonesian Chinese are deeply rooted in local society, yet Dear You proves that the shared "going to Nanyang" memory remains a soft bond linking generations and connecting Chinese societies in China and abroad. Qiaopi is no longer merely family mail but a cultural capital that can be drawn upon positively — allowing ethnic Chinese to hold their identity while serving as a natural bridge in China–world cultural exchange.

## Further reading

**Qiaopi (银信 / yinxin)**: Remittance-cum-letter instruments of overseas Chinese in the 19th–20th centuries — the lifeline connecting Nanyang migrants to their homeland; inscribed by UNESCO on the Memory of the World Register in 2013.

**Going to Nanyang**: The historical wave of migration from southern China (especially Fujian, Teochew and Hakka communities) to Southeast Asia, forming the main source of today's Southeast Asian Chinese diaspora.

**Lan Hongchun**: A Chinese director from Teochew; Dear You (《给阿嬷的情书》) is the finale of his "Teochew trilogy," based on true qiaopi stories and released in 2026 as a breakout Mandarin-language film.

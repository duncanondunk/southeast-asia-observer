# 「黑金」褪色：印尼煤企在价格反弹时急着找后路

当中东战事把煤价重新推回高位、印尼煤企账面刚刚回血之际，阿达罗（Alamtri）、印多卡（Indika）这些煤炭巨头却在加速转身——奔向铝、金、锂与光伏。据《日经亚洲》报道，从锂、铝到太阳能与煤制气，印尼煤矿企业正把业务的边界越扩越宽。这看似矛盾的画面，其实是一笔再清楚不过的算计：靠天吃饭的「黑金」时代正在落幕，矿主们要在窗口关闭前给自己找好退路。

## 一笔被战争催肥的「横财」

很多人没想到，2026年上半年煤炭企业账面会这么好看。美国与以色列对伊朗开战引发的能源危机，让从多年低位反弹的煤价再度冲高，印尼矿企上半年利润水涨船高。道理不复杂：煤电厂要烧煤，油气一紧，煤电的性价比就回来了，需求与价格同步抬头。

可正是这笔「战争横财」，反而让煤老板们更急着花钱转型。手里有了现金，才能去填多元化那条烧钱的新赛道。一位分析师说得很直白：煤炭生产商终于意识到，得给自己的商业模式上一份「未来的保险」。换句话说，越是赚到钱，越怕这钱是哪天说没就没。

## 从煤炭到铝、金、锂：矿主的赛道切换

转型往哪转？《日经亚洲》点名的几个方向很说明问题。原阿达罗能源、如今改名阿lamtri资源的公司，正准备从加里曼丹北部的冶炼厂发出首批铝锭——从挖煤直接跳到有色金属冶炼。印多卡能源则把筹码押在黄金上，明年初将在南苏拉威西办起第一座金矿。此外还有锂、太阳能，乃至把煤「气化」成更高附加值产品的技术路线。

这背后是印尼资源禀赋的顺势延伸：本来就在矿里，只不过把「挖出来就卖」升级成「挖出来再炼一道」。铝、锂卡的是新能源与储能的脖子，黄金是乱世里的硬通货，光伏则呼应政府新推出的100吉瓦太阳能计划。矿主们的算盘是：用熟悉的资源底座，接住能源转型的下一波需求。

## 镍矿退潮，煤炭的「自留地」也在缩水

但转向并不是凭空起念，而是被逼出来的。长期以来，印尼煤电最快的增长源，是那些吞电惊人的镍冶炼厂——镍用来炼不锈钢、做电池。可镍价因产能过剩一路走低，冶炼厂开工率下滑，其对煤炭的「自带电厂」式需求，预计2026年见顶后转跌。

更扎心的是出口。据克拉普勒（Kpler）数据，截至2026年6月，印尼煤炭出口量同比下滑12.6%；而对最大买家中国的出口，6月单月骤降30%。中国自己煤够烧、又转向买更便宜的高卡煤，印尼那点中低卡煤，正越来越卖不上价。家里需求见顶、外头买家转身，两头一挤，煤企不转身行吗？

## 普拉博沃的算盘：要税收不要产量

矿企急，政府也急，但急的不是一回事。能源与矿产资源部长巴赫利尔明确说：2026年煤炭产量不会比2025年更大，「我们不要过度生产，国家收入才最重要」。截至2026年8月，矿产与煤炭非税国家收入（PNBP）已达108.13万亿印尼盾（约512亿元人民币），靠控产反而护住了财政盘子。

这招「限产保价」是想把国际定价权攥回自己手里。普拉博沃政府还顺着「下游化（downstreaming）」的老思路，推动把低卡煤变成柴油、汽油，以减少燃油进口依赖。一句话：宁可少挖点，也要把煤的价值留在国内、把外汇留在国内。对国家是算大账，对煤企却是被上了紧箍咒——量上不去，只能往「质」和「链」上找补。

## 煤变油：一场昂贵的豪赌

把煤变成油，听起来很美，算起来很贵。煤制油（CTL）本就是出了名的高耗能、高排放、低性价比，历史上少有能在没有补贴时站住脚的先例。印尼押注它，更多是出于能源安全的执念，而非经济理性。

更要命的是，这条路线和「去煤化」的叙事自相矛盾：一边说要转向清洁、要对冲气候压力，一边又给煤炭找新的出场理由。说白了，煤变油是给旧动能续命，不是给新动能铺路。它能否真商业化，连印尼自己都还在「评估技术可行性与合作伙伴」的阶段，远没到落地。

## 去煤化是真转型，还是缓兵之计？

所以问题来了：这场转身，到底是真转型，还是缓兵之计？从好的方面看，铝、锂、光伏确实踩在能源转型的节拍上，印尼也有资源与政策红利，比单纯守着煤田强。从难的方面看，冶炼与新材料要的是巨额资本和技术积累，不是挖煤那套能直接搬过来的；黄金更像避险仓，撑不起一个产业的未来；光伏则卡在电网与消纳。

更深层的是结构性困局：煤炭至今仍占印尼电力六成以上，是最能挣外汇的出口品之一。一个靠煤长大、靠煤发电、靠煤收税的经济体，想「去煤」谈何容易。多元化更像是在旧引擎还转着时，抢先装一台新引擎——装得上装不上、新引擎能不能接替，都还是未知数。

## 中国买家转身的警示

最该让雅加达警醒的，或许是中国买家的转身。中国既是印尼最大煤炭买家，也是其镍的最大买家（占比曾达七成以上）。当中国转向「多烧自己的煤、买更便宜的煤」，印尼煤企失去的不仅是一个客户，而是一个高增长时代的背书。

这对整个东南亚都是隐喻：依赖单一资源、单一买家、单一外部周期的经济，繁荣来得快，去得也快。印尼煤企的多元化，表面是商业自救，底色是对「资源诅咒」的提前防守。能不能成，取决于它能否把资源红利真正变成技术与产业红利，而不是在铝、金、锂之间换来换去，始终停在原料这一端。

## 写在最后：资源型经济的宿命考题

回望这笔账，最讽刺的莫过于：逼着煤企转型的那场中东战事，恰恰是短期内把煤价推高的同一只手。战争给了煤企最后的现金，也给了它们最后的紧迫感。印尼煤老板们的「Plan B」，到底是无奈的防守，还是主动的重生，时间会给出答案。

但对一个资源型经济体而言，真正的考题从来不是「煤价涨不涨」，而是「除了煤，我们还会什么」。这道题，印尼才刚写到第一行。

## 延伸阅读

**下游化（downstreaming）**：印尼自镍矿出口禁令起推行的产业政策，核心是把原料出口转为境内加工增值；煤炭领域表现为煤制甲醇、二甲醚与煤变油。

**非税国家收入（PNBP）**：印尼中央与地方从资源开采中收取的特许权与 royalty 类收入，不计入税基，是国家财政的重要来源，也是巴赫利尔「限产保收入」逻辑的基础。

**自带电厂（captive power plant）**：镍、煤等冶炼企业自备的燃煤电站，不经国家电网；其需求盛衰直接决定印尼煤炭的国内消纳量。

# "Black gold" fades: why Indonesia's coal giants are seeking an exit while prices rebound

Just as the Middle East war has pushed coal prices back up and padded the balance sheets of Indonesia's miners, the giants — Adaro (now Alamtri Resources) and Indika among them — are pivoting fast: into aluminum, gold, lithium and solar. Reporting by Nikkei Asia shows Indonesian coal companies stretching their business lines from lithium and aluminum to solar power and coal gasification. The picture looks contradictory, but the calculus is simple: the era of living off "black gold" is closing, and the miners want an exit plan before the window shuts.

## A windfall fattened by war

Few expected coal books to look this good in the first half of 2026. The energy crisis triggered by the U.S.–Israel war on Iran sent coal prices rebounding from multi-year lows, and Indonesian miners' first-half profits rose with them. The logic is plain: power plants burn coal, and when oil and gas tighten, coal's cost edge returns, lifting both demand and price.

Yet it is precisely this "war windfall" that makes the owners spend faster on transition. Cash in hand is what funds the cash-hungry new lanes. One analyst put it bluntly: coal producers have finally realised they must buy "future-proofing" for their business models. The more they earn today, the more they fear it could vanish tomorrow.

## From coal to aluminum, gold and lithium: miners switch lanes

Where are they going? The directions Nikkei Asia names are telling. Alamtri Resources Indonesia, formerly Adaro Energy, is preparing to ship its first batch of aluminum ingots from a smelter in North Kalimantan — leaping from digging coal straight to non-ferrous smelting. Indika Energy is betting on gold, opening its first mine in South Sulawesi early next year. Beyond that lie lithium, solar, and the route of turning coal into higher-value products through gasification.

This is Indonesia's resource endowment extended naturally: already in the ground, only now "dig and sell" becomes "dig and refine one more step." Aluminum and lithium sit on the throat of the new-energy and storage chain; gold is hard currency in turbulent times; solar answers the government's newly launched 100 GW program. The owners' bet: use the familiar resource base to catch the next wave of transition demand.

## The nickel retreat shrinks coal's captive market

But the pivot is forced, not invented. For years the fastest-growing source of Indonesian coal demand was its power-hungry nickel smelters — nickel for stainless steel and batteries. Yet nickel prices have fallen on overcapacity, utilization has slipped, and the "captive" coal demand from those smelters is expected to peak in 2026 and then decline.

More painful is exports. Data from Kpler shows Indonesian coal exports down 12.6% year on year by volume through June 2026; shipments to its largest buyer, China, fell 30% in June alone. China burns enough of its own coal and is turning to cheaper, higher-calorific imports, leaving Indonesia's mid- and low-grade coal increasingly unsellable. With domestic demand peaking and external buyers turning away, the squeeze leaves miners little choice but to pivot.

## Prabowo's calculus: revenue over volume

The miners are anxious; so is the government — but for different reasons. Energy and Mineral Resources Minister Bahlil Lahadalia has made clear that 2026 coal output will not exceed 2025's, because "we do not want excessive production; what matters is state revenue." By August 2026, mineral and coal non-tax state revenue (PNBP) had already reached Rp 108.13 trillion (about US$7.1 billion); capping volume actually protected the fiscal base.

This "cut volume to defend price" play is meant to claw back international pricing power. The Prabowo administration, following the old downstreaming script, is also pushing to turn low-calorific coal into diesel and gasoline to curb fuel-import dependence. In one line: dig less, but keep coal's value and foreign exchange at home. For the state it is a macro calculation; for miners it is a tightening vice — with volume capped, they must seek compensation in "quality" and "chain."

## Coal-to-fuel: an expensive wager

Turning coal into oil sounds seductive and computes as costly. Coal-to-liquids (CTL) is notoriously energy-intensive, emissions-heavy and poor on economics; few examples have stood without subsidies. Indonesia's bet rests more on energy-security conviction than on commercial reason.

Worse, the route contradicts the "leave coal" narrative: while pledging a clean shift and hedging climate pressure, it hands coal a new reason to stay. Coal-to-fuel prolongs the old engine rather than paving the new one. Whether it can truly commercialise remains, in Jakarta's own words, at the stage of "assessing technical viability and partners" — far from landing.

## Real transition or a stay of execution?

So the question: is this a real transition, or a stay of execution? On the bright side, aluminum, lithium and solar do ride the beat of the energy transition, and Indonesia holds resource and policy dividends that beat merely sitting on coal fields. On the hard side, smelting and new materials demand huge capital and technical accumulation that mining muscle cannot simply transfer; gold looks more like a haven than a future industry; solar is throttled by grid and absorption.

Deeper still is the structural trap: coal still supplies over 60% of Indonesia's power and is among its top foreign-exchange earners. An economy that grew on coal, powers on coal and taxes on coal cannot "leave coal" easily. Diversification resembles fitting a new engine while the old one still runs — whether it fits, and whether the new one can take over, is still unknown.

## The warning in China's turning away

What should jolt Jakarta most may be China's about-face. China is both Indonesia's largest coal buyer and, for nickel, its biggest (once above 70% of volumes). When China shifts to "burn more of its own coal, buy cheaper coal," Indonesian miners lose not just a customer but the endorsement of a high-growth era.

The metaphor travels across Southeast Asia: an economy leaning on a single resource, a single buyer, a single external cycle booms fast and busts fast. Indonesian miners' diversification is, on the surface, commercial self-rescue; at bottom, preemptive defence against the "resource curse." Whether it works hinges on turning resource dividends into real technology and industry — not swapping between aluminum, gold and lithium while forever stuck at the raw-material end.

## The bottom line: a resource economy's existential test

The irony worth keeping: the same Middle East war that forced the pivot is the very hand that, in the short run, lifted coal prices. War gave the miners their last cash and their last urgency. Whether Indonesia's coal barons' "Plan B" is reluctant defence or active rebirth, time will tell.

But for a resource economy, the real exam was never "will coal prices rise" — it is "besides coal, what else can we do." On that question, Indonesia has only just written its first line.

## Further reading

**Downstreaming:** Indonesia's industrial policy launched with the nickel-export ban, centered on converting raw exports into in-country processing value; in coal it appears as coal-to-methanol, DME and coal-to-fuel.

**Non-Tax State Revenue (PNBP):** Indonesia's central and local royalties and concession fees from resource extraction, outside the tax base; a key fiscal source and the basis of Bahlil's "cap volume to protect revenue" logic.

**Captive power plant:** Self-owned coal stations at nickel, coal and other smelters, bypassing the national grid; their rise and fall directly set Indonesia's domestic coal absorption.

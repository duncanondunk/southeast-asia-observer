# 越南国会批准中越铁路追加拨款：老街—河内—海防线再添38亿新元

越南国会8月24日（星期一）批准了一项连接中国铁路项目的调整方案，为此需额外投入30亿美元（约38亿新元，约合216亿元人民币）。据路透社报道，这条铁路将从与中国接壤的越南北部老街省出发，经首都河内，延伸至主要港口城市海防。

## 一条铁路的"加价"与加码

调整方案把铁路正线及支线总长度从原计划的391公里，拉长到426公里；预计总成本也从原先的203万亿越南盾，提高到289万亿越南盾（约111亿美元，约合800亿元人民币）。越南方面去年曾表示，中国政府将为此项目提供贷款。

"加价"背后，是路线与站点的重新设计。铁路将途经富寿省、北宁省、兴安省等拥有多个工业园区的省份——这些地区正是越南制造业最活跃的地带。把轨道直接铺进产业腹地，意味着这条线从一开始就不只是"客运通道"，更是一条为供应链服务的物流动脉。

## 为什么是老街—河内—海防

老街省紧邻中国云南省河口县，是中越边境最成熟的陆路通道之一。从老街南下河内、再东出海防，这条"内陆—首都—出海口"的折线，恰好把中国西南腹地、越南政治中心与北部湾港口串联起来。对越南而言，它既是对外贸易的骨架，也是平衡南北发展、带动内陆省份的抓手。

海防是越南北方最大的港口，也是中越货物的重要集散地。铁路直通海防，意味着云南方向的货物可以更顺畅地"借港出海"，不必完全依赖既有的公路与米轨。

## 三条通道中的"第一棒"

这条线路，是规划中连接越南与中国的三条铁路之一。越南近年明显加快与中国的铁路对接：从老街、同登到芒街，多条标准轨通道被提上日程。此次率先获批调整方案的，正是老街线——它某种程度上成了"第一棒"，为后续通道探路、定调。

列车最高运行时速可达120公里。与全球高铁相比，这一速度不算惊人，但对以货运优先、兼顾沿线的产业走廊而言，足够把"通道"变成"走廊"。

## 连通中国，也连通产业链

更深一层的意义，在于产业链。铁路途经的北宁、兴安等地，聚集了电子、纺织、零部件等大量出口导向型企业，其中不少与中国供应链深度耦合。一条标准轨铁路，意味着零部件、半成品与成品可以在中越之间更准时、更低成本地流转——这正是近年来"中国+越南"制造网络得以扩张的底层逻辑之一。

对区域而言，老街—河内—海防线也是中国—东盟互联互通的一块拼图。当三条中越铁路逐步成形，中南半岛的陆侧物流版图将被重新绘制。越南的目标，是在2030年底前完成建设。

## 延伸阅读

**标准轨铁路（Standard-gauge railway）**：轨距1435毫米的铁路标准，与中国铁路网一致；中越新建对接线多采用标准轨，以便与中国路网直通，区别于越南既有的米轨（1000毫米）。

**老街省（Lao Cai）**：越南西北部边境省份，与云南省河口县隔红河相望，是中越陆路贸易与传统口岸重镇。

**海防市（Hai Phong）**：越南北方最大港口城市，承接河内及红河三角洲的对外贸易，是多条中越通道的东部出海口。

# Vietnam's Parliament Approves Extra Outlay for China-Linked Railway: Lao Cai–Hanoi–Hai Phong Line Gets Another S$3.8 Billion

Vietnam's National Assembly on Monday, 24 August, approved an adjusted plan for a railway linking the country to China, requiring an additional US$3 billion (about S$3.8 billion). Reuters reported the line will run from the northern border province of Lao Cai, adjacent to Hekou county in China's Yunnan province, through the capital Hanoi to the major port city of Hai Phong.

## A line that grew in cost and scale

The revision extends the main line and branches from a planned 391 km to 426 km. Estimated total cost rises from 203 trillion dong to 289 trillion dong (about US$11.1 billion). Vietnam said last year that the Chinese government would provide loans for the project.

Behind the higher price tag lies a redesign of route and stations. The railway will pass through Phu Tho, Bac Ninh and Hung Yen — provinces dense with industrial parks and among Vietnam's most active manufacturing belts. Laying track directly into the industrial heartland means the line was never merely a passenger corridor; from the start it is a logistics artery serving supply chains.

## Why Lao Cai–Hanoi–Hai Phong

Lao Cai borders Hekou county in China's Yunnan. Running south to Hanoi and east to Hai Phong, this "inland–capital–seaport" line knits together China's southwest hinterland, Vietnam's political centre and a Gulf of Tonkin port. For Vietnam it is both a foreign-trade backbone and a lever to balance north–south development and lift inland provinces.

Hai Phong is northern Vietnam's largest port and a key hub for China–Vietnam goods. A railway straight to Hai Phong lets cargo from the Yunnan direction "borrow the port" more smoothly, without depending solely on existing roads and the old metre-gauge line.

## The "first baton" among three corridors

This line is one of three railways planned to connect Vietnam and China. Hanoi has visibly accelerated rail alignment with Beijing: from Lao Cai and Dong Dang to Mong Cai, several standard-gauge corridors are on the agenda. The Lao Cai line, approved first, is in a sense the "first baton" — probing the way and setting the tone for those to follow.

Top speed will reach 120 km/h. That is unremarkable against global high-speed rail, but for a freight-priority corridor serving industry, it is enough to turn a "channel" into a "corridor."

## Linking China, and linking supply chains

The deeper logic is industrial. The provinces the line traverses — Bac Ninh, Hung Yen and others — cluster export-oriented electronics, textiles and parts makers, many deeply coupled with Chinese supply chains. A standard-gauge railway lets components, semi-finished and finished goods move between the two countries more punctually and cheaply — a bedrock of the "China + Vietnam" manufacturing network's recent expansion.

Regionally, Lao Cai–Hanoi–Hai Phong is a piece of the China–ASEAN connectivity puzzle. As the three China–Vietnam railways take shape, mainland Southeast Asia's land-side logistics map will be redrawn. Vietnam aims to complete construction by the end of 2030.

## Further reading

**Standard-gauge railway**: A track with 1,435 mm spacing, matching China's network; new China–Vietnam link lines mostly adopt it for through-running, distinct from Vietnam's legacy metre-gauge (1,000 mm).

**Lao Cai province**: A northwest border province facing Hekou across the Red River, a traditional hub of China–Vietnam land trade.

**Hai Phong city**: Northern Vietnam's largest port, handling Hanoi's and the Red River Delta's foreign trade and serving as the eastern seaport for several China–Vietnam corridors.

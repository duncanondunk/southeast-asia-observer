# 越南拟购法国"阵风"战机，防务转向西方的信号

9月初，多家媒体披露越南正与法国深入谈判采购"阵风"战斗机。若交易落实，将是越南首次引入西方主力战机，也意味着这个长期依赖俄制装备的国家，国防战略迎来一次标志性转向。

## 一则来自巴黎的军购传闻

据澎湃新闻等媒体报道，越南正与法国就采购"阵风"战斗机展开深入谈判。该机型由法国达索公司研制，属双发多用途四代半战机，已出口印度、埃及、印尼等国。

对越南而言，这不仅是一次装备更新，更是身份象征的切换——从"俄式装备用户"向"西方防务伙伴"迈出的试探性一步。尽管谈判细节未公开，其信号意义已足够清晰。

## 从俄制装备到西方主力

越南人民军的空中力量长期建立在苏制、俄制体系之上：苏-27、苏-30系列构成主力，维护、弹药与训练均深度依赖莫斯科。这种路径在冷战后的数十年里，既便宜又省心。

然而俄乌冲突改变了算计。西方制裁使俄式供应链不稳，备件与升级前景不明。越南若想在装备现代化上"不把鸡蛋放在一个篮子"，引入西方机型便成为合乎逻辑的选项。

## "竹子外交"下的平衡术

越南的"竹子外交"——根扎得深、杆长得柔——要求其在大国之间保持灵活平衡。9月2日越南国庆，美国总统特朗普签署贺电、措辞温和，看似礼遇，但分析普遍认为河内不会因此倒向任何一方。

"竹子外交"的本质是中立与务实。采购法国而非美国战机，恰体现了这种精妙：既实现供应多元化，又避免过度刺激中美任一方的敏感神经，把战略主动权留给自己。

## 南海与周边安全的考量

南海是越南国防规划的核心变量。与多个声索方存在岛礁与海域争端，使越南对装备来源单一化天然警惕。分散供应商，意味着在冲突或制裁情境下，不致因某一来源断裂而陷入瘫痪。

与此同时，越南也清楚任何重大军购都会被地区国家解读。如何在强化自身安全与避免军备竞赛螺旋之间拿捏分寸，是河内必须面对的考题。

## 法国的印太雄心

对法国而言，向越南出售"阵风"不只是商业订单，更是其"印太战略"的落子。法国在太平洋与印度洋拥有海外领地，长期视自己为印太利益攸关方，积极深化与东盟防务合作。

巴黎近年频频向东南亚示好：与印尼的"阵风"大单已落地，如今剑指越南。对法国，这是军工出口与地缘影响力的双重收获；对越南，则是多元平衡的又一筹码。

## 地区军备竞赛的隐忧

越南的动作并非孤例。从印尼到菲律宾，东南亚多国近年在战机、潜艇、护卫舰上密集投入。外界担忧，若各国竞相扩军，区域安全将由"对话优先"滑向"威慑优先"。

值得警惕的是，军备竞赛往往自我强化：一方的采购触发另一方的跟进，最终抬高所有人的安全成本。东盟一贯强调以磋商管控分歧，硬件竞赛却可能稀释这一传统优势。

## 中越关系的韧性与边界

中越是山水相连的邻邦，2023年两国关系升级为"全面战略伙伴"，为最高级别合作框架。经贸、互联互通、地方交往持续升温，双边韧性不容低估。

但防务转向西方，无疑为关系增添新变量。中方更看重务实合作与政治互信，对越南多元军购通常持"不针对第三方"的克制表态。短期内，这会令双边关系更复杂，却未必动摇根本。

## 结语：姿态大于实质？

眼下，越南采购"阵风"仍处于谈判阶段，离签约、交付、形成战斗力尚有漫长征程。其当下意义，更多在于姿态与信号：河内正主动重塑防务版图，拒绝被单一依赖绑定。

对观察者而言，关键不在一架战机，而在它折射的战略曲线——一个更挑剔、更多元、也更成熟的越南，正试图在巨人之争中，为自己留出最大回旋空间。

# Vietnam's Reported Rafale Purchase Signals a Western Defence Turn

In early September, multiple outlets reported that Vietnam is in deep negotiations with France to purchase Dassault Rafale fighter jets. If concluded, it would be Hanoi's first introduction of a Western mainstay fighter — and a landmark shift for a military long built on Russian hardware.

## A Defence Rumor from Paris

According to reports including The Paper (Pengpai), Vietnam is in advanced talks with France over Rafale jets. Produced by Dassault Aviation, the twin-engine multirole 4.5-generation fighter has already been exported to India, Egypt and Indonesia.

For Vietnam this is more than an equipment refresh; it is a shift in symbolic identity — a试探 step from "Russian-hardware user" toward "Western defence partner." Though deal details are undisclosed, the signal is already clear.

## From Russian Hardware to Western Fighters

Vietnam's air force has long rested on the Soviet and Russian system: Su-27 and Su-30 variants form the backbone, with maintenance, munitions and training deeply tied to Moscow. For decades this path was both cheap and convenient.

The war in Ukraine changed the calculus. Western sanctions made the Russian supply chain unstable, with uncertain prospects for spares and upgrades. If Hanoi wants modernisation without "all eggs in one basket," introducing Western aircraft becomes a logical option.

## Hedging Under "Bamboo Diplomacy"

Vietnam's "bamboo diplomacy" — deep roots, flexible stems — demands agile balance among great powers. On Vietnam's National Day, 2 September, U.S. President Trump signed a温和 congratulatory message; analysts broadly agree Hanoi will not tilt to either side.

The essence of bamboo diplomacy is neutrality and pragmatism. Buying French rather than American jets reflects this finesse: diversify supply without over-stimulating the sensitivities of Washington or Beijing, keeping strategic initiative in Hanoi's own hands.

## South China Sea and Neighbourhood Security

The South China Sea is the central variable in Vietnam's defence planning. Overlapping claims with several parties make Hanoi naturally wary of single-source dependence. Diversifying suppliers means that, in a conflict or sanctions scenario, it will not be paralysed by the cutoff of one source.

At the same time, Hanoi knows any major arms purchase will be read across the region. Calibrating between strengthening its own security and avoiding an arms-race spiral is a test it must navigate.

## France's Indo-Pacific Ambitions

For France, selling Rafale to Vietnam is not merely a commercial order but a move in its Indo-Pacific strategy. With overseas territories across the Pacific and Indian Oceans, Paris has long seen itself as an Indo-Pacific stakeholder actively deepening ASEAN defence ties.

In recent years Paris has courted Southeast Asia: a major Rafale deal with Indonesia has landed, and now Hanoi is in its sights. For France it is a double win of defence exports and geopolitical influence; for Vietnam, another chip of diversified balance.

## Worries of a Regional Arms Build-up

Vietnam's move is not isolated. From Indonesia to the Philippines, several Southeast Asian states have ramped up spending on jets, submarines and frigates. Observers worry that if states compete in rearmament, regional security may slide from "dialogue first" to "deterrence first."

The danger is self-reinforcement: one purchase triggers another's follow-on, ultimately raising everyone's security cost. ASEAN has long stressed managing differences through consultation; a hardware race may dilute that traditional edge.

## Resilience and Limits in China–Vietnam Ties

China and Vietnam are neighbours linked by mountains and rivers; their relationship was upgraded to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in 2023, the highest-level framework. Trade, connectivity and subnational exchanges keep warming, and the bilateral resilience should not be underestimated.

Yet a Western defence turn adds a new variable. Beijing prioritises pragmatic cooperation and political trust, typically responding to Vietnam's diversified procurement with the restrained framing of "not targeted at any third party." In the short term this complicates ties without necessarily shaking the foundation.

## Gesture Over Substance?

For now, Vietnam's Rafale purchase remains at the negotiation stage; the road to signing, delivery and operational capability is long. Its present meaning lies more in posture and signal: Hanoi is actively reshaping its defence map and refusing to be bound by single-source dependence.

For observers, the key is not one fighter but the strategic curve it reveals — a more discriminating, diversified and mature Vietnam trying to carve maximum manoeuvring room amid the contest of giants.

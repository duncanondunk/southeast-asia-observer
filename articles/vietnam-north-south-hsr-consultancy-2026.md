# 把南北高铁的蓝图交出去：越南4亿美元咨询合同背后的规则之争

据《日经亚洲》报道，越南已把南北高速铁路一份价值10.24万亿越南盾（约3.97亿美元，约合28.6亿元人民币）的咨询合同，交给法国工程咨询企业赛思达（SYSTRA）牵头的联合体。表面看，这只是一份研究合同，不是施工订单；实际上，这是这条全长1541公里、初步投资约673.4亿美元（约4848亿元人民币）的旗舰工程第一次真正落地，也是技术标准开始被写下来的时刻。值得追问的不是谁拿了一标，而是谁将定义未来十年什么叫“兼容”。

## 先于轨道落地的合同

越南建设部下属升龙项目管理委员会公布了南北高铁咨询标段的中标结果。中标联合体由八家企业组成：赛思达牵头，韩方包括韩国国家铁路公司、三宝工程与惟信工程，越方包括交通运输设计咨询公司、南方交通运输设计咨询公司、交通投资与建设咨询公司及黄龙建设咨询公司。中标价略低于该标段约10.78万亿越南盾的预算估值，合同期30个月，签约手续仍在办理。

这里要先给兴奋降温：拿到咨询标不等于拿到铁路。整条线的技术选择、投资结构、融资安排和后续施工采购，都会在可研报告完成后另行决策。据越通社与彭博社的报道，这份合同覆盖勘察、可行性研究、前端工程设计（Front-End Engineering Design）和招标文件编制。也就是说，中标方画的是赛道，不是跑完全程。

## 30个月里究竟要做什么

按业主单位公布的进度，咨询方须在合同生效后第3个月提出适用标准与基础设计要求，第13个月提交中期报告，明确线路走向、车站、桥梁与隧道等控制点和技术方案；第27个月把可研报告送审，第30个月完成审批。勘察采用固定单价，可研、前端设计与招标文件采用总价包干。

这套时间表把一份“研究合同”变成了治理工具：技术路线要在中期报告中初步定型，融资方案要同步论证，征地边界资料要提前交给地方。越南希望把过去十年反复拖延的前期工作压缩成一个可以倒计时的流程。问题是，倒计时能管住承包商，管不住预算和土地。

## 为什么是法国牵头、韩国跟进

这份名单值得逐字读。赛思达曾参与韩国高铁的招标准备与工程管理，这次把韩国国家铁路公司、三宝工程和惟信工程拉进同一联合体；四家越南企业则从可行性研究阶段就与外国专家共同计算、共同设计。韩国国土交通部把它称为韩国企业在海外铁路规划设计领域承接的最大项目，并非虚言。

更值得注意的是上一轮。首轮投标中有三家中国铁路设计企业参与，2026年10月因提案未满足有效性要求，整轮采购被取消；重新开标后，只剩一个联合体投标，中方设计机构未进入最终名单。韩媒把这件事写成“中国出局”，但严格说，出局的是首轮咨询资格，而不是整条铁路的建设权。把一次评审结果读成最终胜负，是舆论跑在了事实前面。

## 写规则的人拿到什么先手

为什么一家咨询公司愿意用30个月做前期工作？因为标准是有专利倾向的。信号系统如何与列车匹配、轨旁设备如何认证、接触网与车辆接口如何测试，这些选择一旦写进招标文件，后来投标的车辆厂商就得证明自己的产品能跑通这套规则；需要改装的车型，要把验证成本算进报价。

这不是阴谋论，而是工程采购的常识。参与前端设计的一方更熟悉自己推荐的做法，知道哪些设备可以直接用、哪些要重新做。越南要的不是免费午餐，而是技术转让与人员培训：2024年国会批准项目方针时，已要求国际招标包写明外方的技术转让和培训承诺。规则先手能否变成订单，取决于这些承诺是否写进合同、能否验收。

## 673亿美元的问号

初步投资约673.4亿美元，是这条铁路最刺眼的数字。可研报告必须回答技术、投资、融资和经济可行性四道题，还要评估通过政府与社会资本合作（Public-Private Partnership）引入国内外私人资本的空间。咨询方现在做的，是把一个政治口号拆成可以核算的现金流。

越南的难题不在于有没有钱修高铁，而在于用什么结构的钱修。主权信用评级改善打开了国际融资的大门，但高铁的票务收入很难独立覆盖资本成本，沿线土地增值与综合开发往往要承担交叉补贴。若融资方案依赖地方财政和国企兜底，所谓“社会资本参与”就可能只剩一个名字。到那时，大股东是谁、风险由谁承担，才是真正的技术问题。

## 时间表已经开始滑动

原定2026年第四季度至2028年第四季度完成可研与开工准备，但项目此前已把开工时点推后一年，建设部最新口径是力争2027年底或2028年开工，2035年基本建成。越南政府今年提出经济增长至少10%的目标，高铁被寄望于拉动投资，但这样体量的项目在前期每拖一个季度，后续融资成本与材料价格都会重算。

从河内玉回站到胡志明市守添站，设计速度350公里，双线标准轨距1435毫米，拟设23座客运站和5座货运站，穿越15个省市。建成后两地通行时间有望从普通铁路约30小时压缩到约5小时。这个愿景足够动人，但“350公里”写进设计文件，和列车真的以350公里跑起来，中间隔着信号、调度、维保和客流四道门槛。

## 征地这道题，咨询方答不了

2026年11月21日，越南国会修改土地法，为重点战略项目加快征收程序打开通道。征地拆迁被拆成独立项目，由沿线各省市人民委员会决定投资，目标在2028年第四季度前完成。法律把程序缩短了，却没有把补偿谈成。

南北高铁要穿过15个省市，数千户家庭可能因走廊清障搬迁。设计方被要求减少对居民生活和地方规划的干扰，但选线本身就是取舍：绕开密集城区会抬高造价和里程，穿城而过则推高拆迁成本与社会阻力。咨询公司能画出最优线路，画不出一张让所有人都满意的补偿表。土地问题的答案在地方，不在办公室。

## 中企与华商：出局的是一轮，不是整场

首轮咨询评审把三家中国设计机构挡在门外，这对中国企业是一次警醒：越南市场的门槛正在从“报价低、工期短”转向标准、合规与文件质量。但主体施工、轨道、桥梁隧道、动车组、信号通信、牵引供电和运营维护都还没有发包。法国与韩国企业拿下前期咨询，不等于锁定了整条铁路。

对越南华商而言，真正的影响在更日常的地方：建材、物流、劳务、车站周边商业与设备供应，都可能随着线路推进出现新订单。华商的角色是本地承包商和供应商，不是任何国家的代理人。把侨商网络一律读成“影响政策的抓手”，既低估了他们的市场能力，也容易把正常的商业竞争政治化。反过来，中资企业若想重回牌桌，靠的也应当是可验证的技术转让记录和本地培训方案。

## 决定成色的三个问题

第一，中期报告里的技术标准是否开放。若招标文件把特定厂商的接口写死，越南买到的将是一套昂贵且难以更换的系统；若标准保持中立，后续竞争才可能压低价格。第二，融资方案是否经得起压力测试。客流低于预期、利率上行、土地增值不及预期，任何一项失守都会把财政责任推回政府。第三，技术转让与培训能否验收。越南明确希望本地人员能管理、运营和维护这条线，而不是通车后每逢故障都等供货商派人。

这三个问题都不会在中标新闻里出现答案。它们会出现在30个月后的可研报告里，出现在随后几年的招标文件里，也会出现在沿线每一场征地说明会上。一份4亿美元的合同买不到一条高铁，但足以决定这条高铁由谁来定义。越南现在做的，是先把笔握在自己能监督的手里——至于写出来的方程式能不能兑现成轨道，才是真正的考试。

## 延伸阅读

**高铁技术标准（High-speed rail technical standards）**：轨距、信号、接触网与车辆接口等技术规则，一旦写入招标文件，会长期影响后续设备采购与供应商资格。

**前端工程设计（Front-End Engineering Design）**：在正式施工前确定技术方案、投资估算与招标文件的关键阶段，设计方往往对后续采购具有实质影响。

**政府与社会资本合作（Public-Private Partnership）**：政府与企业共担投资与运营风险的融资模式，在客流收入不足覆盖成本的基础设施项目中尤为常见。

# Vietnam Hands the Blueprint of Its North–South Railway to a French-Led Consortium

Nikkei Asia reports that Vietnam has awarded a consultancy package worth VND10.24 trillion (about US$397 million) for its planned North–South high-speed railway to a consortium led by French engineering firm SYSTRA. On paper this is a study contract, not a construction order. In practice, it is the first real step on a 1,541-km flagship project with a preliminary price tag of US$67.34 billion — and the moment its technical rules begin to be written. The interesting question is not who won a contract, but who gets to define what "compatible" means for the next decade.

## The contract that precedes the tracks

The Thang Long Project Management Board under Vietnam's Ministry of Construction announced the selection. The winning consortium has eight members: SYSTRA as leader, Korean partners Korea National Railway, Sambo Engineering and Yooshin Engineering, and four Vietnamese firms — TEDI, TEDI South, TRICC and Hoang Long Construction Consulting. The bid came in slightly below the package's estimated value of about VND10.78 trillion, and the 30-month contract is still being finalised.

Time for a reality check: winning a consultancy package is not winning a railway. Technology choices, the investment structure, financing and subsequent construction procurement will all be decided after the feasibility study. According to Vietnam News Agency and Bloomberg reports, the contract covers surveys, the feasibility study, front-end engineering design and tender documents. The consortium is drawing the course, not running the race.

## What the 30-month brief actually covers

Under the timetable published by the project owner, the consultant must propose applicable standards and basic design requirements by month three, deliver an interim report by month thirteen spelling out the alignment and key control points — stations, bridges, tunnels and technical options — submit the feasibility report for review by month twenty-seven, and obtain approval by month thirty. Surveys are paid at fixed unit rates; the study, design and tender documents are lump-sum work.

This schedule turns a "research contract" into a governance tool: the technical route is broadly fixed in the interim report, financing has to be argued in parallel, and clearance boundaries must be handed to local authorities early. Vietnam wants to compress a decade of repeated delays into a countdown. A countdown can discipline a contractor. It cannot discipline a budget or a cadastral map.

## Why the French-led lineup matters

Read the names carefully. SYSTRA has previously worked on preparing tenders and managing construction for Korea's high-speed rail, and now brings Korea National Railway, Sambo and Yooshin into the same consortium. Four Vietnamese firms will calculate and design alongside foreign experts from the feasibility stage. South Korea's land ministry calling this the largest overseas rail planning and design contract won by Korean companies is not empty boast.

The previous round is more revealing. Three Chinese railway design firms took part in the first tender; in October 2026 the whole round was cancelled because bids failed validity requirements. When the tender reopened, only one consortium bid, and the Chinese design institutes were absent from the final lineup. Korean media framed this as "China's exit". Strictly, what exited was a first-round consultancy eligibility, not the right to build the railway. Reading one evaluation as a final verdict puts rhetoric ahead of facts.

## What the rule-writers gain

Why would an engineering firm spend 30 months on preparatory work? Because standards carry preferences. How signalling talks to trains, how wayside equipment is certified, how catenary and rolling stock interfaces are tested — once these choices are written into tender documents, a future train supplier must prove its product works within that framework. Models needing modification must price in validation.

This is not conspiracy theory; it is procurement common sense. A party involved in front-end design knows which of its own solutions fit and which need rebuilding. Vietnam is not asking for a free lunch: when it approved the project in 2024, the National Assembly required international tender packages to include commitments on technology transfer and personnel training. Whether that first-mover advantage converts into orders depends on those commitments being written into contracts and verified.

## The US$67 billion question mark

US$67.34 billion is the most startling number in the project. The feasibility study must answer four questions — technology, investment structure, financing and economic viability — and assess how much domestic and foreign private capital could be mobilised through public-private partnerships. The consultant's job is to turn a political slogan into cash flows that can be audited.

Vietnam's problem is not whether the country can afford high-speed rail, but what structure of money builds it. An improved sovereign rating opens external financing, yet ticket revenue alone is unlikely to cover capital costs; land-value capture and associated development usually have to cross-subsidise the line. If the financing plan leans on local budgets and state-owned guarantees, "private participation" becomes a label. At that point the real technical question is who holds the equity and who absorbs the risk.

## The calendar is already slipping

Feasibility work, appraisal and construction preparation were scheduled for the fourth quarter of 2026 through the fourth quarter of 2028, but the start date had already been pushed back by a year; the latest ministry position aims to break ground in late 2027 or 2028, with the line largely complete by 2035. The government has set a growth target of at least 10 per cent this year and expects the railway to support investment. Every quarter of delay at this scale reprices financing and materials.

From Ngoc Hoi station in Hanoi to Thu Thiem in Ho Chi Minh City, the design calls for double standard-gauge track of 1,435 mm, a top speed of 350 kph, 23 passenger stations and five freight stations across 15 provinces and cities. Travel time could fall from roughly 30 hours by conventional train to around five. The vision is compelling, but 350 kph on paper and 350 kph in service are separated by signalling, dispatching, maintenance and demand.

## The land question no consultant can design away

On 21 November 2026 Vietnam's National Assembly amended land law to accelerate expropriation for designated strategic projects. Clearance is split into separate projects decided by provincial and municipal people's committees, with completion targeted for the fourth quarter of 2028. Legislation can shorten procedures; it cannot negotiate compensation.

The corridor crosses 15 provinces and cities, and thousands of households may be relocated. Consultants are instructed to limit disruption to local plans and daily life, but alignment is a trade-off: skirting dense urban areas raises cost and distance, while crossing them raises demolition bills and resistance. A consultant can draw the optimal line. It cannot draw a compensation table everyone accepts. The answer sits in local offices, not design studios.

## Chinese firms and Huaqiao businesses: one round lost, not the whole race

The exclusion of three Chinese design institutes in the first evaluation is a warning: the threshold in Vietnam is shifting from low price and fast delivery toward standards, compliance and document quality. But construction, track, bridges and tunnels, trains, signalling, power supply and operations have yet to be tendered. A French-led consultancy does not equal a French-led railway.

For Vietnamese Chinese business communities, the impact is more ordinary: building materials, logistics, labour supply, station-area retail and equipment may all generate orders as the project advances. Their role is that of local contractors and suppliers, not agents of any state. Reading diaspora networks automatically as instruments of influence both understates their market capacity and politicises ordinary competition. If Chinese firms want back at the table, the credible currency is verifiable transfer records and local training plans.

## Three questions that will decide the real value

First, how open are the technical standards in the interim report? If tender documents hard-wire a particular vendor's interfaces, Vietnam buys an expensive system it cannot easily replace; neutral standards keep later competition real. Second, does the financing survive stress tests? Lower-than-expected ridership, higher rates or weaker land-value gains would each push fiscal risk back onto the state. Third, can technology transfer and training be verified? Vietnam wants local staff to manage, operate and maintain the line, not to wait for a supplier's engineer after every fault.

None of these answers appears in the award announcement. They will surface in the feasibility report 30 months from now, in tender documents over the following years and in every compensation meeting along the route. A US$397 million contract cannot buy a high-speed railway, but it can decide who defines it. Vietnam is keeping the pen where it believes it can be supervised. Whether the equation becomes rails is the real examination.

## Further reading

**High-speed rail technical standards**: Gauge, signalling, catenary and rolling-stock interface rules which, once embedded in tender documents, shape procurement and supplier eligibility for decades.

**Front-End Engineering Design**: The pre-construction stage that fixes technical options, cost estimates and tender documents; designers often exert substantial influence over later procurement.

**Public-Private Partnership**: A financing model in which government and private partners share investment and operating risk, especially relevant where ticket revenue cannot cover infrastructure costs.

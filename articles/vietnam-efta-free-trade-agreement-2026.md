# 十四年磨一剑：越南与欧洲自由贸易联盟签署自贸协定

10月18日，越南在河内与欧洲自由贸易联盟签署自由贸易协定。这场谈判从2012年启动，到2018年陷入停滞，再到去年因美国关税引发全球贸易紧张而重启，并于今年7月完成，前后历时14年。签字仪式由越共中央总书记、国家主席苏林见证，越南工贸部长黎孟雄与瑞士、列支敦士登、冰岛、挪威四国代表共同落笔。欧洲自由贸易联盟（EFTA）由瑞士、列支敦士登、冰岛和挪威四个国家组成，虽不及欧盟庞大，却坐拥一批全球创新与高端制造强国。

## 数字：48亿欧元与2.5亿欧元的顺差

协定本身并不复杂：生效后，欧洲自由贸易联盟成员国对越南原产的工业品和海产维持零关税；越南则在11年内逐步降低或取消对联盟大部分同类产品的关税，奶酪、咖啡、巧克力等部分农产品最长有16年过渡期。挪威方面称，协定生效时其对越出口约一半即享零关税，七年内零关税比例将超过98%。

体量上，2025年双方贸易额为48亿欧元（约69亿新元，约合370亿元人民币），越南享有25亿欧元顺差，较十年前的5亿欧元大幅扩张。瑞士在越南已有234个投资项目、注册资本超过24亿美元（约173亿元人民币）；挪威亦有62个项目、规模逾2.01亿美元（约14.5亿元人民币）。这些数字不算惊人，却透出一条主线：越南与欧洲高端经济体的互补性正在加深。

## 时机：特朗普关税下的“战略自主”

为什么偏偏是现在？答案写在华盛顿。美国总统特朗普的系列关税措施，让越南对美出口承受高关税，双方至今仍在谈判。专家指出，在美国对欧洲国家贸易施压日增之际，这份协定能帮助双方把经济一体化转化为更大的战略自主空间，发展替代性的商业关系，而非完全依赖最大的贸易伙伴。对河内而言，道理同样直白：鸡蛋不能放在一个篮子里。当头号市场随时可能加税，多签几份自贸协定就是最现实的风险对冲。

## 不只是关税：技术、投资与价值链爬坡

若只盯着关税表，便小看了这份协定。欧洲自由贸易联盟成员国多是创新强国——瑞士的制药与精密制造、挪威的能源与海事、冰岛的地热与生物科技、列支敦士登的金融与高端元件。协定涵盖服务贸易、投资、知识产权、政府采购与可持续发展，并向农业、采矿、制造业等指定领域的外国投资者给予国民待遇。这正是越南梦寐以求的：用市场换技术、换管理、换价值链上游的位置。越南长期以纺织品、鞋类、电子组装见长，要往上爬，正需要瑞士药企、挪威能源公司的技术与资本注入。

## 竹子外交的经济面：对冲而非选边

越南的“竹子外交”世人并不陌生——根固而枝柔，在大国之间灵活周旋。这份协定让这套外交有了清晰的经济注脚。河内并不打算在中美之间二选一，而是用一张越来越密的自贸网络，把自身嵌进多个经济区，既对冲美国关税风险，也避免过度依赖任何单一伙伴。此前越南已与欧盟签署自贸协定，如今再添欧洲自由贸易联盟，加上《全面与进步跨太平洋伙伴关系协定》（CPTPP）与《区域全面经济伙伴关系协定》（RCEP），越南的自贸网络在亚洲堪称最密之一。

## 中国的视角：谨慎欢迎的区域变局

对中国读者而言，这纸协定值得两面看。一方面，越南深化与欧洲经济体的融合，客观上削弱了对单一市场的依赖，其中也包含对中国的再平衡——河内乐见供应链“中国+1”的叙事，却不愿被绑死。另一方面，中国本身也在与欧洲自由贸易联盟谈判自贸协定，越南的抢先落子未尝不是一种参照。更现实的是，大量中国企业在越南设厂，借道当地原产地规则出口欧美，这份协定生效后，合规的“越南制造”将更顺畅地进入瑞士、挪威等市场。区域供应链的重构，从来不是零和游戏。

## 延伸阅读

**欧洲自由贸易联盟（EFTA）**：由瑞士、列支敦士登、冰岛、挪威组成的政府间组织，专注推动自由贸易与经济一体化，与欧盟并列为欧洲两套经济一体化机制。

**竹子外交（Bamboo Diplomacy）**：越南奉行的灵活平衡外交理念，喻指根基稳固、枝条柔韧，在大国竞争中保持战略自主、不选边站队。

**中国+1（China Plus One）**：跨国企业将部分产能从中国分散至东南亚等地的供应链策略，越南是主要承接国之一。

# Fourteen Years in the Making: Vietnam Signs an FTA with the European Free Trade Association

On 18 October, Vietnam signed a free trade agreement (FTA) with the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) in Hanoi. The talks began in 2012, stalled from 2018, were revived last year as US tariffs triggered global trade tensions, and concluded this July — fourteen years in all. The ceremony was witnessed by Tô Lâm, General Secretary of the Communist Party of Vietnam and the country's president; Vietnam's Minister of Industry and Trade Lê Mạnh Hùng signed alongside representatives of Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Iceland and Norway. EFTA groups four countries — Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Iceland and Norway — smaller than the EU, yet home to several of the world's leaders in innovation and high-end manufacturing.

## The numbers: €4.8 billion and a widening surplus

The mechanics are straightforward. Once the agreement enters into force, EFTA members will maintain zero tariffs on Vietnamese industrial and seafood products, while Vietnam will phase down or eliminate tariffs on most EFTA goods within eleven years; selected farm products such as cheese, coffee and chocolate get a transition period of up to sixteen years. Norway says about half its exports to Vietnam will be tariff-free at entry, rising to over 98 per cent within seven years.

In scale, two-way trade reached €4.8 billion (~US$5.2 billion) in 2025, with Vietnam running a €2.5 billion surplus — up sharply from €0.5 billion a decade earlier. Switzerland has 234 investment projects in Vietnam with registered capital above US$2.4 billion; Norway counts 62 projects worth more than US$201 million. The figures are not spectacular, but they reveal a clear thread: Vietnam's complementarity with Europe's advanced economies is deepening.

## Timing: "strategic autonomy" amid Trump's tariffs

Why now? The answer lies in Washington. US President Donald Trump's tariff measures have left Vietnamese exports to America facing steep duties, and the two sides are still negotiating. As one expert noted, with US trade pressure on European countries rising, the pact helps both sides convert economic integration into greater strategic autonomy and develop alternative commercial relationships rather than relying entirely on the largest trade partner. For Hanoi the logic is plain: do not put all eggs in one basket. When the top market can impose tariffs at will, signing more FTAs is the most pragmatic hedge.

## More than tariffs: technology, investment and value-chain climbing

Read only the tariff schedule and you miss the point. EFTA members are innovation powerhouses — Swiss pharmaceuticals and precision manufacturing, Norwegian energy and maritime expertise, Icelandic geothermal and biotech, Liechtenstein finance and high-end components. The agreement covers services, investment, intellectual property, government procurement and sustainable development, and grants national treatment to foreign investors in designated sectors such as agriculture, mining and manufacturing. This is precisely what Hanoi wants: trading market access for technology, management know-how and a higher rung on the value chain. Long defined by textiles, footwear and electronics assembly, Vietnam needs the capital and expertise of Swiss drugmakers and Norwegian energy firms to climb.

## The economic face of bamboo diplomacy: hedging, not choosing sides

Vietnam's "bamboo diplomacy" is well known — deep roots, supple branches, weaving between great powers. This agreement gives that doctrine a clear economic coda. Hanoi does not intend to choose between Washington and Beijing; instead it uses an ever-denser FTA network to embed itself in multiple economies, hedging US tariff risk while avoiding over-reliance on any single partner. Having already concluded an FTA with the EU, Vietnam now adds EFTA on top of the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP) and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) — leaving it with one of the densest FTA networks in Asia.

## A Chinese perspective: a regional shift to welcome with caution

For Chinese readers, the pact cuts two ways. On one hand, Vietnam's deeper integration with European economies objectively reduces dependence on any single market — including, implicitly, on China; Hanoi welcomes the "China Plus One" supply-chain narrative yet resists being locked in. On the other, China itself is negotiating an FTA with EFTA, and Vietnam's early move is a useful reference point. More practically, many Chinese firms manufacture in Vietnam and export to Europe and America via local rules of origin; once this agreement takes effect, compliant "Made in Vietnam" goods will flow more smoothly into Swiss, Norwegian and other markets. The reordering of regional supply chains is rarely a zero-sum game.

## Further reading

**European Free Trade Association (EFTA):** An intergovernmental body of Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Iceland and Norway that promotes free trade and economic integration — distinct from, yet parallel to, the European Union as a mechanism of European economic integration.

**Bamboo Diplomacy:** Vietnam's flexible balancing foreign-policy doctrine, likened to a plant with firm roots and supple branches that maintains strategic autonomy among great powers without taking sides.

**China Plus One:** A supply-chain strategy in which multinationals shift part of their production from China to Southeast Asia and elsewhere; Vietnam is among its principal beneficiaries.

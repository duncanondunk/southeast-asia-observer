# 印尼"油荒"蔓延巴厘岛：补贴漏斗与群岛物流的脆弱切面

10月中旬，印尼旅游胜地巴厘岛的加油站外排起数小时的长队，部分站点贴出"无油可加"的告示。这场起初被官方解释为"配送延误"的燃油紧张，很快蔓延至全国至少11个省份。据《日经亚洲》（Nikkei Asia）报道，短缺已扩散到巴厘岛及周边地区，西苏门答腊加油要等两三小时，西巴布亚深夜排队车辆超过一公里。表面看是油轮锚具故障与海况不佳，深挖下去却是一张由油价冲击、进口来源萎缩、补贴套利与群岛物流共同织成的脆弱网。

## 一场"油荒"撕开了什么

印尼并非缺油的国家。它是东南亚最大的产油国之一，却也是成品油净进口国——炼厂老化、产能不足，国内需求高度依赖进口。据能源与矿产资源部数据，全国燃油库存仍可支撑18至20天，巴厘岛的紧张"主要源于配送环节受阻"。这话只说对了一半。真正把安全库存压到临界线的，是国际油价暴涨下，民众从非补贴高价油"集体倒灌"进固定价补贴油的选择。

## 表象：配送延误，还是结构性短缺

官方叙事把锅甩给了一艘油轮的锚具故障：卸载延误约15.5小时，加上海况不佳，巴厘岛配额骤降三成以上。听起来像偶发事故。但同一时间，西苏门答腊、西巴布亚、亚齐、廖内、西加里曼丹、南苏拉威西等省份同时出现柴油或汽油断供。11个省的监察专员公署已介入调查。一个锚具故障，解释不了一整个群岛的失灵。更合理的解释是：需求端在变，供应端却没跟上。

## 补贴漏斗：被套利掏空的"国民福利"

问题的内核是补贴差价。2026年以来，非补贴燃油价格随国际油价一路攀升，而政府为稳住购买力，承诺年底前绝不上调补贴燃油（Pertalite，90号研究法辛烷值汽油，与生物柴油BioSolar）的零售价。结果可想而知：私家车主和物流车队几乎一夜之间全部转向疯抢补贴油，把国家库存天数拉到16至18天的临界线以下。更糟的是套利——有人用大罐车成批买走补贴油，再以工业价转售；巴厘岛警方已逮捕多名以每1.5升10万印尼盾（约40元人民币）高价倒卖的贩子。所谓"国民福利"，正从漏斗里悄悄漏走。

## 群岛之痛：巴厘岛为何最脆弱

巴厘岛本身就是一座孤岛，没有炼油能力，完全靠从东爪哇储油终端跨海轮渡、再用陆路槽车转运。运输船一延误，岛上配额立刻告急。这种"岛屿依赖症"在印尼这样的万岛之国被无限放大：任何一环的海上物流卡顿，都会直接传导为民生断点。10月17日起，巴厘省不得不按车牌单双号限加油，并试点用塑料桶人工配送。一个旅游岛要靠"限量加人工背油"来维持运转，本身就说明物流冗余之薄。

## 旅游黄金鹅被自己绊了一跤

巴厘岛是印尼旅游外汇的顶梁柱。2026年上半年，巴厘接待约500万外国游客，贡献约107亿美元（约770亿元人民币）旅游相关外汇，占印尼全国旅游外汇收入的55%。燃油一断，游客出行受阻、部分酒店订单被取消、有人提前三天退房。当地旅行社改道近处景点，机场接送告急。把"黄金鹅"的饲料管道自己掐断，是这场危机里最讽刺的一幕——能源一旦卡顿，最先流血的往往是赖以创汇的服务业。

## 财政红线前的两难

补贴的钱不是从天上掉的。截至8月底，印尼补贴与补偿实际支出已达331.4万亿印尼盾（约1326亿元人民币），同比增长52.1%，眼看要撞上法定的3%财政赤字红线。副财长朱达·阿贡（Juda Agung）明确承诺不上调补贴油价、不扩大赤字，只说要"更精准地定向"补贴。可精准谈何容易？政府正准备把Pertalite从最富裕的顶层一成家庭中剥离。一边是油价冲击推高进口账单，一边是低价承诺锁死售价，财政腾挪的空间正被两头夹死。

## 能源主权旁落的隐喻

更深的隐忧在进口端。自中东冲突爆发以来，印尼失去了约25%的燃油进口来源；9月印尼原油价格（ICP）飙至每桶112.81美元（约813元人民币），布伦特也站上102.02美元（约734元人民币）。以往依赖全球公开招标的采购机制，如今因跨国油商投标意愿低而大面积流标，政府被迫派人直接去尼日利亚、安哥拉、美国、澳大利亚等库存盈余国"溢价扫货"。一个地区大国，能源安全却要仰仗别人家多余的库存，这本身就是能源主权被稀释的隐喻。

## 结论：应急之后，改革不能绕行

好消息是，巴厘岛的排队已随增派油轮和限购措施逐步缓解，政府誓言一周内恢复供应。但这场"油荒"留下的，远不止几条长队。它把印尼能源结构的老毛病一次性摊开：炼厂老化、进口依赖、补贴渗漏、群岛物流脆弱。应急可以靠增派油轮、靠单双号限行、靠警察抓投机来顶过去；可若不在油价回落窗口里推进炼厂升级、补贴精准化与能源多元化，下一次"锚具故障"，仍会演变成全国性的民生警报。

## 延伸阅读

**印尼原油价格（ICP）**：印尼政府每月公布的国产原油参考价，是国际油价与国内成品油定价的枢纽指标。
**Pertalite**：印尼国家石油公司（Pertamina）旗下的90号研究法辛烷值补贴汽油，售价被政府锁定，是本次套利与抢购的核心标的。
**东盟能源安全（ASEAN energy security）**：东南亚国家普遍面临的能源自给与进口依赖张力，印尼"油荒"是区域能源脆弱性的一个缩影。

# Indonesia's Fuel Crunch Spreads to Bali: The Subsidy Funnel and an Archipelago's Fragile Logistics

In mid-October, motorists on Indonesia's resort island of Bali queued for hours outside petrol stations, some of which posted signs saying simply "no fuel." What officials first described as a "distribution delay" soon spread to at least eleven provinces. Nikkei Asia reported that the shortage had reached Bali and surrounding areas, with waits of two to three hours in West Sumatra and queues longer than a kilometre stretching through the night in West Papua. On the surface it looked like a tanker's anchor failure and rough seas. Dig deeper and a far more tangled web appears — one woven from an oil-price shock, shrinking import sources, subsidy arbitrage, and the fragile logistics of an island nation.

## What the fuel crunch has exposed

Indonesia is not a country short of crude. It is among Southeast Asia's largest oil producers, yet it is also a net importer of refined products: its refineries are ageing and under-capacity, and domestic demand leans heavily on imports. The Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources said national fuel stocks still covered 18 to 20 days of demand and that Bali's tension was "mainly due to distribution bottlenecks." That is half the story. What pushed safety stocks to the critical line was a mass shift by consumers from unsubsidised, higher-priced fuel to fixed-price subsidised fuel as world oil prices spiked.

## The surface story: logistics, or structural scarcity

The official narrative pinned the blame on a single tanker's anchor gear: unloading was delayed by roughly 15.5 hours, compounded by poor sea conditions, and Bali's allocation dropped by more than 30 percent. It sounds like an isolated accident. Yet in the same window, West Sumatra, West Papua, Aceh, Riau, West Kalimantan and South Sulawesi all reported diesel or petrol outages. The Ombudsman has opened investigations across eleven provinces. One faulty anchor cannot explain the failure of an entire archipelago. The more plausible reading is that demand changed while supply simply failed to keep pace.

## The subsidy funnel: a welfare scheme leaking through arbitrage

At the core sits the subsidy gap. Since the start of 2026, unsubsidised fuel prices have climbed with world oil, while the government — to protect purchasing power — promised not to raise the retail price of subsidised fuel (Pertalite, a 90-RON petrol, and BioSolar diesel) before year-end. The result was predictable: private car owners and logistics fleets almost overnight stampeded into subsidised fuel, dragging national stock cover below the 16-to-18-day danger threshold. Worse, arbitrage drained the scheme. Hoarders used large tank trucks to buy up Pertalite and resell it at industrial prices; police in Bali have arrested vendors reselling at 100,000 rupiah (about US$5.50) for 1.5 litres. A "national welfare" programme was quietly leaking out of the funnel.

## The archipelago's pain: why Bali is most exposed

Bali itself is an island with no refining capacity, wholly dependent on cross-sea ferries from East Java storage terminals followed by road tankers. When a vessel was delayed, the island's allocation collapsed overnight. This "island dependency" is magnified across an archipelagic state of thousands of islands: any snag in maritime logistics transmits directly into a livelihood cutoff. From October 17, Bali resorted to odd-even licence-plate rationing and even trial manual distribution by jerrycan. That a tourist island must survive on rationing plus "fuel carried by hand" says everything about how thin the logistics redundancy really is.

## The golden goose stumbles on itself

Bali is the pillar of Indonesia's tourism foreign exchange. In the first half of 2026 the island welcomed roughly five million foreign visitors and generated about US$10.7 billion in tourism-related foreign-exchange earnings, around 55 percent of the national total. Cut the fuel and the tourists stall: cancelled hotel bookings, guests checking out three days early, travel agencies rerouting to nearer sights, airport transfers in disarray. Throttling the feed pipe of your own golden goose is the most ironic scene in this crisis — once energy stutters, the first to bleed is usually the services sector that earns the foreign currency.

## A dilemma at the fiscal red line

The subsidy bill does not fall from the sky. By the end of August, actual spending on subsidies and compensation had reached 331.4 trillion rupiah (about US$18.4 billion), up 52.1 percent year on year, brushing against the statutory 3 percent fiscal-deficit ceiling. Deputy Finance Minister Juda Agung explicitly promised no raise in subsidised fuel prices and no wider deficit, only to "target subsidies more precisely." Precision is easier said than done. The government is preparing to strip Pertalite from the wealthiest decile of households. On one side, the oil shock inflates the import bill; on the other, a low-price promise locks the retail tag. Fiscal room to manoeuvre is being squeezed from both ends.

## A metaphor for slipping energy sovereignty

The deeper worry lies at the import end. Since the Middle East conflict began, Indonesia has lost roughly 25 percent of its fuel import sources; its crude price (ICP) surged to US$112.81 per barrel in September from US$89.43 in August, while Brent stood at US$102.02. The tender-based procurement that Jakarta once relied on now attracts few bidders, and the government has been forced to "hunt" directly for surplus stocks in Nigeria, Angola, the United States and Australia — often at a premium. That a regional power must depend on other nations' spare inventories for its energy security is itself a metaphor for diluted sovereignty.

## Conclusion: after the emergency, reform cannot wait

The better news is that Bali's queues have gradually eased as extra tankers and purchase limits took effect, and the government vows to restore supply within a week. But what this "fuel crunch" leaves behind is far more than a few long lines. It has laid bare, all at once, the old ailments of Indonesia's energy structure: ageing refineries, import dependence, subsidy leakage, and fragile archipelagic logistics. An emergency can be weathered by doubling the tanker fleet, by odd-even plate rationing, by police chasing speculators. Yet unless refinery upgrades, subsidy precision and energy diversification are pushed through in the window while oil prices retreat, the next "anchor failure" will again become a national alert for everyday life.

## Further reading

**ICP (Indonesia Crude Price)**: The reference price Jakarta publishes monthly for domestic crude, the linchpin linking world oil to Indonesia's domestic product pricing.
**Pertalite**: The 90-RON subsidised petrol sold by state oil firm Pertamina, its retail price frozen by government — the central object of this round of arbitrage and panic buying.
**ASEAN energy security**: The universal tension between self-sufficiency and import reliance across Southeast Asia; Indonesia's crunch is one snapshot of the region's energy vulnerability.

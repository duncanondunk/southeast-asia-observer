在马尼拉，副总统的保释金和一碗面的价格，正被同一场政治风暴卷起。当莎拉·杜特尔特（Sara Duterte）在法院门口停留数小时、控诉自己遭政治迫害时，菲律宾两大家族的较量已从权力厅堂烧到了街头与民调。

## 一张针对在任副总统的逮捕令

9月5日，奎松市地区法院就"严重威胁"案对副总统莎拉签发逮捕令——菲律宾史上首张针对在任副总统的逮捕令。她缴纳36万比索（约4.1万元人民币）保释后暂获自由。司法程序未终结，却已把政治裂痕公开化。

## 弹劾进入参议院审判

更早的弹劾案于2026年5月在众议院以257票对25票通过，指控她挪用公款、财产来源不明、受贿，并涉嫌威胁总统马科斯（Marcos Jr.）。7月参议院启动弹劾审判，这是菲律宾首次对现任副总统开审。若三分之二参议员定罪，她将被免职并终身禁选。

## 她把战场从法庭移到街头

9月8日，莎拉在社交平台发布视频，控诉马科斯政府对自己"政治骚扰"，称对方已"控制参议员、检察官、法官和警方"。她不纠缠法条，而是把个人遭遇与全民民生绑定：比索跌至1美元兑62.77比索的历史新低，物价飙升，民众叫苦。这套"弃法庭、战权力"的打法，把司法打压反转为悲情动员。

## 马科斯阵营的"大义灭亲"

戏剧性反转来自对手内部：9月7日，马科斯的表弟、前众议长罗穆亚尔德斯因防洪工程贪腐案以掠夺罪被捕，涉案高达74.4亿比索（约8.5亿元人民币）。亲信巨贪与对政敌的"重拳清算"同框，让马科斯的清廉叙事雪上加霜，也意外放大了杜特尔特阵营的翻盘空间。

## 民调为何反而领先

压力之下，莎拉民调不降反升。民调机构WR Numero数据显示，她在2028年总统竞选中领先，3月支持率36%、领先对手拉菲·图尔福（Raffy Tulfo）17个百分点；5月更有51%受访者表示会投她。分析认为，父亲老杜特尔特被移交海牙国际刑事法院（ICC）激起的同情，是支撑基本盘的关键。

## 两大家族的旧盟与新仇

马科斯与杜特尔特曾在2022年大选结盟，分掌正副总统。执政后因权力分配与政策分歧决裂，2024年莎拉"暗杀威胁"言论引爆公开战。如今弹劾与逮捕令，被外界视为马科斯削弱潜在对手的系统动作；而莎拉以民意对冲司法，恰是菲律宾家族政治的经典剧本。

## 一场给东南亚的镜鉴

菲律宾的闹剧并不孤例：当司法可成武器、家族可定国运、民生可被绑上派系战车，民主的质量便悬于权力平衡。莎拉能否翻盘尚在未定之天，但有一点已清晰——在家族政治里，最稳的锚往往不是法律，而是人心。

## 延伸阅读

**弹劾（Impeachment）**：立法机关对高级官员提起的罢免程序；菲律宾宪法规定参议院三分之二票数方可定罪，副总统将被免职并终身禁选。

**国际刑事法院（ICC）**：设于海牙的常设国际刑事法庭，前总统老杜特尔特因扫毒战争相关指控被移交受审。

**家族政治（Political Dynasty）**：菲律宾地方势力与全国权力长期由少数家族把持，是理解其选举与政局的结构性线索。

In Manila, a vice president's bail and the price of a bowl of noodles are swept up by the same political storm. As Sara Duterte lingered for hours outside a courthouse accusing political persecution, the duel of two Filipino dynasties had moved from power halls to streets and surveys.

## A Warrant for a Sitting Vice President

On September 5 a Quezon City court issued an arrest warrant in a "grave threat" case against Vice President Sara—the first such warrant for a sitting vice president in Philippine history. She posted bail of 360,000 pesos (roughly US$5,700) and walked free, yet the legal step had already laid the rift bare.

## Impeachment Reaches the Senate

An earlier impeachment passed the House in May 2026 by 257 to 25, charging her with misusing public funds, unexplained wealth, bribery and an alleged death threat against President Marcos Jr. The Senate opened its trial in July—the first against a sitting vice president. A two-thirds guilty vote would remove her and bar her for life.

## She Moves the Fight From Court to Crowd

On September 8 Sara posted a video accusing the Marcos government of "political harassment," claiming it now "controls senators, prosecutors, judges and police." Rather than argue law, she tied her plight to public hardship: the peso at a record low of 62.77 to the dollar, prices climbing, lives strained. This "abandon the court, fight the power" play turns prosecution into a victim's mobilization.

## Marcos's "Housecleaning" Backfires

The twist came from within the rival camp: on September 7 Marcos's cousin, former House Speaker Romualdez, was arrested for plunder over a flood-control graft case worth 7.44 billion pesos (roughly US$119 million). A kin's giant graft beside a "crusade" against opponents battered Marcos's clean-government story and, oddly, widened the Duterte camp's opening.

## Why Her Polls Still Lead

Under pressure, Sara's numbers rose. Pollster WR Numero shows her ahead for 2028: 36% in March, a 17-point lead over Raffy Tulfo; by May, 51% said they would vote for her. Analysts tie the base to sympathy stirred by her father's transfer to the ICC at The Hague.

## Old Alliance, New Feud

Marcos and Duterte allied in the 2022 election, splitting the top two offices. They broke over power-sharing and policy; Sara's 2024 "assassination" remark opened open war. The impeachment and warrant are read as Marcos pruning a rival, while Sara answers justice with public will—a classic script of Philippine dynasty politics.

## A Mirror for Southeast Asia

The Philippine drama is not alone: where justice becomes a weapon, dynasties decide destiny, and livelihood is lashed to faction, the quality of democracy hangs on a balance of power. Whether Sara turns the table stays open—but one thing is clear: in dynasty politics the steadiest anchor is often not law but public hearts.

## Further reading

**Impeachment**: A legislative removal process; the Philippine constitution requires a two-thirds Senate vote to convict, which would remove a vice president and bar office for life.

**International Criminal Court (ICC)**: The permanent tribunal at The Hague where former president Rodrigo Duterte faces charges tied to his drug war.

**Political Dynasty**: A structural clue to Philippine elections and politics, where local and national power long rests with a few families.

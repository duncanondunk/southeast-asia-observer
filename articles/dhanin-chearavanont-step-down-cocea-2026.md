# 谢国民交棒：中国侨商联合会易帅透出的信号

2026年9月15日，中国侨商联合会（China Overseas Chinese Entrepreneurs Association）第六次会员代表大会在北京落幕。表面看，这只是一条人事更迭的常规新闻：泰国正大集团（Charoen Pokphand，卜蜂集团）资深董事长、八十七岁的谢国民（Dhanin Chearavanont）在执掌十八年后卸任会长，受聘为荣誉会长；接替他的，是四十七岁的马来西亚对华裔企业家郭孔华（Kwok Kong-Wah）——已故“糖王”郭鹤年之子、香港嘉里控股（中国）有限公司董事长。

若只把它当作一次寻常的换帅，便错过了重点。这场交接是一扇很小的窗口，却清晰地映出北京如何在“高水平对外开放”遇上远比一九七九年险峻的国际环境之时，重新设想华侨华人资本的角色。

## 一个时代的注脚：十八年与一场交接

谢国民登上这个讲台，本就不是中国的陌生人。他一九三九年生于曼谷一个来自广东汕头澄海的潮汕华侨家庭，上世纪六十年代接掌正大，并于一九七九年在改革开放后的深圳领到第一张外资企业批准证书（0001号）。这一步，让正大成为“华侨回国投资”的范本，也让谢国民本人成为一代离散资本押注中国崛起的活符号。

他自二〇〇八年起担任中国侨商投资企业协会首届会长，连任四届；二〇一八年该会整体并入中国侨商联合会后，又续任第五届会长。十八年不是一届任期，而是一段政治传记。它说明北京对东南亚华侨华人商界耆老寄予了多高的制度性信任。

## 从“引进来”到“带出去”：侨商角色的再定义

在卸任感言里，谢国民用一组干净的对照，点出了角色的转换。他说，改革开放初期，华侨把先进的技术、资金和管理“引进来”，投资生产、出口创汇；而今天，要向中国的高科技学习，把好的产品和技术“带出去”，到世界各地去发展。

这不是场面话。它抓住了离散群体经济功能的真实转向：从帮助中国接入世界经济的管道，变为助力中国企业在全球落地的接力棒。对一门心思想突破“卡脖子”、又急于把标准、品牌与平台国际化的北京而言，华侨华人企业家的忠诚与人脉仍是战略资产——前提是他们愿意接下这个新角色。谢国民的正大用四十年在中国铺开六百余家企业；摆在年轻一代面前的考题，是能否同样利落地把这条路反着走一遍。

## 代际更替：从“老侨”到“华裔新生代”

两位会长的反差，本身就是故事。谢国民属于“老侨”那一辈：儿时被送回汕头念中文，受早期改革开放的感恩政治塑造，一九九〇年还获邓小平接见。郭孔华则是另一种人——一九七九年出生，手握哈佛大学经济学学士学位（二〇〇三年），在父亲创立的嘉里体系内历练，在香港、吉隆坡与北京的董事会之间游刃有余。

这正是北京正在应对的人口现实。侨务工作的天然基本盘正在老去，把离散长者与一片他们亲身记忆的故土绑在一起的情感纽带正在变淡。新领导层被期待讲的是基金、母基金、绿地制造与人工智能的语言，而不是祖厝村塾的语言。谢国民说得很直白：年轻一代创新能力更强，“是侨商事业的未来”。因此，这次交棒与其说是换岗，不如说是一场刻意压注青春的接班。

## 东南亚底色：两位会长的共同地缘基因

请看地缘。离任与就任的两位会长，都是东南亚对华裔——一泰一马，绝非偶然。东南亚华侨华人，论规模与经济分量，至今仍是北京对外工作与对东盟经济纽带中最重要的海外侨群。正大与嘉里不只是在华投资者，更是贯穿泰国、马来西亚及更广区域的华资锚点。

权杖从一家东南亚华商交到另一家东南亚华商手中，传递的是延续而非断裂。它向区域释放信号：北京在离散群体中偏好的对话者，仍是半岛与群岛上那些根基深厚、信得过的商界老号——正是这些网络，让中国—东盟经贸在强权博弈最烈时仍守住了一份从容。在一个族裔华资时而于旅居国遭遇疑虑的时代，这份定心丸自有其安静的外交分量。

## 新周期里的旧命题：人工智能、出海与地缘风浪

谢国民自己点出了真正的断层。“十五五”（今年是启动之年）开启的，是高质量发展与高水平开放的时代，人工智能与科技革命的冲击正重塑生产与商业。他说，这些都是侨商的“大好机遇”。

他点出了利害所在，说得没错，但新领导层接手的是比他那一辈艰难得多的牌局。一九七九年的任务是进口；今天是把产品出口到被战略竞争、出口管制与产业回迁重重围住的市场。帮中国企业“走出去”，如今意味着在美中科技围堵、欧盟碳边境与碎片化的全球南方之间穿行——离散网络是优势，却不再是盾牌。下任会长必须像谢国民当年熟悉饲料厂那样，熟悉半导体供应链。

## 平稳接力背后的信号

拨开仪式的包装，道理很直白。北京正在释放三重信号：华侨华人商界仍是其开放叙事的核心；它信任东南亚华商老号来执掌这个故事；它也在为一段由科技驱动、被地缘博弈切割的十年，有意识地更新领导层。

对观察中国—东盟关系的人而言，这场交棒是个有用的路标：离散之桥正在为一个不同的时代重建，换上了更年轻的手，也换上了一份更硬的考卷。谢国民的十八年证明，信任可以复利的滚成投资；新生代能否在导师从未遭遇的条件下，把信任复利的滚成影响力，是第六次会员代表大会刚刚交到他们手上的开放题。

# Dhanin's Baton: What the Leadership Handover of China's Overseas Chinese Entrepreneurs Signals

On 15 September 2026, the sixth national congress of the China Overseas Chinese Entrepreneurs Association closed in Beijing with a piece of news that, on the surface, looked like routine housekeeping: Dhanin Chearavanont, the 87-year-old senior chairman of Thailand's Charoen Pokphand (CP) Group, stepped down as president after 18 years and was named honorary president. In his place, the congress elected Kwok Kong-Wah, a 47-year-old Malaysian-Chinese businessman and son of the late tycoon Robert Kuok, chairman of Kerry Holdings (China).

To dismiss this as a mere change of officers would be to miss the point. The handover is a small but telling window into how Beijing conceives the role of ethnic Chinese capital at a moment when "high-level opening up" meets a far less hospitable global environment than the one Dhanin first bet on in 1979.

## A closing note to an era: 18 years and a handover

Dhanin did not arrive at the podium as a stranger to China. Born in Bangkok in 1939 to a Teochew immigrant family from Shantou, Guangdong, he took the helm of CP in the 1960s and, in 1979, led the group to register the first foreign-invested enterprise license (No. 0001) in reform-era Shenzhen. That single move made CP the archetype of "overseas Chinese returning to invest," and Dhanin himself the living symbol of a generation of diaspora capitalists who staked their fortunes on China's rise.

He served as founding president of the Association of Overseas Chinese Investment Enterprises from 2008, was re-elected four times, and—after that body merged into the China Overseas Chinese Entrepreneurs Association in 2018—continued as its fifth-term president. Eighteen years is not a tenure; it is a political biography. It tells you how much institutional trust Beijing places in the senior statesmen of the Southeast Asian Chinese business world.

## From "bringing in" to "taking out": redefining the overseas Chinese entrepreneur

In his farewell remarks, Dhanin distilled the shift in one neat contrast. In the early reform era, he said, overseas Chinese brought in advanced technology, capital and management, built factories and earned foreign exchange. Today, he argued, the task is to learn from China's high technology and "take good products and technology out" to the world.

That is not boilerplate. It captures a real reorientation of the diaspora's economic function: from a conduit that helped China plug into the global economy, to a relay that helps Chinese firms go global. For a Beijing anxious about "chokepoints" and eager to internationalise its standards, brands and platforms, the loyalty and the networks of ethnic Chinese business leaders remain a strategic asset—provided they are willing to play the new role. Dhanin's own CP has spent four decades building a China footprint of more than 600 companies; the question for the next generation is whether it can do the reverse as deftly.

## A generational shift: from "old overseas Chinese" to "a new Chinese diaspora generation"

The contrast between the two men is the story. Dhanin is of the "old overseas Chinese" cohort: sent back to Shantou as a boy to learn Chinese, formed by the gratitude politics of the early reform period, personally received by Deng Xiaoping in 1990. Kwok belongs to a different breed—born in 1979, armed with a Harvard economics degree (2003), groomed inside the Kerry Group founded by his father, comfortable in boardrooms from Hong Kong to Kuala Lumpur to Beijing.

This is the demographic reality Beijing is now managing. The natural constituency of "overseas Chinese work" is ageing, and the emotional ties that once bound diaspora elders to a homeland they physically remembered are thinning. The new leadership is expected to speak the language of funds, funds-of-funds, greenfield manufacturing and AI—not the language of ancestral villages. Dhanin said it plainly: the young generation is more innovative and "is the future of the overseas Chinese enterprise cause." The succession is therefore less a changing of the guard than a deliberate bet on youth.

## The Southeast Asian thread: a shared regional DNA

Note the geography. Both the outgoing and incoming presidents are Southeast Asian Chinese—Thai and Malaysian respectively. That is no accident. The Chinese diaspora in Southeast Asia remains, by sheer scale and economic weight, the single most important overseas Chinese community for Beijing's external outreach and for its economic ties with ASEAN. CP and Kerry are not merely investors in China; they are also anchors of Chinese-connected capital across Thailand, Malaysia and beyond.

The smooth passage of the baton from one Southeast Asian Chinese family to another signals continuity, not rupture. It tells the region that Beijing's preferred interlocutors in the diaspora are still the established, trusted business houses of the peninsula and the archipelago—the very networks that insulated China–ASEAN commerce from the worst of great-power friction. In an era when ethnic Chinese capital faces occasional suspicion in host societies, that reassurance has quiet diplomatic value.

## Old questions in a new cycle: AI, going global, and geopolitical headwinds

Dhanin flagged the real discontinuity himself. "Fifteen-Five" (the 15th Five-Year Plan, launched this year) opens, he noted, into an age of high-quality development and high-level opening, where artificial intelligence and the tech revolution are reshaping production and commerce. Those are, he said, "great opportunities" for overseas Chinese entrepreneurs.

He is right to name the stakes, but the new leadership inherits a harder playing field than his generation knew. In 1979 the task was to import; today it is to export into markets ringed by strategic competition, export controls and reshoring pressures. Helping Chinese firms "go out" now means navigating US–China tech containment, the EU's carbon border and a fragmented Global South—terrain where a diaspora network is an advantage but no longer a shield. The next president must be as comfortable with a semiconductor supply chain as Dhanin was with a feed mill.

## The signal behind a smooth baton pass

Strip away the ceremony and the lesson is straightforward. Beijing is signalling that the overseas Chinese business community remains central to its opening-up story, that it trusts the senior Southeast Asian Chinese houses to steward that story, and that it is consciously renewing the leadership for a tech-driven, geopolitically contested decade.

For observers of China–ASEAN relations, the handover is a useful marker: the diaspora bridge is being rebuilt for a different era, with younger hands and a harder brief. Dhanin's 18 years proved that trust compounded into investment. Whether the new generation can compound trust into influence—under conditions his mentor never faced—is the open question the sixth congress has just handed them.

**Charoen Pokphand / CP Group:** Thailand's largest agri-food and retail conglomerate, founded in Bangkok in 1921 by Chia Ek Chor (谢易初); the first foreign group to invest in reform-era China (Shenzhen License No. 0001, 1979).

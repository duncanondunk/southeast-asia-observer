# 运河、侨商与东盟（ASEAN）：一条水道如何把离散网络织进产业链

8月26日，南宁迎来一场主题颇为讲究的大会——"2026中国（广西）—东盟跨境产业链供应链合作大会暨侨商侨领广西行"。广西壮族自治区主席韦韬在致辞中把三件看似不相干的事拧到了一起：一条即将于今年9月通航的运河、一个正在重构的全球产业链，以及一批被寄予厚望的"侨商侨领"。据中国新闻网报道，韦韬希望广大侨商侨领"发挥自身技术、人才、研发、市场等优势，积极布局广西"，把上下游企业与合作伙伴聚到广西，打造"优势互补、互利共赢"的跨区域跨境产业链供应链。

一句话点破：北京（Beijing）正在学习用一种更成熟的方式调度华侨华人网络——不再只是"引资引智"的资本游戏，而是把它当作区域产业链的"软基础设施"。

## 改写地图的运河

平陆运河不是普通的河道。它是新中国成立以来第一条通江达海的大运河，西起南宁平塘江口，经钦州陆屋镇沿钦江进入北部湾，全长134.2公里，全线按内河Ⅰ级航道标准建设，可通航5000吨级船舶。总投资约727.2亿元，截至2026年6月底累计完成投资705.54亿元，占工程总投资的97%，三大枢纽主体工程全部完成，全面进入有水调试阶段，预计今年9月正式建成通航。

它改变的不是一段风景，而是一张经济地理图。西南地区货物经平陆运河出海，比经广州港出海缩短内河航程约560公里，综合物流成本预计降低18%至30%，每年可节省运输费用超过50亿元。适配该运河航行的内河船舶已超过2000艘。换句话说，广西由"有江临海却江海难通"的边角，升级为江海联运枢纽，西江航运干线与北部湾港直连互通——这条"黄金水道"将直接重塑中国西南通往东盟的运距与成本结构。

## 要的是商人，不只是混凝土

但运河再宏大，也不会自己把货装船。基础设施提供的是可能性，真正把可能性变成贸易流的，是商人、信任与跨境网络。这正是广西在8月26日这场大会上刻意拉来"侨商侨领"的原因。

据广西日报报道，中国侨联副主席吴换炎领衔发布了《中国（广西）—东盟跨境产业链供应链合作倡议》，呼吁侨商侨领"架设桥梁，拓展合作空间"，聚焦先进制造、新能源、新材料领域，"让项目落地生根"。柬埔寨、老挝、马来西亚（Malaysia）、缅甸、泰国（Thailand）、越南（Vietnam）六国驻南宁总领事馆代表均到场参会——这份区域买账，本身就说明问题。

这里藏着一个被反复验证、却常被浪漫化的逻辑：在东南亚，华侨华人的商业网络往往比任何官方经贸协议都更早触达本地市场、更懂在地规则。当一条运河把广西推到离东盟最近的位置，最自然的动作，就是请那些已经在东盟扎了根的商人回来"搭桥"。

## 离散网络：产业链的黏合剂

浪漫归浪漫，这次会议给出的案例是实打实落地的。

玖龙纸业（控股）有限公司董事长张茵表示，企业正借平陆运河开通之机，打通面向东盟的跨境物流链路，在广西建设林浆纸零碳全链条产业园，产品已辐射东南亚市场。深圳市中鑫泰投资发展有限公司总经理尹力此次组织100多家越南企业参会，其企业已在广西东兴市建设智能制造产业园，引进粤港澳大湾区相关企业落户，深化与越南合作，构建面向东盟的电子信息制造基地。国轩高科股份有限公司中国业务总裁陈炜则说，公司将依托广西柳州基地的制造能力，以"国内基地供货+海外工厂本地化生产"联动越南、泰国、印尼（Indonesia）等海外生产基地，快速响应东盟市场订单，深耕动力电池及储能赛道。

越南中国总商会广西企业联合会会长银伦的话更直白："广西是我们开拓东盟市场的关键枢纽与前沿支点。"他提到大会发布的"中国（广西）—东盟跨境采购双向清单"，为广西与越南在数字经济、绿色能源等领域合作提供了机遇，将抢抓平陆运河开通与中越智慧口岸建设的时机，"帮助更多企业找到合作项目"。

这些不是口号。它们呈现的是一种可操作的结合：运河降低物理成本，侨商侨领降低制度与文化成本，两者叠加，才构成"跨境产供链"四个字真正的含义。

## "桥梁"隐喻的边界

但"华侨华人是天然桥梁"这句话，边界必须说清楚。

其一，网络是碎片化的。东南亚的华商网络按方言、祖籍、行业、国别高度分层，并不存在一个能统一调度、为广西产业整体代言的"总开关"。把"侨"当成一个整体来动员，容易高估其协同效率。

其二，也是最敏感的：在东南亚多个族群政治高度敏感的社会里，华人资本与中国的靠近本身就会被部分在地舆论读成"经济支配"乃至"治外忠诚"的注脚。广西越是高调地把"侨"推到产业链前台，越要避免让合作看上去只是为中国企业"借道出海"服务的通道。这一点，恰恰需要克制——也是本站一贯的调性。

其三，网络替代不了更难的那部分工作：监管对接、标准互认、相互的市场准入。东盟国家当下处在全球"去风险"与"友岸外包"的再平衡中，对中国既是机遇也是提防对象。运河与侨商能降低摩擦，却无法单方面消除制度性壁垒。

## 更聪明的打法：为东盟造，而非只借道

那么，什么才是更聪明的打法？这次大会其实露出了一条线索：韦韬在致辞中反复强调"打造面向东盟的人工智能开放高地、集成高地、应用高地、创新高地"，提及"南A中心""中国—东盟智算走廊""东盟场景适配实验室"，并呼吁"不断开发适配东盟市场需求的各类产品"。

关键词是"适配"。如果"跨境产供链"只是把广西建成中国产品通往东盟的转运站，那它和其他沿海加工区没有本质区别；只有当合作能生产出真正适配东盟本地需求、由双方共同增值的产品，侨商侨领的"桥梁"才不会变成单方面的"渠道"，离散网络的价值才经得起在地社会的审视。

换言之，衡量这场"运河+侨商"实验成败的，不是又签了多少项目、发了多少倡议，而是东盟伙伴是否从中拿到了可核验的、双向的收益。

## 值得观察的

平陆运河9月通航在即，广西把"侨"写进了区域一体化的剧本。这既是一次对离散网络经济价值的再发现，也是一次对分寸感的考验：用得好，它是中国与东盟之间成本更低、韧性更强的黏合剂；用得过界，它可能重新激活那些本就未散的疑虑。

接下来最值得盯的，是两个指标：一是运河通航后，经北部湾港发往东盟的实际货量是否如期放量；二是那些在大会上"搭桥"的侨商侨领，能否把合作从签约席真正带进厂房与码头。水道已经挖好，剩下的，是人与人之间那部分最难、也最关键的工程。

# The Canal, the Diaspora, and ASEAN: How One Waterway Weaves Overseas Chinese Networks into a Supply Chain

On 26 August, Nanning hosted a conference with a deliberately layered title: the 2026 China (Guangxi)–ASEAN Cross-Border Industrial and Supply Chain Cooperation Conference, paired with an "Overseas Chinese Merchants and Leaders Guangxi Tour." In his address, Guangxi chairman Wei Tao braided three apparently disjointed threads into one narrative: a canal due to open in September, a global supply chain in the middle of reconstruction, and a group of "overseas Chinese merchants and leaders" (qiaoshang qiaoling) expected to do the heavy lifting. According to China News Service, Wei urged these merchants and leaders to "leverage their strengths in technology, talent, R&D, and markets" and "actively deploy in Guangxi," gathering upstream and downstream firms and partners to build a "complementary and mutually beneficial" cross-regional, cross-border industrial and supply chain.

One sentence lays bare the subtext: Beijing is learning to mobilise the overseas Chinese network in a more mature way — no longer merely a capital game of "attracting investment and talent," but as the "soft infrastructure" of a regional supply chain.

## The canal that rewrites the map

The Pinglu Canal is no ordinary waterway. It is the first river-to-sea canal built since the founding of the People's Republic, starting at the Pingtang River mouth in Nanning, running through Luwu town in Qinzhou along the Qinjiang River into the Beibu Gulf, spanning 134.2 kilometres, built to Class I inland-waterway standards that allow 5,000-tonne vessels. Total investment is about 72.72 billion yuan (roughly US$10.2 billion); by the end of June 2026 some 70.55 billion yuan (about US$9.9 billion) had been spent — 97 per cent of the total — with the three major hubs essentially complete and the whole system entering water testing, on track for formal opening in September.

What it changes is not a view but a map. Goods from southwest China reaching the sea via Pinglu will travel about 560 kilometres less than via the Port of Guangzhou, with comprehensive logistics costs projected to fall by 18 to 30 per cent and annual transport savings exceeding 5 billion yuan (roughly US$700 million). More than 2,000 inland vessels are already adapted for the route. In short, Guangxi is promoted from a peripheral corner that "faced the sea yet could not easily reach it" to a river–sea intermodal hub, directly linking the Xijiang waterway to the Beibu Gulf port — a "golden waterway" that reshapes the distance and cost structure between southwest China and ASEAN.

## Why merchants, not just concrete

But no matter how grand, a canal will not load its own cargo. Infrastructure supplies possibility; what turns possibility into trade flows is merchants, trust, and cross-border networks. That is precisely why Guangxi deliberately pulled "overseas Chinese merchants and leaders" into the 26 August conference.

As reported by Guangxi Daily, Wu Huanyan, vice chairman of the All-China Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese, led the release of the China (Guangxi)–ASEAN Cross-Border Industrial and Supply Chain Cooperation Initiative, calling on merchants and leaders to "build bridges and expand space for cooperation," focusing on advanced manufacturing, new energy, and new materials, so that "projects take root." Consular representatives from Cambodia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, Thailand, and Vietnam all attended in Nanning — that regional buy-in itself speaks volumes.

Here lies a logic repeatedly validated yet often romanticised: in Southeast Asia, ethnic Chinese business networks often reach local markets earlier than any official trade agreement and understand local rules better. When a canal pushes Guangxi to the spot closest to ASEAN, the most natural move is to invite the merchants already rooted there to "build the bridge."

## The diaspora as supply-chain glue

Romance aside, the cases offered at this conference are concretely grounded.

Zhang Yin, chair of Nine Dragons Paper (Holdings), said her company is using the canal's opening to open a cross-border logistics link toward ASEAN and is building a zero-carbon forest–pulp–paper integrated industrial park in Guangxi, with products already reaching Southeast Asian markets. Yin Li, general manager of Shenzhen Zhongxintai Investment, organised more than 100 Vietnamese enterprises to attend and has built a smart-manufacturing park in Dongxing city on the Guangxi–Vietnam border, bringing in firms from the Greater Bay Area and deepening cooperation with Vietnam to form an ASEAN-oriented electronics manufacturing base. Chen Wei, president of China business at Gotion High-Tech, said the firm will use its Liuzhou base's manufacturing capacity to serve overseas plants in Vietnam, Thailand, and Indonesia through a "domestic base supplies, overseas factory localises production" model, responding quickly to ASEAN orders and deepening its battery and energy-storage presence.

Yin Lun, head of the Guangxi Enterprise Federation of the China Chamber of Commerce in Vietnam, put it more bluntly: "Guangxi is the key hub and forward pivot for us to open the ASEAN market." He pointed to the conference's "China (Guangxi)–ASEAN two-way cross-border procurement list," which offers opportunities for Guangxi–Vietnam cooperation in digital economy and green energy, and said he would seize the timing of the canal's opening and the China–Vietnam smart port to "help more enterprises find cooperative projects."

These are not slogans. They show an operable combination: the canal lowers physical cost, the merchants and leaders lower institutional and cultural cost, and only together do they give real meaning to the four words "cross-border industrial and supply chain."

## The limits of the "bridge" metaphor

But the claim that "overseas Chinese are a natural bridge" has boundaries that must be stated plainly.

First, the networks are fragmented. Southeast Asia's ethnic Chinese business networks are highly stratified by dialect, ancestral home, industry, and country; there is no unified switch that can orchestrate them on behalf of Guangxi's industries as a whole. Mobilising "the qiao" as a monolith risks overstating its coordination efficiency.

Second, and most sensitive: in several Southeast Asian societies where ethnic politics are acute, the proximity of Chinese capital to China itself is read by parts of local opinion as "economic dominance" or even a footnote of "extra-territorial loyalty." The more loudly Guangxi puts the "qiao" at the front of its industrial chain, the more it must avoid letting the cooperation look like a mere corridor serving Chinese firms "going out." This calls for restraint — the very tone this outlet keeps.

Third, networks cannot substitute for the harder work: regulatory alignment, mutual standards recognition, reciprocal market access. ASEAN countries are now rebalancing within global "de-risking" and "friend-shoring"; China is to them both an opportunity and a wariness. The canal and the merchants can lower friction, but they cannot unilaterally dissolve institutional barriers.

## A smarter play: build for ASEAN, not just through it

So what is the smarter play? The conference actually exposed a clue. In his address Wei Tao repeatedly stressed building "an open, integrated, applied, and innovative highland facing ASEAN" in artificial intelligence, mentioning the "Southern AI Centre," the "China–ASEAN Computing Corridor," and "ASEAN scenario-adaptation labs," and called for "continuously developing products adapted to ASEAN market needs."

The keyword is "adapted." If "cross-border industrial and supply chain" merely turns Guangxi into a trans-shipment station for Chinese products headed to ASEAN, it differs little from other coastal processing zones. Only when cooperation produces goods genuinely adapted to local ASEAN demand, with value co-created by both sides, will the merchants' "bridge" avoid becoming a one-way "channel," and the diaspora network's worth survive scrutiny by local societies.

In other words, the success of this "canal plus qiao" experiment should be measured not by how many projects were signed or initiatives released, but by whether ASEAN partners take away verifiable, two-way gains.

## What to watch

With the Pinglu Canal's September opening imminent, Guangxi has written the "qiao" into its regional-integration script. This is both a rediscovery of the diaspora network's economic value and a test of proportion: used well, it becomes a lower-cost, more resilient adhesive between China and ASEAN; pushed too far, it may reawaken suspicions that never fully dissipated.

The two indicators worth watching next: first, whether actual cargo from the Beibu Gulf port to ASEAN scales up as expected once the canal opens; second, whether the merchants and leaders who "built bridges" at the conference can carry cooperation from the signing table into actual factories and docks. The waterway is dug; what remains is the hardest and most crucial part of all — the human engineering between people.

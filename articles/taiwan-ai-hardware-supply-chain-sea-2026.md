# 台系AI硬件供应链「东南飞」：数据中心热潮下，被忽视的电路板暗线

## 聚光灯与暗线
当全世界的镜头都对准东南亚拔地而起的AI数据中心时，很少有人注意另一条更安静、却更坚实的产业链正在悄然南迁。据《日经亚洲》报道，在台积电与鸿海之外，真正成规模转移的，是那些支撑AI服务器的「无名」零部件——印刷电路板（PCB）、散热模组、光通讯器件，以及半导体封装测试（OSAT）。数据中心的霓虹很亮，但把算力真正堆起来的，是这些藏在机箱里的「钢筋水泥」。

## 泰国的静默押注：瞄准全球第二大电路板基地
泰国正把自己押注在一条「不上镜」的赛道上。《日经亚洲》走访了十余家在泰投资的中、台、日、泰供应商，得到的共识是：AI已成为泰国科技制造业唯一的引擎。以英伟达AI服务器与显卡PCB核心供应商、中国胜宏科技（VGT）为例，它在距曼谷以北约60公里的阿瑜陀耶（大城）火速建起在泰第二座工厂——距离其从台湾APCB集团收购首厂还不到一年。胜宏科技凭借AI浪潮，市值已突破2330亿元人民币（约324亿美元），反超按营收计全球最大PCB厂、台湾臻鼎科技（市值约1830亿新台币，折合412亿元人民币）。短短不到三年，已有近60家来自中国大陆与台湾的PCB厂商在泰国落子，其中囊括全球前30大厂商的绝大多数——欣兴（Unimicron）、臻鼎、深南电路、胜宏皆在其中。泰国甚至喊出了「成为全球仅次于中国的第二大PCB基地」的目标。金像电子（Gold Circuit）——英伟达交换机托盘的关键PCB供应商——其在泰首厂已全负荷运转。

## 为何是电路板，为何是现在
这一轮「台湾+1」并非凭空而来。业界普遍将它的加速节点追溯至2022年8月佩洛西访台后，北京围绕台湾周边举行的实弹军演：那次事件让全球科技巨头真切意识到，把芯片与电子产能过度压在一个岛上风险有多大。此后美中博弈持续加码，微软、亚马逊、苹果、惠普、戴尔纷纷要求供应商把组装与关键零部件搬离中国大陆。特朗普政府对泰国课以19%「对等关税」，却对PCB产业冲击有限——因为这类零件往往先运往越南、印度等地完成下一工序，再装入最终产品输美。换句话说，关税打在成品上，却绕开了散件。

## 马来西亚与越南：光通讯零件与散热模组
南下的不只是电路板。台湾光通讯元件供应商FICG在马来西亚柔佛的工厂今年初刚量产，便宣布至2028年底将当地产能扩充两倍以上——据《日经亚洲》，这是应美国客户要求、典型的「中国+1」布局。光收发器与连接器，正是AI数据中心高速互联的咽喉瓶颈。越南北部同样热闹：散热零部件厂商奇鋐科技（Auras）9月宣布斥资约77.8亿新台币（约17.5亿元人民币）在越南设厂；鸿海旗下讯芯科技则计划在越南投建半导体布线产线，最高可达6500万美元（约4.7亿元人民币）。这些投资指向同一个逻辑：把上游、且相对标准化的环节，放到离客户与风险都更远的东南亚。

## 新加坡与封测的尾巴
封装测试（OSAT）——半导体后道的关键工序——也在向东南亚聚集。日月光（ASE）9月宣布，其新加坡子公司以约12.6亿新台币（约2.8亿元人民币）购入设备，并斥资约21.5亿新台币（约4.8亿元人民币）收购一座工厂。台厂将先进封装能力留在岛内、却通过收购与扩产在东南亚抬高产能，正是「核心留台、外围南移」的缩影。

## 数字会说话
最硬的证据在贸易账上。据台湾财政部数据，2026年1至8月，台湾自东盟进口额同比增长75.4%，达到704亿美元（约5069亿元人民币）——台湾方面把这一跳升归因于AI供应链国际分工深化带来的零部件需求扩张。一边是台湾对东盟的零部件采购暴增，一边是东南亚厂房接连动工，两条曲线指向同一结论：AI硬件的「制造重心」，正在以台湾为枢纽、向周边扇形铺开。

## 是避险，不是脱钩
需要点破的是：这场南迁本质是「避险」，而非「脱钩」。台厂的策略很清晰——把先进技术与核心产能留在台湾，把上游电路板、零部件的生产能力在东南亚抬高，再把服务器与半导体成品产能布局到更靠近美国客户的地方。这是一种「台湾+1」的弹性安排：既保住岛内的高附加值环节，又在地理上分散一旦台海生变的断供风险。对东南亚而言，这是一笔带着条件的红利——它带来就业、技术转移与产业升级的契机（泰国便是从汽车迈向量更高电子业的跳板），但这些投资本质上是「出口平台」，依附于美国终端需求，而非本地消费。

## 狂飙之下的隐忧
潮水之下也有暗礁。第一，若AI资本开支退潮，今天疯抢的PCB产能明天可能便是过剩产能，泰国雄心勃勃的「第二大基地」目标将面临产能出清的考验。第二，泰国自身的软肋并未消失：周期性洪灾、电网能否同时支撑数据中心与工厂的耗电、以及劳工技能结构，都可能成为瓶颈。第三，这一轮迁移高度绑定美国需求与地缘政治风向，一旦美方政策或AI景气反转，东南亚承接的产线首当其冲。第四，所谓「台湾+1」里，中国大陆厂商（如胜宏、深南）同样是南下主力，这让「供应链安全」的叙事多了几分复杂的成色——它既是友岸外包，也是借道出海。

## 延伸阅读
**印刷电路板（PCB）**：承载并连接电子元器件的基材，是AI服务器、显卡与数据中心交换机的核心载体，被称为「电子系统的骨架」。
**外包半导体封装测试（OSAT）**：将晶圆切割、封装并测试成品芯片的代工环节，是半导体制造的后道工序，日月光、安靠（Amkor）为头部企业。
**中国+1（China+1）**：跨国企业为分散单一市场与地缘政治风险，在保留中国产能的同时于中国以外另设备份产线的策略，东南亚是主要承接地。

# Taiwan's AI hardware supply chain moves south: the unglamorous PCB undercurrent behind the data-center boom

## The spotlight and the shadow
While the world's cameras train on the AI data centers rising across Southeast Asia, a quieter but more concrete supply chain is quietly migrating south. Beyond TSMC and Foxconn, what is actually moving at scale are the "invisible" components that hold an AI server together — printed circuit boards (PCB), thermal modules, optical communications parts, and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT). The data-center neon is bright, but what actually stacks the compute is the "steel and concrete" hidden inside the chassis.

## Thailand's quiet bet: the world's No. 2 PCB hub
Thailand is staking itself on an unglamorous lane. Nikkei Asia spoke with more than ten Chinese, Taiwanese, Japanese and Thai suppliers investing in the country and found a consensus: AI has become the sole engine of Thailand's tech manufacturing. Take Victory Giant Technology (VGT), the Chinese core PCB supplier to Nvidia's AI servers and graphics cards — it is rushing to build a second Thai factory in Ayutthaya, some 60 km north of Bangkok, less than a year after acquiring its first plant there from Taiwan's APCB Group. Riding the AI wave, VGT's market capitalization has crossed RMB 233 billion (about US$32.6 billion), overtaking Zhen Ding Tech, the world's largest PCB maker by revenue, at TWD 183 billion (about US$5.9 billion). In under three years, nearly 60 PCB makers from mainland China and Taiwan have set up new capacity in Thailand, including most of the global top 30 — Unimicron, Zhen Ding, Shennan Circuits and Victory Giant among them. Thailand has even declared its ambition to become the world's second-largest PCB base after China. Gold Circuit Electronics, a key PCB supplier for Nvidia's switch trays, is already running its first Thai facility at full capacity.

## Why circuit boards, why now
This "Taiwan plus one" push did not appear from nowhere. Those inside the industry trace its acceleration to the live-fire drills Beijing staged around Taiwan after Nancy Pelosi's Taipei visit in August 2022 — the moment global tech giants grasped how risky it was to pile chip and electronics capacity onto one island. Since then the US–China contest has only intensified, with Microsoft, Amazon, Apple, HP and Dell all pressing suppliers to move assembly and critical components out of mainland China. The Trump administration's 19% "reciprocal" tariff on Thailand has had limited bite on the PCB sector, because these parts are typically shipped on to Vietnam, India or elsewhere for the next step before being folded into finished goods bound for the US. In other words, the tariff lands on the finished product, not the loose component.

## Malaysia and Vietnam: optical parts and thermal modules
It is not only boards heading south. Taiwanese optical-components supplier FICG opened its first Malaysian plant in Johor earlier this year and has already announced it will more than triple local capacity by the end of 2028 — a textbook "China plus one" move, Nikkei reported, made at the request of its US customers. Optical transceivers and connectors are precisely the bottleneck of high-speed interconnects inside AI data-center clusters. Northern Vietnam is busy too: thermal-parts maker Auras Technology announced in September it would invest around NT$7.78 billion (about US$250 million) in a Vietnamese factory, while Foxconn's interconnect unit plans a semiconductor wiring line in Vietnam with up to US$65 million. The logic is uniform — push the upstream, relatively standardized steps to Southeast Asia, farther from both customers and risk.

## Singapore and the packaging tail
OSAT — the back-end of chipmaking — is also clustering in Southeast Asia. Advanced Semiconductor Engineering (ASE) said in September its Singapore subsidiary spent about NT$1.26 billion (roughly US$41 million) on equipment and a further NT$2.15 billion (about US$70 million) to acquire a factory. Taiwanese firms keep advanced packaging at home while lifting capacity abroad through acquisitions and upgrades — a microcosm of "core stays in Taiwan, periphery moves south."

## The numbers tell the story
The hardest evidence sits in the trade ledger. Taiwan's Ministry of Finance reports that from January to August 2026 Taiwan's imports from ASEAN jumped 75.4% year on year to US$70.4 billion (about RMB 506.9 billion) — which Taipei attributes to the deepening international division of labor in the AI supply chain expanding component demand. On one side, Taiwan's parts purchases from ASEAN are surging; on the other, Southeast Asian factories keep breaking ground. The two curves point to the same conclusion: the "manufacturing center of gravity" of AI hardware is fanning out from Taiwan across its neighbors.

## A hedge, not a decoupling
What must be said plainly: this southward shift is a hedge, not a decoupling. The Taiwanese playbook is clear — keep advanced technology and core capacity in Taiwan, lift upstream board and component capacity in Southeast Asia, and locate server and semiconductor finished-goods capacity closer to US customers. This is a "Taiwan plus one" elasticity: it protects the high-value links on the island while geographically diversifying the risk of a Taiwan Strait disruption. For Southeast Asia, it is a conditional dividend — it brings jobs, technology transfer and a chance at industrial upgrading (Thailand, for instance, leaps from autos toward higher-value electronics), but these investments are essentially "export platforms" tied to US end-demand rather than local consumption.

## Risks beneath the rush
Under the tide lie reefs. First, if AI capital spending cools, the PCB capacity being scrambled for today may become excess tomorrow, and Thailand's ambitious "No. 2 base" target will face a brutal capacity shakeout. Second, Thailand's own frailties have not vanished: cyclical floods, whether the grid can power both data centers and factories, and the labor skill structure all remain bottlenecks. Third, this migration is tightly bound to US demand and the geopolitical weather; should Washington's policy or AI sentiment reverse, the lines Southeast Asia absorbed would be hit first. Fourth, within "Taiwan plus one," mainland Chinese makers such as VGT and Shennan are themselves leading the southward march — lending the "supply-chain security" narrative a more complicated hue: it is both friend-shoring and a detour for going global.

## Further reading
**Printed circuit board (PCB):** The substrate that carries and connects electronic components; the core carrier of AI servers, graphics cards and data-center switches, often called the "skeleton of electronic systems."
**Outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT):** The contract segment that dices, packages and tests finished chips — the back-end of chipmaking; ASE and Amkor are the leaders.
**China plus one:** A strategy in which multinationals keep China capacity while adding backup lines elsewhere to spread single-market and geopolitical risk; Southeast Asia is the main recipient.

近年，缅甸一位名为杨家迅的华裔青年因用编程搭建华文学习平台而受到关注。他并非孤例——在东南亚多国，越来越多华侨华人新生代正以数字手段接过了文化传承的接力棒。这一现象折射的，是华文教育在动荡地缘环境中顽强的生命力，以及中国侨务政策"柔性支撑"的成效。

## 一道代码写就的"文化桥梁"

杨家迅的故事之所以动人，在于他把"写代码"这件看似冰冷的事，变成了连接故乡语言的桥梁。他开发的工具帮助缅甸本地华校学生更便捷地练习汉字、识记词汇。对许多生长于斯的华裔青少年而言，母语课堂往往受师资与教材所限；技术手段恰好补上了这块短板。

## 缅甸华文教育的百年沉浮

缅甸的华文教育并非新生事物。早在殖民时期，闽粤移民便在仰光、曼德勒等地兴办义学；独立后华人社团继续维持华校网络。然而1965年奈温政府将外侨办学收归国有，大量华校被迫关闭或转入地下。此后数十年，华文教育在夹缝中延续，靠的正是侨社自发的坚守。

## 从"禁办"到"复兴"的政策转圜

转折出现在世纪之交。随着中缅经贸往来升温、中国成为缅甸最大贸易伙伴，华文作为实用语言的地位回升。近年缅甸对华文学校的管理趋于务实，部分华校获准在周末开班。政策的松动虽有限，却足以让一度沉寂的校园文化重新有了呼吸。

## 青年侨胞为何接过传承的接力棒

与父辈"为谋生而保文化"不同，新一代华侨华人更多出于身份认同而投入传承。他们熟悉中英缅三语，也懂技术。杨家迅们看到的，是祖辈口耳相传的课文若再不数字化，就可能随老人离世而散佚。用代码留下语言，成为这代人自然的表达。

## 数字工具如何重塑华文课堂

在缅甸，网络不稳、纸质教材稀缺是华校常态。开源课件、离线汉字库、语音评测小程序，让偏远华校也能开起标准课。数字工具不取代老师，而是把优质内容"分发"到原本覆盖不到的角落。这也正是杨家迅尝试的方向。

## 中国侨务政策的"柔性支撑"

北京将支持海外华文教育列为侨务重点。从"中华文化大乐园"到"寻根之旅"夏令营，从师资培训到教材捐赠，手段多为文化而非政治。这类柔性投入不追求即时回报，意在维系华侨华人与祖籍国的情感纽带——杨家迅们的探索，恰是这套网络末梢的自发回响。

## 东南亚华文教育的共同考题

缅甸的处境并非孤例。泰国、印尼、柬埔寨的华文教育也都经历过限制与复兴的轮回。共同考题是：如何在住在国法律框架内，既融入当地又不丢掉母语？数字化提供了新解法，但师资与可持续性仍是普遍短板。

## 杨家迅们的象征意义

一个人的代码改变不了一整代人的语言命运，但杨家迅代表的方向值得记录：华侨华人的文化传承，正从"老一辈守护"走向"新生代创造"。当代码、平台与社群结合，华文教育获得了跨越国界与代际的韧性。这或许正是侨务工作最乐见的民间活力。

A Myanmar-born ethnic Chinese student, Yang Jiaxun, has drawn attention for building coding tools that help local Chinese schools teach the language. He is not alone—across Southeast Asia, a rising generation of overseas Chinese is using digital means to carry forward heritage. The phenomenon shows the resilience of Chinese-medium education amid turbulent geopolitics, and the quiet effect of Beijing's soft-backed diaspora policy.

## A Cultural Bridge, Written in Code

Yang Jiaxun's story resonates because he turned coding—something seemingly cold—into a bridge for his ancestral language. The tools he built help students at local Chinese schools in Myanmar practise characters and vocabulary more easily. For many ethnic Chinese teenagers raised there, mother-tongue classes are constrained by teachers and textbooks; technology fills exactly that gap.

## A Century of Rise and Fall for Chinese-Medium Education in Myanmar

Chinese-medium education in Myanmar is no novelty. As early as the colonial era, migrants from Fujian and Guangdong ran community schools in Yangon and Mandalay; after independence, Chinese associations kept the network alive. Yet in 1965 the Ne Win government nationalised foreign-run schooling, forcing many schools to close or go underground. For decades, survival depended on the community's quiet persistence.

## From "Ban" to "Revival": A Policy Turn

The turn came around the turn of the century. As China–Myanmar trade warmed and China became Myanmar's largest trading partner, Chinese gained practical value. In recent years Yangon has taken a pragmatic line on Chinese schools, allowing some to run weekend classes. The relaxation is limited, yet enough to let dormant campus life breathe again.

## Why Young Overseas Chinese Pick Up the Baton

Unlike their parents, who preserved culture "to get by," the new generation engages out of identity. They command Chinese, Burmese and English, and they know technology. What Yang and his peers see is simple: if the oral textbooks of their grandparents are not digitised, they may vanish with the elders. Encoding the language becomes their natural expression.

## How Digital Tools Reshape the Chinese Classroom

In Myanmar, unstable networks and scarce printed materials are the norm for Chinese schools. Open-source lessons, offline character libraries and pronunciation apps let remote schools run standard classes. Digital tools do not replace teachers; they deliver good content to corners otherwise unreached. That is precisely the direction Yang is exploring.

## The Soft Backing of China's Diaspora Policy

Beijing lists support for overseas Chinese education as a diaspora priority. From the "Chinese Culture Carnival" to "Root-Seekers" camps, from teacher training to textbook donations, the means are cultural rather than political. Such soft investment seeks no instant return; it sustains the emotional tie between ethnic Chinese abroad and their ancestral land—and Yang's effort is a spontaneous echo at the edge of that network.

## A Shared Test for Chinese-Medium Education in Southeast Asia

Myanmar is not alone. Chinese education in Thailand, Indonesia and Cambodia has also cycled through restriction and revival. The shared question: how to integrate locally without losing the mother tongue, within each host country's legal frame? Digitisation offers a new answer, yet teachers and sustainability remain universal gaps.

## What Yang Jiaxun Symbolises

One person's code will not fix a generation's language fate, but the direction Yang represents deserves note: the cultural transmission of overseas Chinese is shifting from "the old guarding" to "the young creating." When code, platforms and community combine, Chinese education gains resilience across borders and generations. That, perhaps, is the grassroots vitality diaspora work most welcomes.

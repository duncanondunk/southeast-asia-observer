# 一句“好开心”的粤语：泰国（Thailand）总理阿努廷与海外华人的身份政治

2026年11月18日，泰国总理阿努廷在深圳用一句地道粤语“好开心”，形容自己来到广东的心情。当天，他在2026年亚太经合组织工商领导人峰会发表演讲时，以中文问候“大家下午好”，现场响起掌声。这不是他第一次展示中文，但场合值得玩味：这是一场多边经济峰会，不是文化节，也不是寻根之旅。当一位政府首脑放弃外交套话，改用一种方言开口，祖籍就从家族内部的家谱，变成了公开可见的政治信号。

## 一句粤语，把祖籍带进了会场

阿努廷曾接受中国媒体采访，说自己的父母、祖父母、外祖父母都来自中国；他在泰国出生，有中文名字，家里说粤语。他还说，源于这种亲切感，每次见到中国人都很开心。对一位泰国领导人而言，这是个人履历；对区域内外的听众而言，这同时是一段可以被解读的政治文本。

关键在于表达的方式。如果使用正式外交辞令，信息量几乎为零；改用粤语，信息量骤增——它同时传递了血缘、地域、家庭记忆和一个刻意选择的亲近姿态。据中新社报道，这句“好开心”出现在深圳行程的第一天，随后被中文社交平台广泛转发。到这里不妨点评一句：能引发转发的从来不是粤语本身，而是“泰国总理说粤语”这个身份反差。人们消费的，是海外华人身份与国家权力结合时产生的那种戏剧性。

## 从机场迎客到峰会讲台：身份的公开展演

阿努廷的中文展示不是孤立事件，而是一条清晰的时间线。2023年1月，时任泰国副总理兼卫生部长的他亲赴曼谷素万那普国际机场迎接中国游客，用中文说“泰国人欢迎朋友们从中国来泰国玩”，消息传到中国引发广泛讨论。2026年7月，他就任总理后首次正式访华，在社交媒体写下“中泰一家亲”“上海中国”。10月，他又发布中文“很好”，配图是与父亲差瓦拉在桌旁交谈的照片。

这位父亲中文名陈景镇，是泰国联泰工业建筑公司创始人；1998年曾回广东新会祭祖，并为家乡村庄捐资修路。把这些片段连起来看，中文在这里与其说是被动继承的遗产，不如说是一种可被反复调用的公开资源。该问一句：这是真情流露，还是精心表演？诚实的答案是两者兼有——家族记忆提供剧本，外交场合提供舞台。政治上真正要紧的，不是情感是否真实，而是它被反复放置在与中国相关的关键节点上，并且被看见。

## 近三千万粤籍侨胞：一张被低估的关系网

数字在这里很关键。有数据显示，粤籍海外华侨华人近3000万。这不是一个抒情数字，而是分布在区域内外的一整套关系、资本、语言能力与在地知识的存量。

五邑大学教授张国雄接受中新社采访时认为，阿努廷父子的经历体现了海外华人对中华文化的认可，以及华人与故乡割舍不断的血脉亲情。这一解读在侨务话语中很常见，但并不完整。更值得强调的是，这张网络实质上是一种基础设施：1998年回新会为村庄修路，建立的是任何双边协议都难以人工合成的长期地方联系；一位总理在深圳说出粤语，则以几乎为零的政策代价，降低了双方接触的情感门槛。在信任稀缺且昂贵的区域政治里，低成本信号并不廉价。

同一篇报道里的两个案例也在说明同一件事。2025年，南太平洋岛国瑙鲁总统戴维·阿迪昂回到外曾祖父的祖籍地——广东江门开平市赤坎镇中股村寻根谒祖；2026年春节前夕他再度到访，并写下“福”字赠与族亲。2026年9月，马来西亚（Malaysia）南侨机工后人张慧清在昆明街头“偶遇”外公邝金源的照片，她把这段经历拍成视频发布在社交媒体，视频发出十几天后，在广东台山市台城街道岭背村完成认祖。这些家族故事的共性，不是名人效应，而是寻亲行为本身正在成为一门日渐成熟的民间外交技艺。

## 血缘不是政策：软实力的边界

接下来这一点，中文评论常常略过：血缘不是外交政策。阿努廷是泰国总理，对泰国选民负责，在泰国制度内行事；祖父母来自广东，并不意味着他是中国的代理人。把它读成忠诚测试，既不公平，在分析上也懒惰。

这种误读的风险是双向的。过度解读血缘，会把侨胞的文化认同变成忠诚度审查；这在东南亚（Southeast Asia）屡试屡败，因为当地华人社会长期在文化情感与公民归属之间寻找平衡，任何来自外部的压力都会让这种平衡更难维持。而完全低估血缘，又会丢掉一条官方礼宾难以替代的沟通渠道。所以正确的提问不是“他是不是自己人”，而是“这种身份打开了什么样的通道，通道又被如何使用”。海外华人身份的政治价值，恰恰在于它无法被简化为一个亲华标签。它为接触提供了一套比国家间礼宾更温暖、又比盟约更松散的语汇。这种模糊性是有用的——前提是双方都明白它是模糊的。

## 寻根之后，侨务还能做什么

寻根故事刷屏之后，侨务还能做什么？祭祖、档案展、靠谱牒与线索寻亲，这一串动作带来了可观的情绪回报，但情绪本身不是制度。

三件事值得优先。其一，把族谱变成可及的公共档案：村落记录、侨批、移民名册。没有文献，下一代的寻根只能依赖正在变薄的家族记忆。其二，把语言传承当作双向资产。泰国总理口中的粤语不是猎奇谈资，而是一种区域语言生态顽强存续的证据；在学校与媒体支持它，既服务文化传承，也提升区域理解力。其三，始终把返乡者的尊严放在中心。1998年捐资修的那条路之所以重要，是因为它是村庄的路，而不是捐资者的合影背景。祠堂的香火与峰会的讲台不是一回事，但当一位领导人站在讲台上，说出祠堂里使用的语言时，值得追问：一种什么样的区域秩序，正在被一句方言、一次祭祖、一条村路，一点点搭建起来。

## 延伸阅读

**祖籍外交（roots diplomacy）**：以祖籍地、语言与家族记忆为媒介的非官方对外接触方式，通常成本低、象征性强。

**离散群体软实力（diaspora soft power）**：依靠海外侨民的语言、资本与在地网络产生的吸引力，区别于政府主导的文化推广。

**侨批（qiaopi）**：近代侨胞寄回家乡、兼有汇款与家书功能的信函，是研究移民网络的一手档案。

# "So happy" in Cantonese: Thai PM Anutin and the identity politics of overseas Chinese

On 18 November 2026, Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul used a colloquial Cantonese phrase — "hou hoi" ("so happy") — to describe arriving in Guangdong. Earlier that day, speaking at the 2026 APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen, he had greeted the audience in Chinese with "good afternoon, everyone," drawing applause. This was not his first display of Chinese, but the setting is worth noting: a multilateral economic summit, not a cultural festival or a heritage tour. When a head of government abandons protocol formulae and reaches for a dialect, ancestry stops being private genealogy and becomes a public political signal.

## One sentence of Cantonese brings ancestry into the room

Anutin has told Chinese media that his parents, paternal and maternal grandparents all came from China; he was born in Thailand, has a Chinese name, and Cantonese is spoken at home. He added that this sense of closeness makes him happy whenever he meets Chinese people. For a Thai leader, that is biography. For audiences across the region and beyond, it is also a political text inviting interpretation.

The manner of expression is what matters. Formal diplomatic language carries almost no information; a dialect carries a great deal — bloodline, region, family memory and a deliberately chosen posture of closeness. According to China News Service, the remark came on the first day of his Shenzhen trip and was widely reposted on Chinese social platforms. One might ask: what drove the reposts was never Cantonese itself, but the identity gap of "a Thai prime minister speaking Cantonese." What audiences consumed was the drama produced when overseas-Chinese identity intersects with state power.

## From airport welcome to summit podium: identity made public

Anutin's Chinese-language performances form a clear sequence. In January 2023, as deputy prime minister and health minister, he went in person to Bangkok's Suvarnabhumi airport to greet Chinese tourists, telling them in Chinese that Thais welcome friends from China — a story that circulated widely in China. In July 2026, on his first formal visit to China as prime minister, he posted "China and Thailand are one family" and "Shanghai, China" on social media. In October came another post — "very good" — alongside a photo of him talking with his father at a table.

That father, Chawalat Charnvirakul, whose Chinese name is Chen Jingzhen, founded Sino-Thai Engineering and Construction. In 1998 he returned to Xinhui, Guangdong, to worship his ancestors and funded a road for his home village. Read together, these episodes suggest that Chinese is being used less as passively inherited heritage than as a publicly available resource that can be called upon repeatedly. Is this sincerity or performance? The honest answer is both: family memory supplies the script, diplomatic occasions supply the stage. What matters politically is not whether the feeling is genuine, but that it has been placed, again and again, at moments of significance for China — and that it has been seen.

## Thirty million: an underrated network

The numbers matter here. Available data suggest that nearly 30 million overseas Chinese trace their ancestry to Guangdong. This is not a sentimental figure; it is a stock of relationships, capital, linguistic competence and local knowledge distributed across the region and far beyond it.

Zhang Guoxiong, a professor at Wuyi University, told China News Service that the father-and-son story demonstrates overseas Chinese recognition of Chinese culture and an unbreakable blood tie to the hometown. That framing is familiar in diaspora discourse, but it is incomplete. More worth stressing is that this network functions as infrastructure. Returning to Xinhui in 1998 to repair a village road built a durable local connection that no bilateral agreement could manufacture. A prime minister speaking Cantonese in Shenzhen lowered the emotional cost of contact at almost zero policy price. In regional politics where trust is scarce and expensive, cheap signals are not trivial.

Two other cases in the same report make the point. In 2025, David Adeang, president of Nauru, visited the village of Zhonggu in Chikan, Kaiping, Guangdong, from which his great-grandfather came; he returned shortly before the 2026 Spring Festival and wrote the character *fu* (blessing) for relatives. In September 2026, Zhang Huqing, a descendant of a Nanqiao war-mechanics member, "ran into" a photograph of her grandfather Kuang Jinyuan on a Kunming street, filmed the encounter and posted it online; days later she completed ancestral recognition in Taishan, Guangdong. What these family stories share is not celebrity, but the fact that root-seeking itself is becoming a refined craft of unofficial diplomacy.

## Blood is not policy: the limits of soft power

Here is the part Chinese commentary often skips: ancestry is not a foreign-policy position. Anutin is Thailand's prime minister, accountable to Thai voters and operating within Thai institutions. The fact that his grandparents came from Guangdong does not make him a Chinese proxy; reading it as a loyalty test is both unfair and analytically lazy.

The risk runs in both directions. Over-reading bloodlines turns diaspora cultural identity into a screening test for loyalty — an approach that has repeatedly backfired in Southeast Asia, where Chinese communities have long negotiated between cultural attachment and civic belonging, and external pressure only makes that balance harder to hold. Under-reading it, meanwhile, discards a channel of communication that official protocol cannot easily replace. The right question, then, is not "is he one of us?" but "what kind of channel does this identity open, and how is that channel used?" The political value of overseas-Chinese identity lies precisely in its resistance to reduction into a simple pro-China label. It offers a vocabulary for contact warmer than state-to-state protocol yet looser than an alliance. That ambiguity is useful — provided both sides understand it as ambiguous.

## After root-seeking: what diaspora work can still do

Once the root-seeking story has gone viral, what can diaspora work actually do? Ancestral worship, archive exhibitions and searches built on genealogies and fragments of memory produce impressive emotional returns, but emotion is not an institution.

Three priorities stand out. First, turn genealogy into accessible public archives: village records, *qiaopi* letters, migration manifests. Without documentation, the next generation's search depends on family memory that is already thinning. Second, treat language transmission as a two-way asset. Cantonese in a Thai prime minister's mouth is not a curiosity; it is evidence that a regional language ecology has outlived nation-building narratives. Supporting it in schools and media serves both heritage and regional literacy. Third, keep the returnee's dignity at the centre. The road funded in 1998 mattered because it was a village's road, not a donor's photo opportunity. An ancestral altar and a summit podium are not the same thing. Yet when a leader stands at the second and speaks the language of the first, it is worth asking what kind of regional order is being built — one dialect, one ancestral visit, one village road at a time.

## Further reading

**Roots diplomacy**: unofficial engagement mediated by ancestral place, language and family memory — typically low-cost and highly symbolic.

**Diaspora soft power**: attraction generated by migrants' language, capital and local networks, as distinct from state-led cultural promotion.

**Qiaopi letters**: remittance-and-letter documents sent home by overseas Chinese in earlier eras, now a primary archive for studying migration networks.

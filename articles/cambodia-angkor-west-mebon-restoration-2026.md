# 法国修好一座吴哥古寺交还柬埔寨，也顺手提醒北京：软实力是谁的

## 一座古寺，一段世纪的守护
西梅奔寺坐落在吴哥古迹群西池中央的一座小岛上，是吴哥王朝大型水利工程的遗存之一。这座近1000年的古寺，16座塔楼与外围围墙已由一支以法国人为主的团队基本修复完毕。工程于2012年启动，历时14年，是法国这个前殖民宗主国近一个世纪以来主导的吴哥保护系列工程中的最新一项。早在1940年，法国古迹保护团队就曾修缮过它。这种延续本身就是新闻：时至今日，昔日的殖民者仍是柬埔寨最珍贵文化遗产的实际看护人。

## 法国为何执着于吴哥
巴黎为何肯一直为柬埔寨的石头买单？老实说，图的是软实力。吴哥不只是一处废墟，它是柬埔寨国旗上的图案，也是这个国家旅游经济的命脉。替它修缮，就等于在殖民政府撤走很久之后，依然留在柬埔寨人的集体记忆里。法国远东学院（EFEO）自20世纪初便研究并守护吴哥，这份机构记忆如今成了一种不动声色的筹码。正如吴哥保护管理局的一名柬埔寨官员所说，法国人握有大量文献与技艺，而柬埔寨人眼下还不具备独立修复这些古迹所需的全部能力。这话听着刺耳？也许是。但它也坦率地承认了一种依赖。

## 软实力，不只是一堆石头
不妨拿中国做个对照。无论怎么算，北京都是柬埔寨头号的经济伙伴——最大的投资国、援助国与债权方，也是公路、桥梁和大坝的建造者。然而在吴哥的文化声望上，法国至今仍占着上风。中国在区域内也资助过若干修复项目，但柬埔寨遗产的「王冠明珠」，眼下仍是在巴黎手中打磨。马克龙此行裹着两国元首揭幕的典礼外衣，恰恰是在提醒世人：影响力不止看贷款与港口。一座修好的古寺所生成的善意，是国际收支顺差买不来的。

## 柬埔寨的平衡术：巴黎、北京与石构
金边心里很清楚自己在做什么。它一手接下中国的基础建设资金，一手笑纳法国的文化庇护。这两笔账并不矛盾，而是一种策略。柬埔寨从中国拿到公路与软贷款桥梁，又从另一个首都拿到遗产声望与外交掩护。对于一个夹在大国博弈之间的小国而言，这正是教科书式的对冲。西梅奔寺的揭幕，让柬埔寨得以展示一位西方伙伴，而又不至于触怒东方那一位——因为法国的角色恰好不具威胁，且披着「人类共同遗产」的外衣。

## 当地人的算盘
常被忽略的是人的维度。参与修复的柬埔寨工人对记者说，他们很乐意与法国人合作：既能接受专业培训，又能拿到薪水，退休后还有养老金。在一个熟练文保人才稀缺的国家，这项工程同时是一条人才培养的管道。法国实际上是在培育下一代柬埔寨守护人——一批会记得「谁教过自己」的人。软实力落到最细处，就是一份养老金、一门手艺。

## 法语国家峰会的舞台
时间点绝非偶然。在金边举行的法语国家峰会，是马克龙投射法国「印太」姿态的舞台——一个把法国塑造成太平洋与印度洋强国、并在前法属印度支那深耕多年的叙事。遗产外交是这一战略里最温和的锋刃：与军售或贷款包不同，修一座庙几乎不会招来反弹。这是一种穿着凉鞋、面带微笑的影响力。至于它能否扛过中国的支票簿，则是另一个问题。

## 延伸阅读
**法国远东学院（EFEO）**：自20世纪初便研究并守护吴哥的法国研究机构，长期掌握吴哥修复的文献与技艺。
**法语国家峰会**：法语国家定期举行的首脑会议，被巴黎用作在印太地区施展文化外交的平台。
**吴哥保护管理局（APSARA Authority）**：负责吴哥考古公园保护与管理事务的柬埔寨政府机构。

# France Restores a Thousand-Year-Old Angkor Temple and Returns It to Cambodia — a Quiet Reminder to Beijing of Who Wields Soft Power

## A temple, a century in the making
West Mebon Temple sits on an island in the Western Baray, one of the great hydraulic works of the Khmer Empire, within the Angkor complex. Built roughly 1000 years ago, its sixteen towers and enclosing walls have now been largely restored by a French-led team. The work began in 2012 and took 14 years; it is the latest in a series of conservation projects that France, the former colonial power, has directed at Angkor for almost a century. A French team first repaired the temple in 1940. That continuity is itself the headline: in the twenty-first century, the former coloniser is still the de facto custodian of Cambodia's most valuable cultural asset.

## Why France clings to Angkor
Why does Paris keep paying for Cambodian stone? The honest answer is soft power. Angkor is not merely a ruin; it is the emblem on Cambodia's flag and the backbone of its tourism economy. To tend it is to remain present in the Cambodian imagination long after the colonial administration left. The French School of the Far East (École française d'Extrême-Orient, or EFEO) has studied and conserved Angkor since the early twentieth century, and that institutional memory is now a quiet form of leverage. As one Cambodian official from the APSARA Authority put it, the French hold the documents and the know-how that Cambodians do not yet possess to restore the temples on their own. Flattering? Perhaps. But it is also a frank admission of dependence.

## Soft power, not just stone
Consider the contrast with China. By any measure, Beijing is Cambodia's dominant economic partner — its largest investor, aid donor and creditor, and the builder of roads, bridges and dams. Yet in the cultural prestige of Angkor, France still holds the upper hand. China has funded some restoration work elsewhere in the region, but the crown jewel of Cambodian heritage is, for now, polished in Paris. Macron's visit, wrapped in the ceremony of a heads-of-state unveiling, is a reminder that influence is not measured only in loans and ports. A restored temple generates goodwill that a balance-of-payments surplus cannot buy.

## Cambodia's balancing act: Paris, Beijing and the stones
Phnom Penh knows exactly what it is doing. It takes Chinese infrastructure money with both hands while welcoming French cultural patronage with a smile. The two accounts are not contradictory; they are strategy. Cambodia gets roads and soft-loan bridges from one capital, and heritage prestige and diplomatic cover from another. For a small state wedged between great-power competition, this is textbook hedging. The West Mebon unveiling lets Cambodia display a Western partner without upsetting the Eastern one — because the French role is, conveniently, non-threatening and cloaked in the language of universal heritage.

## What the locals count on
The human dimension is easy to overlook. Cambodian workers on the restoration project told reporters they were glad to partner with the French: they receive professional training, a wage, and even a pension after retirement. In a country where skilled conservators are scarce, the project is also a training pipeline. France is, in effect, cultivating the next generation of Cambodian custodians — people who will remember who taught them. Soft power, at its most granular, is a pension and a skill.

## The Francophonie stage
The timing is no accident. The Francophonie Summit in Phnom Penh is Macron's platform to project France's Indo-Pacific posture — a vision of France as a Pacific and Indian Ocean power with deep roots in the former Indochina. Heritage diplomacy is the gentle edge of that strategy: unlike arms sales or loan packages, restoring a temple rarely provokes a backlash. It is influence that wears sandals and a smile. Whether it can outlast China's chequebook is another question.

## Further reading
**École française d'Extrême-Orient (EFEO)**: the French research institution that has studied and conserved Angkor since the early twentieth century, holding much of the documentation and craft behind its restoration.
**Francophonie Summit**: a regular gathering of French-speaking states, used by Paris as a platform for cultural diplomacy across the Indo-Pacific.
**APSARA Authority**: the Cambodian government body responsible for the protection and management of the Angkor Archaeological Park.

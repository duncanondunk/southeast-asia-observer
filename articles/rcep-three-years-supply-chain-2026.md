# RCEP 生效三年：亚太产业链的"去风险"与重构

《区域全面经济伙伴关系协定》（RCEP）于 2022 年生效，至 2026 年已运行近四年、全面落地逾三年。作为覆盖全球约三成 GDP 的最大自贸协定，它本应是亚太产业链的"稳定器"。但在地缘张力与"去风险"叙事交织下，RCEP 的三年，既是贸易红利释放的三年，也是产业链被重新计算的三年。

## 红利：关税减让与"区域原产"的粘合

RCEP 最直接的红利，是累计的关税减让与统一的原产地规则。其"区域累积"规则允许成员国的中间品相互抵扣，使"在中国设计、在越南组装、在东盟销售"的链条更顺。三年里，区域内贸易占比上升，中小企业借累积规则降低了合规成本。对东盟而言，RCEP 是把"世界工厂"外延到整个东亚—东盟网络的关键黏合剂。

## "去风险"的逆风：规则之外的现实

红利之外是逆风。美欧推动的"去风险"与友岸外包，使部分高敏感环节（半导体、关键矿物）被刻意移出东亚网络。RCEP 的规则红利，难以对冲政治驱动的有意脱钩。更微妙的是，区域内也出现"近岸""友岸"的偏好，使纯粹的成本逻辑被安全逻辑部分改写。

## 重构：产业链的"韧性"再布局

面对逆风，企业用脚投票，推动产业链重构。其一是"中国+1"的延续——把部分产能布点东盟，以分散单一依赖。其二是"回岸"与"友岸"的试探——高端环节回流发达经济体或转向盟友。其三是区域内部的深化——东盟借 RCEP 提升自身在链条中的节点地位。三种力量拉扯，使亚太产业链从"效率优先"转向"效率与韧性并重"。

## 悬念：RCEP 能否顶住地缘

RCEP 的最大悬念，是其规则框架能否在 geopolitical 张力中保持吸引力。若成员间的政治互信继续磨损，规则红利会被现实抵消；若能在分歧中守住"经济优先"的底色，RCEP 仍将是亚太最务实的压舱石。印度的缺席，也始终是协定扩容的未竟之题。

## 值得观察的几点

其一，区域累积规则的利用率，是否随企业熟悉度上升而提高。其二，"去风险"对高敏感产业的实质搬离规模。其三，东盟能否借 RCEP 提升节点地位，而非仅做承接转移的中转站。

归根结底，RCEP 生效三年，是一份"有红利、有逆风"的答卷。它证明了规则能降低交易成本，却证明不了规则能战胜地缘政治。亚太产业链的下一程，取决于各国愿不愿意在算计安全的同时，仍给效率留一扇门。

## 延伸阅读

**RCEP（Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership）**：东盟十国与中国、日本、韩国、澳大利亚、新西兰组成的自贸协定，2022 年生效，覆盖全球约三成 GDP。

**区域累积规则（Regional Cumulative Rules of Origin）**：允许成员国的原材料与中间品在协定区域内累计计算，以判定产品是否享惠，降低供应链合规成本。

**去风险（De-risking）**：发达经济体在不完全脱钩前提下，降低对单一国家关键供应链依赖的策略，常伴随友岸外包。

# RCEP at Three Years: 'De-risking' and the Restructuring of Asia-Pacific Supply Chains

The Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) took effect in 2022 and, by 2026, has run nearly four years with over three years of full implementation. As the world's largest FTA covering roughly 30% of global GDP, it was meant to be the 'stabiliser' of Asia-Pacific supply chains. But amid geopolitical tension and the 'de-risking' narrative, RCEP's three years are both a period of trade-dividend release and a period of supply chains being recalculated.

## Dividends: tariff cuts and the glue of 'regional origin'

RCEP's most direct dividend is cumulative tariff cuts and a unified rules of origin. Its 'regional accumulation' lets intermediates among members offset each other, smoothing chains like 'designed in China, assembled in Vietnam, sold in ASEAN'. Over three years, intra-regional trade's share rose, and SMEs lowered compliance costs via accumulation. For ASEAN, RCEP is the key adhesive extending the 'workshop of the world' across the East-Asia–ASEAN network.

## Headwinds of 'de-risking': reality beyond the rules

Beyond dividends are headwinds. The US–Europe push for 'de-risking' and friend-shoring deliberately moves some high-sensitivity links (semiconductors, critical minerals) out of the East-Asian network. RCEP's rule dividends struggle to offset politically driven decoupling. More subtly, preferences for 'near-shoring' and 'friend-shoring' emerge within the region too, partly rewriting pure cost logic with security logic.

## Restructuring: the 'resilience' re-layout of chains

Facing headwinds, firms vote with their feet, driving restructuring. One is the continuation of 'China Plus One' — placing some capacity in ASEAN to diversify single-country dependence. Two is the试探 of 'reshoring' and 'friend-shoring' — high-end links returning to developed economies or shifting to allies. Three is deepening within the region — ASEAN raising its own node status via RCEP. These three forces pull, shifting Asia-Pacific chains from 'efficiency first' to 'efficiency and resilience together'.

## Suspense: can RCEP withstand geopolitics

RCEP's biggest suspense is whether its rule framework keeps attracting under geopolitical tension. If members' political trust keeps eroding, rule dividends are offset by reality; if they hold the 'economy first' baseline amid differences, RCEP remains the region's most pragmatic ballast. India's absence also remains the unfinished question of the pact's enlargement.

## A few things to watch

First, whether utilisation of regional accumulation rises as firms grow familiar. Second, the actual scale of high-sensitivity industries' relocation under 'de-risking'. Third, whether ASEAN raises node status via RCEP rather than merely being a transit station for shifted capacity.

In the end, RCEP at three years is a report card of 'dividends and headwinds'. It proves rules can lower transaction costs, but not that rules can defeat geopolitics. The next leg of Asia-Pacific chains depends on whether countries, while calculating security, still leave a door open for efficiency.

## Further reading

**RCEP (Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership)**: An FTA of ASEAN's ten plus China, Japan, South Korea, Australia and New Zealand, in force since 2022, covering about 30% of global GDP.

**Regional Cumulative Rules of Origin**: Rules letting raw materials and intermediates among members accumulate within the agreement area to determine preferential eligibility, lowering supply-chain compliance cost.

**De-risking**: A strategy by developed economies to reduce dependence on a single country's critical supply chains without full decoupling, often paired with friend-shoring.

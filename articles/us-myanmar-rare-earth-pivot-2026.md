# 美国对缅政策悄然转向：以和平进程换稀土资源

缅甸这块被内战撕裂的土地，最近成了华盛顿重新盘算的棋子。据路透社报道，美国正调整自2021年政变以来长达数年的对缅孤立政策，打算借推动和平进程扩大自身影响力，换取美国企业涉足缅甸丰富的稀土矿藏，以此撬动中国在关键矿产供应链上的垄断地位。说白了，美国想把「调停人」这层身份，兑换成「分矿人」的门票。

## 政策转向：从「孤立」到「以和谈换资源」

过去几年，西方对缅甸的做法简单直接：制裁军方、孤立政权、在道义上站队民主派。这套打法政治上没错，实际效果却有限——军方照样掌权，内战愈演愈烈，而缅甸地下的稀土，几乎全部流向了中国。

如今风向变了。美国官员近期低调会晤缅甸政府代表，连负责情报与研究事务的助理国务卿也参与其中，双方甚至专门讨论了如何获取缅甸的稀土资源。这已不再是「孤立」，而是一场带着资源算计的接触。

## 一份被公开的「国家整合战略」

这场转向并非空穴来风。美国驻缅甸使团起草了一份「国家整合战略（Integrated Country Strategy）」文件，8月31日获批，10月16日在美国国务院网站公开。文件写得直白：使团将致力于推动缅甸交战各方达成停火，争取在2030年前达成持久和平协议。

更关键的是手段。文件称，美国计划协助举办至少三场多方论坛，推动各方就财政联邦制（fiscal federalism）与领土划界达成具体协议，其目的之一，是为开发稀土资源、打击电信诈骗园区（scam compounds）创造机会。换言之，和平框架本身就是一份资源开发的前置合同。

## 稀土为何成为棋眼

为什么是稀土？缅甸北部克钦邦、掸邦一带蕴藏大量稀土，尤以重稀土为甚，是电动车马达、风力发电、军工与半导体不可或缺的原料。更微妙的是，这些矿藏恰恰位于冲突地区，目前几乎全部出口到中国。中国因此握住了从开采到精炼的关键环节。

对美国而言，这是一块心病。在电动汽车与军工供应链「去风险」的叙事下，任何能绕开中国、直接触达矿源的路径，都被视为战略资产。缅甸的稀土，于是从「遥远的内战副产品」，升级为「大国竞争的必争之地」。

## 理想丰满，现实骨感

但这笔账，算得有点早。文件自己都承认，并未说明美国是否打算解除对缅制裁，或解决阻碍投资的其他合规障碍。现实是，美国至今仍对缅甸领导人敏昂莱及一批军方支持的官员和企业实施金融制裁；只不过特朗普政府已开始将部分缅甸个人与企业移出制裁名单。

更要命的是矿在哪里。缅甸主要稀土矿藏位于冲突前线，武装派系林立——既有亲民主武装，也有与军方对抗数十年的少数民族武装。美国想「参与各方谈判」，等于要同时与昔日对手坐到一张桌子上。联合国估算，这场内战已造成约10万人死亡、超过350万人流离失所。在这样一个烂摊子里谈「2030年前持久和平」，与其说是战略，不如说是愿望。

## 算盘之外，还有两笔账

稀土之外，美国还打着另外的算盘。其一，扩大对缅出口以缩小双边贸易逆差，并争取至少三项、每项价值超过500万美元（约3600万元人民币，报道折算约640万新元，亦约3600万元人民币）的商业或投资交易。其二，与缅甸执法机构合作，打击针对美国公民的电信诈骗园区，并扩大在美缅甸公民的遣返。

这两笔账，反倒暴露了美国的真实焦虑：一边想分矿，一边又头疼诈骗园区与移民问题——而这两件事，恰恰都根植于缅甸的失序。

## 延伸阅读

**稀土（Rare earths）**：17种关键矿产（critical minerals）的统称，广泛用于电动车马达、风电、军工与半导体；中国占全球精炼产能约九成，是供应链博弈的核心。

**财政联邦制（Fiscal federalism）**：中央与地方之间税收与支出权力的分配框架，是缅甸和平谈判中绕不开的制度难题，也直接关系资源收益的归属。

**电信诈骗园区（Scam compounds）**：以博彩、诈骗为业的园区，常牵涉人口贩运，既是缅甸失序的缩影，也是中美共同关心的治理盲区。

# Washington Quietly Rewrites Its Myanmar Playbook: Peace Process for Rare Earths

The war-torn soil of Myanmar has become, once again, a piece on Washington's chessboard. According to Reuters, the United States is recalibrating the isolation policy it has maintained since the 2021 coup, intending to use the push for a peace process to expand its own leverage — and to win American firms access to Myanmar's abundant rare-earth deposits, thereby prying open China's monopoly over critical-mineral supply chains. In plain terms, Washington wants to convert the status of "mediator" into a ticket to a share of the mines.

## The pivot: from isolation to "peace for resources"

For the past few years, the Western approach to Myanmar was simple: sanction the military, isolate the regime, and take the moral side of the democrats. The playbook was politically correct but practically limited — the military kept its grip, the civil war intensified, and Myanmar's rare earths flowed almost entirely to China.

The wind has shifted. US officials have recently met quietly with representatives of the Myanmar government, and even the Assistant Secretary of State for Intelligence and Research took part in related talks, where the two sides discussed specifically how to access Myanmar's rare earths. This is no longer "isolation"; it is an engagement laced with a resource calculus.

## A published "Integrated Country Strategy"

The shift did not come from nowhere. The US mission in Myanmar drafted an "Integrated Country Strategy" document, approved on 31 August and published on the State Department website on 16 October. The language is blunt: the mission will work to broker a ceasefire among Myanmar's warring parties and aim for a durable peace agreement by 2030.

The means are the crux. The file states that the United States plans to help convene at least three multi-party forums to advance concrete agreements — or policy proposals — on fiscal federalism and territorial demarcation, creating opportunities to develop rare earths and crack down on scam compounds. In other words, the peace framework itself is a pre-contract for resource extraction.

## Why rare earth is the prize

Why rare earths? Northern Myanmar's Kachin and Shan states hold vast deposits, especially heavy rare earths, indispensable to electric-vehicle motors, wind power, defence hardware and semiconductors. The twist is that these deposits sit precisely in conflict zones and are exported almost entirely to China, which thus controls the chain from extraction to refining.

For Washington, this is a chronic headache. Under the "de-risking" narrative for EV and defence supply chains, any path that bypasses China and reaches the ore directly is treated as a strategic asset. Myanmar's rare earths have thus been upgraded from a distant by-product of civil war to contested ground in great-power competition.

## Ambition meets a brutal reality

But the accounting is premature. The document itself admits it does not clarify whether the United States intends to lift sanctions on Myanmar or resolve other compliance obstacles blocking investment. The reality is that Washington still imposes financial sanctions on the junta leader Min Aung Hlaing and a roster of military-backed officials and firms — though the Trump administration has begun removing some Myanmar individuals and entities from the sanctions list.

The deadlier problem is where the ore lies. Myanmar's main rare-earth deposits are on active conflict fronts, amid a thicket of armed factions — pro-democracy militias alongside ethnic armed organisations that have fought the military for decades. For the United States to "join the negotiations among all parties" means sitting at one table with yesterday's adversaries. The United Nations estimates the civil war has killed roughly 100,000 people and displaced more than 3.5 million. Talking of a "durable peace by 2030" amid such wreckage is less a strategy than a wish.

## Two more calculations beyond the minerals

Beyond rare earths, Washington is running other tallies. First, it plans to expand exports to Myanmar to narrow the bilateral trade deficit and secure at least three commercial or investment deals, each worth over US$5 million (roughly S$6.4 million). Second, it lists cooperation with Myanmar's law-enforcement agencies as a priority — to step up action against scam compounds that target American citizens and to broaden the repatriation of Myanmar nationals in the United States.

These two tallies expose Washington's real anxiety: it wants a share of the mines, yet is also bedevilled by scam compounds and migration — two problems that both grow directly out of Myanmar's disorder.

## Further reading

**Rare earths**: A collective term for 17 critical minerals, used widely in EV motors, wind power, defence and semiconductors. China accounts for roughly 90 per cent of global refining capacity, making it the core of supply-chain contestation.

**Fiscal federalism**: The framework for dividing taxation and spending powers between central and local governments — an inevitable institutional hurdle in Myanmar's peace talks, and directly tied to who collects the rents from resources.

**Scam compounds**: Zones built around gambling and fraud, often entangled with human trafficking. They are both a symptom of Myanmar's breakdown and a governance blind spot of shared concern to both China and the United States.

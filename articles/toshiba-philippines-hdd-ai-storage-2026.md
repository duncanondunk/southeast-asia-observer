# 沉默的底座：东芝600亿日元扩建菲律宾硬盘厂，人工智能算力的另一场军备竞赛

当全世界的目光都盯着图形处理器（GPU）与数据中心的液冷塔时，在菲律宾吕宋岛拉古纳科技园（Laguna Technopark）的一间厂房里，另一场关乎人工智能（AI）算力的军备竞赛正悄然打响——这一次，主角不是芯片，而是被认为"老套"的机械硬盘。

## 现场：拉古纳科技园的第三十年

东芝（Toshiba）在菲律宾的硬盘制造基地，最近迎来第三十周年；也正是在这个节点，它发出了第一批来自扩建"近线"（nearline）硬盘专用产线的产品。所谓近线硬盘，是介于频繁读写的"热数据"与长期归档的"冷数据"之间、为人工智能数据湖与训练集提供海量近线存储的那一层。东芝把约600亿日元（约30亿元人民币）砸向这里的扩产，计划在2027财年把面向人工智能数据中心的硬盘产能较2025财年翻一番。这是它近五年来首次在硬盘领域下如此重注。

## 数字：600亿日元，份额从10%冲向30%

机械硬盘是一个高度成熟、由西部数据（Western Digital）、希捷（Seagate）与东芝三家垄断的市场。按存储容量计算，东芝目前份额仅略高于10%，它的中期目标却是要冲到约30%。新产线还将把单盘容量提升最高40%，并推进检测与洁净室作业的自动化——据称可将扩建通常所需的新增用工缩减约40%。技术路线上，东芝计划2027年量产40TB级（采用微波辅助磁记录技术）、2030年推出65TB级、此后迈向100TB级产品。换句话说，它押注的不是今天，而是人工智能数据量暴增后那个更"重"的未来。

## 错位：GPU闪耀，硬盘沉默

这里恰恰藏着一个被忽视的错位。生成式人工智能的叙事里，主角永远是算力与显卡；但在每一个图形处理器集群背后，是被称作"温存"与"冷存"的存储层级——固态硬盘（SSD）虽快，成本却约为机械硬盘的20倍，这把海量存储牢牢钉在机械硬盘上。研究机构国际数据公司（IDC）预计，到2030年全球数据量将达718泽字节（ZB），是2024年的四倍，其中约六成仍将落在机械硬盘上。当希捷早在今年1月就宣布2026年的近线产能已全部售罄，短缺早已不是传闻：高容量硬盘的交付周期已从行业均衡时的16周拉长到约50周。

## 博弈：菲律宾制造与"中国+1"的算盘

东芝把扩产的主场放在菲律宾，而非本土日本，本身就是一部微缩的供应链迁徙史。在"中国+1"（China plus one）与人工智能硬件本土化的双重压力下，东南亚正成为承接高端电子制造的关键节点——从马来西亚的光学元件、越南的半导体封装，到菲律宾的硬盘与汽车电子。菲律宾的劳动力成本与既有产业集群，让拉古纳这样的园区成了精密制造的理想落脚点。但对马尼拉而言，这既是就业与升级的机遇，也意味着把经济命脉更深地系于跨国公司的资本周期与地缘风向。

## 隐忧：扩产能解近渴，难解远忧

把视角拉回更普遍的位置：东芝的豪赌，本质上是人工智能基础设施"底座化"的缩影——当算力竞赛向下游蔓延，连最不起眼的螺丝钉（这里是磁盘）都被卷入了产能军备。但隐忧同样清晰：希捷与西部数据的股价在东芝消息后一度下挫（盘前分别跌约一成与近7%），市场担忧的是两年后供过于求、价格雪崩。更长远看，机械硬盘终要面对固态硬盘的成本下探与容量追赶。东芝能否在30%的份额目标兑现前，跑赢技术替代的时钟，仍是未知数。一场围绕"沉默底座"的竞赛已经开场，而它的胜负，未必写在今天的股价里。

## 延伸阅读

**近线硬盘（nearline HDD）**：介于高频读写的"热存储"与长期归档的"冷存储"之间的大容量硬盘层级，主要为人工智能数据湖、训练集与云服务的海量温冷数据提供近线存储。

**微波辅助磁记录（Microwave-Assisted Magnetic Recording, MAMR）**：一种通过微波暂时降低介质矫顽力以提升写入密度的硬盘技术，是东芝40TB级产品拟采用的关键路线。

**泽字节（zettabyte, ZB）**：数据量的计量单位，1泽字节等于10的21次方字节；国际数据公司预计2030年全球数据量将达718泽字节。

# The Silent Base: Toshiba's ¥60bn Philippine Plant and the Other AI Arms Race

While the spotlight stays on GPUs and liquid cooling, Toshiba is betting ¥60 billion (about US$380 million) on a hard-disk line in the Philippines — the unglamorous storage layer the AI buildout cannot do without.

## The scene: Laguna Technopark at thirty

Toshiba's hard-disk manufacturing base in the Philippines recently marked its thirtieth anniversary — and, at that very milestone, shipped its first units from a newly expanded nearline production line. Nearline drives sit between frequently accessed "hot" data and long-term archived "cold" data, providing the massive warm-and-cold storage that AI data lakes and training sets depend on. Toshiba is pouring roughly ¥60 billion (about US$380 million) into expanding the site, aiming to double its AI data-center hard-disk capacity by fiscal 2027 relative to fiscal 2025. It is the company's first major HDD capital outlay in roughly five years.

## The numbers: ¥60bn, a push from 10% toward 30%

The hard-disk market is mature and concentrated, monopolised by Western Digital, Seagate and Toshiba. By storage capacity Toshiba holds just over 10% today, yet its medium-term target is about 30%. The new lines will lift per-unit capacity by up to 40% and automate inspection and cleanroom work — reportedly cutting the extra labour an expansion would normally require by around 40%. On the technology roadmap, Toshiba plans a 40-terabyte-class drive in fiscal 2027 using microwave-assisted magnetic recording across twelve platters, a 65-TB-class product by 2030, and 100-TB-class drives thereafter. In short, it is betting not on today but on a heavier data future.

## The mismatch: GPUs shine, drives stay quiet

Here lies an overlooked mismatch. In the generative-AI story the hero is always compute and graphics cards; yet behind every GPU cluster sits a storage tier of "warm" and "cold" layers. Solid-state drives are fast but cost roughly 20 times a hard disk, which pins massive storage to spinning platters. IDC projects global data will reach 718 zettabytes by 2030 — four times the 2024 level — with about 60% still on hard disks. When Seagate said back in January that its 2026 nearline capacity was already sold out, the shortage was no rumour: lead times for high-capacity drives have stretched from a balanced-market 16 weeks to roughly 50.

## The game: Philippine manufacturing and the "China plus one" calculus

That Toshiba placed its expansion in the Philippines rather than in Japan is itself a miniature of supply-chain migration. Under the twin pressures of "China plus one" and AI-hardware localization, Southeast Asia is becoming a critical node for high-end electronics — from optical components in Malaysia and semiconductor packaging in Vietnam to hard disks and auto electronics in the Philippines. The country's labour costs and existing industrial clusters make parks like Laguna ideal landing spots for precision manufacturing. For Manila, though, this is both an opportunity for jobs and upgrading and a deeper tethering of its economic fate to the capital cycles and geopolitical winds of multinational firms.

## The catch: near-term relief, long-term doubt

Step back to the broader proposition: Toshiba's gamble is a microcosm of how AI infrastructure is being "basified" — as the compute race spills downstream, even the humblest component (here, the disk) is pulled into a capacity arms race. But the doubts are just as clear. Seagate and Western Digital shares slipped after Toshiba's news (down about 10% and nearly 7% in premarket), with the market fearing oversupply and a price collapse two years out. Longer term, hard disks must still face SSDs' cost decline and capacity catch-up. Whether Toshiba can hit its 30% share target before the clock of technological substitution runs out remains an open question. A contest over the "silent base" has begun, and its outcome will not necessarily be written in today's stock prices.

## Further reading

**Nearline HDD**: A high-capacity drive tier between frequently accessed "hot" storage and long-term "cold" archival, providing the warm-and-cold bulk storage that AI data lakes, training sets and cloud services rely on.

**Microwave-Assisted Magnetic Recording (MAMR)**: A hard-disk technique that uses microwaves to temporarily lower the medium's coercivity and raise areal density; the route Toshiba's 40-TB-class drive is set to adopt.

**Zettabyte (ZB)**: A unit of data volume equal to 10²¹ bytes; IDC projects global data will reach 718 zettabytes by 2030.

# "蓝潮"来袭？马来西亚巫统与伊党的马来票大整合及其天花板

2026年8月8日，马来西亚（Malaysia）独立民调机构巧思中心（Ilham Centre）给出一组刺眼的数字：若国民阵线（国阵）与国民联盟（国盟）把在森美兰州选举中的合作模式扩及全西马，集中马来选票掀起"蓝潮"，有机会在来届大选拿下西马166席中的122席——比组阁门槛多出10席。这对当前由希望联盟主导的联邦政府，无疑是一记重锤。

## 什么是"蓝潮"

"蓝潮"指的是国阵（以巫统 Umno 为主、传统蓝色）与国盟（以伊斯兰党 PAS 为主）的马来民族主义选票整合。双方最近的"蜜月"始于柔佛与森美兰两场州选：伊党在无国盟候选人上阵的选区号召支持者投给国阵，国阵也在森州选前达成共识，避免同区互搏，集中火力对抗希盟。结果大胜，令两阵营看到"马来人政治大整合"的甜头。

西马有138个选区马来选民占比过半，其中86个更达65%以上。对国阵与国盟而言，这是"蓝潮"的主战场；而城市与城镇边缘、希盟与之旗鼓相当的选区，才是真正决定胜负的"绞肉机"。

## 整合的关键：基层互投

巧思中心执行总监希索慕丁点出前提：主导国阵的巫统与主导国盟的伊党，能否动员各自基层接受对方候选人、并把票投给对方——避免多角战稀释选票。这在柔、森两州相对容易，因为伊党在当地实力较弱、让步空间大。

但真正的考验在彭亨、霹雳、雪兰莪——三方（国阵、国盟、希盟）势均力敌，议席协商将极其艰难。换言之，"蓝潮"的物理基础存在，政治操作却卡在"谁让谁"的零和博弈上。

## 天花板：伊党强州与"造王者"逻辑

巫统主席阿末扎希坦言，"蓝潮"未必能抵达吉兰丹、登嘉楼、吉打等伊党执政州——合作仍取决于国盟的诚意。新加坡尤索夫伊萨东南亚研究院的哈钦森也质疑：预言过于乐观，国阵与国盟未必能在彼此势均力敌的州达成合作。

更微妙的是东马逻辑。巧思中心测算，若国阵国盟联手拿下122席，将无需向来扮演"造王者"的沙巴、砂拉越政党即可组阁——这既削弱东马议价权，也可能反过来刺激东马政党重新校准阵营。马来西亚政治的天平，从来不只由西马的马来票决定。

## 一个克制的判断

"蓝潮"未必真能席卷全马，但它揭示的结构性事实足够清晰：马来选民的板块整合，正在重塑马来西亚的政党政治地图；而整合的上限，既不只在选票数学，更在巫统与伊党能否让渡利益、以及东马与城市选民如何回应。2022年以来的悬峙与重组尚未终结，下一届大选，仍可能生出新的变数。

## 延伸阅读

**国民阵线（Barisan Nasional, 国阵）**：马来西亚长期执政联盟，以巫统（UMNO）为核心，传统代表马来民族主义与世俗右翼，党色为蓝。

**国民联盟（Perikatan Nasional, 国盟）**：以伊斯兰党（PAS）为核心的马来—伊斯兰政治联盟，与国阵在争取马来选票上既是对手也是潜在伙伴。

**巧思中心（Ilham Centre）**：马来西亚独立民调与政治分析机构，常就大选与马来政治发布选情评估。

# A "Blue Wave" in Malaysia? UMNO–PAS Malay-Vote Consolidation and Its Ceiling

On 8 August 2026, independent pollster Ilham Centre floated a striking number: if the National Front (Barisan Nasional, BN) and the National Alliance (Perikatan Nasional, PN) extended their electoral cooperation in Negeri Sembilan across Peninsular Malaysia and consolidated Malay votes into a "blue wave," they could take 122 of the 166 Peninsular seats in the next general election — 10 above the threshold to form a government. For the current Pakatan Harapan-led federal administration, that is a heavy hammer.

## What the "blue wave" means

The "blue wave" refers to the consolidation of Malay-nationalist votes between BN — built around UMNO and its traditional blue — and PN, built around the Islamic Party (PAS). The recent "honeymoon" began in the Johor and Negeri Sembilan state polls: PAS urged supporters to vote BN where no PN candidate stood, and BN agreed before the Negeri Sembilan poll to avoid clashing in the same seats, focusing fire on Pakatan Harapan. The sweeping wins convinced both camps of the lure of "Malay political integration."

Of the 166 Peninsular seats, 138 have Malay-majority electors, 86 of them above 65 per cent. For BN and PN these are the blue wave's main battlefield; the urban and peri-urban seats where PH is competitive are the real "grinders" that decide outcomes.

## The key to consolidation: grassroots mutual voting

Ilham Centre's executive director Mohamad Hisomuddin spelled out the premise: whether UMNO, which leads BN, and PAS, which leads PN, can mobilise their bases to accept each other's candidates and vote for the other side — avoiding multi-cornered fights that split the vote. That was easier in Johor and Negeri Sembilan, where PAS was weak and had room to concede.

The real test lies in Pahang, Perak, and Selangor — three-way contests where seat negotiations will be brutally hard. In other words, the blue wave's physical base exists, but the political choreography snags on the zero-sum question of who yields to whom.

## The ceiling: PAS strongholds and the "kingmaker" logic

UMNO president Ahmad Zahid Hamidi conceded the blue wave may not reach Kelantan, Terengganu, and Kedah — PAS-led states — and cooperation still depends on PN's sincerity. ISEAS – Yusof Ishak Institute's Malcolm Hutchinson also questions the over-optimistic forecast, doubting BN and PN can cooperate in states where they are evenly matched.

More subtly, there is the East Malaysia logic. Ilham's math says a 122-seat BN–PN haul would let them form a government without the Sabah and Sarawak parties that have long played "kingmaker" — weakening East Malaysian leverage and possibly pushing those parties to recalibrate. Malaysia's balance of power has never been decided by Peninsular Malay votes alone.

## A restrained judgment

The blue wave may not sweep the whole country, but the structural fact it reveals is clear enough: the consolidation of the Malay electoral bloc is redrawing Malaysia's party-political map; and its ceiling lies not only in the arithmetic but in whether UMNO and PAS can cede interests, and how East Malaysia and urban voters respond. The hung-parliament flux and recombination since 2022 is not over, and the next election may yet produce new variables.

# 日清制粉落子越南：外资重估"工厂+市场"双枢纽

据日经亚洲（Nikkei Asia）报道，日本面粉与食品巨头日清制粉（Nippn）将在越南南部同奈省设立当地法人，并建设一座预拌粉工厂，计划2027年投产，年产能约4300吨，注册资本约合1300万美元（约9360万元人民币）。对一家年营收数千亿日元的综合食品企业而言，这笔钱不算大；但放在"日本食品企业争相落子越南"的图景里，它释放的信号并不小——越南正在从单纯的低成本出口平台，被重新定价为"工厂+市场"的双重枢纽。

## 一笔不算大的投资，一个不小的信号

据日经亚洲报道，日本面粉与食品巨头日清制粉将在越南南部同奈省设立当地法人，并建设一座预拌粉工厂，计划2027年投产，年产能约4300吨。注册资本约合1300万美元（约9360万元人民币）。对一家年营收数千亿日元的综合食品企业来说，这笔钱不算大；但放在"日本食品企业争相落子越南"的图景里，它释放的信号并不小——越南正在从单纯的低成本出口平台，被重新定价为"工厂+市场"的双重枢纽。

## 从"中国+1"到"市场导向"的投资转向

过去十余年，外企赴越南，多半是奔着"中国+1"去的：把原本放在中国的产能分流一部分到越南，图的是更低的人工、更稳的关税，以及躲开中美贸易摩擦的火力。日清制粉这次不一样。它在公告里说得直白：建厂是为了"夯实东盟区域内的供给体制"，并强化在越南国内的布局。换句话说，它看中的不只是越南作为生产基地的效率，更是越南本土那1亿人口正在长大的消费市场，以及它作为东盟供应链节点的辐射能力。这正是"效率导向"向"市场导向"的一次典型转向。

## 越南的双重身份：既是工厂，也是市场

为什么是越南？答案藏在它的双重身份里。一方面，越南是公认的制造承接国，电子、纺织、鞋类早已成链，劳动力年轻、成本低、外贸协定网络密集——欧盟—越南自贸协定、全面与进步跨太平洋伙伴关系协定都给足了出海口。另一方面，越南有近1亿人口，中产扩容、外食产业扩张、加工食品需求上扬，正从"为世界生产"走向"为自己生产"。日清制粉的预拌粉（premix）主要供给烘焙与油炸食品的商家，这类需求恰恰跟着城市化与餐饮连锁化走。工厂建在胡志明市周边约45公里的同奈，图的就是贴近这片最活跃的消费与物流腹地。

## 日清制粉的算盘：把东盟供应网"盘石化"

日清制粉早就在美国、中国、泰国、印尼落了子，越南是第五个。它把新厂定位为东盟供给网络的一块拼图：在泰国、印尼的工厂先供货，越南厂2027年接力，区域内调拨更顺、更稳。这背后是一条清晰的日本企业逻辑——与其每次都从本土或第三国远渡重洋，不如把关键产能贴近需求中心，用区域内部的"小循环"对冲全球"大循环"的不确定性。说白了，就是用本地化换确定性。

## 不是孤例：日本食品企业的越南抢滩

日清制粉绝非个例。就在2026年1月，另一家日本粮商昭和产业位于越南的新预拌粉工厂竣工，被明确定位为集团在东盟的"中核据点"。两家公司不约而同把预拌粉——这种看似不起眼的食品工业中间品——押在越南，说明日本食品资本看中的不是一时一地的税收优惠，而是越南食品工业整体被激活的长期趋势。从调味料到方便食品，从烘焙到冷冻，日本企业正把成熟市场的打法，整建制搬到越南。

## 与中国企业同台：越南成为制造必争之地

有意思的是，越南这桌"制造宴席"上，中日企业正同台竞合。中国汽车品牌奇瑞把东南亚最大工厂放在越南，比亚迪、吉利也在此布局；电子、纺织领域，中日韩产能早已交织。日清制粉们的食品加工，与中国品牌的终端消费品，最终都指向同一块蛋糕——越南与东盟的消费升级。一个负责上游供给与中间品，一个冲在终端品牌，表面上各做各的，底层却是同一逻辑：谁先卡住越南与东盟的供给与渠道，谁就握住下一程增长的门票。

## 隐忧：成本上涨与本土化难题

当然，故事没那么顺。越南工资近年水涨船高，土地与环保门槛也在抬升，"低成本"的旧红利正在褪色。对日清制粉这类食品企业而言，真正的考验是本土化：预拌粉要适配本地口味与料理习惯，渠道要下沉到分散的餐饮与零售终端，管理要跨过语言与制度的沟坎。更现实的变量是东盟内部的同质竞争——泰国、印尼同样在抢食品与制造投资，越南并不缺对手。能否把"建厂"变成"生根"，才是这道押注能否兑现的关键。

## 结语：越南故事远未结束

一笔1300万美元的小投资，未必能撬动什么，但它像一面镜子，照见外资对越南的重新定价：不再只把它当避风港式的廉价工厂，而是当成一个有肉身的消费市场与绕不开的供应链枢纽。对东南亚观察而言，越南的故事远未结束，它只是换了一种写法——从"承接"，走向"共生"。

## 延伸阅读

**预拌粉（premix）**：食品工业中间品，将多种粉类与添加剂预先混合，供烘焙、油炸等商家按需使用，能统一口味、简化工序，是日本食品企业出海越南的切入口。

**中国+1（China+1）**：指企业将部分产能从中国分散至越南、印度等地，以规避关税与地缘风险的供应链策略；近年正从纯避险转向兼顾市场开拓。

**全面与进步跨太平洋伙伴关系协定（CPTPP）**：越南参与的高标准自贸框架之一，与欧盟—越南自贸协定共同构成其出口与引资的制度红利。

# Japanese food maker Nippn bets on Vietnam: a small plant, a dual 'factory-plus-market' wager

Reporting by Nikkei Asia says Nippn, Japan's flour and food group, will set up a local subsidiary in Dong Nai province in southern Vietnam and build a premix plant, with production scheduled to begin in 2027 at an annual capacity of around 4,300 tonnes. Registered capital is roughly US$13 million (about ¥2 billion). For a diversified food company with billions in annual revenue, the sum is not large. Yet placed against the wider picture of Japanese food firms rushing into Vietnam, the signal is far from trivial: Vietnam is being repriced — from a mere low-cost export platform into a dual hub of factory and market.

## A small investment, a telling signal

According to Nikkei Asia, Japan's flour and food group Nippn will set up a local subsidiary in Dong Nai province in southern Vietnam and build a premix plant, with production scheduled to begin in 2027 at an annual capacity of around 4,300 tonnes. Registered capital is roughly US$13 million (about ¥2 billion). For a diversified food company with billions in annual revenue, the sum is not large. Yet placed against the wider picture of Japanese food firms rushing into Vietnam, the signal is far from trivial: Vietnam is being repriced — from a mere low-cost export platform into a dual hub of factory and market.

## From 'China+1' to market-seeking capital

For more than a decade, foreign firms went to Vietnam largely for "China+1": shifting a slice of capacity out of China to chase cheaper labour, steadier tariffs, and shelter from the US–China trade crossfire. Nippn's move is different. In its own statement the company is blunt — the plant exists to "solidify the supply structure within ASEAN" and to deepen its footprint inside Vietnam. In other words, it is eyeing not only Vietnam's efficiency as a production base but also the consumer market of its 100 million people and its reach as a node in regional supply chains. This is the classic pivot from efficiency-seeking to market-seeking investment.

## Vietnam's dual identity: workshop and marketplace

Why Vietnam? The answer lies in its double role. On one side, it is the established manufacturing destination — electronics, textiles and footwear are already clustered, the workforce is young, costs are contained, and a dense web of trade pacts (the EU–Vietnam Free Trade Agreement and the Comprehensive and Progressive Trans-Pacific Partnership among them) opens export channels. On the other, it is a country of nearly 100 million whose middle class is expanding, whose food-service sector is scaling up, and whose demand for processed food is climbing — moving from "making for the world" toward "making for itself." Nippn's premix, supplied mostly to bakeries and fried-food operators, rides exactly on urbanisation and the chaining of dining-out. Sited some 45 kilometres from Ho Chi Minh City, the plant hugs the most active consumer and logistics heartland.

## Nippn's calculus: cementing its ASEAN supply web

Nippn already operates in the United States, China, Thailand and Indonesia; Vietnam is its fifth country. It frames the new plant as a tile in its ASEAN supply mosaic: Thai and Indonesian factories supply for now, Vietnam takes the baton in 2027, and intra-regional flow becomes smoother and steadier. Behind this lies a clear Japanese playbook — rather than shipping from the home base or a third country each time, place critical capacity near the centre of demand and use regional "small cycles" to hedge the uncertainties of the global "big cycle." In plain terms: trade localisation for certainty.

## Not an isolated case: Japan's food makers rush in

Nippn is no outlier. In January 2026 another Japanese grain trader, Showa Sangyo, completed a new premix plant in Vietnam that it explicitly positioned as the group's "core base" for ASEAN. Two companies independently betting premix — an unglamorous intermediate good of the food industry — on Vietnam tells you they are not chasing a one-off tax break but a long-run trend in which Vietnam's entire food industry is being switched on. From seasonings to instant food, from baking to frozen lines, Japanese firms are relocating mature-market playbooks wholesale into Vietnam.

## Sharing the stage with Chinese firms

What makes the story more interesting is that on Vietnam's "manufacturing table" Japanese and Chinese firms are now competing and coexisting. Chinese car brand Chery is placing its largest ASEAN plant in Vietnam, with BYD and Geely also present; in electronics and textiles, Japanese, Chinese and Korean capacity has long been interwoven. Nippn's food processing and Chinese brands' consumer products ultimately point at the same prize — the upgrading consumption of Vietnam and ASEAN. One works the upstream supply and intermediate goods, the other charges at the terminal brand; on the surface they do different things, but underneath sits the same logic: whoever first locks in Vietnam and ASEAN's supply and channels holds the ticket to the next leg of growth.

## The caveats: rising costs and the localisation wall

Of course the story is not that tidy. Vietnamese wages have climbed in recent years, and land and environmental thresholds are rising too — the old "low-cost" dividend is fading. For a food firm like Nippn, the real test is localisation: premix must suit local tastes and cooking habits, channels must reach scattered food-service and retail endpoints, and management must cross language and institutional gaps. A more immediate variable is intra-ASEAN competition — Thailand and Indonesia are equally hunting food and manufacturing investment, so Vietnam has no shortage of rivals. Whether "building a plant" becomes "taking root" is what decides if this wager pays off.

## Conclusion: the Vietnam story is far from over

A US$13 million outlay will not move mountains, but it acts like a mirror, reflecting how foreign capital is repricing Vietnam — no longer merely a cheap factory to shelter in, but a consumer market with flesh and blood and a supply-chain hub that cannot be bypassed. For observers of Southeast Asia, Vietnam's story is far from finished; it has simply changed its grammar — from "taking on" to "growing with."

## Further reading

**Premix**: An intermediate good in the food industry — multiple flours and additives blended in advance for bakeries and fried-food operators to use as needed. It standardises taste and simplifies process, and is Japanese food firms' entry point into Vietnam.

**China+1**: A supply-chain strategy in which firms shift part of their capacity from China to Vietnam, India and others to dodge tariffs and geopolitical risk; recently it has moved from pure hedging toward also opening new markets.

**Comprehensive and Progressive Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP)**: One of the high-standard trade frameworks Vietnam belongs to; together with the EU–Vietnam Free Trade Agreement it underpins the country's export and investment dividends.

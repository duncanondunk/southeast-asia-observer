# 汉字为媒：曼谷艺术节为"中泰一家亲"写下文化注脚

第二届曼谷汉字艺术节于9月1日在泰国朱拉隆功大学开幕。来自中泰两国政府、文化界、教育界与工商界的嘉宾，与艺术家、学者及师生代表齐聚一堂，以汉字为媒、以艺术为桥，开启一场跨越山海的文明对话。这场为期五天的活动，通过汉字艺术展览、艺术交流与文化体验，推动中华优秀传统文化在泰国更深入、更持续地传播。

表面看，这是一场艺术活动；往深里看，它是一枚精巧的文化外交棋子。据中新社报道，本届艺术节现场展出六十余幅中泰艺术家作品，既有中国书法的沉雄笔力，也有泰国艺术风格的灵动多彩，被中国驻泰国大使馆参赞杨晓龙称为"中泰一家亲"的艺术写照，也诠释了"各美其美、美美与共"的文明互鉴理念。泰国文化部一级巡视员佩差拉·赛通则指出，汉字与书法的价值不仅在于记录与传递信息，更在于它们是体现精神、哲学与专注力量的高雅艺术，泰国青少年由此打开一扇理解中国文化根源的大门。

## 为什么是"汉字"：文明互鉴的低摩擦入口

值得追问的是：在众多文化符号中，为何偏偏选中"汉字"作为对话的媒介？答案在于，汉字是最低摩擦的文明互鉴入口。与影视、流行音乐等需要语言转译与审美磨合的载体不同，书法与汉字艺术以线条、结构与气韵直抵观者，即便不识其义，也能感受其美。对泰国观众而言，汉字既陌生又亲切——陌生在于文字系统迥异，亲切在于"中泰一家亲"的政治与民间叙事早已铺好情感地基。

这正是软实力（soft power）发挥作用的典型场景。约瑟夫·奈所谓"软实力"，是指通过文化与价值观的吸引力而非强制来达成目标。一场艺术节当然不会立刻改变地缘格局，但它悄然塑造着泰国社会各界对中国文化的亲近感，而这种亲近感，恰恰是双边关系中最柔软、也最耐久的缓冲层。

## 从展览到机制：驻留计划与青少年成长营

更值得关注的是，本届艺术节不止于"展完即走"。主办方同步启动"中泰国际艺术家驻留计划"与"中泰青少年汉字文化成长营"。前者以三十至九十天的在地创作、联合研讨与公共美育工坊，推动艺术思维的深度交融；后者则为中泰青年搭建常态化美育平台，推进跨国研学，让泰国青少年走进中国，沉浸式感受湖笔文脉与汉字艺术魅力。

这一步，把一次性的文化展示升级为可累积的机制化安排。文化外交最怕"热闹三天、冷清一年"，而当驻留计划与成长营把人留住、把时间拉长，文化接触就从事件变成了关系。对海外华侨华人而言，这样的机制也提供了一种纽带——汉字既是他们与祖籍国之间的文化锚点，也是他们可以向住在国社会展示的、可被共同欣赏的文明资产。

## "AI数字化传播"：传统文化出海的新变量

本届艺术节还提出探索"汉字艺术+美育心育+AI数字化传播"的新模式，意在打造面向东南亚的文化交流实践样本。这是传统艺术节里一个耐人寻味的现代注脚。

应当看到，人工智能与数字化传播正在改写文化出海的成本结构。过去，一场书法展的影响力止于展厅与报道；如今，借助数字工具，单幅作品的笔法、章法可被拆解、转译、再创作，触达东南亚乃至全球的年轻受众。对主办方而言，这是把"低频的线下展"升级为"高频的线上触达"的务实尝试。但也要保持清醒：技术能放大声量，却替代不了面对面研习中那种手把手、心传心的文化体认。工具是放大器，内核仍是人与人的相遇。

## 观察：文化先行，是"一家亲"最柔软的注脚

把视野拉回区域层面。在中国与东南亚国家的关系图谱中，文化往往是政治与经贸之外"最不敏感、也最易共鸣"的接口。相较于高铁、产业园区或海上争议，一场以汉字为名的艺术节几乎不会引发战略焦虑，却能在社会层面持续沉积好感。曼谷并非孤例——从柬埔寨华文学校复校，到马来西亚华文文学的多元共生，再到泰国汉字艺术节，中华文化在东南亚的落点正从"侨社内部"走向"社会共享"。

当然，文化外交不是万能钥匙。它不能替代硬实力的支撑，也无法消解结构性分歧。但它的价值恰恰在于"慢"与"柔"：在不被注意的地方，年复一年地种下理解与亲近的种子。当泰国青少年在成长营里第一次提起毛笔，当两国艺术家在驻留计划里共同完成一件作品，那些关于"我们"与"他们"的边界，便悄然松动了一寸。

曼谷汉字艺术节或许只是一场五天活动，但它提示了一个朴素的道理：国与国之间最稳固的纽带，往往不是写在协议里的条款，而是长在人心里、落在笔尖上的那一点文化共鸣。

## 延伸阅读

**汉字文化圈（Sinosphere）**：历史上受汉字书写系统影响的东亚与东南亚文化区域，是理解"汉字外交"历史纵深的概念框架。

**美美与共（Harmony in diversity）**：费孝通提出的文化共处理念，意指各美其美、美美与共，常被用于中国对外文明互鉴叙事。

**软实力（Soft power）**：约瑟夫·奈提出的概念，指通过文化与价值观吸引力而非强制达成目标，是解读此类艺术节战略意涵的理论工具。

# Characters as Bridges: How Bangkok's Chinese-character Festival Reframes China–Thailand Ties

The second Bangkok Chinese Character Art Festival opened on 1 September 2026 at Chulalongkorn University in Thailand. Guests from government, cultural, educational, and business circles in both China and Thailand joined artists, scholars, and students to launch a civilizational dialogue "across mountains and seas," using Chinese characters as the medium and art as the bridge. Running for five days, the festival promotes a deeper and more sustained transmission of traditional Chinese culture in Thailand through exhibitions, artistic exchange, and cultural experience.

On the surface, this is an art event. Look closer, and it is a delicately placed piece on the chessboard of cultural diplomacy. According to China News Service, the festival displayed more than 60 works by Chinese and Thai artists — some bearing the vigorous strokes of Chinese calligraphy, others the lively colours of Thai artistic styles. Yang Xiaolong, counsellor at the Chinese embassy in Thailand, called the show an artistic portrait of the "China–Thailand one-family" bond and an embodiment of the ideal of "appreciating each other's beauty and sharing a harmonious world." Phetchara Saitong, a senior official at Thailand's Ministry of Culture, noted that the value of Chinese characters and calligraphy lies not merely in recording and conveying information, but in being a refined art that expresses spirit, philosophy, and the power of concentration — opening a door for Thai youth to understand the roots of Chinese culture.

## Why "Chinese characters": a low-friction entry point for mutual learning

One question deserves asking: among so many cultural symbols, why choose "Chinese characters" as the medium of dialogue? The answer is that the written character is the lowest-friction entry point for civilizational exchange. Unlike film or pop music, which require translation and aesthetic acclimatisation, calligraphy reaches the viewer through line, structure, and rhythm — its beauty felt even without understanding its meaning. To a Thai audience, the character is at once strange and familiar: strange because the writing systems differ, familiar because the "one family" political and popular narrative has already laid an emotional foundation.

This is soft power at work in its classic form. Joseph Nye's "soft power" means shaping outcomes through the attraction of culture and values rather than through coercion. A festival will not redraw the geopolitical map overnight, but it quietly cultivates Thai society's affinity for Chinese culture — and that affinity is precisely the softest, most durable buffer in bilateral relations.

## From exhibition to institution: residency programme and youth camp

What deserves more attention is that this festival does not merely "exhibit and leave." The organisers simultaneously launched a China–Thailand International Artists Residency and a China–Thailand Youth Chinese-character Culture Camp. The residency, lasting 30 to 90 days, fosters deep artistic exchange through on-site creation, joint seminars, and public education workshops. The camp builds a standing platform for aesthetic education and cross-border study, bringing Thai youth to China for an immersive experience of the brush-and-ink lineage and the charm of Chinese characters.

This step upgrades a one-off cultural showcase into a cumulative, institutional arrangement. Cultural diplomacy's perennial risk is "three days of fanfare, a year of silence." By retaining people and extending time, the residency and the camp turn cultural contact from an event into a relationship. For overseas Chinese, such mechanisms also offer a lifeline: the Chinese character is both their cultural anchor to the ancestral homeland and a civilizational asset they can display, and share, with the wider host society.

## "AI-powered digital dissemination": a new variable for cultural export

The festival also proposed a new model of "Chinese-character art + aesthetic and psychological education + AI-powered digital dissemination," intended as a practical template for cultural exchange across Southeast Asia. This is a thought-provoking modern footnote to a traditional art festival.

One should note that AI and digital dissemination are rewriting the cost structure of cultural export. In the past, a calligraphy exhibition's influence ended at the gallery and the news report. Today, with digital tools, a single work's brushwork and composition can be decomposed, translated, and recreated, reaching young audiences across Southeast Asia and beyond. For the organisers, this is a pragmatic attempt to upgrade "low-frequency offline exhibitions" into "high-frequency online touchpoints." Yet a note of caution is warranted: technology amplifies volume but cannot replace the hand-to-hand, heart-to-heart cultural embodiment of face-to-face study. The tool is an amplifier; the core remains the meeting of people.

## Observation: culture first is the softest footnote to "one family"

Zooming out to the regional level, culture is often the "least sensitive and most resonant" interface between China and Southeast Asian states, sitting apart from politics and trade. Compared with high-speed rail, industrial parks, or maritime disputes, a character-themed art festival rarely triggers strategic anxiety yet continuously deposits goodwill at the social level. Bangkok is not alone — from the reopening of a Chinese school in Cambodia, to the multicultural symbiosis of Malaysian Chinese literature, to Thailand's character festival, Chinese culture in Southeast Asia is moving from "within the diaspora" toward "shared by society."

Of course, cultural diplomacy is no master key. It cannot substitute for hard-power backing, nor dissolve structural disagreements. Its value lies precisely in its "slowness" and "softness": planting seeds of understanding and affinity, year after year, where they are least noticed. When Thai teenagers lift a brush for the first time in a camp, when artists from both countries complete a work together in a residency, the boundary between "us" and "them" loosens, inch by inch.

The Bangkok Chinese Character Art Festival may be only a five-day event, but it points to a simple truth: the most durable bond between nations is often not the clauses written in agreements, but the cultural resonance that grows in people's hearts and settles at the tip of a brush.

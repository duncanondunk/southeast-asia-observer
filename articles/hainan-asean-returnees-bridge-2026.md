# 海南借力东盟海归：自贸港把离散人才变成通往东南亚的桥

2026年10月30日，第二届海南-东盟海归交流大会在澄迈启幕。这座海南岛上的县城，正是中国最具雄心自由贸易试验的「实验室」所在地。三百余名与会者——来自多个东盟国家的商协会代表、高校与智库专家、海内外优秀海归人才，以及「出海」企业的高管——坐到一起，探讨海南自贸港与东南亚（Southeast Asia）「双向开放」的新机遇。

这个表述值得停下来想想。北京（Beijing）通常不把侨胞称作「天然纽带」。但此次大会的官方通稿恰恰这么写了，把「海归」——即出国留学或工作后归国的中国人——塑造成连接中国与东盟市场、技术、资源的有机节点。这不是空话。这是一句政策表态。

## 聚首澄迈：三百余人的会场

澄迈作为旗舰级侨务活动的举办地，看似出人意料，实则正中要害。东道主县城打出的口号是「要出海、来澄迈」，展示了一套围绕企业「走出去」而搭建的产业布局与扶持体系。与此同时，海南省经济发展部门借讲台深度解读自贸港即将落地的「封关运作」核心政策——这一机制自2025年起把海南视作独立关税区，这是内地任何其他地区都不享有的地位。

对一群在东南亚拥有家族、教育与商业根基的归国侨胞而言，信号是具体的：海南向商业敞开大门，而它希望由你来做那个通道。

## 海归作为「天然纽带」

为何倚重海归，而非国有企业或正式外交渠道？答案在于不对称。东盟（ASEAN）不是单一市场，而是十个市场，各自有监管迷宫、语言与政治敏感。一位在曼谷长大、或在吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）求学的归国创业者，掌握着默会知识——该打给谁、如何读懂气氛、红线在哪里——这是任何贸易代表团在一周内都学不会的。

大会自身的议程把这一点戏剧化了。一场分论坛聚焦OPC创业生态；一场讨论AI机器人与具身智能；一场关于跨境文化艺术；还有一场是数字「出海」项目路演。贯穿其中的逻辑是：离散人才不只是政策的受益者，更是政策的操作者——那个能把中国清洁能源平台或AI产品，为北京难以直接打入的市场做本地化的人。

## 从联谊到硬核签约

空谈廉价，签字不廉。大会拿出了一摞看得见的协议。中国国际商会海南商会与亚洲和平发展青年商会、泰国（Thailand）江西总会等东盟国家商协会代表签署战略合作协议。海南省青年联合会、海南欧美同学会与东盟各国学联签署《海南-东盟人才联盟合作协议》。

项目层面，四个聚焦人工智能与产学研融合的科创项目——包括「AI6S店」与南洋理工AI校友产业联盟——以及三个产业项目，如新能源智能网联汽车出海全生命周期服务平台、企业出海服务基地，签约落地海南。现场还举行「海外引才联络点」授牌，并特聘一批行业人才担任招商、文化交流及东盟青年友好大使。

说白了：这与其说是一场联谊，不如说是一次对「联结组织」的采购——那些把自贸港口号变成实际运作能力的合作关系与落地平台。

## 自贸港的离散人才账本

退一步看，逻辑很清楚。海南自贸港需要它尚未拥有的外部引力。它的经济总量只是珠三角的一小部分，产业基础薄弱。要成为连接中国与东盟的真正枢纽，它必须借用自己并不拥有的网络。华侨华人——尤其是与东盟联系新鲜的归国群体——是现有最廉价、文化上最通顺的资产。

其中还有一层软实力考量。北京对东南亚的拓展，正与相互竞争的大国叙事角力。一条被框定为「民心相通」与「人才」的离散通道，比起国家主导的基础设施推进，更难被描绘成胁迫。大会的「海归之夜」音乐会，用文化温情包裹了一整天的签约交易，绝非偶然——它是经济推介的情感包装。

## 不止于合影：真正要害何在

质疑者会指出，这类大会制造的新闻稿多于持久结构。这是一记公平的挑战。人才联盟与联络点，其成色只取决于后续落实——归国人才是否真正落地、签约项目能否存活、一年后真实跨境交易密度几何。

但信号不应被轻视。通过把侨胞制度化地定位为「桥」而非事后补丁，海南正在试验一种其他沿海省份可能效仿的模式：把归国华侨华人当作区域一体化的分布式基础设施，而非欢迎宴会的座上宾。若澄迈所签项目得以延续，「天然纽带」或将证明自己不止是隐喻，而是机制。

- **海归（Returned Overseas Chinese）**：出国留学或工作后归国的中国人，近年被日益纳入侨务与人才政策。
- **侨乡（Qiaoxiang）**：中国南方拥有深厚移民网络的故乡，是传统侨务工作的根基。

# Hainan Courts the ASEAN Diaspora: How Returnees Become the Free Trade Port's Bridge to Southeast Asia

On 30 October 2026, the second Hainan–ASEAN Returned Overseas Chinese Exchange Conference opened in Chengmai, a county-level city on the island province that has become the laboratory for China's most ambitious free-trade experiment. More than 300 participants — chamber-of-commerce representatives from multiple ASEAN states, university and think-tank experts, outstanding returned overseas Chinese talents, and executives of firms "going global" — sat down to talk about "new opportunities for two-way opening" between the Hainan Free Trade Port and Southeast Asia.

The framing is worth pausing on. Beijing does not usually describe its diaspora as a "natural bridge." Yet the official readout of the conference did exactly that, casting haigui — the Chinese term for those who studied or worked abroad and came home — as the organic connector of Chinese and ASEAN markets, technology and resources. That is not rhetoric for its own sake. It is a policy position.

## The gathering: 300 voices in Chengmai

Chengmai is an odd choice for a flagship diaspora event, and that is precisely the point. The host county pitched itself with the slogan "if you want to go global, come to Chengmai," showcasing an industrial layout and support system built around outbound business. The province's economic development bureau, meanwhile, used the podium to explain the core policies of the Free Trade Port's upcoming "customs closure" operation — the mechanism that, from 2025 onward, treats Hainan as a separate customs territory, a status no other part of mainland China enjoys.

For an audience of returned overseas Chinese with family, educational and commercial roots across Southeast Asia, the message was concrete: Hainan is open for business, and it wants you to be the channel.

## Returnees as a "natural bridge"

Why lean on returnees rather than, say, state-owned enterprises or formal diplomatic channels? The answer lies in asymmetry. ASEAN is not a single market but ten, each with its own regulatory maze, language and political sensitivity. A returned entrepreneur who grew up in Bangkok or studied in Kuala Lumpur carries the tacit knowledge — whom to call, how to read a room, where the red lines are — that no trade delegation acquires in a week.

The conference's own agenda dramatised this. One sub-forum focused on OPC entrepreneurial ecosystems; another on AI robotics and embodied intelligence; a third on cross-border culture and the arts; a fourth on digital "going-out" project roadshows. The through-line was that diaspora talent is not merely a beneficiary of policy but an operator of it — the person who can localise a Chinese clean-energy platform or an AI product for a market Beijing struggles to enter directly.

## From networking to hard commitments

Talk is cheap; signatures are not. The conference produced a visible stack of deals. The China International Chamber of Commerce's Hainan chapter signed strategic cooperation agreements with the Asian Peace and Development Youth Chamber of Commerce and the Thailand Jiangxi General Association, among others. The Hainan Youth Federation and the Hainan Western Returned Scholars Association signed a "Hainan–ASEAN Talent Alliance Cooperation Agreement" with student unions from ASEAN countries.

On the project side, four science-and-technology initiatives — including an "AI6S store" and an NTU-alumni AI industry alliance — plus three industrial projects such as a full-lifecycle service platform for new-energy smart vehicles going overseas and an enterprise outbound service base, were signed to land in Hainan. An "overseas talent recruitment liaison point" was unveiled, and a batch of industry figures were appointed as investment, cultural-exchange and ASEAN-youth friendship ambassadors.

Read plainly: this was less a networking event than a procurement exercise for connective tissue — the relationships and landing pads that turn a free-trade slogan into operating capacity.

## The Free Trade Port's diaspora calculus

Step back and the logic is clear. Hainan's free-trade port needs external gravity it does not yet have. Its GDP is a fraction of the Pearl River Delta's, and its industrial base is thin. To become a genuine hub linking China and ASEAN, it must borrow networks it does not own. The overseas Chinese — particularly the returnee cohort with fresh ASEAN ties — are the cheapest, most culturally fluent asset available.

There is also a soft-power dimension. Beijing's outreach to Southeast Asia is contested by competing great-power narratives. A diaspora channel framed as "people-to-people" and "talent" is harder to paint as coercive than a state-led infrastructure push. The conference's "Returnees' Night" concert, wrapping the day's dealmaking in cultural warmth, was not incidental — it was the affective packaging of an economic pitch.

## Beyond the photo-op: what's at stake

Cynics will note that such conferences produce more press releases than permanent structures. That is a fair challenge. The talent alliance and liaison points are only as strong as the follow-through — the actual placement of returnees, the survival of the signed projects, the density of real cross-border deals a year on.

But the signal should not be dismissed. By institutionalising the diaspora as a bridge rather than an afterthought, Hainan is testing a model other coastal provinces may copy: treat returned overseas Chinese not as recipients of welcome-back banquets but as distributed infrastructure for regional integration. If the projects signed in Chengmai endure, the "natural bridge" may prove less metaphor than mechanism.

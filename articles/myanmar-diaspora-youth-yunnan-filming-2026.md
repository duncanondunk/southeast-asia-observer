# 镜头里的胞波：缅甸华裔青年博主为什么重走云南

据中国侨网（China Qiao Wang）报道，11月25日，缅甸华裔青年新媒体博主采风活动在昆明启动。20余名青年将在6天里走进昆明、普洱与西双版纳，拍瓦猫制作、看锯瓷技艺、品茶与咖啡。这类活动常被写成「民心相通」的注脚，但真正值得追问的是：让离散青年拿镜头讲祖籍地，究竟是在生产可信故事，还是在批量制造统一口径？答案取决于一个很朴素的条件——他们有没有被允许说自己的话。

## 六天行程：从剪辑课到茶山

据中国侨网转引中国新闻网报道，活动由云南省侨务办公室主办，中新社（北京）国际传播集团有限公司云南分公司承办，持续至12月1日。启动仪式本身就带着明确的培训意图：专门安排短视频拍摄剪辑技巧专题授课，逐条拆解视频立意、手法与技巧；青年们还参访了昆明振华1958摄影文创园的秘境影像艺术中心。

接下来的路线更有意思。参与者将走进昆明市、普洱市和西双版纳傣族自治州，体验瓦猫制作与锯瓷技艺，感受「亚洲花都」「世界茶源」「中国咖啡之都」三张地方名片。这不是一条随机排列的旅游线：非遗手作对应文化记忆，茶与咖啡对应产业与消费，西双版纳对应跨境民族与生态。主办方想讲的云南，是一个既有根、又能赚钱、还足够绿色的云南。问题是，镜头会不会只拍到这张精心布置的桌面，而把桌下的生活一并裁掉？

## 「天然桥梁」：一句最动听也最危险的话

云南省侨务办公室侨务综合处处长马昌治在启动仪式上说，云南与缅甸边境线绵延千里，村寨相望；自古先辈沿茶马古道、南方丝绸之路往返，结下情谊。「滇缅一家亲」不只是一句问候，更是一代代侨胞同舟共济的写照。他称缅甸华裔青年熟悉缅甸社会、了解中华文化，又活跃于新媒体平台，是连接两国的天然桥梁，也是新时代「胞波」情谊最有活力的讲述者。

数据支撑了这层期待：云南是中国五大侨乡之一，云南籍海外侨胞逾200万人，分布在80多个国家和地区，其中缅甸是分布最集中、人数最多的国家。据侨务部门的口径，让青年用短视频向缅甸及国际社会传递友好正能量，是活动的直接目标。

「天然桥梁」却是最需要警惕的表述。桥梁之所以叫桥梁，是因为它要承受重量、要有人维护；「天然」二字恰恰容易把政治与社会工程说成一桩血缘自动完成的事。缅甸华裔并非单一群体：云南籍、闽粤籍，移民代际不同、公民身份不同、语言习惯不同，对「祖籍地」的情感也远非一致。把差异抹平成一句「亲戚」，短期好传播，长期会透支可信度。

## 从侨汇到内容：侨务工作的指标变了

过去衡量侨务成效，看的是侨汇、捐资、华校数量、回国投资额。这些指标有个共同特点：可统计、可汇报。如今多了一项更难量化的产出——叙事。据中国侨网报道，主办方希望青年把真实立体的云南分享出去，让缅甸朋友看见鲜活温暖的邻国。换言之，离散社群的价值正从「出钱」转向「出镜」。

这一转向有现实逻辑。在信息高度平台化的环境里，由本地人用本地语言讲出的故事，比官方外宣更容易穿透信任壁垒。缅甸华裔青年同时掌握两套语境，这种双语双文化能力，确实比任何翻译工具都更接近传播的本质。

来自缅甸的华裔青年邝嘉俊说，云南的山水与多元民族文化一直吸引着他，近年到过香格里拉、普洱等地，拍的短视频和图文在社交平台上颇受欢迎；这次他希望用真实、接地气的镜头，把亲眼所见的云南分享给缅甸朋友。这番话点出了要害：年轻人自己的动机是记录，不是背书。培训里那句「拍得更准、剪得更好、传得更开」，前两项是手艺，第三项却涉及分发权力。如果「准」被预先定义成只能说好话，那么产出的不过是套着年轻面孔的新闻稿。真正有传播力的内容，往往允许镜头在赞美之后多停三秒，拍一拍堵车、物价和邻里间的争执。

## 胞波的两面：亲戚叙事遇上复杂现实

「胞波」在缅语中意为亲戚，是中缅关系里极珍贵的情感资产。但情感资产也有折旧率。今天的缅甸仍面对冲突、族群政治与经济压力，华裔社群处在一个并不轻松的位置：他们被期待充当桥梁，却也可能被两端同时追问立场。一个在云南拍短视频的华裔青年，其受众不只在中国，更在缅甸；观众会用自己的生活经验核对画面，而不是照单全收。

「亲戚」叙事能降低误解的门槛，却也容易抹平差异：云南籍与闽粤籍华人之间的差别，公民与新移民之间的差别，以缅甸身份为先和以华人身份为先之间的差别，都不是一句「一家亲」可以覆盖的。软实力最怕的不是批评，而是被识破的表演。一个敢说「我看到了咖啡产业的繁荣，也看到了山路的拥堵」的博主，比只会念台词的博主更可信；而可信，恰恰是民间外交唯一无法采购的原材料。

## 六天之后：把采风变成网络

活动结束不等于项目结束。若想让这6天产生复利，至少需要三件事落地。其一是延续机制：把20余名博主纳入可回访的校友网络，提供小额联合制作经费与双语字幕支持，让一次行程长成一条持续生产的链条。其二是换一套评价指标：不以播放量单独论英雄，而看评论质量、回访率、缅甸本地媒体的转载与二次讨论——信任比流量慢，但更耐久。其三是改写参与者身份：云南的社区、匠人与茶农不应只是被拍摄的背景，而应成为共同署名的作者；否则「真实」只会是一场布景真实。

据中国侨网报道，主办方期待更多青年成为友谊传承者、务实合作推动者与民心相通促进者。这三重期待不低。检验方法也很简单：半年之后，这些青年还能不能按自己的判断发一条不讨喜的短片，而不被要求删掉。若答案是能，胞波才算真正从口号走进了镜头。

## 延伸阅读

**胞波（Paukphaw）**：缅语中「亲戚、兄弟」之意，长期被用来形容中缅民间亲近感，是中缅关系特有的情感符号，也是官方与民间叙事共同使用的框架。

**侨乡（Qiaoxiang）**：指大量居民出洋谋生、与海外保持长期联系的地方。云南籍侨胞逾200万人、分布80多个国家和地区，缅甸是其中最集中的目的地。

**民心相通（People-to-people bonds）**：「一带一路」（Belt and Road Initiative）合作中的人文支柱，强调民间往来、文化交流与媒体互鉴；其成效依赖持续信任，而非单场活动的曝光量。

# Paukphaw through a lens: why Myanmar's young Chinese-descended bloggers are going back to Yunnan

According to China Qiao Wang, a field tour for young Chinese-descended new-media bloggers from Myanmar was launched in Kunming on 25 November. More than 20 participants will spend six days travelling through Kunming, Pu'er and Xishuangbanna, filming clay roof-cat craftsmanship, porcelain repair, tea and coffee. Such events are routinely filed under "people-to-people bonds". The more interesting question is this: when diaspora youths are handed cameras to narrate their ancestral home, are they producing credible stories or manufacturing a single approved line? The answer rests on a plain condition — whether they are allowed to speak in their own voices.

## A six-day route from editing class to tea hills

China Qiao Wang, citing China News Service, reports that the tour is hosted by the Yunnan Provincial Overseas Chinese Affairs Office and organised by the Yunnan branch of China News Service (Beijing) International Communication Group, running until 1 December. The opening ceremony carried an unmistakable training purpose: a specialised workshop on short-video shooting and editing broke down how to frame a subject, choose Technique and cut a story, followed by a visit to the Secret Realm Image Art Centre in the Zhenhua 1958 photography and cultural-creative park in Kunming.

The itinerary is itself an argument. Participants will visit Kunming, Pu'er and the Xishuangbanna Dai Autonomous Prefecture, try their hand at making wa mao clay roof cats and the craft of sawing and mending porcelain, and sample three local labels — the "Flower Capital of Asia", the "World's Tea Source" and China's "Coffee Capital". This is no random tourist circuit. Intangible heritage stands for cultural memory, tea and coffee for industry and consumption, and Xishuangbanna for cross-border ethnicity and ecology. The Yunnan on offer is a place with roots, money-making potential and enough green credentials. The question is whether the cameras will capture only this neatly arranged tabletop, cropping out the life beneath it.

## The claim of a "natural bridge"

Ma Changzhi, director of the general office for overseas Chinese affairs at the Yunnan Provincial Overseas Chinese Affairs Office, told the launch ceremony that Yunnan and Myanmar share a border running for a thousand li, with villages facing one another. Generations of ancestors travelled the Tea Horse Road and the Southern Silk Road between them, forging ties. "Yunnan and Myanmar are one family", he said, is not merely a greeting but a record of mutual help. Young Chinese-descended Myanmar residents, he added, know Myanmar society, understand Chinese culture and are active on new-media platforms: they are natural bridges between the two countries and the most dynamic narrators of paukphaw in the new era.

The numbers back the expectation. Yunnan is one of China's five major qiaoxiang, or home counties of emigrants. More than two million people of Yunnan origin live overseas, across over 80 countries and regions, with Myanmar hosting the largest and most concentrated community. The stated goal is for these young people to use short videos to send positive messages about friendship to audiences in Myanmar and beyond.

"Natural bridge" is nevertheless the most seductive and the most dangerous phrase here. A bridge is called a bridge precisely because it bears weight and needs maintenance; calling it "natural" smuggles in the idea that kinship alone completes a political and social project. Chinese-descended communities in Myanmar are not a single bloc: those of Yunnan origin and those from Fujian and Guangdong differ in migration generation, citizenship, language habits and emotional attachment to the ancestral home. Flattening those differences into one word — "relatives" — travels well in the short run and spends credibility in the long run.

## From remittances to content: the metric of diaspora work changes

Overseas Chinese work used to be measured in remittances, donations, the number of Chinese-language schools and inbound investment. Those indicators had one thing in common: they were countable and reportable. Now a harder-to-quantify output has been added — narrative. China Qiao Wang reports that the organisers want the young participants to show a real, multidimensional Yunnan so that Myanmar friends can see a warm and living neighbour. In other words, the value of a diaspora community is shifting from writing cheques to appearing on camera.

The logic is sound. In a hyper-platformed information environment, stories told by locals in local languages cut through trust barriers more effectively than official outreach. Young Chinese-descended Myanmar residents command two contexts at once; that bilingual, bicultural competence is closer to the essence of communication than any translation tool.

Kuang Jiajun, one of the participants, says Yunnan's landscapes and ethnic diversity have long fascinated him. He has visited Shangri-La and Pu'er, and the short videos and photo posts he shared drew a warm response. This time he wants to show what he actually sees, in a grounded way, to friends in Myanmar. His motivation is documentation, not endorsement. That distinction matters. The training slogan — shoot more accurately, edit better, circulate further — treats the first two as craft, but the third as distribution power. If "accuracy" is pre-defined as saying only pleasant things, the output is a press release wearing a young face. The most persuasive content usually lets the camera linger three seconds longer after the praise, catching the traffic jams, the prices and the neighbourhood arguments as well.

## Two sides of paukphaw: kinship meets a messy reality

Paukphaw means "relatives" in Burmese and is a precious emotional asset in China–Myanmar relations. But emotional assets depreciate. Myanmar still faces conflict, ethnic politics and economic pressure, and its Chinese-descended communities occupy an uneasy position: expected to act as bridges, they may be questioned from both ends about where they stand. A young blogger filming in Yunnan speaks not only to a Chinese audience but to viewers in Myanmar, who will check the images against their own lives rather than accept them wholesale.

The kinship frame lowers the threshold for misunderstanding, yet it also erases differences: between Yunnan-origin and Fujian/Guangdong-origin Chinese, between citizens and new migrants, between those who put their Myanmar identity first and those who put their Chineseness first. Soft power fears no criticism so much as a performance seen through. A blogger willing to say "I saw the coffee boom and I saw the mountain traffic" is more credible than one who recites a script — and credibility is the one raw material public diplomacy cannot purchase.

## After six days: turning a tour into a network

The end of the trip is not the end of the project. If those six days are to compound, three things must follow. First, a continuity mechanism: bring the twenty-odd bloggers into a revisitable alumni network, with small co-production grants and bilingual subtitling support, so a single journey becomes a production chain. Second, a different scorecard: judge less by view counts and more by comment quality, return visits, and whether Myanmar local media pick up and debate the stories — trust moves more slowly than traffic, but it lasts. Third, rewrite the participants' roles: Yunnan's communities, artisans and tea farmers should be co-authors rather than scenery, or "authenticity" becomes a carefully built set.

China Qiao Wang reports that the organisers hope more young people will become inheritors of friendship, promoters of practical cooperation and facilitators of people-to-people bonds. Those are large ambitions. The test is simple: six months from now, can these young people post one unflattering video on their own judgement without being asked to delete it? If the answer is yes, paukphaw will have moved from slogan into the lens.

## Further reading

**Paukphaw (胞波)**: A Burmese term meaning "relatives" or "kin", long used to describe the closeness between the Chinese and Myanmar peoples; a shared frame in both official rhetoric and grassroots storytelling.

**Qiaoxiang (侨乡, hometown of emigrants)**: A locality whose residents have migrated abroad in large numbers and maintain long-standing ties overseas. More than two million people of Yunnan origin live in over 80 countries and regions, with Myanmar the largest single destination.

**People-to-people bonds**: The human dimension of Belt and Road cooperation, emphasising everyday exchanges, cultural dialogue and media interaction. Its results depend on sustained trust, not the exposure generated by a single event.

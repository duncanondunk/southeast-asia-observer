# 九届不缺席：一个华商会折射出的中马关系

## 连续九届不缺席：一个商会的分量

第九届中国国际进口博览会将于2026年11月5日在上海开幕，马来西亚（Malaysia）中华总商会（简称中总，英文简称ACCCIM）将连续第九届组织企业参展。按中总自己的说法，它是马来西亚唯一从未缺席过任何一届进博会的组展机构。今年中总将率领166人的代表团赴沪，并在国家馆下集结27家参展企业，展品涵盖饮料、燕窝、榴莲、咖啡与鲜果，也迎来塑料包装、房地产与旅游服务等新展商。

有人或许会把这当成商会例行的公关动作。但「连续九届」本身就是故事。在一个贸易代表团随政府更迭来来去去的地区，一个华人商协会近十年风雨无阻地出现，绝非小事。它说明，对马来西亚相当大一块资本而言，中国市场不是一阵风似的机会，而是一种能够跨越吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）与北京（Beijing）政治周期的结构性承诺。

## 从「卖货」到「做品牌」：升级背后的逻辑

中总总会长吴逸平对这层转变说得很直白。协助马来西亚企业开拓中国市场仍是首要目标，但他也承认，单纯套利的时代正在结束。中国消费者如今更在意产品品质、品牌形象、安全标准与供应能力。长期习惯出口大宗商品的马来西亚企业，必须学会做品牌、拓渠道、建长期关系。

这正是标题里那句「模式升级」的真意，也比表面听起来更有信息量。多年来，中马贸易的故事是用「量」来讲的：棕榈油、电子、榴莲，以及双边贸易额的持续攀升。而中总现在谈的是品牌与渠道，而非吨位——这其实是在承认，低垂的果实已经摘完。下一阶段的市撤准入，将偏爱那些会讲故事、能认证标准、可维持存在感的企业，而不是仅仅运出一集装箱货物的商家。

## 夹在中美之间：族群资本的微妙站位

下面这层意思，新闻稿不会明说。中总是一个在马来人占多数、奉行马来土著优先政策的国家里运作的华人机构。它充当对华桥梁的能力，既是最大资产，也是永久的政治软肋。在马来西亚政治里，华人资本历来容易招来「双重忠诚」的猜疑——在马来民族主义者看来离北京太近，却又不被任何一方完全信任。

于是，当一个华商会组织赴华经贸团，它走的是一条钢丝。其表述刻意保持商业属性，并牢牢框定在马来西亚国家利益之内：为中小企业开门、分散销售渠道、降低对单一客户的依赖。注意那个措辞——「降低对单一市场的依赖」是一箭双雕的：它推销的是进入中国市场的通道，却从不显得效忠任何一方。在既要在华盛顿与北京之间平衡的安华总理任内，这种刻意保持的中立不是短板，而恰恰是一种生存智慧。华人资本，正是在「先被读作马来西亚人、其次才是华人」时，才最具回旋余地。

## 从买卖到共投：第三方市场的想象

中总推介中最耐人寻味的一句话，是关于未来的那句轻声表述。吴逸平主张，马中企业应从单纯的买卖，升级为优势互补、共同投资、共同开发市场。他提出，马来西亚可借电子电气、半导体与国际供应链的基础，与中国企业在零部件、生产配套与供应链协作上寻找机会；也可在本地布局加工、组装、仓储或区域配送，再借助中国企业的技术与商业网络，开拓东盟（ASEAN）及其他国际市场。

细读之下，这是一幅「第三条路」的草图。它既不是旧的「大宗出口」模式，也不是对中国资本的彻底依赖，而是一种制造与物流的伙伴关系：马来西亚借自身的区位、对马来世界与伊斯兰市场的熟悉，以及华人商业网络，成为中国企业全球扩张中的一个节点，而非旁观者。对一直看着供应链在中美博弈下重组的吉隆坡而言，这个「节点」地位才是奖品。巧的是，这恰恰也是一个在华盛顿与北京两边都说得通的故事。

## 青年与人工智能：接班人的必修课

代表团里不只有白发企业家。中总安排46名青年企业家于11月7日至10日前往苏州大学，参加人工智能高端研修班，了解中国人工智能与智能制造的发展经验。这是一个不起眼却意味深长的细节：商会正在为下一代预做准备。在一个靠找买家、谈价格的古典贸易技能已不再够用的经济里，年轻一代必须另学本领。

这里的逻辑很坦率。如果「模式升级」是真的，那么把它落地的，不会是上世纪八十年代贸易行的创办人，而是那些对算法与供应链软件得心应手的二十几岁青年。把这批人送进中国的人工智能生态，中总押的赌注是：华人商业的未来，既在于贸易利差，也在于技术转移。这个赌注能否兑现，与其说取决于商会，不如说取决于马来西亚自身的数字经济能否接住这46名青年带回家的东西。

## 一碗燕窝里的南洋故事：经贸之外的软实力

吴逸平最后还点出一处容易被忽略的意思。在订单与渠道之外，他希望借助传统食品与特色产品，向中国乃至国际观众讲述「南洋故事」，让更多人认识马来西亚的文化与历史。一碗燕窝、一缕榴莲香：它们不只是出口品类，更是使者。

对一个用两个世纪证明自己属于东南亚（Southeast Asia）的离散社群而言，能够讲述自身——把自身传承变成在全球舞台上既好卖又有尊严的故事——本身就是一种安静的力量。那间最初主要为保护华人商业利益而存在的商会，如今不论是否点名，都已是一种文化外交的工具。进博会这台商业机器，也成了马来西亚华人向中国与全世界说明「我是谁」的舞台。

## 延伸阅读

**马来西亚中华总商会（ACCCIM）**：马来西亚华商的最高联合总机构，由全国各地中华总商会组成，长期扮演马中经贸桥梁角色，也是进博会马方唯一从未缺席的组展机构。

**进博会（中国国际进口博览会）**：2018年起每年在上海举办的国家级进口展会，是中国主动扩大市场开放、面向全球采购的重要平台，对东南亚农产品与消费品进入中国尤为关键。

**马来土著优先政策（Bumiputera）**：马来西亚独立后确立的、以马来人等土著为先的经济与社会政策框架，深刻塑造了华人资本的政治站位与「双重忠诚」叙事。

# Nine Fairs In, Never Out: What One Ethnic-Chinese Chamber Tells Us About Malaysia–China Ties

## Nine times, never absent: the weight of one chamber

When the ninth China International Import Expo (CIIE) opens in Shanghai on 5 November 2026, Malaysia's Associated Chinese Chambers of Commerce and Industry (ACCCIM) — known at home as 中总 — will be there for the ninth consecutive year. By its own account, it is the only Malaysian organiser that has never missed a single edition. This year the chamber is leading a 166-strong delegation and gathering 27 exhibitors under its national pavilion, with products ranging from drinks, bird's nest, durian, coffee and fresh fruit to newer entrants in plastic packaging, real estate and tourism services.

One could dismiss this as routine chamber PR. But the persistence is the story. In a region where trade missions come and go with each change of government, an ethnic-Chinese business association showing up without fail for nearly a decade is not nothing. It signals that, for a large slice of Malaysian capital, the China market is not a passing opportunity but a structural commitment — one that survives political cycles in both Kuala Lumpur and Beijing.

## From "selling goods" to "building brands": the logic of upgrading

ACCCIM president Ng Yee Ping is candid about the shift. Helping Malaysian firms crack the China market remains the first goal, he says, but the easy-arbitrage era is ending. Chinese consumers now care about product quality, brand image, safety standards and supply capacity. Malaysian companies, long comfortable simply exporting commodities, must learn to build brands, develop channels and cultivate long-term relationships.

This is the "model upgrade" the headline promises — and it is more revealing than it sounds. For years the Malaysia–China trade story was told in volumes: palm oil, electronics, durian, the relentless growth of two-way commerce. What ACCCIM is now describing is the exhaustion of that volume-first logic. When a chamber of commerce starts talking about branding and channels rather than tonnage, it is admitting that the low-hanging fruit has been picked. The next phase of market access will favour firms that can tell a story, certify a standard and sustain a presence — not those that merely ship a container.

## Caught between the United States and China: the delicate stance of ethnic capital

Here is the part the press release will not say out loud. ACCCIM is an ethnic-Chinese institution in a Malay-majority, Bumiputera-preferential state. Its very ability to act as a bridge to China is both its greatest asset and its permanent political vulnerability. In Malaysian politics, ethnic-Chinese capital has historically attracted suspicion of "dual loyalty" — too close to Beijing for comfort among Malay nationalists, yet never fully trusted by either side.

So when a Chinese chamber organises a trade mission to China, it walks a line. The framing is studiously commercial and squarely in Malaysia's national interest: opening doors for small and medium enterprises, diversifying their sales channels, reducing dependence on any single customer. Notice the careful phrasing — "reducing dependence on a single market" cuts both ways. It sells China access without ever appearing to pledge allegiance. Under Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, who has worked hard to balance Washington and Beijing, that studied neutrality is not a weakness but a survival skill. Ethnic-Chinese capital thrives precisely when it can be read as Malaysian first, Chinese second.

## From buyer–seller to co-investor: the third-market imagination

The most interesting sentence in the chamber's pitch is the quiet one about the future. Ng argues that Malaysian and Chinese firms should move beyond simple buying and selling toward complementary strengths, joint investment and co-development of markets. Malaysia, he suggests, could draw on its base in electronics, semiconductors and international supply chains to find opportunities with Chinese firms in components, production support and supply-chain collaboration; it could also host processing, assembly, warehousing or regional distribution at home, then ride Chinese firms' technology and business networks to reach ASEAN and other international markets.

Read closely, this is a sketch of a "third road." Not the old commodity-export model, and not outright dependence on Chinese capital either, but a manufacturing-and-logistics partnership in which Malaysia leverages its location, its familiarity with the Malay world and Islamic markets, and its ethnic-Chinese commercial networks to become a node — not a bystander — in Chinese firms' global expansion. For Kuala Lumpur, which has watched supply chains reorganise under U.S.–China rivalry, that node status is the prize. It is also, conveniently, the kind of story that plays well in both Washington and Beijing.

## Youth and AI: a required course for the next generation

The delegation is not only grey-haired tycoons. ACCCIM is sending 46 young entrepreneurs to Soochow University from 7 to 10 November for a high-end artificial-intelligence programme, to study how China is deploying AI and smart manufacturing. It is a small detail with large symbolism: the chamber is explicitly preparing its next generation for an economy in which the classical trading skills — finding a buyer, negotiating a price — are no longer enough.

The logic is plain. If the "model upgrade" is real, the people who must execute it are not the founders of 1980s trading houses but twenty-somethings fluent in algorithms and supply-chain software. By routing them through China's AI ecosystem, ACCCIM is betting that the future of ethnic-Chinese commerce lies as much in technology transfer as in trade margins. Whether that bet pays off depends less on the chamber than on whether Malaysia's own digital economy can absorb what those 46 young people bring home.

## A bowl of bird's nest and the Nanyang story: soft power beyond trade

Ng makes one last point that is easy to overlook. Beyond the orders and the channels, he wants the pavilion to tell the "Nanyang story" — to let Chinese and international visitors encounter Malaysia's culture and history through its traditional foods and distinctive products. A bowl of bird's nest, a whiff of durian: these are not just export lines but ambassadors.

For a diaspora that has spent two centuries proving it belongs in Southeast Asia, the ability to narrate itself — to turn its own heritage into a story that is both marketable and dignified on a global stage — is its own quiet form of power. The chamber that once existed mainly to protect ethnic-Chinese business interests is now, whether it names it or not, an instrument of cultural diplomacy. CIIE, for all its commercial machinery, becomes a stage on which Malaysia's Chinese community tells China, and the world, who it is.

## Further reading

**ACCCIM (Associated Chinese Chambers of Commerce and Industry of Malaysia)**: The apex ethnic-Chinese business federation in Malaysia, long serving as a bridge between Malaysian and Chinese markets; the only Malaysian organiser never to miss a CIIE edition.

**China International Import Expo (CIIE)**: A state-level import fair held annually in Shanghai since 2018, central to China's pledge to open its market and a key gateway for Southeast Asian agricultural and consumer goods.

**Bumiputera policy**: Malaysia's post-independence framework favouring ethnic Malays and other indigenous groups, which has shaped the political positioning of ethnic-Chinese capital and the recurring "dual loyalty" narrative.

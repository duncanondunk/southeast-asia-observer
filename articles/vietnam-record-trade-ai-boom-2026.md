# 越南前8月贸易额冲上7700亿美元：AI浪潮下的"中国加一"样本

9月3日，越南统计总局公布：2026年前8个月进出口总额达到7700亿美元，创下同期历史最高纪录。据日经亚洲援引该机构数据，这一跳升很大程度上由人工智能（AI）带动的电子产品与零部件需求驱动。当全球算力建设拉动硬件订单，越南这个曾被贴上"纺织与鞋类"标签的经济体，正被重新定价为AI硬件供应链的关键一环。

## 一个创纪录的数字

7700亿美元，是越南今年前8个月进出口的总和，意味着若节奏延续，全年贸易额有望逼近1.15万亿美元，体量已接近数倍于其经济规模的传统认知。这个数字本身是中性的——它同时计入进口与出口，却清晰勾勒出一条被AI需求重塑的贸易曲线。对河内而言，这是"中国加一（China Plus One）"战略十年来最响亮的注脚。

## AI是那把 swing factor

日经亚洲的报道将AI明确为这次跳升的摆动因子。超大规模数据中心与消费电子品牌为分散单一生产基风险，过去数年持续把产能转向越南；而AI服务器、加速器以及相关线缆、冷却与印制电路板的需求，进一步拉满了当地装配线的负荷。越南扮演的，是承接上游算力硬件外溢的角色，从成品设备一路延伸到上游组件。

## "中国加一"的十年赌注

越南用十年时间把自己营销为跨国公司分散风险的首选目的地。起点是中美贸易争端，继而是疫情对单一基地的冲击，企业被迫多源布局。河内的逻辑很直接：用稳定的劳动力、持续改善的基建与可观的关税优惠，换取产业转移的窗口。7700亿美元，正是这笔赌注累积至今的证据，在AI capex成为拉动全球电子链最大单力的时候集中兑现。

## 加工型经济的底色

贸易额是一把粗尺，它同时量两个方向。越南结构上仍是加工型经济体：零部件进来，成品电子出去。其 deepening 的AI相关制造——包括外包的半导体封装测试、印制电路板，以及塞进数据中心机架的线缆与冷却硬件——大量吸入中间品、吐出附加值偏薄的成品。这不是越南独有的批评，而是东南亚后段电子制造的标准架构。

## 繁荣下的隐忧

规模是新，但结构旧。当一国出口画像被AI需求重估，它对单一外部需求的暴露也随之升高。若全球算力投资退潮，或主要客户的资本开支转向，越南的高位贸易额可能迅速回撤。更现实的约束在电网与物流：AI硬件产线耗电凶猛，而越南电力与港口吞吐的瓶颈，会直接框定这条曲线能走多高、走多久。

## 同一周的两种叙事

贸易数字并非独自到来。就在公布前后，胡志明市一法庭在一起毒品运输案中判处十一人死刑。两个画面并置令人玩味：同一个国家，一边向全球电子买家推销自己为稳定、讲规则的基地，一边在国内法庭以终极刑罚处置跨境贩毒。一体化的高端供应链，并不发生在无菌洁净室，而发生在同时也是走私 coastline 的海岸线上。

## 区域产业链的再平衡

越南的跃升，是区域产业链再平衡的一个刻度。它既承接了由中国外溢的订单，也倒逼东盟邻国在招商、供电与技工供给上加速补短板。对东南亚整体而言，AI硬件红利不是零和——马来西亚抢半导体封测，泰国拼电动车与数据基建，越南吃组装与组件，各自的位次在重新排定，而共同的前提，是稳住制度与能源供给。

## 数字之外，看什么

7700亿的 headline 很抓眼，但真正要盯的，是这条曲线背后的构成与韧性。AI需求是真实的结构性力量，还是被提前透支的订单？越南能否把"组装红利"升级为"技术红利"？当外部资本因算力叙事涌入，治理与电网能否接住？对这些问题的回答，比一个数字更能判断越南这轮跃升能走多远。

## 延伸阅读

**中国加一（China Plus One）**：跨国企业为降低对单一生产基地依赖，将产能分散至中国的替代目的地的策略，越南是最大承接国之一。

**半导体封装测试（Semiconductor Assembly, Test and Packaging）**：芯片制造后段环节，越南、马来西亚等东盟国家在此环节深度嵌入全球电子供应链。

**附加值（Value-added）**：产品在加工环节新创造的价值；加工型经济常因大量进口中间品而呈现"贸易额高、本土附加值薄"的特征。

# Vietnam's Eight-Month Trade Hits a Record $770bn: A "China Plus One" Tested by the AI Wave

On 3 September, Vietnam's General Statistics Office reported that total trade in the first eight months of 2026 reached US$770 billion, the highest figure for the period on record. Citing the agency, Nikkei Asia attributed much of the jump to demand for electronics and components driven by artificial intelligence. As global compute build-out pulls hardware orders, Vietnam — long tagged with "textiles and footwear" — is being repriced as a critical link in the AI hardware supply chain.

## A record number

US$770 billion is the sum of Vietnam's imports and exports over eight months; if the pace holds, full-year trade could approach US$1.15 trillion, a scale that upends the conventional view of an economy several times smaller. The number is neutral — it counts both directions — yet it sketches a trade curve reshaped by AI demand. For Hanoi, it is the loudest footnote yet to a decade of "China Plus One" strategy.

## AI as the swing factor

Nikkei Asia names AI explicitly as the swing factor. Hyperscalers and consumer-electronics brands, diversifying away from a single base against US–China tensions and pandemic shocks, have shifted capacity to Vietnam for years; demand for AI servers, accelerators and the cabling, cooling and printed-circuit boards around them has maxed out local assembly lines. Vietnam's role is to absorb the upstream spillover of compute hardware, from finished devices down to components.

## A decade-long China Plus One bet

Vietnam spent a decade marketing itself as the preferred destination for multinationals spreading risk. The trigger was the US–China trade dispute, then pandemic disruption to single bases; firms were forced to diversify. Hanoi's logic was direct: stable labour, improving infrastructure and sizable tariff preferences in exchange for a window of industrial relocation. US$770 billion is the accumulated evidence of that bet, realised in bulk just as AI capex became the single largest pull on global electronics chains.

## The substrate of a processing economy

Trade volume is a blunt ruler; it measures both ways. Structurally Vietnam remains a processing economy: components in, finished electronics out. Its deepening AI-adjacent manufacturing — outsourced semiconductor assembly and test, printed-circuit boards, and the cabling and cooling hardware that goes into data-centre racks — pulls in intermediates at scale and ships out finished product with thinner domestic value-added. This is not a Vietnam-specific critique but the standard architecture of late-stage Southeast Asian electronics.

## Worries beneath the boom

The scale is new; the structure is old. When an export profile is repriced by AI demand, its exposure to a single external demand rises too. If global compute investment recedes, or key customers trim capital spending, Vietnam's elevated trade could retract quickly. The more immediate constraint is power and logistics: AI hardware lines are power-hungry, and Vietnam's electricity and port bottlenecks will directly cap how high and how long this curve can run.

## Two narratives in one week

The trade figure did not arrive alone. Around the announcement, a Ho Chi Minh City court handed down eleven death sentences in a drug-trafficking case. The juxtaposition is telling: the same state sells itself to global electronics buyers as a stable, rules-based base, while its domestic courts impose capital punishment at scale in narcotics cases that often run through the very logistics hubs now handling AI-server components. Integrating into high-value supply chains does not happen in a sterile clean room.

## Rebalancing the regional chain

Vietnam's leap is one tick on the dial of regional rebalancing. It absorbs orders spilled from China and pushes ASEAN neighbours to close gaps in investment promotion, power supply and technical labour. For Southeast Asia as a whole, the AI hardware dividend is not zero-sum — Malaysia chases semiconductor test and packaging, Thailand bets on EVs and data infrastructure, Vietnam takes assembly and components — and the shared prerequisite is stable institutions and energy supply.

## Beyond the number, what to watch

The US$770bn headline grabs attention, but what matters is the composition and resilience behind the curve. Is AI demand a real structural force or front-loaded orders? Can Vietnam upgrade "assembly dividends" into "technology dividends"? Can governance and the grid absorb the capital rushing in on the compute narrative? The answers, more than a single figure, will decide how far this leap carries.

## Further reading

**China Plus One**: A strategy by which multinationals disperse capacity beyond a single base to reduce dependence; Vietnam is among the largest recipients.

**Semiconductor Assembly, Test and Packaging (ATP)**: The back-end of chip manufacturing in which ASEAN countries such as Vietnam and Malaysia are deeply embedded in global electronics supply chains.

**Value-added**: The new value created in a processing stage; processing economies often show "high trade, thin domestic value-added".

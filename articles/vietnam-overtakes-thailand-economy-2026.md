# 越南经济逼近泰国：基建红利与制造转移，正在改写东南亚座次

## 一道算术题：越南何时越过泰国

按名目GDP计，越南2025年实际增速约8.02%，经济总量已达约5140亿美元（约3.69万亿元人民币），人均突破5026美元（约3.6万元人民币）。若保持这一势头，越南名目GDP有望在2026至2027年间达到约5000亿至5500亿美元区间，超越泰国，成为仅次于印尼的东南亚第二大经济体。一道原本被认为遥不可及的算术题，正被改写。

## 8%增长的底气：公共投资撑起加速度

增长引擎是空前规模的公共投资。越南规划2026至2030年投入相当于GDP一成、约1500万亿越南盾（约4308亿元人民币）用于基建；2026年公共投资预算预计年增约26%，据国营越南投资与开发银行（BIDV）首席经济学家陈文禄（Can Van Luc）估算，单此一项可额外拉动经济增长1.6个百分点。河内为2026年及之后设定了逾10%的增速目标。

## 从装配车间到物流节点：基建重塑版图

一批旗舰项目正提速：胡志明市近郊的隆城国际机场预计2026年启用；北南高速公路与深水港网络渐成；获中国支持的北部铁路已开工。越南正从低成本的"组装车间"转向区域物流节点。基础设施的乘数效应，正在把外资与制造能力沉淀为长期竞争力。

## 高科技占比破三成，电子出口超泰

结构升级同样明显：电子、半导体、精密工程等高科技已占制造业产出逾三成，电子产品出口突破1200亿美元（约8616亿元人民币），在细分领域已超过泰国。逾百万IT从业者的数字服务业以两位数增长，服务业贡献约42%的GDP，使越南向多元增长模式靠拢。

## 泰国的一边：老龄、负债与车企退潮

反观泰国，经合组织（OECD）预计其2026年实际增速仅1.5%，较前一年再降0.5个百分点。中位数年龄已达40岁（越南约30岁），老龄化推高医疗与劳动力成本；家庭高负债压抑内需，旅游业复苏缓慢，美国关税又施压制造。2025年铃木退出泰国整车生产、本田缩减产能，凸显汽车业这一国家名片正失色。

## 争议未止：超越究竟是几年内的事

并非所有人都认同"今年即超车"。台湾东盟研究中心主任徐遵慈（Kristy Hsu）2026年2月指出，即便越南增10%、泰国仅增2%，按IMF数据两者名目缺口当年仍难弥合。英国经济与商业研究中心（CEBR）则更长远：预计越南2028年后超越泰国，2035年GDP约9940亿美元（约7.13万亿元人民币），进而超过新加坡。超越是大概率，时间却是变量。

## 区域含义：供应链重组的真实剖面

这场追赶的本质，是全球供应链重组与中等国家基建竞赛的叠加。越南借势而上，泰国则被内部结构与外部冲击夹击。泰柬边境紧张更添变数。座次易位不会一夜完成，但它揭示的趋势已然清晰：东南亚的增长重心，正在重新分布。

## 延伸阅读

**供应链（Supply Chain）**：产品从原料到终端的全过程网络；近年"中国+1"与友岸外包推动产能向越南等东南亚国家转移。

**基础设施（Infrastructure）**：交通、能源、通信等基础硬件；被视为拉动增长、改善营商环境的核心公共投资。

**外商直接投资（Foreign Direct Investment）**：境外资本以获取长期经营利益为目的的直接投资，是越南制造崛起的关键外力。

# Vietnam closes in on Thailand: infrastructure dividends and supply-chain shift redraw Southeast Asia's economic order

## A simple sum: when does Vietnam pass Thailand

Measured by nominal GDP, Vietnam grew about 8.02% in real terms in 2025, reaching an economy of roughly US$514 billion and a per-capita income above US$5,026. If this momentum holds, Vietnam's nominal GDP could reach the US$500–550 billion range in 2026–2027, overtaking Thailand to become Southeast Asia's second-largest economy after Indonesia. An arithmetic problem once thought distant is being rewritten.

## The confidence of 8% growth: public investment as accelerator

The growth engine is public investment on an unprecedented scale. Vietnam plans to spend the equivalent of 10% of GDP — about 1,500 quadrillion dong — on infrastructure between 2026 and 2030; the 2026 public-investment budget is set to rise about 26% year-on-year, which, according to Can Van Luc, chief economist at the state-owned Bank for Investment and Development of Vietnam (BIDV), could add 1.6 percentage points to growth. Hanoi has set a target of over 10% expansion for 2026 and beyond.

## From assembly workshop to logistics node: infrastructure redraws the map

A batch of flagship projects is accelerating: Long Thanh International Airport near Ho Chi Minh City is expected to open in 2026; the North–South expressway and deep-sea port network are taking shape; a China-backed railway in the north has broken ground. Vietnam is shifting from a low-cost "assembly workshop" to a regional logistics node. The multiplier effect of infrastructure is turning foreign capital and manufacturing capacity into durable competitiveness.

## High-tech share tops 30%, electronics exports surpass Thailand

Structural upgrading is equally visible: electronics, semiconductors and precision engineering already account for over 30% of manufacturing output, and electronics exports have surpassed US$120 billion, exceeding Thailand in that segment. Digital services backed by more than a million IT workers are growing at double digits, and services contribute about 42% of GDP, pulling Vietnam toward a multi-engine growth model.

## Thailand's side: ageing, debt and automakers retreating

Thailand, by contrast, is forecast by the OECD to grow just 1.5% in real terms in 2026, down another 0.5 percentage points. The median age has reached 40 (versus about 30 in Vietnam); ageing lifts healthcare and labour costs, high household debt suppresses domestic demand, the tourism recovery is slow, and US tariffs pressure manufacturing. In 2025 Suzuki withdrew from Thai automobile production and Honda scaled back, exposing the fading lustre of a national flagship industry.

## The debate is not settled: is the overtaking a matter of years

Not everyone accepts that Vietnam will pass Thailand "this year." Kristy Hsu, director of the Taiwan ASEAN Research Center, argued in February 2026 that even with Vietnam growing 10% and Thailand just 2%, IMF data show the nominal gap cannot close within the year. The Centre for Economics and Business Research (CEBR) in London takes a longer view: it expects Vietnam to surpass Thailand after 2028 and reach about US$994 billion by 2035, then overtaking Singapore. Overtaking is probable; the timing is the variable.

## Regional implications: a real cross-section of supply-chain recomposition

At its core, this catch-up is the superposition of global supply-chain recomposition and a mid-power infrastructure race. Vietnam rides the wave; Thailand is squeezed between its own structural weaknesses and external shocks, with Thai–Cambodian border tensions adding uncertainty. The reordering will not happen overnight, but the trend it reveals is already clear: Southeast Asia's growth centre of gravity is redistributing.

## Further reading

**Supply Chain**: The full network through which a product moves from raw material to end user; recent "China+1" and friend-shoring strategies have shifted capacity toward Vietnam and other Southeast Asian states.

**Infrastructure**: Foundational hardware such as transport, energy and communications, treated as core public investment that drives growth and improves the business environment.

**Foreign Direct Investment**: Direct investment by external capital seeking long-term operating returns, the key external force behind Vietnam's manufacturing rise.

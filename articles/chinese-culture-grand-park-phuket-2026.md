# 普吉“中华文化大乐园”闭园：一场落在南洋故地的文化再连接

9月30日，2026“中华文化大乐园”（泰国普吉园）在普吉中学举行闭园仪式暨汇报演出，十余天的文化研学之旅就此落幕。活动由华侨大学主办、普吉中学承办。表面看，这只是一场面向当地青少年的假期文化营；但如果把它放进泰国华人的历史脉络与中泰关系的现实坐标里去看，意味就大不相同了。

## 背景：一场落在普吉的“文化大乐园”

“中华文化大乐园”是中国面向海外华侨华人青少年推出的品牌文化营，通常由国内高校或侨务机构牵头，在海外开设短期的沉浸式中华文化课程。这一次的特别之处，在于选址——普吉不是泰国随便哪座城市，而是历史上南洋华人最重要的落脚点之一。

从19世纪锡矿开采的浪潮算起，大批福建、广东沿海移民南下，把普吉变成了一座讲闽南话、供妈祖的南洋商港。今天的普吉，旅游业是绝对支柱，而中国游客与中泰商贸又是旅游业的命脉之一。在这样一个地方办“中华文化大乐园”，本身就带着“回乡认祖”与“面向未来”的双重意味。

## 十天课程里发生了什么

据中国侨网（China Qiao Wang）报道，本届大乐园面向普吉当地青少年开设多元化中华文化课程，以沉浸式、体验式教学为主。闭园仪式上，营员们登台献演舞龙《龙腾四海》、歌曲《如愿》、葫芦丝《望春风》、古典舞《红扇》等节目，把十几天的学习成果搬上了舞台。

普吉中学校长瓦查拉萨·宋班在致辞中说，学校作为本地区中文教学与文化交流的中心，未来将持续完善中文课程体系，深化与泰南各校的学术交流，助力当地华文教育可持续发展；他还勉励学生学有所成，投身普吉的旅游、商贸、服务等领域，推动泰中两国世代友好。

这些话不能只当客套来听。在普吉这样的旅游城市，中文能力几乎直接等于就业筹码——会说中文的本地青年，在酒店、导游、跨境商贸里都更有底气。华人文化传承在这里因此不只是“乡愁”，更是实打实的人力资本。

## 为什么是普吉：南洋华人故地的再连接

泰南素来是东南亚华人最密集的区域之一，普吉、攀牙、董里一带的闽南、潮州社群，族谱大多能追溯到清代下南洋的先民。华侨大学泰国校友会会长黄滨出席闭园式，这个细节值得玩味：校友会的存在，说明这所诞生于福建、以“为侨服务”立校的大学，在泰国已经扎下了自己的校友网络。

换句话说，大乐园不是“空降”的短期项目，而是架在既有华人社团、校友网络与华校之上的“增量”。它补的正是日常华文教育之外的体验短板——书法、民乐、舞蹈、舞龙，这些在课堂上不常系统教、却在身份认同里极为关键的文化符号。

## 华侨大学的角色：从侨校到文化外交平台

华侨大学华文教育处处长刘杰在致辞中表示，本届大乐园成效斐然，学员积极投入、学有所获，普吉的孩子文明有礼、勤学善思。这番话背后，是华侨大学自身的转型叙事：建校之初为归侨子女就学而设，如今已成为中国华文教育与离散文化外交的重要支点。

近年来，类似“中华文化大乐园”的项目在印度尼西亚、泰国、马来西亚等地轮番落地，由省级海外联谊会、地方高校分段承办。它们的共同点很清晰——不以意识形态输出为目的，而是以语言、艺术、节俗为媒介，把“中华文化”从教科书里请出来，变成可触摸、可表演、可带回家给祖辈看的活东西。

## 软实力的毛细血管：文化课如何织就民间纽带

普吉府办公室主任纳隆·翁因在观摩后盛赞营员表现，并指出普吉与中国人文纽带深厚，中华文化长期浸润在当地饮食、建筑与民俗之中。这个观察其实道出了文化外交的底层逻辑：比起宏大叙事，真正能沉淀为民间友好的，往往是这些“毛细血管”级别的日常接触。

一个泰国少年学会吹一段葫芦丝、舞一段龙，他未必立刻理解背后的文明脉络，但他记住了“中文很好玩、中华文化很美”。当千千万万个这样的瞬间累积起来，中泰关系的民意基础便不再是抽象口号，而是一张张具体的、带着温度的脸。

## 尾声与延伸：闭园不是终点

闭园仪式落幕，但课程留下的种子才刚发芽。对主办方的华侨大学、承办方的普吉中学，以及台下的营员家庭来说，这场“大乐园”更像一次周期性的文化“充电”——它提醒这些南洋华人的后裔：你们的来路，与一片叫中国的土地相连。

从更宽的视角看，当中泰经贸、旅游、基建合作一路升温，像“中华文化大乐园”这样的项目，恰恰补上了硬联通之外的“软连接”。它不喧哗，却持久；不强势，却扎实。这或许正是文化外交最该有的样子。

## 延伸阅读

**华文教育（Chinese-Medium Education）**：面向海外华侨华人及当地民众的中文与中华文化教学体系，是维系离散族群语言与身份认同的核心机制。

**南洋（Nanyang）**：近代以来中国人对东南亚的统称，特指“下南洋”移民潮所及的环南海贸易与移民网络。

**文化走出去（Cultural Going-Out）**：中国以文化产品、语言教育与人文交流为载体，在海外塑造国家形象、增进民间互信的对外战略取向。

# Phuket's "Chinese Culture Grand Park" Closes: A Cultural Reconnection in an Old Nanyang Homeland

On 30 September 2026, the 2026 "Chinese Culture Grand Park" (Thailand·Phuket Park) held its closing ceremony and gala performance at Phuket Middle School, bringing a ten-plus-day cultural study tour to a close. The event was hosted by Huaqiao University and undertaken by Phuket Middle School. On its face, this was just a holiday culture camp for local teenagers. Read it against the historical fabric of Thailand's ethnic Chinese and the current coordinates of China–Thailand relations, however, and the meaning changes sharply.

## The backdrop: a "Grand Park" lands in Phuket

The "Chinese Culture Grand Park" is a flagship cultural camp that China runs for overseas-Chinese youth. Typically led by a domestic university or diaspora-affairs body, it offers short-term, immersive Chinese-culture courses overseas. What makes this edition notable is its location: Phuket is not just any Thai city — it was one of the most important landing points for Nanyang Chinese in history.

From the tin-mining boom of the 19th century, waves of emigrants from Fujian and Guangdong moved south and turned Phuket into a Nanyang trading port that spoke Hokkien and enshrined Mazu. Today tourism is the island's absolute backbone, and Chinese tourists plus Sino-Thai commerce are among its lifeblood. To run a "Chinese Culture Grand Park" in such a place carries a double meaning — both "returning home to honour one's ancestors" and "facing the future."

## What happened in ten days

According to China Qiao Wang, this edition offered Phuket's local youth a diversified menu of Chinese-culture courses built around immersive, experiential teaching. At the closing ceremony, campers took the stage with a dragon dance titled "Dragons Soaring Across the Four Seas," the song "As You Wish," a hulusi solo "Yearning for the Spring Breeze," and a classical dance "Red Fan," parading more than ten days of learning.

Watcharasa Songban, principal of Phuket Middle School, said in his remarks that the school — a regional hub for Chinese-language teaching and cultural exchange — would keep refining its Chinese curriculum, deepen academic exchange with schools across southern Thailand, and sustain local Chinese-medium education. He urged students to master the language and culture and then "put their learning to use" in Phuket's tourism, trade and services, advancing the two countries' friendship across generations.

These words should not be heard as mere politeness. In a tourist city like Phuket, Chinese proficiency is all but directly convertible into employment leverage — local youth who speak the language stand taller in hotels, tour-guiding and cross-border commerce. Cultural inheritance here is not only nostalgia; it is hard human capital.

## Why Phuket: reconnecting an old Nanyang Chinese homeland

Southern Thailand has long been among the densest concentrations of ethnic Chinese in Southeast Asia. The Hokkien and Teochew communities around Phuket, Phang Nga and Trang can mostly trace their genealogies to Qing-dynasty emigrants who "went down to Nanyang." A telling detail: Huang Bin, president of Huaqiao University's Thailand Alumni Association, attended the closing ceremony — the very existence of that alumni network shows that a Fujian-born university founded to "serve the overseas Chinese" has put down roots in Thailand.

In other words, the Grand Park is not a parachuted, one-off project. It is an "increment" built atop existing clan associations, alumni networks and Chinese schools. What it supplies is precisely the experiential gap that daily Chinese education rarely covers — calligraphy, folk music, dance, dragon dance: cultural symbols seldom taught systematically in class, yet central to identity.

## Huaqiao University's role: from overseas-Chinese school to cultural-diplomacy platform

Liu Jie, director of the Office of Chinese-Medium Education at Huaqiao University, said the camp had been "remarkably effective," with students investing themselves and gaining much, and praised Phuket's children as polite, diligent and thoughtful. Behind those words sits Huaqiao University's own transformation narrative: founded for the children of returned overseas Chinese, it has become a key pivot of China's Chinese-language education and diaspora cultural diplomacy.

In recent years, projects like the "Chinese Culture Grand Park" have landed in turn across Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia, parcelled out to provincial overseas-friendship associations and local universities. Their common thread is clear — they are not aimed at ideological export. Using language, art and festival customs as their media, they lift "Chinese culture" out of the textbook and turn it into something touchable, performable and carry-homeable to show one's grandparents.

## Soft power's capillaries: how culture classes weave people-to-people ties

After watching the students perform, Narong Wongyin, director of Phuket's provincial office, lavished praise on the campers and observed that Phuket and China share deep humanistic bonds, with Chinese culture long permeating local food, architecture and folk life. That observation captures the logic of cultural diplomacy at its base: more than grand narratives, what settles into genuine public goodwill is precisely these capillary-level, everyday encounters.

When a Thai teenager learns a phrase on the hulusi or a step of the dragon dance, he may not immediately grasp the civilisation behind it — but he remembers that "Chinese is fun, and Chinese culture is beautiful." Multiply that moment by the thousands, and the popular foundation of China–Thailand relations ceases to be an abstract slogan and becomes a set of concrete, warm faces.

## After the closing: not an endpoint

The closing ceremony ends, but the seeds sown by the courses have only just sprouted. For the host Huaqiao University, the undertaker Phuket Middle School, and the camper families in the audience, this "Grand Park" was more like a periodic cultural "recharge" — a reminder to these descendants of Nanyang Chinese that their origins are tied to a land called China.

Seen from a wider angle, as Sino-Thai trade, tourism and infrastructure cooperation keep warming, projects like the "Chinese Culture Grand Park" supply precisely the "soft connection" that hard connectivity alone cannot. It is not loud, yet durable; not forceful, yet solid. Perhaps that is exactly what cultural diplomacy ought to look like.

## Further reading

**Chinese-Medium Education**: The system of Chinese-language and Chinese-culture instruction for overseas Chinese and local communities abroad — the core mechanism for sustaining the language and identity of diaspora groups.

**Nanyang**: The modern Chinese term for Southeast Asia, specifically the maritime trade and migration network reached by the "going down to Nanyang" emigration waves.

**Cultural Going-Out**: China's external strategy of shaping its image and building mutual trust abroad through cultural products, language education and people-to-people exchange.

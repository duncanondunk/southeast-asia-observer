# 越南跨过投资级门槛：日系评级先动，河内赢了面子更要补里子

2026年10月8日，评级与投资信息公司（R&I）将越南外币发行人评级从 BB+ 上调至 BBB-、展望稳定。这是越南史上第一次被任何国际评级机构列入投资级。R&I 隶属于日经集团，此番由日系机构抢下「首家」头衔，耐人寻味。

## 跨过门槛：越南首获投资级主权评级

BBB- 看似只比此前的 BB+ 高了一级，却是投机级与投资级之间那道真正的分水岭。R&I 在公告里给出的理由并不复杂：越南今年前9个月经济增速约9%，在东南亚一众经济体里明显领跑；公共债务占国内生产总值比重仍相对偏低，留出了举债搞发展的空间；经常账户保持顺差，外商直接投资（FDI）持续流入，外债负担不重。换言之，基本面「过得去」。

但河内要的从来不止一个符号。投资级意味着越南政府和企业未来在国际市场借钱，理论上能少付一点「风险溢价」。据菲因评级（FiinRatings）分析，在巴塞尔协议第三版标准法下，符合条件的日本金融机构持有越南外币主权债的风险权重，可能从100%降至50%——这本账，日本资金看得最清楚。

## 为什么是日本的评级机构先动？

一个值得玩味的问题：标普、惠誉仍把越南停在 BB+，穆迪停在 Ba2，全是「非投资级」；为什么率先放行的是 R&I？

答案藏在日本与越南深度捆绑的利益里。日本是越南最大的政府开发援助（ODA）提供国，在基建、电力、制造业里盘踞数十年；日企把越南当「中国+1」的关键落脚点，供应链利益实实在在。R&I 既是日经集团一员，其评级结论天然带着东京的视角——给河内贴上投资级，受益最直接的，恰恰是受日本金融监管约束、投资授权（mandate）要求「投资级以上」的那批日系资金。

换句话说，这不是单纯的信用判断，也是一笔地缘经济的账。当美国关税压力下越南急需多元融资、又想减少对单一外部依赖时，日系资本的「投票」来得恰到好处。

## 投资级的光环，与它的边界

但光环有边界。R&I 的 BBB- 是「最低一档」投资级，而三大国际评级机构按兵不动，意味着越南距离真正「面向全球便宜借钱」还差一口气。菲因评级说得直白：新资本大概率先来自日本投资者，短期内不会出现国际债务资金的潮水般涌入。

这也点出一个常被忽略的事实：评级是「信心指标」，不是「提款权」。越南要的是长期资金成本下行、资本市场深化、本币债与国际接轨，这些都不会因为一纸日系评级自动兑现。河内若把此番升级当作庆功宴，那就看错了它的分量。

## 河内的算盘：2030 路线图与富时罗素

河内显然早有筹谋。越南的路线图写得很清楚：到2030年，争取穆迪给出 Baa3、或标普与惠誉给出 BBB- 及以上。今年9月，越南被富时罗素（FTSE Russell）纳入「次级新兴市场」类别，正是同一盘棋的先行子——股市从前沿市场向新兴市场的身份切换，与主权评级升档互为表里。

财政部在2026年4、5月的主权评估中就主动与 R&I 对接，提供宏观、财政、公债与改革数据。这种「主动管理评级」的功夫，说明河内把信用工程当作国家战略来经营，而非坐等市场赏饭。

## 光环之下的暗面

观察者不能只看见光。R&I 自己列出的风险清单，恰恰戳中越南最软的腹部：信贷过快增长、银行体系流动性、房地产相关贷款、金融体系资本充足率、外汇储备水平。这些不是边角问题，而是高速增长的「里子」。

更根本的疑问在于增长质量。越南前三季度9%的增速里，工业与建筑业贡献了近一半，但国内增加值（domestic value added）长期偏低，经济仍带着浓厚的「组装车间」色彩；前8个月贸易逆差创下纪录，电力瓶颈时有发作。评级押注的是「势头」，结构性的短板并不会因为一纸评级自动补齐。

## 区域意涵：信用地理正在重画

把镜头拉远，越南跨线不是孤立事件。东南亚的「投资级俱乐部」原本由新加坡、马来西亚、泰国（勉强）、印尼（近期纳入新兴市场观察）等占据；越南以最快增速跻身其中，意味着区域资本地理正在重画——增长重心从传统枢纽向新兴制造带偏移。

但对其他前沿经济体而言，越南的样本既是激励也是提醒：一张日系评级可以打开一扇门，却替代不了三大机构的集体背书。柬埔寨、老挝等国若想复制，先要回答「里子」是否撑得住的问题。

## 里程碑，而非终点

一句话收束：越南首获投资级，是多年改革与高增长换来的里程碑，值得记一笔。但它更像一个起点而非终点——真正要看的，是标普、惠誉、穆迪何时跟进，以及河内能否把「势头」兑现为「结构」。否则，这级光环再亮，也照不亮增长里的暗室。

## 延伸阅读

**投资级（investment grade）**：评级机构将 BBB- 及以上（标普/惠誉）或 Baa3 及以上（穆迪）列为投资级，低于此即「投机级/垃圾级」，机构买债受投资授权约束。

**巴塞尔协议第三版（Basel III）**：全球银行监管框架，标准法下主权债风险权重影响资本占用，投资级下调风险权重可释放银行放贷能力。

**富时罗素（FTSE Russell）**：指数公司，其市场分类（前沿/新兴/发达）影响被动资金流向，越南2026年9月获纳「次级新兴」类别。

# Vietnam Crosses the Investment-Grade Threshold: A Japanese Rating, Hanoi's Face, and the Homework Still Due

On 8 October 2026, Rating and Investment Information (R&I), a Japanese agency that is part of the Nikkei Group, raised Vietnam's foreign-currency issuer rating from BB+ to BBB- with a stable outlook. It was the first time any international rating agency had placed Vietnam in the investment-grade category. That a Japanese house grabbed the "first" title is, itself, telling.

## Across the threshold: Vietnam's first investment-grade sovereign rating

BBB- looks like a single notch above the old BB+, but it sits on the true dividing line between speculative and investment grade. R&I's reasoning is straightforward enough: Vietnam grew close to 9% in the first nine months of 2026, clearly leading its Southeast Asian peers; its public debt-to-GDP ratio remains relatively low, leaving room to borrow for development; and it runs a current-account surplus with steady foreign direct investment (FDI) and a light external debt burden. In short, the fundamentals "pass."

But Hanoi has never wanted merely a symbol. Investment grade means, in theory, that the government and companies can borrow on international markets at a thinner risk premium. As FiinRatings notes, under the Basel III standardised approach, the regulatory risk weight that qualifying Japanese financial institutions apply to Vietnam's foreign-currency sovereign bonds could fall from 100% to 50% — a line item Japanese money reads most clearly.

## Why did a Japanese agency move first?

A fair question: S&P and Fitch still hold Vietnam at BB+, Moody's at Ba2 — all sub-investment-grade. Why was R&I the one to open the gate?

The answer lies in the deep entanglement of Japanese and Vietnamese interests. Japan is Vietnam's largest provider of official development assistance (ODA), entrenched in infrastructure, power and manufacturing for decades; Japanese firms treat Vietnam as a key "China+1" landing spot, with real supply-chain stakes. R&I is part of the Nikkei Group, and its verdict naturally carries Tokyo's lens — stamping Hanoi as investment grade benefits, most directly, the pool of Japanese capital whose investment mandates require an investment-grade rating from a recognised agency.

In other words, this was not pure credit judgement; it was also a geo-economic calculation. As Vietnam, pressed by US tariffs, seeks diversified financing and wants to reduce reliance on any single external partner, the Japanese "vote" arrived at an opportune moment.

## The halo of investment grade, and its limits

But the halo has boundaries. R&I's BBB- is the lowest rung of investment grade, and with the three global agencies standing pat, Vietnam is still a breath away from truly "borrowing cheaply on a global scale." FiinRatings is blunt: new capital will likely come first from Japanese investors, and there will be no tidal wave of international debt inflows in the near term.

This also exposes a frequently overlooked truth: a rating is a confidence indicator, not a drawdown right. What Vietnam wants — lower long-term funding costs, a deeper capital market, and integration of its local-currency debt with global markets — will not arrive automatically with one Japanese rating. If Hanoi throws a victory banquet over this upgrade, it has misread the rating's weight.

## Hanoi's calculus: the 2030 roadmap and FTSE Russell

Hanoi plainly planned for this. Vietnam's roadmap is explicit: by 2030, secure at least Baa3 from Moody's, or BBB- and above from S&P and Fitch. In September 2026, Vietnam was added by FTSE Russell to its "secondary emerging market" category — an opening move in the same game, where the equity market's shift from frontier to emerging status and the sovereign rating upgrade are two sides of one coin.

The Ministry of Finance engaged R&I directly during the April–May 2026 sovereign review, supplying macro, fiscal, public-debt and reform data. This "active rating management" shows Hanoi treats credit engineering as state strategy, not something to await as a market handout.

## The shadows beneath the halo

An observer cannot see only the light. The risk list R&I itself laid out strikes at Vietnam's softest flank: rapid credit growth, banking-sector liquidity, real-estate-linked lending, the financial system's capital adequacy, and foreign-exchange reserves. These are not edge issues; they are the "substance" of fast growth.

A deeper doubt concerns growth quality. In the 9% expansion of the first three quarters, industry and construction contributed nearly half, yet domestic value added stays low and the economy still wears a heavy "assembly workshop" colour; the trade deficit hit a record in the first eight months, and power bottlenecks flare up from time to time. The rating bets on momentum; structural shortfalls will not be patched by a single rating.

## Regional implications: the credit geography is being redrawn

Zoom out, and Vietnam's crossing is not an isolated event. Southeast Asia's "investment-grade club" was long occupied by Singapore, Malaysia, a struggling Thailand, and Indonesia (recently on emerging-market watch); Vietnam's entry at the fastest growth rate means the regional capital geography is being redrawn — the centre of gravity is shifting from traditional hubs toward emerging manufacturing belts.

But for other frontier economies, Vietnam is both an inspiration and a caution: a Japanese rating can open a door, yet it cannot substitute for the collective backing of the three global agencies. If Cambodia, Laos and others wish to copy the script, they must first answer whether their "substance" can hold.

## A milestone, not a destination

To close in one line: Vietnam's first investment grade is a milestone earned through years of reform and high growth, and it deserves a mark in the ledger. But it is more a starting point than an end — what truly matters is when S&P, Fitch and Moody's follow, and whether Hanoi can convert "momentum" into "structure." Otherwise, however bright this halo, it will not light up the dark rooms inside the growth story.

## Further reading

**Investment grade**: Agencies classify BBB- and above (S&P/Fitch) or Baa3 and above (Moody's) as investment grade; below that is "speculative/junk," and institutional bond-buying is constrained by mandates.

**Basel III**: The global bank-regulation framework; under the standardised approach, sovereign risk weights affect capital charges, and an investment-grade cut can free up banks' lending capacity.

**FTSE Russell**: An index provider whose market classification (frontier/emerging/developed) steers passive flows; Vietnam entered its "secondary emerging" category in September 2026.

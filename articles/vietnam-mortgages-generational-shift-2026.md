## 从"全款买房"到"按揭上车"：越南年轻人的居住革命

在河内与胡志明市，一套中小户型公寓的价格正逼近普通人年收入的三十倍。传统上，越南家庭习惯攒足现金、甚至动用几代人积蓄再买房；如今，越来越多的千禧一代与"Z世代"却选择先贷款、先上车。这种代际价值观的转变，正在把一个曾带有"负债污名"的金融工具，变成城市生活的常规选项。

越南经济增速已连续多个季度位居东南亚前列，世界银行最新预测2026年全年增速可达7.4%。在高速增长带来的中产阶级膨胀、城市化加速和房价持续攀高共同作用下，仅靠家庭储蓄已难以跟上房价上涨速度。房贷因此从边缘产品走向主流。

## 利率上行与政策托底的双重博弈

然而，信贷买房的热潮正遭遇利率压力。2026年8月，越南主要银行针对新客户的12至24个月优惠房贷利率平均约为10.9%，优惠期结束后浮动利率通常升至13%至15%。与今年第二季度8.5%至9.2%的优惠利率区间相比，购房者的月供负担明显加重。

为对冲住房可负担性压力，越南国家银行自7月1日起推出面向35岁以下首次购房者的优惠贷款：前5年固定年利率6.5%，之后10年7.5%。此外，商业银行向保障性住房、工业园区和出口加工区发放的贷款，可不计入信贷增长限额。这些政策意在"精准滴灌"真实居住需求，而非投机需求。

## 房价与收入的剪刀差：光鲜数据下的脆弱性

根据业内机构统计，2026年第二季度河内公寓均价约达每平方米1.23亿越南盾（约3.5万元人民币），胡志明市约达每平方米1.08亿越南盾（约3.1万元人民币）。按60平方米户型估算，河内一套公寓总价约73.8亿越南盾（约211万元人民币），胡志明市约64.8亿越南盾（约185万元人民币）。

对部分家庭而言，房价收入比已超过30倍，远高于国际上通常认为合理的3至8倍区间。这意味着，即便有房贷支撑，购房门槛仍在快速抬高；而利率一旦继续上行，杠杆家庭的现金流风险将显著放大。

## 银行端的隐忧：房地产贷款占比与期限错配

世界银行数据显示，2025年越南房地产信贷扩张42%，远超银行业整体信贷增速，房地产贷款余额已占总贷款余额的25.5%。银行体系向地产的高度集中，叠加存款短期化与房贷长期化的期限错配，使金融系统对房价波动与利率上行更为敏感。

监管层显然意识到这一风险，近来收紧了对收入证明、贷款上限和投机性项目的审查。如何在满足合理住房需求与防范系统性金融风险之间取得平衡，将是越南楼市下一阶段的核心命题。

## 延伸阅读

**Mortgage（住房抵押贷款）**：购房者以所购房产作为抵押向银行借款，分期偿还本息；在越南，这一工具正从传统观念中的"负债"转变为实现城市居住权的常见手段。

**Affordability（住房可负担性）**：衡量普通家庭收入与房价之间关系的指标，通常用房价收入比表示；国际经验认为3至8倍为相对合理区间。

**Maturity mismatch（期限错配）**：银行以短期存款支持长期房贷所形成的流动性风险，一旦利率快速上升或存款大量流失，可能引发资金链紧张。

## From cash-only to leveraged homeownership: Vietnam's generational housing revolution

In Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City, a modest apartment can cost roughly thirty times an average household's annual income. Traditionally, Vietnamese families saved for years, sometimes pooling money across generations, before buying a home. Today, growing numbers of millennials and Gen Z buyers are choosing to borrow first and move in later. This shift in generational values is turning a product once associated with personal shame into a routine path to urban living.

Vietnam's economy has grown faster than most of its neighbours for several quarters, with the World Bank forecasting 7.4% growth for 2026. A swelling middle class, rapid urbanisation and steadily rising property prices mean family savings alone can no longer keep pace with the housing market. Mortgages are moving from the margins to the mainstream.

## Rising rates and policy support: a two-way tug of war

The credit-driven housing boom is now running into higher borrowing costs. In August 2026, promotional mortgage rates for the first 12 to 24 months averaged about 10.9% across major Vietnamese banks, with floating rates after the introductory period typically resetting to 13-15%. That is a marked jump from the 8.5-9.2% range seen in the second quarter, increasing monthly payments for new buyers.

To ease the affordability squeeze, the State Bank of Vietnam introduced preferential loans from 1 July for first-time buyers under 35: a fixed 6.5% for the first five years and 7.5% for the next ten. Commercial banks were also allowed to exclude loans for social housing, industrial parks and export-processing zones from their credit-growth caps. The aim is to channel credit to genuine housing needs rather than speculation.

## The widening gap between prices and incomes

Industry figures show the average price of an apartment in Hanoi reached about VND123 million per square metre (about US$4,920) in the second quarter of 2026, while in Ho Chi Minh City it was around VND108 million per square metre (about US$4,320). For a 60-square-metre unit, that works out to roughly VND7.38 billion (about US$295,000) in Hanoi and VND6.48 billion (about US$259,000) in Ho Chi Minh City.

For many households the price-to-income ratio has climbed above 30, far above the international benchmark of three to eight. Even with mortgage financing, the entry barrier is rising; and if rates climb further, leveraged households could face serious cash-flow stress.

## The bank-side risk: concentration and maturity mismatch

According to World Bank data, real-estate credit in Vietnam expanded by 42% in 2025, far outpacing overall credit growth, and real-estate loans now account for 25.5% of total outstanding bank loans. That concentration, combined with a maturity mismatch between short-term deposits and long-term mortgages, makes the financial system more sensitive to property-price and interest-rate shocks.

Regulators have responded by tightening scrutiny of income verification, loan limits and speculative projects. Balancing legitimate housing demand against systemic financial risk will be the central question for Vietnam's property market in the next phase.

## Further reading

**Mortgage**: A loan secured against a property, repaid in instalments over time; in Vietnam this instrument is shifting from a cultural marker of debt to a common means of urban homeownership.

**Affordability**: A measure of the relationship between household income and property prices, usually expressed as the price-to-income ratio; an internationally recognised reasonable range is three to eight.

**Maturity mismatch**: The liquidity risk created when banks fund long-term mortgages with short-term deposits; it becomes dangerous if interest rates rise quickly or deposits flee.

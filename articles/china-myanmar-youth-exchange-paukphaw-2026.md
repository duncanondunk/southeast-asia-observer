# 中缅青年以技术与文化续写"胞波"情谊

9月3日，仰光外国语大学迎来一场特殊的聚会——2026年第三季度中缅青年交流活动暨中缅媒体茶话会。约300名来自两国的青年、外交官与媒体代表围坐一堂。在"胞波"（Paukphaw，缅语"亲戚"之意）的叙事里，青年正成为延续两国友好最鲜活的载体。

## 仰光的一场第三季度之约

9月3日，由中国驻缅甸使馆与仰光外国语大学共同主办的活动落下帷幕。会场内外，近40家中缅媒体、智库与学术机构代表齐聚，参与的青年远不止语言能力的提升。主办方把重点放在"可带走的合作思路"上：让两国的"00后"在共同生活、学习彼此语言的过程中， firsthand 理解那段共享的历史。

这场活动之所以被看作民心相通的范本，正因为它把宏大的双边关系，落到了青年个体的日常相遇里——一起上课、一起聊天、并肩而坐，友谊便在细微处生长。

## 从茶马古道到数字丝路

从云南普洱到缅甸仰光，这条路让人想起古老的茶马古道。当年马帮驮着茶叶与盐巴穿行于横断山脉，今天的连接则更多发生于实验室、直播间与跨境电商的界面之上。

历史的通路仍在，只是载体重了。缅甸青年在交流中看到的中国，不再是遥远的北方邻国，而是能提供智慧农业、清洁能源与数字工具的切实伙伴。新老通道在此叠合，构成中缅关系最朴素也最坚韧的底色。

## 中国青年眼中的"胞波"

来自普洱学院、正在仰光外国语大学研习缅语的交换生刘昌亚说，正是通过日常的语言学习、与同学的闲谈、肩并肩的共处，他们这一代才得以 firsthand 触摸这份共享的历史，并把"胞波"情谊接过来。

对许多中国学生而言，缅甸不再只是地理课本上的名字。当语言不再是壁垒，历史的亲近感便自然回流——这正是民间外交最难得的部分：它不靠宣言，而靠人对人的理解。

## 技术成为现代化的支点

缅甸战略与国际问题研究所资深顾问乃梭乌在会上指出，中国的智慧农业技术与新能源方案，已成为缅甸国家现代化进程中不可或缺的战略支撑。他鼓励缅甸青年向中国学习先进技术，并推动其本地化应用。

这番话点出了青年交流的实质：它不止于文化温情，更指向发展能力的转移。当技术以可负担、可本地化的方式进入缅甸的农田与电网，中缅合作便有了比口号更硬的落点。

## 媒体交流织就全球南方纽带

缅甸《 Swe Myo Pauk Phaw 》杂志资深编辑德蒙吞分享了她参与中国面向全球南方媒体交流项目的体会。她说，把各国媒体工作者聚到一起，本身就是中国倡导的全球南方团结的具体象征。

在信息极化、叙事争夺加剧的当下，这样的媒体茶话会有其特殊价值：它让缅甸青年看到的不只是单一信源，而是南南国家彼此讲述、彼此倾听的可能。媒体因而成为民心相通的"软基础设施"。

## 侨社与华文教育的桥梁作用

长期在缅的华侨华人，是中缅交流中常被忽视却至关重要的节点。华文学校、侨团与华文媒体，既是缅甸华人维系文化认同的载体，也是中缅民间往来的天然翻译器。

当青年交流强调"把友谊带回各自的国家"，华侨华人社区的价值愈发凸显：他们既懂本地语境，又通中方话语，能在语言、习俗与商机之间穿针引线，使合作从活动本身延伸至日常经贸与社群网络。

## 青年交流的三重意义

其一在语言——打破误解的第一道墙；其二在职业——为青年提供跨境就业与创业的真实选项；其三在机制——澜湄合作等框架把零散的民间互动，固化为可持续的制度化交流。

专家在会上强调，澜湄合作等机制持续促成两国青年深入的技术交流与务实合作。当交流从"一次性活动"变为"常态化机制"，民心相通才真正有了根系。

## 结语：把友谊带回各自的国家

"胞波意为亲戚，而我们本就是亲戚。"仰光大学校长通岱在闭幕时如是说。这句朴素的话，恰是整场活动最好的注脚。

与会青年约定，把在缅甸收获的理解与资源带回国，把"看到的中国"讲给更多人听。也许十年、二十年后，他们中的许多人不会再相见，但这段同行结下的情谊，会成为中缅关系最柔软也最持久的那根线。

## 延伸阅读

**胞波（Paukphaw）**：缅甸语中"亲戚、兄弟"之意，用以形容中缅人民间天然的亲近与信任，是中缅关系特有的情感符号。

**澜湄合作（Lancang–Mekong Cooperation）**：中国、缅甸、老挝、泰国、柬埔寨、越南六国的水资源与区域合作机制，青年交流是其人文支柱之一。

**全球南方（Global South）**：泛指发展中国家群体，中缅青年交流被视为南南合作与民心相通的微观缩影。

# China–Myanmar Youth Renew "Paukphaw" Ties Through Tech and Culture

On 3 September, the Yangon University of Foreign Languages hosted a gathering that felt anything but ordinary — the third-quarter China–Myanmar Youth Exchange and China–Myanmar Media Tea Party. Around 300 young participants, diplomats and media representatives sat together. Within the narrative of "Paukphaw" — the Burmese word for "relatives" — youth are emerging as the liveliest carriers of a friendship between the two countries.

## A Third-Quarter Gathering in Yangon

On 3 September, the event co-hosted by the Chinese Embassy in Myanmar and Yangon University of Foreign Languages concluded. Nearly 40 Chinese and Myanmar media outlets, think tanks and academic institutions took part. The organisers emphasised not language skills but "actionable ideas to take home": letting the post-2000 generation understand a shared history firsthand through daily study and conversation.

What makes this a model of people-to-people bonds is precisely its scale — it reduces a grand bilateral relationship to the everyday encounters of young individuals. Learning together, chatting, sitting side by side: friendship grows in the small details.

## From the Tea Horse Road to a Digital Silk Road

The route from Pu'er in China's Yunnan to Yangon recalls the ancient Tea Horse Road, where caravans carried tea and salt across the Hengduan Mountains. Today the connection runs more often through laboratories, livestreams and cross-border e-commerce interfaces.

The historic passage endures; only the载体 has changed. The China that Myanmar's youth encounter is no longer a distant northern neighbour but a practical partner offering smart agriculture, clean energy and digital tools. Old and new corridors overlap, forming the most plain yet resilient base of China–Myanmar ties.

## "Paukphaw" Through Young Chinese Eyes

Liu Changya, an exchange student from Pu'er University studying the Myanmar language at Yangon University of Foreign Languages, said that it is through everyday language learning, chatting with classmates and simply spending time together that his generation grasps this shared history firsthand and carries forward the Paukphaw friendship.

For many Chinese students, Myanmar is no longer a name in a textbook. When language ceases to be a barrier, historical closeness flows back naturally — the rarest part of people-to-people diplomacy: it rests not on declarations but on human understanding.

## Technology as a Lever for Modernisation

Naing Swe Oo, senior advisory board member of the Myanmar Institute of Strategic and International Studies, noted that Chinese smart-agriculture technologies and new-energy solutions have become indispensable strategic support for Myanmar's modernisation drive. He encouraged Myanmar youth to learn advanced technologies from China and localise their application.

His remark captures the substance of youth exchange: it is more than cultural warmth; it points to the transfer of development capacity. When technology enters Myanmar's farms and grids in affordable, localisable ways, China–Myanmar cooperation gains a harder foothold than slogans.

## Media Exchange Weaves Global South Solidarity

Thet Mon Tun, senior editor at Swe Myo Pauk Phaw Magazine, shared her experience of China-initiated media exchange programmes for Global South countries. By bringing media workers from these countries together, she said, such events stand as a concrete symbol of China-sponsored solidarity among the Global South.

Amid information polarisation and narrative competition, such media tea parties have special value: they let Myanmar's youth see not a single source but the possibility of Global South countries telling and listening to one another. Media thus becomes the "soft infrastructure" of people-to-people bonds.

## The Bridge Role of Diaspora and Chinese-Medium Education

The overseas Chinese long settled in Myanmar are an often-overlooked yet vital node in bilateral exchange. Chinese-medium schools, hometown associations and Chinese-language media are both vehicles for the local ethnic-Chinese community to sustain cultural identity and a natural translator for China–Myanmar grassroots interaction.

When youth exchange stresses "taking friendship back to one's own country," the value of the overseas-Chinese community stands out: fluent in local context and Chinese discourse alike, they thread needles among language, custom and business opportunity, extending cooperation from the event itself into daily economic and community networks.

## Three Meanings of Youth Exchange

First, language — breaking the first wall of misunderstanding. Second, vocation — offering youth real cross-border employment and entrepreneurship options. Third, mechanism — frameworks like Lancang–Mekong Cooperation turn scattered grassroots interactions into institutionalised, sustainable exchange.

Experts stressed that Lancang–Mekong and similar mechanisms continuously facilitate in-depth technological exchange and pragmatic cooperation between the two countries' youth. When exchange shifts from one-off events to routine mechanism, people-to-people bonds truly take root.

## Conclusion: Taking Friendship Back Home

"Paukphaw means relatives and we are relatives," said Thaung Htike, rector of Yangon University, at the closing. The plain sentence is the best footnote to the whole event.

Participants pledged to carry the understanding and resources gained in Myanmar back to their countries and tell more people "the China they saw." Perhaps in ten or twenty years many will never meet again, but the friendship forged in this shared journey will remain the softest yet most durable thread of China–Myanmar relations.

## Further reading

**Paukphaw**: A Burmese term meaning "relatives" or "siblings," used to describe the natural kinship and trust between Chinese and Myanmar peoples — a distinctive emotional symbol of the bilateral relationship.

**Lancang–Mekong Cooperation (LMC)**: A six-country mechanism (China, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam) for water-resource and regional cooperation, with youth exchange among its people-to-people pillars.

**Global South**: A broad grouping of developing countries; China–Myanmar youth exchange is read as a microcosm of South–South cooperation and people-to-people bonds.

# 重庆面向新加坡等东盟国家发布75个航空产业合作机会

2026年9月28日，重庆市发展和改革委员会一次性端出两份清单——航空产业领域的"需求清单"与"应用清单"，向新加坡等东盟国家的企业、科研机构和投资主体，一口气释放了75个潜在合作机会。这不是一次普通的项目推介。把"资源禀赋、产业基础、开放通道"翻译成市场主体"可读懂、可对接、可投资"的"项目语言"，重庆这次把中新（重庆）战略性互联互通示范项目，从金融、物流的旧赛道，正式推到了天空。

过去我们谈中国—东盟合作，往往停留在宏观叙事；重庆这次的好处，是把它拆成了可投、可算、可对账的具体机会。

## 两张清单：58.7亿元招商与45项技术出海

需求清单一端，重庆梳理出航空制造、航空维修、航材流通、航空物流、航空+低空、航空+商业、航空+文旅、航空+培训八大板块，拿出30个重点招商项目，总投资额约58.7亿元人民币。供给清单另一端，汇集全市骨干企业的特色产品与服务，列出45个优势技术产品，覆盖航空制造、维修、物流、低空、文旅、培训七大板块。

一进一出，恰好把"引资"与"出海"两张牌同时打出。58.7亿元不是小数目，但更值得注意的，是清单把"合作"从口号变成了带价格、带板块、带责任主体的项目包。资本最怕悬空叙事，清单的意义，正在于把一个区域的产业野心，摊开给东盟伙伴看。

有人会问，航空产业门槛高、回报周期长，重庆凭什么？

## "项目语言"：把通道优势翻译成资本能读懂的话

重庆在清单里反复强调"商业可行性"与"项目语言"。说白了，就是不再讲"我们有区位优势"这种空话，而是告诉新加坡的资本：你要的供应链节点、你要的维修产能、你要的低空应用场景，我都已经帮你归好类、算好账、备好地了。

这恰恰是西部省份对接东盟时最稀缺的能力。过去，中西部城市谈开放，常陷于"我有通道、你有市场"的笼统对接；重庆这次用两张清单，把"通道"具体成了"可投资的清单"。据中新社报道，渝新双方下一步将依托清单开展精准招商、供需对接与"揭榜挂帅"——后者意味着，技术难点直接张榜，谁有本事谁来揭。

把招商变成"张榜求贤"，比单纯开推介会实在得多。

## 中新（重庆）项目：从金融物流走向天空

理解这份清单，要先看懂它挂在哪个框架下——中新（重庆）战略性互联互通示范项目。这是中国与新加坡第三个政府间合作项目，前些年主打金融、航空、物流、信息通信四大通道。航空本就是其题中之义，但过去多停留在航线加密、货运互通；如今把"航空产业"整体做成机会清单，意味着合作从"通航线"升级到"建产业"。

新加坡本身没有庞大的航空制造业，却握有资本、适航标准话语权和全球航空服务网络。重庆有制造基础、有市场纵深、有西部陆海新通道的联运能力。两者一拍即合的逻辑很清晰：新加坡出规则与资本，重庆出场景与产能。

这不是谁补贴谁，而是各取所需的互补。

## 为什么是航空？重庆的雄心与东盟的缺口

为什么偏偏选中航空？答案藏在两端。重庆方面，正处产业升级窗口，想从汽车、电子的老本行，往更高附加值的航空制造与低空经济延伸；东盟方面，东南亚民航市场是全球增长最快的区域之一，印尼、越南、菲律宾等国机队扩张、维修需求旺盛，却普遍缺乏本土航空产业链。

一边有产能想走出去，一边有需求却补不上链。重庆把"航空+低空""航空+文旅""航空+培训"打包，本质上是在为东盟的航空缺口，提供一个就近的、可中国标准的解决方案。低空经济更是新风口，谁先立住场景，谁就握住下一轮区域竞争的门票。

航空合作看着高大上，根子上是生意——东盟的增长红利，重庆想分一杯。

## 双向门：东盟资本入渝，渝企出海

75个机会的妙处，在于它是双向的。需求清单吸引东盟资本、技术落地重庆；供给清单则帮重庆企业把产品与服务卖进新加坡及更广阔的东盟市场。这种"引进来"与"走出去"在同一份文件里闭环，比单向招商更可持续。

当然，清单只是起点。58.7亿元的项目能否真正落袋，取决于后续对接效率、要素成本与营商环境。重庆过去在笔电、汽车集群上证明过自己"把链做厚"的能力，航空这块新战场，考验的是它能否把制造优势转成标准优势。

中国—东盟合作讲了多年，缺的从来不是愿景，而是像这样能算账、能对账、能落地的具体抓手。重庆这次，给了东盟一个看得懂的入口。

## 延伸阅读

**中新（重庆）战略性互联互通示范项目（China–Singapore Chongqing Connectivity Initiative）**：中国与新加坡第三个政府间合作项目，以金融、航空、物流、信息通信四大通道为支柱，是中国西部与东盟互联互通的制度化平台。

**低空经济（Low-Altitude Economy）**：以无人机、电动垂直起降飞行器等低空飞行器为载体，融合物流、文旅、巡检等场景的新兴经济形态，正成为中国与东盟产业合作的新增长极。

**西部陆海新通道（New Western Land–Sea Corridor）**：由重庆牵头的多式联运国际物流通道，连接中国西部与东盟，是重庆对接东盟的"开放骨架"，也是本次航空产业清单的联运依托。

# Chongqing Unveils 75 Aviation Industry Opportunities for Singapore and ASEAN

On 28 September 2026, the Chongqing Development and Reform Commission released two lists at a stroke — a "demand list" and a "supply list" in the aviation sector — opening 75 potential cooperation opportunities to enterprises, research institutes and investors across Singapore and the wider ASEAN bloc. This was no ordinary investment pitch. By translating "resource endowments, industrial foundations and open corridors" into "project language" that market players can "read, match and invest in," Chongqing has pushed the China–Singapore (Chongqing) Connectivity Initiative from its old lanes of finance and logistics firmly into the sky.

When we talk about China–ASEAN cooperation, the conversation too often stalls at macro narrative. Chongqing's useful move here is to break it down into concrete, investable, auditable opportunities.

## Two lists: 5.87 billion yuan in projects and 45 export-ready technologies

On the demand side, Chongqing mapped eight segments — aircraft manufacturing, MRO (maintenance, repair and overhaul), material circulation, aviation logistics, aviation-plus-low-altitude, aviation-plus-commerce, aviation-plus-tourism and aviation-plus-training — and tabled 30 priority investment projects worth about 5.87 billion yuan (roughly US$820 million). On the supply side, it pooled the signature products and services of the city's backbone firms into 45 competitive technologies spanning seven segments.

One list pulls capital in, the other pushes capacity out — Chongqing is playing both the "attract investment" and "go global" cards at once. 5.87 billion yuan is no small sum, but the more telling detail is that the list turns "cooperation" from slogan into a priced, segmented, accountable project package. Capital hates floating narratives; the value of a list is precisely that it lays a region's industrial ambition open for ASEAN partners to read.

One might ask — aviation is capital-intensive with long payback cycles. What does Chongqing actually have?

## "Project language": translating corridor advantages into investor-readable terms

The list keeps stressing "commercial viability" and "project language." In plain terms, it stops saying "we have location advantage" and instead tells Singaporean capital: here are the supply-chain nodes, the MRO capacity and the low-altitude scenarios you want — already classified, costed and land-ready.

This is exactly the capability western provinces lack most when engaging ASEAN. In the past, inland cities courted openness with vague matches of "we have corridors, you have markets." Chongqing's two lists turn "corridor" into investable, matchable lists. Per Xinhua-affiliated reporting, Chongqing and Singapore will next run targeted investment promotion, supply-demand matching and an open "call-for-talent" mechanism, where technical bottlenecks are posted publicly for any capable player to claim.

Turning recruitment into "posting a challenge" beats a plain roadshow by a long margin.

## The Chongqing–Singapore project: from finance and logistics to the sky

To read the list, first read its framework — the China–Singapore (Chongqing) Connectivity Initiative, the third intergovernmental project between the two countries. For years its four corridors were finance, aviation, logistics and information communications. Aviation was always part of the brief, but mostly meant more flights and freight links; packaging the entire "aviation industry" into an opportunity list signals a leap from "opening routes" to "building industries."

Singapore has no giant airframe industry, yet it holds capital, a voice in airworthiness standards and a global aviation service network. Chongqing has manufacturing depth, market hinterland and the multimodal reach of the New Western Land–Sea Corridor. The fit is obvious: Singapore brings rules and capital, Chongqing brings scenarios and capacity.

This is not one side subsidising the other; it is complementarity each side wants.

## Why aviation? Chongqing's ambition meets ASEAN's gap

Why aviation, specifically? The answer sits at both ends. Chongqing is at an industrial-upgrade window, eager to stretch from its staple sectors — autos and electronics — into higher-value aircraft manufacturing and low-altitude economy. ASEAN, meanwhile, runs one of the world's fastest-growing civil-aviation markets; Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines are expanding fleets and hungry for MRO, yet mostly lack a native aviation supply chain.

One side has capacity it wants to export; the other has demand it cannot fill. By bundling "aviation-plus-low-altitude," "aviation-plus-tourism" and "aviation-plus-training," Chongqing is essentially offering ASEAN a nearby, China-standard fix for its aviation gap. Low-altitude economy is the fresh frontier; whoever plants scenarios first grabs the ticket to the next round of regional competition.

Aviation cooperation looks lofty, but at root it is business — Chongqing wants a slice of ASEAN's growth dividend.

## A two-way door: ASEAN capital in, Chongqing firms out

The elegance of 75 opportunities is their two-wayness. The demand list draws ASEAN capital and technology into Chongqing; the supply list helps Chongqing firms sell products and services into Singapore and beyond. "Bring in" and "go out" close the loop in a single document — more sustainable than one-way recruitment.

Of course, a list is only a start. Whether the 5.87 billion yuan lands depends on follow-up matching efficiency, factor costs and the business environment. Chongqing has proven it can "thicken a chain" in laptops and autos; aviation is a new battlefield that tests whether it can convert manufacturing strength into standard-setting strength.

China–ASEAN cooperation has been preached for years; what it has lacked is never vision but concrete, auditable, deliverable handles like this. Chongqing has just handed ASEAN a readable entry point.

## Further reading

**China–Singapore (Chongqing) Connectivity Initiative**: The third intergovernmental cooperation project between China and Singapore, built on four corridors — finance, aviation, logistics and information communications — and serving as an institutional platform linking western China with ASEAN.

**Low-Altitude Economy**: An emerging economic form built on drones, eVTOLs and other low-altitude aircraft, fusing logistics, tourism and inspection scenarios; it is becoming a new growth pole in China–ASEAN industrial cooperation.

**New Western Land–Sea Corridor**: A multimodal international logistics corridor led by Chongqing, connecting western China with ASEAN; it is the city's "open skeleton" for engaging the bloc and the multimodal backbone behind this aviation list.

# 中国黄金周救场：泰国旅游业的第四季度保卫战

泰国旅游与体育部通常不会因为一周的数据登上新闻头条，10月5日是个例外。据其发布的最新统计，9月27日至10月3日，泰国共接待外国游客522532人次，环比增长8.24%；其中中国游客入境133946人次，环比大涨41.98%，是当周增速最快的主要客源市场之一。短短一周，中国游客就占到了泰国入境外国游客总量的约四分之一。

驱动因素并不神秘：中国国庆“黄金周”。泰方此前预计，9月25日至10月7日将有约25万名中国游客赴泰；从首周数据看，这一预估还偏保守。截至10月3日，泰国今年累计接待外国游客2345万余人次，其中中国游客约400万人次，仍然是头号客源市场。

## 数字：一周13万人，涨幅四成

把同期其他市场的数据摆在旁边，格局一目了然：马来西亚游客59871人次，印度46942人次（环比增长16.53%），俄罗斯22326人次（增长14.23%），韩国21219人次。邻国市场提供的是泰国旅游业的“基本盘”，中国黄金周提供的则是“脉冲”。平常月份，中国游客与马来西亚、印度的差距不过数万；一到长假，这个差距就被迅速拉开。换句话说，泰国旅游业的月度曲线，越来越取决于另一个国家的放假安排。

## 为什么又是泰国：免签、短途与成熟度

黄金周的客流为何流向曼谷、普吉和清迈？首先是制度红利。2024年3月起中泰互免签证正式生效，中国游客赴泰从“办签出行”变成“说走就走”，决策成本被压到最低。其次是地理与价格：三四个小时的飞行距离，加上泰铢汇率处于相对友好区间，令泰国在周边目的地的比价中占优。再加上数十年积累的中文服务、中餐供给与直航网络，泰国承接中国客流的“基础设施”在东南亚几乎无可替代。泰国旅游部门也直言，中国国庆假期是第四季度旅游市场的重要客流来源。

## 一半是海水：单一客源的依赖症

但硬币的另一面同样清晰。一个国家的放假安排，能够直接改写另一个国家的旅游月报——这本身就是结构性风险的注脚。2026年的泰国旅游业并不轻松：二季度经济同比仅增1.9%，在东盟主要经济体中垫底；9月下旬洪灾波及曼谷与数十个府；与此同时，政府还在整顿旅游业内资外资代持乱象，并收紧了免签停留时长。在这样的背景下，中国黄金周客流无异于一场“及时雨”——而恰恰是“及时雨”三个字，暴露了这场雨的可贵与不可控。安全事件、汇率波动、舆论风向，任何一环出问题，都会第一时间传导到曼谷机场的入境大厅。

## 旅游即外交：民间纽带的含金量

不过，只算经济账会低估这件事的分量。旅游是中泰关系里最扎实的民间基础设施：每年数百万人次的相互流动，比任何联合声明都更具体地塑造着两国民众的相互认知。互免签证之所以能够落地，本身就是政治互信的产物；而每一位在泰国顺利、安全、被善待的游客，都是两国关系最日常的广告。反过来说，一旦游客的安全感受损，被侵蚀的不只是旅游收入，还有这份民间信任的存量。

## 把流量变留量：泰国的真正考题

对泰国而言，入境人次是最好统计的指标，却不是最重要的指标。周边竞争者都在盯着中国出境游这块蛋糕：马来西亚、日本、越南各有招数，免签与补贴的门槛越抬越高。黄金周把中国游客送到了泰国，能不能让他们明年再来，靠的是安全感、服务品质与体验的一致性，而不是一次性的人潮。泰国旅游与体育部表示，将持续关注天气、航空运输与旅游安全，保障游客体验——这句话听起来平淡，却是全部问题的核心。黄金周证明泰国旅游业离不开中国客源；接下来的考题是，如何让中国游客离不开泰国。

## 延伸阅读

- **中泰互免签证（China–Thailand mutual visa exemption）**：2024年3月起生效，中国游客赴泰从“办签出行”变为“说走就走”，是此轮客流回升的制度前提。
- **旅游外交（Tourism diplomacy）**：人员往来被视为国家间信任的社会基础，游客既是消费者，也是民间相互认知的传播者。
- **客源集中度（Source-market concentration）**：当单一客源国占比过高，其节假日、汇率与安全事件都会直接传导为东道国旅游业的波动。

# China's Golden Week to the Rescue: Thailand's Fourth-Quarter Tourism Battle

Thailand's Ministry of Tourism and Sports rarely makes headlines with a weekly release. On October 5 it did. Between September 27 and October 3, the country received 522,532 foreign visitors, up 8.24% from the previous week; arrivals from China reached 133,946, a surge of 41.98% that made China the fastest-growing major source market of the week. In just seven days, Chinese travellers accounted for roughly a quarter of all foreign arrivals.

The driver is no mystery: China's National Day "Golden Week". Thai authorities had projected around 250,000 Chinese visitors between September 25 and October 7, and the first week's numbers suggest the estimate was conservative. As of October 3, Thailand had recorded more than 23.45 million foreign arrivals in 2026, about 4 million of them Chinese — still the country's top source market.

## The numbers: 134,000 in a week, up 42 percent

Place the other markets alongside and the pattern is unmistakable: Malaysia sent 59,871 visitors that week, India 46,942 (up 16.53%), Russia 22,326 (up 14.23%) and South Korea 21,219. Neighbouring markets supply Thailand's baseline traffic; China's Golden Week supplies the spike. In ordinary months the gap between Chinese arrivals and those from Malaysia or India runs to a few tens of thousands; a single long holiday blows it wide open. Thailand's monthly tourism curve, in other words, increasingly tracks another country's holiday calendar.

## Why Thailand again: visa-free, short-haul and maturity

Why does Golden Week money flow to Bangkok, Phuket and Chiang Mai? Institutional dividends come first. The mutual visa exemption between China and Thailand, in force since March 2024, has turned a Thai holiday from a visa application into a spontaneous decision, cutting the cost of booking to nearly zero. Geography and price follow: a three-to-four-hour flight, a baht exchange rate that still feels friendly, and a widening price advantage over rival destinations. Add decades of accumulated Chinese-language services, Chinese food and direct flights, and Thailand's "infrastructure" for absorbing Chinese tourists is nearly unrivalled in Southeast Asia. Thai tourism officials say it plainly: the Chinese National Day holiday is a key source of fourth-quarter traffic.

## The other side of the coin: dependence on a single market

But the flip side is equally clear. One country's holiday schedule can rewrite another country's monthly tourism report — a footnote to structural risk in itself. Thailand's 2026 has not been easy: the economy grew just 1.9% year-on-year in the second quarter, the weakest among major ASEAN economies; September floods hit Bangkok and dozens of provinces; and the government is simultaneously cracking down on nominee-shareholding in tourism businesses while tightening visa-free stays. Against that backdrop, the Chinese Golden Week flow is a timely rain — and it is precisely the phrase "timely rain" that exposes both how welcome and how uncontrollable it is. A safety incident, an exchange-rate swing, a shift in public sentiment: any of these transmits straight to the arrival halls of Bangkok's airports.

## Tourism as diplomacy: the value of people-to-people bonds

Counting only the money, however, understates the stakes. Tourism is the most substantial piece of civil infrastructure in China–Thailand relations: millions of journeys in both directions each year shape how the two publics see each other far more concretely than any joint statement. That the mutual visa exemption exists at all is itself a product of political trust; and every visitor who travels through Thailand smoothly, safely and well treated is daily advertising for the relationship. Conversely, when tourists' sense of safety is damaged, what erodes is not just tourism revenue but the stock of popular trust.

## From footfall to loyalty: Thailand's real exam

For Thailand, arrivals are the easiest metric to count and not the most important one. Rivals are all eyeing China's outbound travel: Malaysia, Japan and Vietnam each have their playbook, and the bar of visa-free access and subsidies keeps rising. Golden Week delivered Chinese tourists to Thailand; whether they return next year depends on safety, service quality and consistency of experience — not a one-off surge. The Ministry of Tourism and Sports says it will keep monitoring weather, air transport and travel safety to protect the visitor experience. The sentence sounds bland; it is the heart of the matter. Golden Week has proved that Thai tourism cannot do without Chinese visitors. The exam now is how to make Chinese visitors reluctant to do without Thailand.

## Further reading

- **China–Thailand mutual visa exemption**: In force since March 2024, it turned a Thai trip from a visa application into a spontaneous decision — the institutional precondition for this wave of arrivals.
- **Tourism diplomacy**: People-to-people flows are treated as the social foundation of trust between states; tourists are both consumers and carriers of mutual perception.
- **Source-market concentration**: When one source country dominates arrivals, its holidays, exchange rates and safety incidents transmit directly into the host country's tourism cycle.

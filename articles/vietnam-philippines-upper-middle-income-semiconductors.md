# 芯片托起的跃迁：越南、菲律宾跻身中高收入国家，下一关是"高收入陷阱"

世界银行（World Bank）今年7月发布的年度国家收入分类显示，越南（Vietnam）与菲律宾（Philippines）去年人均国民总收入分别升至4970美元与4850美元，超过4636美元的中高收入门槛，首次跻身这一组别。两国经济格局的升级，正由半导体（semiconductor）及其他电子产品出口的增长所驱动。

## AI投资浪潮下的东南亚拐点

随着全球人工智能（AI）投资蓬勃发展，东南亚迎来由半导体与电子产品出口拉动的增长。《日经亚洲》报道，处于这一浪潮最前沿的是越南。韩国LG Innotek、三星电子（Samsung）与美国英特尔（Intel）等巨头，都计划在越南新建或扩建半导体工厂。越南政府宣布，目标在2030年前培养5万名半导体专业人才，并在2027年前具备半导体设计、制造与测试能力。

## 菲律宾：出口的半壁江山

在菲律宾，半导体及其他电子产品占商品出口总值的一半以上。马尼拉政府正推动以"后端工艺"为主导的产业全面改革，并于今年加入美国推出的供应链安全倡议"硅和平"（Pax Silica）。对马尼拉而言，这是在中美科技博弈中锚定自身位置的一次布局。

## 中等收入陷阱的幽灵

《日经亚洲》同时指出，若要跻身高收入国家，东盟（ASEAN）成员国须摆脱对廉价劳动力的依赖，培育本土企业向高附加值产业转型。泰国（Thailand）与印度尼西亚（Indonesia）虽早于越南、菲律宾跨入中高收入行列，却因工业化滞后而增长停滞，双双跌入"中等收入陷阱（middle-income trap）"。泰国因尖端产业滞后、老龄化快于邻国，已成地区增速最慢者；印尼（Indonesia）经济亦呈放缓。马来西亚（Malaysia）自1996年跻身中高收入后长期乏力，直到借半导体先进封装与设计研发重回增长轨道，正朝2030年前成为高收入国家迈进。

## 窗口期与悬念

泰国《民族报》估算，若越南延续过去十年的增速，其国民总收入将在2037年超过泰国，并于2040年前后跻身高收入之列。但能否真正跨越陷阱，取决于产业链能否向"微笑曲线"上游爬升——从组装走向设计与品牌。芯片带来的东风固然珍贵，却不是终点。

*话题参考：联合早报《受半导体业发展推动 越南菲律宾成中高收入国家》（2026年8月18日）；并参见《日经亚洲》、泰国《民族报》相关报道。*

## 延伸阅读

- **中高收入国家（Upper-Middle-Income Country）**：世界银行按人均国民总收入划分的国家收入组别，2026年门槛为4636美元。
- **硅和平（Pax Silica）**：美国推动的半导体供应链安全倡议，菲律宾已加入，意在分散高端制造的地缘风险。
- **中等收入陷阱（Middle-Income Trap）**：中等收入经济体因未能升级产业、陷入增长停滞的现象，泰国与印尼被视为典型。

# Lifted by Chips: Vietnam and the Philippines Join the Upper-Middle-Income Club, but the High-Income Trap Looms

The World Bank's annual country-income classification, released in July, shows that Vietnam and the Philippines last year saw their gross national income per capita rise to US$4,970 and US$4,850 respectively — above the US$4,636 upper-middle-income threshold — entering that group for the first time. The upgrade in their economic profile is being driven by growth in exports of semiconductors and other electronics.

## A Southeast Asian Inflection Point Riding the AI Wave

As global artificial-intelligence investment booms, Southeast Asia is enjoying growth propelled by semiconductor and electronics exports. Nikkei Asia reports that Vietnam sits at the very front of this wave. Giants including South Korea's LG Innotek, Samsung Electronics and the US firm Intel all plan new or expanded chip plants in the country. Hanoi says it aims to train 50,000 semiconductor professionals by 2030 and to possess design, manufacturing and testing capabilities by 2027.

## The Philippines: Half of All Exports

In the Philippines, semiconductors and other electronics account for more than half the total value of merchandise exports. Manila is pushing a comprehensive reform of its predominantly "back-end process" industry and this year joined the US-led supply-chain-security initiative "Pax Silica." For Manila, it is a move to anchor its position amid the US–China tech rivalry.

## The Spectre of the Middle-Income Trap

Nikkei Asia also notes that to reach high-income status, ASEAN members must shed reliance on cheap labour and nurture home-grown firms moving up the value chain. Thailand and Indonesia, though they entered the upper-middle-income group earlier than Vietnam and the Philippines, have stalled because of lagging industrialisation and fallen into the "middle-income trap." Thailand, held back by weak frontier industries and ageing faster than its neighbours, is now the region's slowest grower; Indonesia's economy is also slowing. Malaysia, mired after joining the group in 1996, only returned to growth by leveraging advanced chip packaging and domestic design-and-R&D — and now targets high-income status before 2030.

## Window and Question Mark

Thailand's The Nation estimates that if Vietnam sustains its pace of the past decade, its GNI will overtake Thailand's in 2037 and reach high-income status around 2040. Whether it can truly clear the trap, however, depends on whether its value chain can climb the "smile curve" — from assembly toward design and brands. The tailwind from chips is precious, but it is not the finish line.

## Further reading

- **Upper-Middle-Income Country**: A World Bank income group classified by GNI per capita; the 2026 threshold is US$4,636.
- **Pax Silica**: A US-led semiconductor supply-chain security initiative that the Philippines has joined, aimed at diversifying geopolitical risk in advanced manufacturing.
- **Middle-Income Trap**: The stagnation that befalls middle-income economies failing to upgrade their industries; Thailand and Indonesia are often cited as cases.

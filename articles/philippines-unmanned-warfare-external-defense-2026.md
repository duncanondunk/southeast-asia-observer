# 菲拟扩编无人作战体系：从水炮对峙到"算法边海防"

## 一句话：对峙常态化，无人机上桌

7月23日，中国与菲律宾公务船在黄岩岛附近海域再度对峙，中方海警船用水炮拦阻驱离。这样的画面正从偶发变成常态。8月25日，国防部长特奥多罗在众议院预算听证会上顺势把"无人化"摆上桌面：菲律宾武装部队已有一批无人机用于情报、监视与侦察，随着能力成熟，将进一步扩大采购。一句话，马尼拉要把海上对峙的筹码，从有人舰艇延伸到无人系统。

## 数字：500亿比索里的无人化权重

钱袋子说出了重心。特奥多罗争取把2027年国防预算较今年获批的3055亿比索（约63亿新元，约合382亿元人民币）提高6.3%；其中拟议明年拨出500亿比索（约62亿元人民币）用于军队现代化计划，较今年增加25%，而扩充无人机是重点之一。在南海摩擦频仍的语境下，这笔增量清楚地指向"看得更远、打得更准、代价更小"的海上感知与拒止能力。

## 转向：安全重心从内安到外防

这组动作背后，是菲律宾近年安全重心的根本位移：从应对国内威胁，转向加强外部防御。面对与北京在争议海域日益频繁的对峙，马尼拉不仅扩无人机，还计划采购更多导弹护卫舰等主战装备。特奥多罗强调，比起单纯增加无人机数量，更关键的是建立能有效运用无人机的通信、网络连接与作战能力——把无人机整合进军事行动，比多配操作员更难，而安全通信与网络能力同样不可或缺。他拒绝透露具体采购对象，仅称信息高度敏感，尤其担心人工智能技术导致资料外泄。

## 隐忧：买得来系统，接得进网络吗

外部的助力已经在路上。乌克兰驻菲大使费迪夫6月透露，基辅正与马尼拉讨论无人机技术合作；美国同月向菲方提供四艘太阳能无人艇，用于海上监视。但真正考验不在"买不买得到"，而在"接不接得进"。无人平台的战场价值，取决于通信链路、数据融合与指挥体系的成熟度。若网络与频谱受制于人，再多的无人机也只是零散的玩具。马尼拉这场"算法边海防"的实验，才刚翻开第一页。

## 延伸阅读

**外部防御（External defense）**：菲律宾安全战略术语，指将军队重心由国内治安与反叛乱，转向应对领土与海域外的传统军事威胁，南海对峙是其最直接驱动。

**无人水面艇（Uncrewed surface vessel, USV）**：以太阳能或电池驱动的无人艇，可执行海上监视、巡逻与侦察，是美国等向菲方输送的低烈度、高频率援助载体。

**军队现代化计划（Military modernization program）**：菲律宾多年期国防采购框架，2027年拟议经费500亿比索，无人机与导弹护卫舰为重点方向。

# Manila Moves to Scale Up Unmanned Warfare: From Water-Cannon Standoffs to "Algorithmic Coast Guard"

## The headline: standoffs normalize, drones take the table

On 23 July, Chinese and Philippine ships faced off again near Scarborough Shoal, with a Chinese coast-guard vessel firing water cannon to block and drive off the Filipino boat. Such scenes are drifting from occasional to routine. On 25 August, Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jr. used a House budget hearing to put "unmanned" on the agenda: the Armed Forces already field a batch of drones for intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance, and as capabilities mature, procurement will expand. In a sentence, Manila wants to extend the chips of maritime standoff from manned vessels to unmanned systems.

## The numbers: the unmanned weight in 50 billion pesos

The purse strings reveal the priority. Teodoro seeks a 6.3% rise in the 2027 defence budget from this year's approved 305.5 billion pesos (about S$6.3 billion, ~US$5.3 billion); of that, a proposed 50 billion pesos (about US$860 million) would go to the modernization program, up 25% from this year, with drone expansion a top item. Amid frequent South China Sea friction, the increment clearly points to maritime sensing and denial that sees farther, strikes more precisely and costs less.

## The shift: from internal security to external defense

Behind the move is a structural pivot in Philippine security: from domestic threats toward external defense. Facing ever-more-frequent standoffs with Beijing in disputed waters, Manila is not only scaling drones but also planning more missile frigates and major kit. Teodoro stressed that beyond merely adding drones, what matters more is building the communications, network connectivity and operational capacity to use them well — integrating drones into operations is harder than adding operators, and secure comms and cyber capability are equally vital. He declined to name systems under consideration, citing sensitivity and a worry that AI tech could leak data.

## The catch: can you buy the system, link the network

Outside help is already arriving. Ukraine's ambassador to Manila, Yuliia Fediv, said in June that Kyiv is discussing drone-tech cooperation with Manila; the United States that same month delivered four solar-powered uncrewed boats for maritime surveillance. But the real test is not "can you buy it" but "can you link it in." The battlefield value of unmanned platforms hinges on comms links, data fusion and command maturity. If networks and spectrum are captive, more drones are just scattered toys. Manila's experiment in "algorithmic coast guard" has only turned its first page.

## Further reading

**External defense**: A Philippine security-strategy term for shifting the military's centre of gravity from internal policing and counter-insurgency toward conventional threats beyond territory and waters — South China Sea standoffs are its sharpest driver.

**Uncrewed surface vessel (USV)**: Solar- or battery-driven drone boats that perform maritime surveillance, patrol and reconnaissance; a low-intensity, high-frequency aid Washington is feeding to Manila.

**Military modernization program**: The Philippines' multi-year defence procurement framework; the proposed 2027 fund of 50 billion pesos prioritises drones and missile frigates.

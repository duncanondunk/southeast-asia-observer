# 六千万人的侨务读本：理解中国海外侨民

> 从"侨牌"到侨务政策，一组数字背后的政策逻辑与张力。

"六千多万海外侨胞，再加四千万归侨侨眷"——这组数字像一句副歌般反复出现。把它们加总，人口规模超过一亿，比世界上绝大多数国家的人口都多，区别只在于这一亿多人散布在地球上几乎每一个国家。

## 一个数字的政治分量

"打好侨牌"（*dahao qiaopai*）是紧随这组数字出现得最频繁的动词。它指向一种政策自觉：海外侨民既是连通中国与世界的桥梁，也是一项必须被组织、被服务的政策客体。

把侨民当作一张"牌"来打，本身已暗示了一种工具性的视角——这既是侨务政策的普遍逻辑，也带着中国语境特有的那重张力。

## 谁算"华侨华人"

一条常被混淆的概念链条：*huaqiao*（华侨，仍持中国国籍、居海外的中国公民）、*huaren*（华人，已入外籍的华裔）、*guiqiao*（归侨，回国定居的华侨）、*qiaojuan*（侨眷，其在国内的亲属）。四种身份对应不同的权利与义务，也对应不同的政策抓手。

这一区分并非无关紧要。在法律上，华侨仍是中国公民，华人则不是。混淆二者，不只是一个术语上的失手，而恰恰是外国政府、有时连离散群体自身都会由此生出焦虑的那条精确断层线。

## 张力所在

侨务的核心张力，落在"连接"与"忠诚"之间那条微妙的边界上。侨民被期待发挥融通中外的优势，去桥接中国与世界——但在所在国，同一份期待却可能被解读为政治效忠的越界。当华侨华人在某些语境下被塑造成"威胁"，这一指控真正暴露的，正是这条边界的模糊与敏感。

这并非中国独有的难题。以色列、印度、亚美尼亚、爱尔兰——凡拥有可观离散人口的国家，都在和同一条线谈判：如何与那些在法律上、往往也在情感上已不再属于自己的人，保持联结。中国情形的特殊之处，在于规模，在于一个崛起大国每一次朝向其侨民的姿态，都会被放大。

## 在桥梁与负担之间

侨民对中国的价值是具体的：侨汇、投资、知识转移、软实力的传递。但这段关系双向都成立。对泗水的小店主、硅谷的研究员而言，"身为海外华人"可以是一项资产、一种身份，或——在某些政治气候下——一种负担。把侨民仅视作工具的政策，有透支它所希望动员的那份情谊的风险。

因此，最明智的侨务政策，或许不在于"打牌"，而在于降低身居海外做华人的成本：领事保护、文化可及性、一份与母国未来的联结——同时不去索取一种所在国无法容忍、侨民自身也负担不起的忠诚。

## 结语

理解这六千万人，不是去把握一个统计口径，而是去理解一项庞大的跨国人口治理工程，连同它所有的成就与困局——而这些，说到底，是离散群体在当代世界的普遍困局。

数字很大。鉴于环绕它的那些政治，容许出错的空间，却并不大。

# China's Sixty Million: A Reader on the Overseas Chinese

> From "qiao card" to diaspora policy — the logic and tension behind a set of numbers.

"The figure travels like a refrain: sixty million overseas Chinese, plus another forty million returned overseas Chinese and their relatives at home. Together they amount to a population of more than 100 million, larger than that of almost any other country — except this one is scattered across nearly every country on earth."

## The Political Weight of a Number

"Playing the qiao card well" (*dahao qiaopai*) is the verb that most often trails this set of figures. It points to a policy self-awareness: the overseas Chinese are simultaneously a bridge connecting China to the world, and a policy subject that must be organized and served.

> Treating a diaspora as a "card" to be played already implies an instrumental lens — at once the universal logic of diaspora policy and a tension particular to the Chinese context.

## Who Counts as "Overseas Chinese"

A chain of concepts that is often conflated: *huaqiao* (Chinese nationals residing abroad), *huaren* (foreign nationals of Chinese descent), *guiqiao* (returned overseas Chinese who have resettled in China), and *qiaojuan* (their relatives at home). Four identities map to different rights and obligations — and to different policy levers.

The distinction matters. A *huaqiao* is, in law, still a Chinese citizen; a *huaren* is not. Confusing the two is not merely a terminological slip — it is the precise fault line along which foreign governments, and sometimes the diaspora itself, grow anxious.

## Where the Tension Lies

The core tension of overseas Chinese affairs sits at the delicate boundary between "connection" and "loyalty." The diaspora is expected to leverage its bicultural advantage to bridge China and the world — but in host countries, that same expectation can be read as an overreach of political allegiance. When overseas Chinese are cast as a "threat" in certain settings, what the accusation really exposes is the blur and sensitivity of exactly this boundary.

This is not a Chinese problem alone. Israel, India, Armenia, Ireland — every state with a substantial diaspora negotiates the same line: how to sustain ties with people who are, in law and often in sentiment, no longer yours. What distinguishes the Chinese case is scale, and the asymmetry of a rising power whose every gesture toward its diaspora is magnified.

## Between Bridge and Burden

The diaspora's value to China is concrete: remittances, investment, knowledge transfer, soft-power relay. But the relationship runs both ways. For a shopkeeper in Surabaya or a researcher in Silicon Valley, being "overseas Chinese" can be an asset, an identity, or — in certain political weather — a liability. Policy that treats the diaspora only as an instrument risks straining the very affection it hopes to mobilize.

The smartest diaspora policy, then, is less about "playing a card" than about lowering the cost of being Chinese abroad: consular protection, cultural access, a stake in the home country's future — without demanding a loyalty that host countries will not tolerate and the diaspora itself cannot afford.

## Conclusion

Understanding the sixty million is not a matter of grasping a statistical category. It is to understand a vast project of transnational population governance, with all its achievements and its predicaments — which are, in the end, the universal predicaments of diaspora in the contemporary world.

The number is large. The margin for error, given the politics that surround it, is not.

**Overseas Chinese** — A broad term for ethnic Chinese residing abroad.

9月12日晚，海口的一场音乐会上，台上歌手用一句“老乡”开场。舞台灯光并不华丽，观众也不算多，但这句话落下时，现场分明暖了一层。说话的是马来西亚华裔歌手朱主爱（Joyce Chu），她的祖辈来自海南；对她而言，这不是一次普通的巡演停靠，而是回乡。

## 一句“老乡”的重量

真正让这场演出区别于其他站点的，不是曲目单，而是台上下之间的关系。朱主爱在曲间与乐迷唠起家常，报出一长串家乡风味：椰子鸡、椰奶鸡、糟粕醋、海南粉、屯昌黑猪。食物是最不需要翻译的语言，也是离散社群记忆里最顽固的部分——味觉往往比族谱更早抵达“我是谁”的答案。海口站原本并不在今年的巡演计划里，是经纪人年初结识主办方后才促成的临时调整；而这场迟到又临时的安排，恰恰说明情感有时候比商业排期更有优先权。

## 从马六甲到椰城

值得注意的是她对自己的身份表述。朱主爱说，父亲曾告诉她，曾祖父一辈当年从海南渡海到马六甲，“我们是马来西亚的峇峇娘惹（Baba Nyonya）族群”。这句话看似家常，实则牵出一条跨越数百年的移民链：下南洋的男性劳工、与本地通婚形成的混血社群、在海峡殖民地形成的独特语言与饮食体系。峇峇娘惹的身份认同，长期处在“既在此地又自外处”的状态——他们落地数代，却始终保留着对祖籍地的想象。今天的新生代用音乐、影像与社交媒体重新讲述这条脉络，讲述方式变了，问题的内核没有变。

## “三分之一”的刻度

这场巡回演唱会的主题叫“三分之一”。朱主爱给出的解释是：若一个人足够幸运活到一百岁，现在的自己快要走完人生的三分之一。她把这一阶段的困惑写进今年推出的创作EP《废物手册》，主题是内心状态的探索。这种表述放在海外华人青年身上颇有意味：如果说上一代的关键词是安身立命，这一代的关键词则是自我辨认——从“问别人为什么”转向“问自己为什么”，也因此更在意个人需求与社会期待如何共处。身份问题从户口本上的祖籍栏，迁移到了个体的心理层面。

## 寻根不是怀旧

海口之行的日常细节，比舞台更说明问题。她提前一周多抵达，把父母与哥哥一同请来海南，一家人在“老爸茶·海口巴士”上沿街慢行，去海大南门夜市一站式打卡；她笑称这些天不知“毁灭”了多少颗椰子、吃了多少只文昌鸡，还说马来西亚的天气和海南很像。这类“像”，正是寻根最真实的形态：它不是祠堂里的肃穆仪式，而是气候的相似、口味的重逢、亲属同行时的松弛。把寻根从朝圣降维成生活，反而让它更容易被年轻一代接住。

## 侨联角色的位移

此行另一个被低估的细节，是她在9月7日专程拜访海南省侨联座谈。侨务系统过去的功能偏重接待、联谊与协助；而这场座谈的内容是“之后还有很多机会来到海南”，指向演出市场与文旅产业的合作可能。换句话说，侨务工作的接口正在从“招待归侨”转向“对接资源”：把海外华人的专业能力、影响力与文化资本，嵌入地方发展的具体赛道。对海南这样同时拥有侨乡资源与自贸港政策的地方来说，这条路径的想象空间不小；难点在于如何用常态化机制替代人情式联络，避免侨务资源只在高光时刻被想起。

## 文旅与演出市场的接口

朱主爱关注到的是真实的机会窗口。当前海南正在推动演出市场与文旅产业发展，大型演出对消费的带动效应已被反复验证；而一位在东南亚拥有听众基础的华裔艺人返乡演出，成本低、传播准、情感黏性强，是文旅营销里性价比颇高的抓手。问题的另一面同样清楚：单点演出的热度来得快去得快，若缺少后续的创作驻留、青年互访与合制计划，很容易沦为一次性消费。把“演唱”变成“常驻”，需要的是项目化的耐心，而不是一次合影。

## 青年桥梁的可能

她的职业身份也提供了稀缺的公共价值。朱主爱此前参演《天涯小娘惹》系列剧，与下南洋的历史叙事渊源颇深；她观察到，当下越来越多年轻人开始主动关注侨乡文化与峇峇娘惹文化。她表示希望以海南为纽带，推动更多音乐演出与文化交流，搭建海外琼籍青年交流的桥梁。这一表述值得认真对待：当认同议题被娱乐工业承接，它的传播半径会远超学术与官方话语。风险则在于，流行文化天然偏好符号化，娘惹服饰、老宅与美食一旦被过度景观化，历史的沉重感就会被磨平。文化统战也好，民间交流也好，最终考验的是能否把热度转化为理解。

## 认同的代际迁移

把个例放回区域背景，这条线索更耐人寻味。东南亚华人社会的认同结构正在经历第三次重排：第一代是落叶归根的侨民意识，第二代是落地生根的国民认同，当下的新生代则在此基础上叠加了一层可被自由调用的“文化资源库”——祖籍地不再是唯一的归宿，而是可以往返、采样与再创作的坐标。朱主爱把东南亚音乐元素写进作品，又来海南采风寻找灵感，正是这种双向采样的样本。它提示公共政策的着力点应随之调整：与其单向号召“回报桑梓”，不如提供可持续的双向通道；与其强调血脉的责任，不如创造合作的价值。情感可以被唤醒，但要让它留下，靠的是利益与机制的持久咬合。

## 延伸阅读

**峇峇娘惹（Baba Nyonya）**：明清以来移居马来半岛的华人男性与本地女性通婚形成的混血社群，在马六甲、槟城与新加坡最为集中；保留大量华人习俗的同时吸收马来语言、饮食与服饰，形成独特的海峡华人文化。

**侨务**：中国面向海外侨胞的事务性工作统称，涵盖联谊、服务、引资引智与文化传播；其重心近年从单向接待转向以侨为桥的资源对接与合作平台搭建。

**寻根**：海外华人回到祖籍地探访家族源流的活动，近年参与者从年长一代向青年群体扩散，形式由祭祖谒祖扩展至音乐、影视、美食与文旅等日常场景。

At a concert in Haikou on the evening of 12 September, the singer on stage opened with a single word addressed to the crowd: fellow townspeople. The lighting was modest and the audience not especially large, yet something in the room warmed. The singer was Malaysian Chinese artist Joyce Chu, whose ancestors came from Hainan. For her, this was not another tour stop — it was a homecoming.

## The weight of one word

What set this night apart was not the setlist but the relationship between stage and floor. Between songs Chu chatted with her fans and reeled off a list of hometown flavours: coconut chicken, coconut-milk chicken, vinegar hotpot, Hainan noodles, and black pork from Tunchang. Food needs no translation, and it is the most stubborn part of a diaspora's memory — taste usually answers "who am I" long before a family tree does. The Haikou leg was not even on this year's itinerary; her manager met the local promoter early this year and the show was slotted in. That a stop both late and improvised happened anyway says something simple: feeling sometimes outranks scheduling.

## From Malacca to Haikou

Her own account of her identity deserves attention. Chu says her father told her that her great-grandfather's generation crossed from Hainan to Malacca, and that "we are Baba Nyonya." The sentence sounds domestic, but it pulls a centuries-long migration chain into view: the labourers who went south, the mixed-heritage communities formed through local marriage, the distinctive language and cuisine that took shape in the Straits settlements. Baba Nyonya identity has long occupied a position of "here, yet also from elsewhere" — rooted for generations and still holding an inherited image of the ancestral home. Today's generation retells that lineage through music, video and social media. The medium changes; the question does not.

## "One Third": a milestone of self-inquiry

The tour's title, "One Third", carries her own reading of it: if you are lucky enough to reach a hundred, she is now approaching the end of the first third of that span. She folded this stage's confusions into this year's EP *Waste Manual*, an exploration of the self. The framing resonates with overseas Chinese youth. If the last generation's keyword was settling down, this one's is self-definition — a turn from "asking others why" to "asking yourself why," and therefore a sharper interest in reconciling personal needs with social expectation. Identity has migrated from the ancestral-native-place column of a household register into individual psychology.

## Root-seeking is not nostalgia

The mundane details of the trip say more than the stage. Chu arrived more than a week early and brought her parents and brother along. The family rode the "Old Man's Tea · Haikou Bus" through the streets and worked their way through the night market at Hainan University's south gate. She joked that she had lost count of how many coconuts she had destroyed and how many Wenchang chickens she had eaten, adding that Malaysia's weather is much like Hainan's. That resemblance is what root-seeking actually looks like at ground level: not solemn ritual in an ancestral hall, but similar weather, rediscovered flavours, and the ease of having family along. Downgrading root-seeking from pilgrimage to everyday life is precisely what makes it bearable for younger people.

## The qiaolian's changing role

One underplayed detail: on 7 September she paid a dedicated visit to the Hainan Provincial Returned Overseas Chinese Federation. The qiaowu apparatus once focused on reception, fellowship and assistance. This meeting, however, turned on "many more opportunities to come to Hainan" — the performing arts market and cultural tourism. In other words, the interface of diaspora work is shifting from hosting returning sojourners to matching resources: inserting overseas Chinese talent, reach and cultural capital into concrete local development tracks. For a place that holds both diaspora ties and free-trade-port policies, the room to manoeuvre is real. The hard part is replacing personal-connection diplomacy with standing mechanisms, so that diaspora resources are not remembered only for set-piece moments.

## An interface with the performance economy

The opportunity she noticed is genuine. Hainan is pushing hard on live entertainment and cultural tourism, and the spending pull of large events has been demonstrated repeatedly. An ethnic Chinese artist with a Southeast Asian audience base performing in her ancestral province is low-cost, precisely targeted and emotionally sticky — high value for money in tourism marketing. The other side of the ledger is equally clear: a single show's heat arrives fast and leaves fast. Without follow-on residencies, youth exchanges and co-production plans, it collapses into one-off consumption. Turning a concert into a continuing presence takes programme-level patience, not a photo opportunity.

## A bridge for diaspora youth

Her profession carries scarce public value. Chu previously appeared in the *Tianya Little Nyonya* series, tying her to the history of going south; she observes that more young people are now actively engaging with qiaoxiang and Baba Nyonya culture. She says she wants to build bridges for overseas Hainanese youth through music and cultural exchange. That deserves to be taken seriously: once identity is carried by the entertainment industry, its reach far exceeds anything academic or official language achieves. The risk is that popular culture instinctively symbolises. When Nyonya dress, old houses and food become spectacle, the weight of history is flattened. Heritage diplomacy and people-to-people exchange alike are ultimately tested on whether heat converts into understanding.

## Identity across generations

Placed back in its regional context, the case looks more interesting still. Southeast Asian Chinese identity is undergoing a third rearrangement: the first generation's sojourner consciousness of returning to the roots, the second's national belonging where they settled, and now a generation that adds a freely drawable "cultural resource library" on top. The ancestral homeland is no longer the sole destination; it is a coordinate to visit, sample and remake. Writing Southeast Asian musical elements into her songs and then coming to Hainan for inspiration is exactly that two-way sampling. The implication for policy is direct: instead of one-way appeals to give back to the native place, build durable two-way channels; instead of invoking the obligations of blood, create the value of cooperation. Sentiment can be awakened. Making it stay requires interests and mechanisms that lock together.

## Further reading

**Baba Nyonya**: Mixed-heritage communities formed from the early modern onward as Chinese men who settled in the Malay Peninsula married local women, concentrated in Malacca, Penang and Singapore. They retained many Chinese customs while absorbing Malay language, cuisine and dress, producing a distinct Straits Chinese culture.

**Qiaowu**: The umbrella term for China's work concerning overseas Chinese nationals and ethnic Chinese abroad, covering fellowship, services, investment and talent attraction, and cultural outreach. Its centre of gravity has shifted from one-directional reception toward using diaspora ties as bridges for resource matching and joint platforms.

**Root-seeking (xungen)**: Visits by overseas Chinese to their ancestral hometowns to trace family origins. Participation has been spreading from older cohorts to younger ones, and the format has expanded from ancestral worship to everyday scenes such as music, film, food and travel.

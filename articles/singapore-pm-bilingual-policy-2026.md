# 新加坡总理黄循财：双语政策不容动摇，华语是华人共同语言

## 双语政策不容动摇

据联合早报报道，8月23日，新加坡总理黄循财以华语发表公开演说，强调新加坡绝不应沦为单语社会，必须坚守双语政策，并巩固华语作为新加坡华族共同语言的地位。他表示，经过数十年努力，华语已成为新加坡华人的共同语言与双语政策的根基，也让华人得以与全球华语社群沟通。"双语政策和推广华语的方向是正确的，我们必须坚持。"

## 精英之外更要精华

黄循财回顾两年前在一场群众大会上关于母语教育的讲话。他当时提出，新加坡不但要有"精英"，也要有"精华"，呼吁社会各界重视母语传承。这一表述被视为对实用主义教育取向的温和提醒：语言不只是职场工具，更是文化根基与身份认同。精英可由考试造就，文化上的"精华"却要靠长期浸润方能养成。

## 高华放宽惠及千人

据中国侨网报道，黄循财宣布，今年起放宽中学生修读高级华文（高华）的条件，让更多学生有机会接触更高阶的华文学习。政策落地后，逾千名学生因此受惠。今年升读中一、修读华文的学生中，约四分之一选修高华。在家长推动下，部分传统英校也首次开办高华班，显示家庭对母语的重视正悄然改变精英学校的课程供给。

## 听周杰伦看逐玉学华语

谈及如何学好华语，黄循财援引《海峡时报》一位记者的亲身经验：她只让孩子看华语节目、听华语歌曲——从周杰伦的歌到热播剧《逐玉》，三个月后自身与孩子的华语明显进步，孩子也更有兴趣。总理笑言："看戏听歌还真的对学习华语有帮助。"这一轶事颇为巧妙：它把学习者带向注意力本就在的地方，而非枯燥的死记硬背。

## 家庭与社会环境并重

黄循财指出，家庭环境固然重要，社会环境同样关键。据透露，到2026年底，新加坡国家图书馆将与中国国家图书馆联合举办"四大名著"专题展览，涵盖《三国演义》《红楼梦》《水浒传》《西游记》，为公众提供浸润式的中文文化场景。这类合作显示，国家机构能够营造家庭无法单独提供的语言氛围。

## 方言是值得珍惜的文化

黄循财提到电影《给阿嬷的情书》引发热烈讨论，他本人观看了潮州话版本，并引用片中"做人要有情有义"一句。他认为，方言让人倍感亲切，是珍贵的文化遗产，值得珍惜与传承。这番表态耐人寻味：当年的讲华语运动曾劝导华人以华语取代方言，如今总理却把方言视为同一文化谱系中的珍贵层次，而非对立物。

## 延伸阅读

**高华（Higher Chinese）**：新加坡中学为高华文能力学生开设的进阶华文课程，程度高于普通华文。

**四大名著（Four Great Classical Novels）**：中国文学史上四部经典章回小说，即三国、红楼、水浒、西游。

**讲华语运动（Speak Mandarin Campaign）**：新加坡自1979年起推动华人以华语取代方言的常年运动，成果得来不易。

# Singapore PM Lawrence Wong: Bilingualism Is Non-Negotiable, Mandarin the Common Tongue

## Bilingualism Must Not Be Undermined

On 23 August, Prime Minister Lawrence Wong delivered a Mandarin-language address in which he warned that Singapore must never drift into a monolingual society and must hold fast to its bilingual policy. He reaffirmed Mandarin as the common language of the ethnic Chinese community and the bedrock of bilingual education. In his words, the direction of bilingualism and Mandarin promotion "is correct, and we must persevere." The speech matters because it pushes back against a quiet drift: as English cements its place as the language of work and the internet, mother tongues risk becoming ornamental. Wong's framing treats bilingualism not as nostalgia but as strategic necessity, a bond that also lets ethnic Chinese communicate across the global Chinese-speaking world.

## "Elite" and "Essence" in Mother-Tongue Education

Wong looked back to a rally two years ago where he spoke on mother-tongue education. His message then—that Singapore needs not only "elite" talent but also cultural "essence"—was a gentle rejoinder to an overly utilitarian view of schooling. Language, he implied, is more than a workplace instrument; it is the root of identity and a bridge to a civilisation of 1.4 billion people. The distinction between elite and essence reframes success: examinations produce the former, but only sustained cultural literacy produces the latter, and the two are not the same achievement.

## Eased Higher Chinese Rules Benefit Thousands

From this year, conditions for secondary students to take Higher Chinese have been relaxed, opening the door to more advanced study. Over 1,000 students have already benefited. Among Secondary 1 pupils taking Chinese this year, roughly one in four opts for Higher Chinese. Under parental pressure, several traditionally English-medium schools have also launched Higher Chinese classes for the first time. The shift signals demand from families who refuse to treat Chinese as optional, and it quietly reshapes the supply side of elite schooling from below.

## Learning Mandarin Through Jay Chou and "Zhu Yu"

On how to learn the language well, Wong cited a Straits Times journalist who improved her own and her children's Mandarin simply by watching Chinese programmes and listening to Chinese songs—Jay Chou's tracks and the hit drama Zhu Yu among them. After three months, her Mandarin improved and her children grew keener. The Prime Minister quipped that watching shows and hearing songs "really does help with learning Chinese." The anecdote is shrewd: it meets young learners where their attention already lives, rather than prescribing rote drill, and it implicitly trusts popular culture as a legitimate teacher.

## Family and Society Both Matter

Wong noted that while the family environment counts, the broader social environment matters too. By the end of 2026, Singapore's National Library will co-host with China's national library an exhibition on the Four Great Classical Novels—Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Dream of the Red Chamber, Water Margin and Journey to the West—offering the public an immersive Chinese cultural setting. The partnership shows how state institutions can manufacture the ambient culture that households alone cannot supply, turning policy into lived experience.

## Dialects as Cherished Heritage

Wong pointed to the film Dear You, which sparked lively debate; he watched the Teochew-language version and quoted its line "one must be a person of feeling and righteousness." Dialects, he argued, feel more intimate and are precious cultural heritage worth cherishing. This is a notable softening: the Speak Mandarin Campaign once discouraged dialects, yet Wong now honours them as layers of the same heritage rather than rivals to it, weaving older tongues back into the national story.

## Further reading

**Higher Chinese (高华)**: An advanced Mandarin track in Singapore secondary schools, pitched above the standard Chinese curriculum.

**Four Great Classical Novels (四大名著)**: The four canonical Chinese chapter novels—Three Kingdoms, Red Chamber, Water Margin and Journey to the West.

**Speak Mandarin Campaign (讲华语运动)**: Singapore's year-round drive since 1979 urging Chinese Singaporeans to use Mandarin over dialects; its gains, Wong said, were hard-won.

# 一万余袋菌种跨山越水：云南榆黄菇"出海"越南的生意与算计

近日，10820袋在云南丽江培育的榆黄菇菌种经海关查验后运往越南。对普通人而言，这不过是一则地方农业口的小新闻；但把它放进中国—东盟农业合作与云南"面向南亚东南亚辐射中心"的定位里看，这桩不起眼的买卖，恰恰折射出边境省份如何把"短平快"的乡村产业，做成了跨境产业链上的一颗螺丝钉。

## 一桩小生意，一条大线索

据中国侨网援引中新网报道，这批榆黄菇（又称金顶侧耳）菌种由丽江本地生产，经昆明海关所属丽江海关属地查检合格后启运越南。至此，丽江食用菌出口的品类，由过去的鲜菇、冷冻菇、干菇，拓展到了"菌种"这一上游环节。

数字本身不大：一万多袋，对应的是一桩试验性出口。但值得追问的是——为什么是"菌种"先走出去，而不是更多成品菇？答案藏在出口模式的设定里。

## "国内发菌、国外出菇"：把工厂搬到国境线那边

这次出口采用的是"国内发菌、国外出菇"模式：菌种在丽江完成培育，运到越南后，由越方合作方完成出菇、就地上市。这个设计很巧妙，也很有算计。

鲜菇娇气，长途跨境运输的保鲜是一道坎；而菌种体积小、便于储运，活性又靠"绿色通道""预约查检""云签发"这类海关便利措施来锁住。换句话说，中方输出的是"技术与种源"，越方承接的是"种植与销售"。这既绕开了鲜菇运输的损耗难题，也给越南本地食用菌的品种改良提供了种源支撑。

我更愿意把这读作一种"轻资产出海"：不建海外大厂、不烧重资产，只把价值最高的"育种"环节留在国内，把劳动密集、损耗高的"出菇"环节放在进口国。对云南这样的边省而言，这是一笔风险可控的买卖。

## 为什么是越南，为什么是榆黄菇

越南是东南亚农业大国，食用菌消费与栽培都有相当基础，但高端与特色品种的种源仍部分依赖外部输入。榆黄菇色泽金黄、口感爽滑、营养丰富，且在云南被验证为"投入少、见效快、不占耕地、可林下种植"的"短平快"产业——换句话说，它特别适合作为跨境技术输出的"样品"。

据丽江海关介绍，今年前7个月，丽江出口农产品1985.5吨，同比增长156.9%，覆盖意大利、比利时、越南等13个国家和地区。榆黄菇菌种只是这条"高原特色农产品组团出海"通道上最新的一环。换言之，今天的"一万余袋"，更像是一次探路，而非孤例。

## 绿色通道背后：边省如何当起"东盟门户"

这桩生意能成，离不开海关的"一企一策"帮扶与鲜活农产品"绿色通道"。事实上，丽江海关正在实施支持农食产品出口的三年专项行动，在企业注册备案、属地查检、通关保障上持续加码。

放到更大的坐标里看，这是云南作为"面向南亚东南亚辐射中心"的具体落地：边省不再只是原料产地或通道，而开始扮演"农业技术输出 + 标准输出 + 通关服务"的复合角色。当菌种、种苗、技术标准随贸易一同跨境，农业合作就从一个个孤立的买卖，慢慢连成了可以复制的产业链。

## 一桩生意的两种读法

有人会把这读作单纯的商业订单，有人会读出"农业软实力"的意味。我的看法是，两者都对，但要分清主次：生意是表，连通是里。

对东南亚观察而言，这类"小切口"故事的价值，正在于它比动辄数十亿美元的大单更真实、更可复制。当中国边省的育种技术、海关服务、质量标准，能以"一万余袋菌种"的方式稳稳落进越南的菇棚，所谓"中国—东盟农业共同体"，才不是会场里的口号，而有了田间地头的注脚。

## 越方这一侧：种源输入的机会与顾虑

不过，种源输出从来不是单向的恩惠。对越南而言，引入榆黄菇菌种可以补上特色品种的缺口，却也意味着在产业链最上游多了一分对外依赖——这正是东盟国家对农业合作最敏感的那根神经。种质资源涉及生物安全与品种主权，检疫审批、知识产权归属、后续改良收益的分配，任何一项谈不拢，都可能让"样品"停在样品阶段。

因此，中方若想把这条通道做成常态，靠的不是再多几船菌种，而是把育种环节的一部分也让渡给越方：共建试验站、联合选育、本地化培训。只有当越方在这条链上既是买方也是共同的改良者，"国内发菌、国外出菇"才不会被人读成一种精巧的依附设计。

当然，也别高估一桩买卖的分量。菌种出海能否真正在越南扎根、能否从"样品"长成"产业"，还要看越方配套、市场接受度与后续服务。但作为一条线索，它足够清晰：农业，正在成为澜湄合作与中国—东盟互联互通里，最不起眼也最扎实的那块拼图。

## 延伸阅读

**榆黄菇（Golden oyster mushroom）**：学名金顶侧耳，一种色泽金黄、营养丰富的食用菌，因投入少、见效快、可林下种植，常被云南等地作为乡村"短平快"产业推广。

**澜湄合作（Lancang–Mekong Cooperation）**：由中国与湄公河五国（柬埔寨、老挝、缅甸、泰国、越南）共同发起的次区域合作机制，农业与水资源是其优先领域之一。

**"国内发菌、国外出菇"（Spawn-at-home, fruit-abroad）**：一种跨境农业分工模式，将育种环节留在输出国、出菇环节放在进口国，以规避鲜品长途运输损耗并带动当地种植。

# From Lijiang to Vietnam: what 10,820 bags of mushroom spawn reveal about China–ASEAN farm connectivity

Recently, 10,820 bags of golden oyster mushroom (Pleurotus citrinopileatus) spawn cultivated in Lijiang, Yunnan, cleared customs inspection and were shipped to Vietnam. To most readers this is a minor item buried in a provincial agriculture roundup. But placed against the backdrop of China–ASEAN agricultural cooperation and Yunnan's ambition to serve as a "radiation centre" for South and Southeast Asia, this unremarkable transaction is a small but revealing clue to how a border province is turning a "short, flat, fast" rural industry into a screw on a cross-border value chain.

## A small deal, a big clue

According to China Qiaowang, citing China News Service, the batch of spawn — produced locally in Lijiang — passed on-site inspection by Lijiang Customs, a subordinate office of Kunming Customs, before departing for Vietnam. With this shipment, Lijiang's edible-mushroom exports have expanded beyond fresh, frozen and dried mushrooms to include "spawn," the upstream input of the whole industry.

The numbers are small: a little over ten thousand bags, an experimental export at best. But the question worth asking is why "spawn" went out first, rather than more finished mushrooms. The answer lies in how the export model was designed.

## Spawn at home, fruit abroad: moving the farm to the border

The shipment used a "domestic spawn, foreign fruiting" model: the spawn is cultivated in Lijiang, shipped to Vietnam, and then Vietnamese partners handle fruiting and local marketing. The design is clever, and calculated.

Fresh mushrooms are delicate; keeping them fresh across a long border haul is a real hurdle. Spawn, by contrast, is compact and easier to store and transport, with its vitality protected by customs conveniences such as a "green channel," appointment-based inspection and cloud-issued certificates. In other words, China exports "technology and germplasm" while Vietnam absorbs "cultivation and sales." This sidesteps the spoilage problem of transporting fresh produce and, at the same time, gives Vietnam's local edible-mushroom sector germplasm support for variety improvement.

I would read this as a form of "asset-light going-out": no overseas mega-factory, no heavy capital burn — only the highest-value "breeding" link is kept at home, while the labour-intensive, high-loss "fruiting" link is placed in the importing country. For a border province like Yunnan, it is a deal with contained risk.

## Why Vietnam, why the golden oyster

Vietnam is an agricultural powerhouse in Southeast Asia with a substantial base in edible-mushroom consumption and cultivation, yet part of its supply of premium and specialty germplasm still relies on external inputs. The golden oyster mushroom — golden in colour, crisp in texture, rich in nutrition — has been validated in Yunnan as a "short, flat, fast" crop: low input, quick returns, no occupation of farmland, and suitable for under-forest growing. In short, it is an ideal "showcase" for cross-border technology transfer.

Lijiang Customs notes that in the first seven months of this year, the city exported 1,985.5 tonnes of agricultural products, up 156.9 percent year on year, reaching 13 countries and regions including Italy, Belgium and Vietnam. The golden oyster spawn is merely the newest link on this "highland speciality exports caravan." Today's "ten-thousand-odd bags," then, look less like a one-off and more like a scouting mission.

## Behind the green channel: how a border province becomes an ASEAN gateway

That the deal worked owes much to the customs "one enterprise, one policy" support and the "green channel" for live agricultural products. In fact, Lijiang Customs is running a three-year special campaign to support farm and food exports, scaling up assistance in enterprise registration, on-site inspection and clearance guarantees.

Seen on a larger map, this is Yunnan's "radiation centre" strategy in concrete form: the border province is no longer merely a source of raw materials or a transit corridor, but is beginning to play a compound role of "agricultural technology export + standards export + clearance services." When spawn, seedlings and technical standards cross borders together with trade, agricultural cooperation stops being a string of isolated transactions and slowly becomes a replicable value chain.

## Two ways to read one deal

Some will read this as a mere commercial order; others will detect a whiff of "agricultural soft power." My view is that both are right, but the order of precedence matters: commerce is the surface, connectivity is the substance.

For observers of Southeast Asia, the value of such "small-cut" stories is precisely that they are more truthful and more replicable than the multi-billion-dollar headline deals. When a border province's breeding know-how, customs service and quality standards can land steadily in a Vietnamese mushroom shed through "ten-thousand-odd bags of spawn," the phrase "China–ASEAN agricultural community" stops being a conference slogan and gains a footnote written in the field.

## The Vietnamese side: opportunity and unease over imported germplasm

Germplasm export, however, is never a one-way favour. For Vietnam, importing golden oyster spawn fills a gap in specialty varieties, but it also means one more upstream dependency — precisely the nerve that makes ASEAN states most sensitive about agricultural cooperation. Germplasm touches biosecurity and varietal sovereignty. Quarantine approval, ownership of intellectual property and the division of gains from later improvement: if any of these is left unresolved, the "sample" tends to stay a sample.

So if China wants to make this corridor routine, the answer is not more shipments of spawn but ceding part of the breeding work to the Vietnamese side: joint trial stations, joint selection, localised training. Only when Vietnam is both buyer and co-improver on this chain will "spawn at home, fruit abroad" cease to read as an elegant design for dependence.

That said, do not overstate a single deal. Whether spawn exports truly take root in Vietnam — whether they grow from a "sample" into an "industry" — depends on Vietnamese complementary capacity, market acceptance and follow-up service. But as a clue, it is clear enough: agriculture is becoming, in Lancang–Mekong cooperation and China–ASEAN connectivity, the most inconspicuous yet most solid piece of the puzzle.

## Further reading

**Golden oyster mushroom (Pleurotus citrinopileatus)**: A golden-coloured, nutritious edible fungus promoted in places like Yunnan as a "short, flat, fast" rural crop because it needs little input, returns quickly and can be grown under forest canopy.

**Lancang–Mekong Cooperation (LMC)**: A sub-regional mechanism launched by China with the five Mekong countries (Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand, Vietnam), with agriculture and water resources among its priorities.

**"Spawn-at-home, fruit-abroad"**: A cross-border farming division of labour that keeps breeding in the exporting country and fruiting in the importing country, avoiding fresh-produce transport losses while boosting local cultivation.

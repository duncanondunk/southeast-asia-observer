# 湄公河畔二十载：清迈大学孔子学院与中文在泰北的扎根

## 从一间教室到一座桥

2006年12月18日，云南师范大学与泰国（Thailand）清迈大学共建的清迈大学孔子学院正式揭牌，成为泰北最早的中文教育基地之一。二十年过去，泰北曾经「散落的珠子」般的中文学习，被串成了一套体系。孔院如今更愿意把自己描述为连接两座社会的「桥」，而不是一间语言学校。这件事值得当作一个案例来观察：软实力究竟怎样在地面上层层落地——不是靠口号，而是靠教师、靠考试、靠一个个能给迷路中国游客指路的警员，慢慢生长出来。

## 从「零散学」到「体系化」：本土师资是地基

建院之初，泰北既没有规范的中文推广模式，也没有系统的本土师资，中小学连一位专职中文教师都难找。孔院的第一项工作，是为中文教育这座大厦打地基。2009年，它走进泰北多所中小学开展剪纸、民族舞蹈等「文化进校园」活动，埋下了「教学＋文化」融合模式的伏笔；2021年又与泰国教育部民校教育委员会签署战略合作协议，把本土教师培训列为核心板块。2023至2025年，孔院累计举办专业研训25场以上，惠及一线教师5000余人次；针对清迈、清莱等边远山区华文学校师资薄弱的痛点，又常态化开办华人村华文学校本土教师专项研修班，截至2026年已办五届，培养山区教师470余人次。关键不在数字本身，而在方向的转变：从「派人去教」到「在本地把老师养出来」。正是这一点，让中文教育扎得下根，而不是只靠外部的支票本。

## 从「学语言」到「中文＋专业」：把桥通到产业一线

清迈孔院真正区别于「孔院只是一间语言课」这一老印象的，是它走向了「中文＋专业」。赖林的故事很有代表性：她1999年作为泰语专业学生沿湄公河到泰国研学，后来进过泰国中资企业，亲见出海企业对「中泰双语＋专业」人才的渴求；2021年以公派教师身份来到孔院后，参与「1＋X电子商务」培训，并配合完成当代国际中文教育、中泰跨境电商、可持续农企与资源管理三个中泰联合培养硕士项目。顺着这一思路，孔院为医护人员开《医院医护实用汉语》、为警务人员开《泰国警察实用中文》，累计培训涉外公职人员750余人次、医护骨干450余人次。最直观的注脚是清迈旅游警察中文班：2024年结业的那一期，学员占该局总人数一半以上，绝大多数此前从未碰过中文，学的也不是寒暄，而是「怎样给中国游客指路」「游客物品丢失时怎样询问」这类一线警员每天都会遇到的场景。中国稳居泰国最大客源国，语言在这里是基础设施，不是意识形态。一位警官结业时说：「学了中文，才能把服务做到中国游客真正需要的点上。」这句话，比任何白皮书都实在。

## 从「纸笔课堂」到「数字空间」：考点、平台与文化活动

2025年，清迈孔院汉语水平考试考量达15086人次，连续三年成为泰国第一大考点，并在全泰建起覆盖33个分考点的网络。数字背后是更深的数智化变革：元宇宙教学平台、海外网络自学微课平台，其中一门课程获评孔子学院精品数字资源。文化也随之走出课堂——2026年与江西景德镇合办的「瓷联中泰」陶瓷教育跨国大展，请来了泰国前副总理；春节游园会、水灯节中国风花车巡游、「中领杯」语言文化大赛，把中文融进了泰北的街头巷尾。学员们也在「汉语桥」赛场上接连摘金：从泰国赛区冠军到亚洲赛区冠军，年轻人们当初为就业、学业走进中文课堂，后来都成了中泰人文交流的纽带。正如赖林所言：「语言是桥，专业是路，而这座桥，要通到年轻人的未来里去。」

## 数字之外：一座孔院与泰北的二十年

把视线拉远，这件事的分量超过任何单项指标。泰北历来聚居着规模可观的华人、潮汕与云南籍社群，中文教育在这里不是外来移植，而是本地传承遇上了新的需求。清迈孔院的低调成功，也落在一个充满争议的背景上：就在院庆前几天，中国外交部公开表示反对针对孔子学院的「抹黑和污名化」。无论我们把孔院网络视作文化外交还是学术交流，清迈的案例都说明，能耐久的模式，是扎在本地需求里的那一种——去培训山区教师、去服务旅游警员、去织起考试网络——而不是只管投射的那一种。站在二十年的节点，孔院即将举办南亚与东南亚（Southeast Asia）国际中文教育大会、编撰纪念图册。由两所大学共同搭建的这座桥，正载着更多像林永祥、赖林这样的「摆渡人」，驶向下一个二十年。

## 延伸阅读

- **孔子学院（Confucius Institute）**：由中外高校合作设立的汉语教学与文化中心网络，既被视作学术交流，也常被讨论为软实力工具。
- **汉语桥（Chinese Bridge）**：面向外国学生的国际中文能力赛事，是全球展示中文学习成果的标志性平台。
- **汉语水平考试（HSK）**：全球通用的标准化中文能力测试，是衡量中文教育覆盖面的关键指标。

# Twenty Years by the Mekong: Chiang Mai University's Confucius Institute and the Quiet Rooting of Chinese in Northern Thailand

## From one classroom to a bridge

On 18 December 2006, Yunnan Normal University and Chiang Mai University jointly inaugurated the Confucius Institute at Chiang Mai University (CMU CI) — among the earliest Chinese-teaching bases in Northern Thailand. Twenty years on, what began as "scattered beads" of Chinese learning in the Thai North has been strung into a system. The institute now prefers to describe itself not as a language school but as a bridge connecting two societies. The story is worth examining as a case study in how soft power actually lands on the ground — not through slogans, but slowly, through teachers, through exams, and through police officers who can give a lost Chinese tourist directions.

## From scattered study to a system: local teachers as the foundation

When it opened, Northern Thailand had no standardised model for promoting Chinese, no systematic local faculty, and schools that struggled to find even one full-time Chinese teacher. The institute's first job was laying the foundations of the building. In 2009 it ran culture-into-schools programmes — paper-cutting, folk dance — planting the seed of a "teaching plus culture" model. In 2021 it signed a strategic cooperation agreement with Thailand's Private Education Commission, putting local teacher training at the core. Between 2023 and 2025 it ran more than 25 professional training sessions, reaching over 5,000 teachers. For the remote mountain Chinese schools of Chiang Mai and Chiang Rai, it has held five specialised training cohorts for village-school teachers, cultivating more than 470 by 2026. The point is not the headline number but the shift in direction: from sending teachers in to growing them locally. That is what lets Chinese education take root, rather than depending on an external chequebook.

## From language to "Chinese plus profession": bridging to industry

What truly distinguishes CMU CI from the old stereotype of the Confucius Institute as a mere language class is its move toward "Chinese plus profession." Lai Lin's story is representative: a Thai-language student who travelled to Thailand along the Mekong in 1999, later worked in a Chinese enterprise in Thailand, and saw first-hand the hunger of going-out firms for "bilingual Chinese–Thai plus professional" talent. Returning as a dispatched teacher in 2021, she joined the "1+X e-commerce" training and helped deliver three joint China–Thailand master's programmes — international Chinese education, cross-border e-commerce, and sustainable agribusiness. Following that logic, the institute opened practical Chinese courses for medical staff and for police and immigration officers, training over 750 public-service personnel and 450 medical professionals. The clearest emblem is the Chiang Mai tourist-police Chinese class: in the 2024 cohort, graduates made up more than half the station, most having never touched Chinese before. They learned not small talk but "how to direct a Chinese tourist" and "what to ask when belongings are lost" — the scenes front-line officers meet every day. With China the largest source of tourists to Thailand, language here is infrastructure, not ideology. One officer put it plainly at the graduation: "Only by learning Chinese can we serve Chinese visitors where they actually need it." That sentence is more honest than any white paper.

## From pen-and-paper to digital space: test centres, platforms, cultural events

In 2025 the institute administered 15,086 HSK test sittings, for the third consecutive year Thailand's largest test centre, with a network of 33 sub-centres across the country. Behind the figure is a deeper digital turn: a metaverse teaching platform, an online self-study micro-course platform, and a resource rated a Confucius Institute premium digital asset. Culture has followed Chinese out of the classroom — a 2026 ceramics exhibition co-run with Jingdezhen drew a former Thai deputy prime minister; Spring Festival fairs, Loy Krathong parades with Chinese-style floats, and language-culture competitions have pulled Chinese into the streets of the North. And the students win: Chiang Mai trainees have taken top prizes at successive "Chinese Bridge" competitions, from a Thailand-region championship to an Asia-region championship. These young people walked in for jobs and studies and left as nodes of people-to-people exchange. As Lai Lin said, "Language is the bridge, profession is the road, and this bridge must reach the future of the young."

## Beyond the numbers: an institute and two decades in Northern Thailand

Pull back, and the weight of the story exceeds any single metric. Northern Thailand has long been home to sizable Chinese, Teochew and Yunnanese communities; Chinese-language education here is not an imported transplant but a local inheritance meeting new demand. The quiet success of CMU CI also lands against a contested backdrop: just days before this anniversary, China's Foreign Ministry publicly opposed what it called the smearing and stigmatisation of Confucius Institutes abroad. Whether one reads the CI network as cultural diplomacy or academic outreach, the Chiang Mai case shows the model that endures is the one rooted in local need — training the mountain-school teacher, serving the tourist police, weaving the exam network — rather than the one that merely projects. Standing at the twenty-year mark, the institute is preparing a South and Southeast Asia international Chinese-education conference and a commemorative album. The bridge built by two universities keeps carrying its ferrymen — people like Lin Yongxiang and Lai Lin — toward the next twenty years.

## Further reading

- **Confucius Institute**: A global network of Chinese-language and cultural centres established jointly by Chinese and foreign universities, read both as academic outreach and as a soft-power instrument.
- **Chinese Bridge**: An international Chinese-language proficiency competition for foreign students, a flagship global platform for showcasing Chinese-learning talent.
- **HSK (Hanyu Shuiping Kaoshi)**: The standardised Chinese proficiency test administered worldwide, a key metric for the reach of Chinese-language education.

在河内熙攘的街头与北部工业园轰鸣的机床之间，越南正跑出一组令区域侧目的数字。前八月工业产出增长百分之十一点九、注册外资增长百分之五十五，这个曾被称作"下一个工厂"的经济体，在供应链重组的浪潮里按下了加速键。

## 一组跑在区域前头的数字

越南统计总局数据显示，二〇二六年一月至八月，全国工业生产指数同比增长百分之十一点九，为二〇一九年以来同期最强；其中八月单月同比增百分之十四点四。同期货物贸易总额达七千七百零一亿美元（约5.47万亿元人民币），同比增长近三成；出口三千七百四十八亿美元（约2.66万亿元人民币），增长逾两成。工业产出在全部三十四个省市实现正增长，显示出广覆盖的复苏成色。

## 外资为何"用脚投票"

资金的流向最具说服力。前八月越南注册外商直接投资达四百零六亿美元（约2880亿元人民币），同比增百分之五十五；加工制造业吸纳了九成出口，是外资最集中的腹地。这背后是供应链重组的大势：企业在中美博弈与成本抬升间寻找"中国+1"的替代支点，而越南凭年轻劳动力、持续改善的基建与多项自贸协定，成了资本用脚投票的去处。新加坡、韩国、日本居来源地前列，来源结构更趋多元。

## 加速度背后的隐忧

亮眼数字也背着新包袱。一是贸易逆差再度扩大：前八月逆差一百三十八亿美元（约980亿元人民币），上年同期还是顺差。二是通胀抬头，上半年居民消费价格涨幅已逼近政府管控上限，部分银行定存利率攀至百分之九，收紧银根的隐患渐显。三是外部风险：中东冲突推高能源价格、美国依据贸易条款对越南多行业发起调查，主力出口品类面临新增关税变数。

## 供应链重组中的越南角色

越南的起伏，是"中国+1"叙事最生动的注脚。它既承接了转移出来的制造与出口，也承接了由此而来的摩擦与波动。对东盟而言，越南像一个压力测试舱：供应链重组带来的不只是订单红利，还有被更大经济体贸易政策牵动的地缘脆弱。越南能否把"加速度"沉淀为"韧性"，取决于其能否在吸引外资与管控风险之间找到平衡。

## 一场给新兴经济体的镜鉴

越南的故事提醒我们：增长的引擎可以来自外部重组的东风，但可持续的成色最终取决于内功。当工业机器全速运转，真正决定走向的，是产业链的深度、宏观的稳健，以及把外部红利转化为内生能力的耐心。风口会转向，唯有韧性长青。

## 延伸阅读

**外商直接投资（Foreign Direct Investment, FDI）**：外国资本在本国设厂、并购或增资的行为，是衡量一国投资环境与产业承接力的重要指标。

**"中国+1"战略（China Plus One）**：企业将部分产能从中国分散至其他国家的供应链布局策略，意在分散地缘与成本风险。

**工业产出指数（Industrial Production Index, IIP）**：衡量采矿业、制造业与公用事业总产出的月度指标，是观察实体经济景气的重要温度计。

Between Hanoi's bustling streets and the humming machines of northern industrial parks, Vietnam is posting numbers that turn regional heads. Industrial output up 11.9% in eight months and registered FDI up 55%, this once-dubbed "next factory" has hit the accelerator amid supply-chain realignment.

## Numbers Outrunning the Region

Vietnam's General Statistics Office reports January–August 2026 industrial production rose 11.9% year on year—the strongest for the period since 2019, with August alone up 14.4%. Goods trade reached US$770.1 billion, up nearly 30%; exports hit US$374.8 billion, up over 20%. Industrial output grew in all 34 provinces and cities, a broad-based recovery.

## Why Capital "Votes With Its Feet"

Capital flows speak loudest. Registered FDI reached US$40.6 billion, up 55%; manufacturing absorbed 90% of exports and is where foreign capital concentrates. Behind it is supply-chain realignment: firms seek "China Plus One" alternatives amid US–China rivalry and rising costs, and Vietnam—with young labour, improving infrastructure and multiple free-trade deals—is where capital votes. Singapore, South Korea and Japan lead sources, now more diversified.

## The Worries Beneath the Pace

The bright numbers carry new baggage. First, the trade deficit widened again to US$13.8 billion, versus a surplus a year earlier. Second, inflation is stirring—first-half consumer prices neared the government's cap, some bank deposit rates hit 9%, hinting at tightening risk. Third, external risks: the Middle East conflict lifted energy prices, and the US launched Section 301-style probes into Vietnamese sectors, threatening new tariffs on key exports.

## Vietnam's Role in Realignment

Vietnam's swing is the liveliest footnote to "China Plus One." It absorbed shifted manufacturing and exports—and the friction and volatility that travel with them. For ASEAN, Vietnam is a pressure chamber: realignment brings not just order dividends but geopolitical fragility tied to bigger economies' trade policy. Whether Vietnam turns "acceleration" into "resilience" hinges on balancing attraction and risk control.

## A Lesson for Emerging Economies

Vietnam's story reminds us: the growth engine can ride external realignment, but durable quality rests on internal strength. As machines run full speed, what decides direction is industrial depth, macroeconomic steadiness, and the patience to convert external windfalls into endogenous capacity. Tailwinds shift; only resilience endures.

## Further reading

**Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: Foreign capital building factories, M&A or topping up stakes locally; a key gauge of investment climate and industrial absorptive capacity.

**"China Plus One"**: A supply-chain strategy spreading some capacity from China to other countries to diversify geopolitical and cost risk.

**Industrial Production Index (IIP)**: A monthly measure of mining, manufacturing and utilities output; a key thermometer of real-economy sentiment.

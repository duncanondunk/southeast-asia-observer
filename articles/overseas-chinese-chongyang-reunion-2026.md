# 重阳节里的海外侨胞：从“云端团圆”到文化寻根

重阳节，这个在中国本土渐被“敬老”与“登高”填充的节日，在海外侨社里却有着更厚重的情感分量。对远隔重洋的华侨华人而言，重阳从来不只是日历上的一天，而是与祖先、与故土、与代际之间的一次“仪式性重连”。2026年的重阳，恰逢中国与东南亚人员往来全面回暖，海外侨胞的过节方式正在从“云端团圆”走向更具实质的文化寻根。

## “云端重阳”：疫情遗产与情感刚需

疫情三年，把“线上祭祖”“视频团圆”推成了海外侨社的常态。2026年，尽管跨境旅行已恢复，许多年长侨胞仍延续着视频连线祭祖、家族群内“云登高发”的习惯。这并非技术依赖，而是一种低成本、低门槛的情感维系——当子女散居多国，一场跨越时区的视频家宴，往往比一年一次的物理返乡更能熨帖日常思念。华人社团也在其中扮演组织者角色：从马来西亚槟城的姓氏宗亲会，到新加坡的会馆重阳晚宴，线上直播让无法到场的海外宗亲“在场”。

## 从“过节”到“寻根”：年轻一代的回流

真正的变化发生在代际之间。与父辈把重阳当作家族内部仪式不同，不少出生在居住国的华裔青年，正把重阳与“寻根”挂钩。他们借重阳返乡参加祭祖，顺道走访祖籍村、查阅族谱、学习方言。这种“寻根式重阳”在东南亚华社尤为明显：印尼、泰国、菲律宾的华裔新生代，越来越多地把回乡祭祖与文化旅游、族谱数字化结合起来。对一些人而言，重阳不再只是缅怀，更是确认“我从哪里来”的身份锚点。

## 侨乡“接得住”：从被动接待到主动设计

需求侧的变化，倒逼供给侧升级。福建、广东、海南等地的侨乡，近年来不再满足于“老乡回来吃顿饭”，而是主动设计寻根产品：开放祖屋、修复宗祠、整理村史、培训“讲古人”。一些侨乡还把重阳与“侨博会”“寻根夏令营”打包，让一次祭祖变成一条集亲缘、文旅、投资于一体的链条。这种“节庆+寻根+招商”的组合，既承接了情感，也转化了资源。

## 文化符号的再编码：重阳如何“本地化”

在居住国，重阳也在被本地重新编码。马来西亚将重阳与“尊老”的多元文化叙事结合，成为族群和谐的表达；新加坡的会馆则把登高、吃重阳糕与华文教育绑定，作为传承语言与价值的载体。这种本地化并非稀释，而是让重阳在异乡获得新的社会功能——它既是华人身份的标志，也成为居住国多元文化版图中的一块拼图。

## 值得观察的几点

其一，代际传递能否持续。若年轻一代的“寻根”仅停留在旅游式体验，重阳的文化厚度可能被消费化稀释。其二，侨乡服务能力能否跟上。寻根需求上升，对村史整理、语言服务、接待规范提出更高要求。其三，数字祭祖的边界。当“云祭扫”愈发便捷，线下仪式的不可替代性如何保存，是侨社需要面对的议题。

归根结底，海外侨胞的重阳，是一面映照离散与归属的镜子。它从“云端团圆”起步，正走向更有根的“文化寻根”——而这条路的宽度，取决于代际、侨乡与居住国能否共同把节日的意义续写下去。

## 延伸阅读

**文化寻根（Cultural Root-Seeking）**：指散居海外的华裔后代通过返乡祭祖、查阅族谱、走访祖籍地等方式，确认自身族群来源与身份认同的行为，常与节庆、文旅结合。

**宗亲会（Clan Association）**：海外华侨华人以姓氏或祖籍为纽带自发组成的互助与联谊组织，在祭祖、养老、华文教育等方面发挥中枢作用。

**会馆（Huiguan / Dialect Association）**：早期移民按方言或地域建立的同乡互助机构，现代多转型为文化传承与社群服务载体。

# At the Double Ninth Festival, Overseas Chinese Reconnect: From 'Cloud Reunions' to Cultural Root-Seeking

The Double Ninth Festival (Chongyang), increasingly framed in mainland China around filial piety and mountain climbing, carries a heavier emotional charge in overseas Chinese communities. For Chinese diasporas far from home, the day has never been merely a calendar entry; it is a ritualised reconnection with ancestors, homeland and generations. In 2026, as people-to-people exchange between China and Southeast Asia fully recovers, how overseas Chinese mark Chongyang is shifting from 'cloud reunions' toward more substantive cultural root-seeking.

## 'Cloud Chongyang': a pandemic legacy and an emotional necessity

The three pandemic years pushed online ancestral worship and video reunions into the norm for overseas communities. In 2026, although cross-border travel has resumed, many elderly migrants still keep the habit of video-linked ancestor rites and family-group 'cloud climbing'. This is less technological dependence than a low-cost, low-threshold way to sustain ties — when children are scattered across countries, a cross-time-zone video feast often soothes daily longing better than one annual physical homecoming. Clan and dialect associations organise too: from Penang's surname associations to Singapore's clan-reunion dinners, livestreaming lets absent kin 'attend'.

## From 'observing' to 'seeking roots': the young return

The real shift is intergenerational. Unlike parents who treat Chongyang as a family ritual, many locally born ethnic Chinese youth now link it to root-seeking. They return for ancestral rites and, en route, visit ancestral villages, consult genealogies and learn dialects. This 'root-seeking Chongyang' is pronounced in Southeast Asia: Indonesian, Thai and Filipino Chinese millennials increasingly combine homecoming rites with cultural tourism and genealogy digitisation. For some, Chongyang is no longer only remembrance but an identity anchor answering 'where I come from'.

## Qiaoxiang that 'catches' them: from passive hosting to active design

Demand-side change forces supply-side upgrade. Qiaoxiang (ancestral hometowns) in Fujian, Guangdong and Hainan no longer settle for 'returning villagers for a meal'; they actively design root-seeking products: opening ancestral homes, restoring clan halls, compiling village histories, training 'storytellers'. Some bundle Chongyang with 'qiaobohui' (overseas Chinese fairs) and root-seeking camps, turning one rite into a chain linking kinship, tourism and investment. This 'festival + roots + investment' mix both receives emotion and converts resources.

## Re-coding the symbol: how Chongyang localises

In countries of residence, Chongyang is being re-coded. Malaysia fuses it with multicultural 'respect for the elderly' narratives as an expression of ethnic harmony; Singapore's associations bind climbing and Chongyang cakes to Chinese-medium education as a vehicle for language and values. This localisation is not dilution but gives Chongyang new social functions abroad — both a marker of Chinese identity and a tile in the host country's multicultural mosaic.

## A few things to watch

First, whether intergenerational transmission persists. If the young generation's root-seeking stays tourist-like, Chongyang's cultural depth risks being consumed away. Second, whether qiaoxiang service capacity keeps up — rising demand raises the bar for village history, language service and reception. Third, the boundary of digital rites: as 'cloud worship' grows convenient, how to preserve the irreplaceability of physical ritual is a question communities must face.

In the end, overseas Chinese Chongyang is a mirror reflecting diaspora and belonging. Starting from 'cloud reunions', it is moving toward a more rooted 'cultural root-seeking' — and the width of that road depends on whether generations, qiaoxiang and host societies can jointly keep rewriting the festival's meaning.

## Further reading

**Cultural Root-Seeking**: The practice by overseas ethnic Chinese descendants of confirming their ethnic origin and identity by returning to worship ancestors, consulting genealogies and visiting ancestral hometowns, often combined with festivals and cultural tourism.

**Clan Association**: A self-organised mutual-aid and fellowship body among overseas Chinese formed around surname or ancestral origin, serving as a hub for ancestral rites, elder care and Chinese-medium education.

**Huiguan (Dialect Association)**: Early immigrant organisations built on dialect or region for mutual aid, now largely transformed into carriers of cultural heritage and community service.

一块指甲盖大小的晶圆，背后是纯度达到十亿分之一杂质的硅。当人工智能催生的存储芯片需求暴涨，这种几乎看不见的材料，正把日本、越南与马来西亚悄悄缝进同一条供应链。

## 德山是谁

德山（Tokuyama）是日本硅材料巨头，半导体级多晶硅全球市占约20%，仅次于德国瓦克（Wacker）等，位列第三。多晶硅是晶圆制造的必需原料，纯度要求极高。

## 越南：后段工序先行

德山在越南胡志明市富美3号特别工业园设厂，2024年8月成立全资子公司，2026年8月举行启用仪式。该厂投资约6000万美元（约4.04亿元人民币），年产能约4000至4955吨，承担多晶硅的破碎、清洗与检测等后段工序，是越南首家半导体级多晶硅工厂，预计2027年商业化量产。

## 马来西亚：前段合成落地

在马来西亚砂拉越，德山与韩国OCI合资成立OCI德山半导体材料公司（OTSM），总投资约4.35亿美元（约31亿元人民币），规划年产能8000至1万吨，负责多晶硅的合成与沉积等前段工序；合资资本约9.22亿林吉特（约15.6亿元人民币），2026年7月动工，预计2027年春季竣工。两厂投产后，德山总产能将提升50%，达到年产1.25万吨。

## 为什么是东南亚

答案在"集中风险"。德山原有产能几乎全集中在日本山口县周南市，单一基地一旦因灾害停工，便会波及全行业。新冠疫情后，客户愈发要求供应来源多元。更关键的是，Rapidus、台积电（TSMC）等芯片厂正落地日本，德山需要把产能贴近"友好"的东南亚，以确保稳定供货。

## 人工智能是隐形推手

投资人工智能带动高带宽存储（HBM）等部件增长，超高纯多晶硅需求随之上升。据半导体产业协会（SEMI）估算，硅片出货量到2028年将达155亿平方英寸，较2025年增约20%。德山预判，到2027年日本本土产能将供不应求，越南与马来西亚的新增产量预计三到四年内被市场消化。

## 日韩联手的一笔旧账

合资方韩国OCI并非新面孔：2016年它曾把一座年产3.5万吨的马来西亚多晶硅厂出售给德山，如今又作为伙伴回来建新厂。从卖厂到合建，折射出供应链重构中企业关系的反复与深化。

## 东南亚接住的高附加值一环

对越南与马来西亚，这意味着从"组装测试"向材料前段升级的可能。一如印尼推动镍矿"下游化"，日本把高纯材料产能外移，让东南亚在半导体价值链中不只是消费端与组装端，也开始卡住上游一环。

## 一盘关于韧性的棋

德山在越马两线落子，表面是扩产，底层是供应链韧性（resilience）的逻辑：把关键材料分散到政治友好、灾害风险低的邻邦，既贴住人工智能时代的客户，也给自己留一条退路。当一块硅的纯度成为国家战略，东南亚已是这盘棋上绕不开的格子。

## 延伸阅读

**多晶硅（Polysilicon）**：纯度极高的硅材料，是制造半导体晶圆与太阳能电池的基础原料，杂质须控制在十亿分之一级别。

**高带宽存储（HBM）**：人工智能芯片关键存储部件，其扩产直接拉动超高纯多晶硅需求。

**下游化（Downstreaming）**：印尼等资源国推动矿产在本地加工增值而非原矿出口的政策，类比于半导体材料产能向东南亚转移。

A wafer the size of a fingernail rests on silicon purified to one impurity per billion. As AI fuels demand for memory chips, this near-invisible material is quietly stitching Japan, Vietnam and Malaysia into one supply chain.

## Who Is Tokuyama

Tokuyama is a Japanese silicon maker holding roughly 20% of the global semiconductor-grade polysilicon market, third after Germany's Wacker. Polysilicon is the essential feedstock for wafers, demanding extreme purity.

## Vietnam: The Back-End First

In Ho Chi Minh City's Phu My 3 special industrial park, Tokuyama's wholly owned subsidiary—set up in August 2024—held its inauguration in August 2026. The plant invests about US$60 million and targets 4,000–4,955 tonnes a year, handling back-end steps of crushing, cleaning and inspection. It is Vietnam's first semiconductor-grade polysilicon plant, with commercial output expected in 2027.

## Malaysia: The Front-End Lands

In Sarawak, Malaysia, Tokuyama formed a joint venture with South Korea's OCI—OCI Tokuyama Semiconductor Materials (OTSM)—investing about US$435 million for 8,000–10,000 tonnes a year of front-end synthesis and deposition, breaking ground in July 2026 and due in spring 2027. Together the two Southeast Asian plants lift Tokuyama's total capacity by 50% to 12,500 tonnes a year.

## Why Southeast Asia

The answer is concentration risk. Tokuyama's output sat almost entirely at Shunan, Yamaguchi prefecture; a single-site shutdown would ripple across the industry. After COVID-19, customers demanded diversified sources. Crucially, chipmakers like Rapidus and TSMC are building in Japan, so Tokuyama needs capacity near "friendly" Southeast Asia to secure supply.

## AI as the Hidden Driver

Investment in artificial intelligence lifts demand for high-bandwidth memory (HBM) and, with it, ultra-high-purity polysilicon. SEMI estimates wafer shipments will reach 15.5 billion square inches by 2028, up about 20% from 2025. Tokuyama expects its Japanese site to run short around 2027; the new Vietnamese and Malaysian output should be absorbed within three to four years.

## A Korea–Japan Deal with a Past

Partner OCI is no stranger: in 2016 it sold Tokuyama a 35,000-tonne Malaysian plant, and now returns as a joint-venture ally. From seller to co-builder, the relationship shows how supply-chain rewiring deepens old ties.

## Southeast Asia Catches the High-Value Link

For Vietnam and Malaysia, this means a possible climb from assembly-and-test toward front-end materials. Much as Indonesia pushes nickel "downstreaming," Japan's shift of high-purity capacity lets Southeast Asia sit not only as consumer and assembler but also upstream.

## A Game of Resilience

Tokuyama's two moves in Vietnam and Malaysia look like expansion but rest on resilience: spreading key material across politically friendly, low-disaster neighbours, hugging AI-era customers while keeping an escape route. When a sliver of silicon becomes state strategy, Southeast Asia is a square no board can skip.

## Further reading

**Polysilicon**: Ultra-pure silicon, the base feedstock for semiconductor wafers and solar cells, with impurities held to one part per billion.

**High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM)**: A key AI-chip memory component whose expansion directly drives ultra-pure polysilicon demand.

**Downstreaming**: Indonesia's policy of processing minerals at home for added value rather than exporting ore, akin to semiconductor-material capacity moving to Southeast Asia.

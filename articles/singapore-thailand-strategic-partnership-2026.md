# 新加坡—泰国领导人会晤：在东盟轮值主席接力前，先把手牵紧

9月2日，新加坡总理黄循财赴曼谷，与泰国总理阿努廷举行第五次新加坡—泰国领导人retreat。两国发表"面向未来战略伙伴"联合声明，把绿色与数字经济、能源韧性、互联互通、粮食安全与打击跨国犯罪列为新合作方向，并约定在两国2027、2028年先后担任东盟轮值主席国时协同推进区域议程。在不确定加剧的当下，两个体量不大的国家选择以务实协作巩固区域中心性，这本身就值得细读。

## 一场"retreat"的含金量

与正式国事访问不同，领导人retreat重在非正式、深谈。据新加坡总理公署发布的联合声明，此次是黄循财与阿努廷的首次领导人retreat，地点选在曼谷。双方重申长期防务与经济合作，并探讨绿色经济、数字经济、人工智能、能源韧性等新领域。形式上的"轻松"，恰恰服务于议题上的"深入"——把需要跨部委跟进的事项，先在两国的领导人层面敲定方向。

## 从"老朋友"到"面向未来"

联合声明将双边关系定位为"面向未来战略伙伴"（Forward-Looking Strategic Partnership）。这一定位并非凭空而来：2025年两国庆祝建交60周年，泰国总理阿努廷此前于2025年11月正式访新，双方已就四大支柱达成战略方向。此次retreat的增量，在于把合作从既有框架，推向绿色、数字、AI、能源韧性等面向下一阶段增长的赛道。

## 绿色与数字：新赛道上的合奏

能源是此次声明的重点。双方欢迎老挝—泰国—马来西亚—新加坡电力一体化项目（LTMS-PIP）作为实现东盟电网的重要一步，并同意推动多边、多向电力贸易。在全球能源市场因中东冲突波动的背景下，低碳、可负担、多元的能源来源成为两国共同诉求。数字经济与AI则被并列提出，显示两国希望把技术密集型产业作为下一轮增长引擎。

## 东盟主席年的"接力棒"

更具地缘政治意味的，是两国对东盟轮值主席年的安排：新加坡将于2027年、泰国将于2028年先后担任主席国。联合声明明确，双方将在各自主席年任内密切协作，推进东盟优先事项、强化区域一体化、深化与外部伙伴合作。这意味着未来两年东盟议程的节奏，很大程度由新泰这对"邻里"共同设定。

## 规则秩序下的默契

声明重申维护"以规则为基础的国际秩序"。对新加坡与泰国而言，这一表述既是外交辞令，也是现实选择：两国都是高度依赖外贸与规则的小国或中等经济体，区域稳定与可预期的规则环境直接关系增长前景。在不确定的全球环境中，重申规则秩序，等于为自身发展买一份"保险"。

## 从双边到区域的外溢

新泰合作历来是区域合作的"试验田"。声明提到，将充分利用新加坡—泰国公务员交流计划（CSEP）与新加坡—泰国强化经济关系（STEER）等既有平台跟进落实。这些机制化安排的意义在于：双边经验可被转化为区域公共产品，例如东盟电网、跨境数字联通，最终惠及整个东南亚。

## 为什么是现在

时机值得玩味。2026年区域格局正处在"再校准"期：缅甸问题持续消耗东盟内聚力，大国竞争挤压中小国家回旋空间。在此背景下，新泰以领导人retreat明确"向前看"的伙伴关系，既是对内稳住合作预期，也是对外释放区域国家自主协作的信号——不必等外部风向，自己先把手牵紧。

## 小国协作的样本意义

新泰此次互动的启示在于：区域中心性不是靠某一个大国施予，而是靠成员国持续的能力建设与务实协作积累。两个体量有限的国家，通过把绿色、数字、能源等新议题做深，反而能在东盟议程中掌握更多主动权。对东南亚其他双边关系而言，这是一种可参照的样本——把"邻里逻辑"转化为"区域逻辑"。

## 延伸阅读

**东盟电网（ASEAN Power Grid）**：旨在连接东盟各国电网、实现多边电力贸易的区域能源一体化倡议，LTMS-PIP 是其跨境互联的先行项目。

**新加坡—泰国强化经济关系（STEER）**：两国部长级经济合作机制，聚焦贸易、投资与可持续发展等领域的政策协调。

**面向未来战略伙伴（Forward-Looking Strategic Partnership）**：2026年新泰领导人retreat后确立的双边关系定位，涵盖绿色、数字、能源与区域合作等方向。

# Singapore–Thailand Leaders' Retreat: Tying Hands Before the ASEAN Chairmanship Baton Passes

On 2 September, Singapore's Prime Minister Lawrence Wong travelled to Bangkok for the fifth Singapore–Thailand Leaders' Retreat with Thai Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul. The two issued a "Forward-Looking Strategic Partnership" joint statement, listing green and digital economy, energy resilience, connectivity, food security and transnational crime as new directions, and agreeing to coordinate regional agendas as the two countries assume the ASEAN chairmanship in 2027 and 2028 respectively. Amid rising uncertainty, two modest-sized states chose pragmatic collaboration to consolidate regional centrality — a move worth reading closely.

## What a "retreat" is worth

Unlike a formal state visit, a leaders' retreat prizes informality and depth. According to the joint statement released by the Singapore Prime Minister's Office, this was Wong and Anutin's first leaders' retreat, held in Bangkok. The two reaffirmed long-standing defence and economic cooperation and explored new areas such as green economy, digital economy, artificial intelligence and energy resilience. The relaxed form serves a substantive purpose: settling the direction of items that require cross-ministry follow-up at the leaders' level first.

## From "old friends" to "forward-looking"

The joint statement frames the bilateral relationship as a "Forward-Looking Strategic Partnership." This framing did not appear from nowhere: the two countries celebrated 60 years of diplomatic relations in 2025, and Anutin visited Singapore in November 2025, where the two sides agreed on a four-pillar strategic direction. The retreat's added value lies in pushing cooperation from existing frameworks toward green, digital, AI and energy-resilience tracks geared to the next phase of growth.

## Green and digital: a duet on new tracks

Energy was a focal point of the statement. Both welcomed the Lao PDR–Thailand–Malaysia–Singapore Power Integration Project (LTMS-PIP) as an important step toward the ASEAN Power Grid, and agreed to advance multilateral, multi-directional power trade. Against volatility in global energy markets due to the Middle East conflict, low-carbon, affordable and diversified energy sources became a shared imperative. Digital economy and AI were named alongside, signalling both countries' wish to make technology-intensive industries the next growth engine.

## The "baton" of the ASEAN chairmanship

More geopolitically suggestive is the arrangement on ASEAN chairmanship years: Singapore will chair in 2027 and Thailand in 2028. The statement explicitly says the two will work closely during their respective chairmanships to advance ASEAN priorities, strengthen regional integration and deepen cooperation with external partners. This means the rhythm of the ASEAN agenda over the next two years will be shaped substantially by this "neighbourly" pair.

## Tacit understanding under the rules-based order

The statement reaffirmed commitment to a "rules-based international order." For Singapore and Thailand, this phrasing is both diplomatic routine and real choice: both are small or middle economies highly dependent on trade and rules, and regional stability with a predictable rules environment directly bears on growth prospects. Reaffirming the rules-based order amid global uncertainty is, in effect, buying insurance for their own development.

## Spillover from bilateral to regional

Singapore–Thailand cooperation has long been a "testing ground" for regional cooperation. The statement notes that existing platforms such as the Singapore–Thailand Civil Service Exchange Programme (CSEP) and the Singapore–Thailand Enhanced Economic Relationship (STEER) will be used to follow up. The value of such institutionalised arrangements is that bilateral experience can be converted into regional public goods — the ASEAN grid, cross-border digital connectivity — ultimately benefiting all of Southeast Asia.

## Why now

The timing is telling. In 2026 the regional landscape is in a "recalibration" phase: the Myanmar issue keeps draining ASEAN cohesion, and great-power competition squeezes the manoeuvring room of smaller states. Against this backdrop, Singapore and Thailand using a leaders' retreat to define a "forward-looking" partnership steadies domestic cooperation expectations inward and signals outward that regional states can coordinate autonomously — without waiting for external winds, they tie their hands first.

## The sample value of small-state collaboration

The lesson of this Singapore–Thailand interaction is that regional centrality is not granted by any great power but accumulated through continuous capacity-building and pragmatic cooperation among members. Two states of limited heft, by deepening new issues such as green, digital and energy, can secure more initiative in the ASEAN agenda. For other bilateral relationships in Southeast Asia, this is a reference model — converting "neighbour logic" into "regional logic."

## Further reading

**ASEAN Power Grid**: A regional energy-integration initiative to connect ASEAN grids and enable multilateral power trade; LTMS-PIP is its pioneer cross-border project.

**Singapore–Thailand Enhanced Economic Relationship (STEER)**: A ministerial economic cooperation mechanism focusing on policy coordination in trade, investment and sustainability.

**Forward-Looking Strategic Partnership**: The bilateral positioning established after the 2026 Singapore–Thailand leaders' retreat, covering green, digital, energy and regional cooperation.

# 不等十月：安华在国庆前夕送出的六项措施

## 布城的一夜：六项措施与一句实话

8月30日晚，马来西亚首相安华在布城国际会议中心发表2026年国庆献词。按惯例，这种场合的台词多半是回顾与勉励。但这一次，他带了六项具体措施上台，而且全部自9月1日起生效。

六项措施分别是：恢复补贴汽油的月度配额、增加学校维修拨款、为青年提供人工智能应用订阅、为公共医疗数字化注资、放宽电子发票的豁免门槛、扩大小微融资规模。它们彼此之间没有明显的逻辑关联，唯一的共同点是都能在短期内减轻家庭或小微企业的现金压力。

安华自己把话说得更直白。他说，通常他会讲"等财政预算案"，但这次不行——"人民等不了，因为生活成本的压力不会等到十月"。

一句实话，比六项措施更值得琢磨。

## 从200升回到300升：一次政策回撤

六项里分量最重的是第一项。政府同意把"Budi Madani RON95"（BUDI95）计划下的基本月度配额，从此前的200升恢复到300升，惠及超过1600万名用户；同时，超过50万名符合条件的柴油皮卡与四轮驱动车主，每月可获得的补贴柴油上限提高到400升。补贴价格分别为每升1.99令吉和2.10令吉。

要理解这项措施的分量，需要把时间往回拨五个月。今年4月1日，政府把BUDI95配额从300升临时下调到200升，理由是全球能源市场的不确定性与供应压力，当时强调这是"暂时的"。现在，五个月不到就恢复原状。

这在政治上是一次回撤。配额下调本意是控制财政支出与打击跨境套利，但它直接压缩了依赖私家车通勤的家庭的可支配空间，在东马与城郊这类公共交通薄弱的地区尤其明显。政府现在把它改回来，等于承认上一次调整的成本被低估了。

不过要注意，这次回归的是"配额"，不是"价格"。补贴汽油的价格维持在每升1.99令吉不变。换句话说，政府修复的是"能买多少"，而不是"便宜多少"——财政的口子并没有重新开大，只是把此前收紧的绳子松回原位。

## 学校、诊所与电子发票：钱花在看不见的地方

后面几项措施看起来不如汽油抢眼，但更能说明政府的取向。

第二项：2027年的学校维修拨款增加50%，从10亿令吉（约16.7亿元人民币）提高到15亿令吉（约25.1亿元人民币）。这份拨款覆盖所有源流学校——国民学校、国民型华文小学与淡米尔文小学、宗教学校、私塾与可兰经背诵学校，以及特殊教育设施。安华以财政部长身份要求教育部从下个月起在县一级加快审批维修申请，让工程能在2027年1月就开工。

把华小与淡小明确写进拨款范围，在马来西亚从来不只是一个财政技术问题。它在释放一个信号：这笔钱不按族群分配。

第四项：马来西亚通讯与多媒体委员会（MCMC）拿出10亿令吉（约16.7亿元人民币）改善公共医疗领域的数字化效率，用于推行电子病历系统，并提升150家医院和2000多间政府诊所的网络连接。

第五项：电子发票的豁免门槛，从年销售额100万令吉（约167万元人民币）提高到300万令吉（约501万元人民币）——年销售额不超过300万令吉的企业，不必再履行电子发票义务。

这三项的共同特点是：都不是直接给钱，而是改规则、修设施、降合规成本。效果不会立刻体现在家庭账本上，但会体现在未来几年学校屋顶漏不漏雨、诊所能不能调出病历、小商家要不要为开票专门雇人。这类支出在政治上的回报慢，但对一个反复强调"财政可持续"的政府来说，它是唯一能同时兼顾民望与账本的做法。

## 10万青年与人工智能：一张远期支票

第三项措施最耐人寻味。政府推出面向18岁至30岁马来西亚人的"人工智能为人民"（AI for the People）计划，10万名完成规定模块的青年，可获得三个月指定人工智能应用的免费订阅，包括IlmuChat、Gemini Enterprise、Wonderclip和MuleRun。

10万人、三个月、订阅制。这个规模放在3400万人口里，象征意义大于实际覆盖面。但它透露的焦虑是真实的：人工智能正在改变入门级白领工作的形态，而马来西亚的青年就业结构中，这类岗位占比不低。政府拿不出一个完整的再培训体系时，先让10万人摸上工具，是一种成本最低的试探。

问题在于，三个月订阅到期之后呢？如果配套的课程、认证与岗位对接跟不上，"让青年用上人工智能"很容易退化成一次发账号的活动。衡量这项措施的标准，不该是发了多少个账号，而是一年后这批人里有多少人的收入结构因此改变。

## 为什么不等十月

回到那句"等不了十月"。

马来西亚的2027年财政预算案按计划在10月提呈，距今不过五周。一个政府愿意在预算案前五周单独宣布一批支出措施，通常只有两种解释：要么经济数据突然恶化，要么政治压力突然上升。从安华的表态看，两者兼有。

他有一段话讲得很坦白：尽管已经推行了各项措施，他、副首相和内阁从来没有假设所有问题都已解决，"这不是一块用来遮掩人民关切的幕布"，他理解人民仍在与生活成本搏斗。这种表述在马来西亚的政治语境里并不常见——它承认了此前一轮措施的失效，而不是把新措施包装成既定成效的延续。

政治背景也摆在那里。反对党近月持续以生活成本为题施压，而安华在献词中同时透露，马来西亚日在砂拉越的庆典和2027年预算案还会再有宣布。这是一次分批次投放，不是一次性的红包。

## 补贴的两难：马来西亚的长期账

最后一项措施把规模补上了：小微融资增加10亿令吉，使2026年总额从50亿令吉（约83.5亿元人民币）提高到60亿令吉（约100亿元人民币）；另设2亿令吉（约3.3亿元人民币）的"昌明繁荣拨款"（Geran Sejahtera MADANI），用于支持小贩、小商人、夜市摊主和居家创业的母亲。

把六项加起来看，这是一份典型的"精准补贴"方案：定向给驾车通勤的家庭、给学生和家长、给青年、给小商户，同时避免回到全民无差别补贴的老路。自2024年以来，马来西亚的补贴改革一直沿着这条线走——用身份识别与配额管理，把财政资源集中到真正需要的人身上。

这条路在技术上是对的，在政治上却永远吃力。因为精准补贴要求政府持续判断"谁算需要"，而任何一次判断失误，都会立刻转化为选票上的损失。4月那次配额下调就是一次判断失误，8月这次恢复是对它的修正。

更根本的问题是，马来西亚至今没有解决一个结构性矛盾：它既要靠补贴维持社会稳定，又要靠财政整顿维持信用评级与外资信心。这两件事在短期可以兼顾，在长期会互相挤压。六项措施能买到几个月的时间，买不到答案。

安华说，经济增长必须归还给人民。这句话没有错，但它回避了另一个问题：如果增长本身不够快，归还的动作就只能是重新分配存量。而对一个中等收入国家来说，靠重新分配撑起来的民生改善，天花板来得很早。

## 延伸阅读

**精准补贴（Targeted subsidy）**：以身份识别、配额或收入门槛限定受益范围的补贴方式，马来西亚自2024年起通过MyKad系统推行，与普惠式燃料补贴相对，意在兼顾财政可持续与社会保护。

**电子发票（e-Invoice）**：企业按统一格式向税务部门实时或准实时报送交易凭证的制度，马来西亚自2024年起分阶段推行；豁免门槛的高低直接决定小微型商户的合规成本。

**生活成本压力（Cost-of-living pressure）**：居民实际购买力受物价、税费与补贴退坡挤压的感受，近两年是马来西亚、印尼、泰国等国最核心的国内政治议题之一。

# Not waiting for October: Anwar's six measures on the eve of Merdeka

## One night in Putrajaya: six measures and one honest sentence

On the evening of 30 August, Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim delivered his 2026 National Day address at the Putrajaya International Convention Centre. By convention, speeches on such occasions are mostly retrospection and encouragement. This time he brought six concrete measures to the podium, all taking effect on 1 September.

They are: restoring the subsidised petrol quota, increasing school maintenance funding, giving young people subscriptions to AI applications, funding digitalisation in public healthcare, relaxing the e-invoicing exemption threshold, and expanding microfinancing. There is no obvious logic linking them. Their only common property is that each reduces short-term cash pressure on households or small businesses.

Anwar put it more plainly himself. Usually, he said, he would say wait for the national Budget. But this time he could not — "the people cannot wait because the pressure of living costs does not wait until October."

One honest sentence is worth more scrutiny than six measures.

## Back to 300 litres: a policy retreat

The heaviest of the six is the first. The government has agreed to restore the basic monthly eligibility under the Budi MADANI RON95 (BUDI95) programme from 200 litres to 300 litres, benefiting more than 16 million users. More than 500,000 eligible owners of diesel pick-up trucks and four-wheel-drive vehicles will be able to receive up to 400 litres of subsidised diesel a month. Subsidised prices remain at RM1.99 and RM2.10 per litre respectively.

To grasp the weight of this, rewind five months. On 1 April the government temporarily cut the BUDI95 quota from 300 litres to 200 litres, citing uncertainty in global energy markets and supply pressure, and stressing that the move was temporary. Now, in under five months, the original level is back.

Politically, this is a retreat. The cut was meant to contain fiscal outlays and curb cross-border arbitrage, but it squeezed the disposable budgets of households that depend on private cars, particularly in East Malaysia and outer suburbs where public transport is thin. Restoring it amounts to conceding that the cost of the previous adjustment was underestimated.

Note what is being restored, though: the quota, not the price. Subsidised petrol stays at RM1.99 per litre. The government has repaired "how much you may buy," not "how cheap it is." The fiscal tap has not been reopened; a rope that had been pulled tight has simply been slackened back to its original length.

## Schools, clinics and e-invoicing: money spent where it cannot be seen

The next items are less eye-catching than petrol, but say more about where the government is heading.

Second: maintenance allocations for schools in 2027 rise by 50 per cent, from RM1 billion to RM1.5 billion. The money covers every stream — national schools, Chinese and Tamil national-type schools, religious schools, tahfiz and pondok institutions, and special education facilities. Speaking also as Finance Minister, Anwar instructed the Education Ministry to accelerate district-level assessment of repair applications from next month so work can begin as early as January 2027.

Naming Chinese and Tamil vernacular schools explicitly has never been merely a fiscal question in Malaysia. It signals that this money is not being distributed along ethnic lines.

Fourth: the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission will provide RM1 billion to improve digital efficiency across public healthcare, supporting the rollout of the Electronic Medical Records system and better internet connectivity at 150 hospitals and more than 2,000 government clinics.

Fifth: the e-invoicing exemption threshold rises from RM1 million to RM3 million in annual sales, releasing businesses below that line from the obligation entirely.

These three share a property: none hands out cash. They change rules, repair facilities and cut compliance costs. The effect will not show up on a household ledger this month, but it will show up over the next few years in whether school roofs leak, whether a clinic can pull up a patient's records, and whether a small trader has to hire someone just to issue invoices. Politically the return is slow — but for a government that keeps stressing fiscal sustainability, it is the only way to serve both popularity and the ledger.

## 100,000 young people and AI: a post-dated cheque

The third measure is the most interesting. The government is opening an "AI for the People" programme to Malaysians aged 18 to 30: 100,000 young people who complete the prescribed modules will receive three-month free subscriptions to selected AI applications, including IlmuChat, Gemini Enterprise, Wonderclip and MuleRun.

One hundred thousand people, three months, subscriptions. Set against a population of 34 million, the symbolism outweighs the coverage. Yet the anxiety behind it is real: AI is reshaping the shape of entry-level white-collar work, and such jobs make up no small share of Malaysian youth employment. Unable to produce a full retraining system, the government is taking the cheapest possible probe — get 100,000 people onto the tools.

The question is what happens when the three months expire. Without matching coursework, certification and job matching, "getting young people onto AI" easily degrades into an account-distribution exercise. The measure should be judged not by how many accounts were issued but by how many of those people have a different income structure a year from now.

## Why not wait for October

Back to that sentence: cannot wait until October.

Malaysia's Budget 2027 is scheduled for tabling in October, five weeks away. A government that announces a batch of spending measures five weeks before its own budget usually has one of two reasons, and often both: the economic data has suddenly worsened, or political pressure has suddenly risen. On Anwar's own account, both apply.

One passage is strikingly candid. Although various measures have been implemented, he said, he, the Deputy Prime Minister and the Cabinet have never assumed that all problems are solved, and this is not a curtain to conceal the people's concerns; he understands that people are still struggling with the cost of living. That formulation is unusual in Malaysian political discourse. It concedes that the previous round of measures underperformed, rather than presenting the new ones as a continuation of established success.

The political context is plain. The opposition has pressed hard on cost of living in recent months, and Anwar signalled in the same address that further announcements will come at the Malaysia Day celebrations in Sarawak and through Budget 2027. This is a phased release, not a one-off handout.

## The subsidy dilemma: Malaysia's long account

The last measure completes the scale. Microfinancing facilities will be increased by RM1 billion, raising the 2026 total from RM5 billion to RM6 billion. A RM200 million Geran Sejahtera MADANI will support hawkers, small traders, night-market operators and mothers running businesses from home.

Taken together, the six measures form a textbook targeted-subsidy package: directed at commuting households, at students and parents, at young people, at small businesses — while avoiding a return to universal untargeted subsidies. Since 2024, Malaysia's subsidy reform has followed this line, using identity verification and quota management to concentrate fiscal resources on those who actually need them.

Technically the road is right. Politically it is permanently exhausting, because targeted subsidy requires the government to keep judging who counts as needy, and every misjudgement converts immediately into lost votes. April's quota cut was a misjudgement. August's restoration is the correction.

The deeper problem remains unsolved. Malaysia has to sustain social stability through subsidies while sustaining credit ratings and investor confidence through fiscal consolidation. In the short run the two can be reconciled; in the long run they squeeze each other. Six measures buy a few months. They do not buy an answer.

Anwar says economic growth must be returned to the people. Nothing wrong with that — except that it dodges the other question. If growth itself is not fast enough, "returning" can only mean redistributing an existing stock. And for a middle-income country, welfare improvement propped up by redistribution hits its ceiling early.

## Further reading

**Targeted subsidy**: A subsidy whose beneficiaries are limited by identity verification, quotas or income thresholds. Malaysia has implemented this since 2024 through a MyKad-based system, in contrast to universal fuel subsidies, aiming to reconcile fiscal sustainability with social protection.

**E-invoice**: A system in which businesses submit transaction records to the tax authority in a standardised format in real or near-real time. Malaysia has phased it in since 2024; the level of the exemption threshold directly determines compliance costs for micro and small merchants.

**Cost-of-living pressure**: The squeeze on real purchasing power from prices, taxes and subsidy withdrawal. Over the past two years it has been one of the most consequential domestic political issues in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand alike.

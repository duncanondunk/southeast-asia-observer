# 20亿林吉特的主权AI赌注：马来西亚在美中之间走钢丝

马来西亚正认真评估用华为昇腾910C芯片，支撑一项预算约20亿林吉特（约4.94亿美元，约35.6亿元人民币）的主权人工智能计划。若最终落地，这将是已知首个外国政府正式弃用美国芯片、改选中国加速器的案例。在华盛顿的出口管制与北京的技术出海之间，吉隆坡正把"数据主权"写成一道外交难题。

## 20亿林吉特的主权AI赌注

这项计划由马来西亚通信及多媒体委员会主导，要求政府、军事与情报数据留在境内。经公开招标，国有电信运营商马电讯（TM）被选为主要运营方——它早与华为合作云与AI基础设施，但现役AI算力用的是英伟达。评估的是华为主力型号昇腾910C，而非产能有限的昇腾950。若采购获批，被替换的将是现役英伟达芯片。

## 昇腾910C：便宜六成的筹码

华为的卖点直白：昇腾910C成本约比同档英伟达H100低60%至70%。对预算有限又想握算力的政府，这是实在的诱惑。但便宜有代价——910C性能仍大幅落后于英伟达，且华为制造受美国多年限制。马来西亚要的，是以更低价格换"不被卡脖子"的叙事，代价则是与美国出口管制体系正面相撞。

## 云端法案：转向的真正推手

真正把安华推向华为的，是美国《云端法案》（Cloud Act）的域外效力。按该法，即便数据存在马来西亚境内，只要经美国科技公司之手，美国法院仍可强制交出。安华今年4月对大学生直言："我认为该法律不合理，但如何与美国总统特朗普争论？"对视数据主权为红线的人，华为虽非无风险，至少不受这部美国国内法辖制。

## 美国为何紧张

美国商务部工业与安全局（BIS）2025年5月13日的全球指引，已点名昇腾910B/C/D"推定未获授权"，全球范围使用、转售、维修或融资都可能被认定违规。国务院虽未点名，但称"不可信供应商"易被威权政体操弄、危及关键基础设施与个人数据。对正全力把AI硬件推向全球南方的华盛顿而言，马来西亚是重要市场，更是规矩的样板。

## 安华的"不当技术消费者"执念

安华多次强调，马来西亚不想只做海外技术的消费者。这套话语，指向一种后殖民式的技术自主焦虑：数据由谁掌握、标准由谁定，关乎国家尊严。但现实是，马来西亚过去三年靠英伟达体系与美方出口管制"可靠伙伴"身份，吸引了微软、谷歌、亚马逊等数百亿美元数据中心投资。自主与依赖，此刻正面拉扯。

## 马电讯与平行供应链

马电讯被选中，既因公开招标，也因其既有华为合作基础。若华为方案落地，马来西亚将出现一条与美国体系平行的供应链：政府敏感负载用中国硅，商业负载仍用英伟达。这正是吉隆坡想要的平衡——可一旦华盛顿认定这越过了红线，平行可能被迫"二选一"。特朗普团队据报正筹备向南半球打包AI软硬件、并可能提供政府融资，意在留住伙伴。

## 一场访华盛顿前的试探

马来西亚国家安全委员会官员计划9月下旬访华盛顿，与美方讨论此事。这既是解释，也是试探：吉隆坡想摸清华府容忍的边界。而2025年5月那次类似的3000片华为计划，曾在宣布后被悄然撤回、官方材料删去华为字样——说明上一次，华盛顿的红线赢了。这一次，决心似乎更大。

## 南亚选择，还是全球南方的样板

若马来西亚真的走通，它递给全球南方的是一个诱人又危险的模板：以主权为名，在美中技术栈之间保留选择权。对越南、泰国、印尼等同样纠结于成本、主权与同盟的国家，吉隆坡的成败将被反复研读。但模板的另一面，是选边压力随中美博弈水涨船高——小国的水果，往往最怕大风。

## 延伸阅读

**主权人工智能（Sovereign AI）**：指一国建设自主可控的AI算力与数据基础设施，使政府敏感数据留在本土、降低对外国技术栈的依赖。

**《云端法案》（Cloud Act）**：美国2018年生效的法律，允许美法院强制美科技公司交出境外存储的数据，引发多国对数据主权的担忧。

**昇腾（Ascend）**：华为自研AI芯片与算力系列，910C为当前主力型号，950系列更先进但产能受限，是美方出口管制的重点对象。

# A RM2 Billion Sovereign-AI Wager: Malaysia Walks the Tightrope Between Washington and Beijing

Malaysia is seriously evaluating Huawei's Ascend 910C chips to power a sovereign artificial-intelligence programme budgeted at about RM2 billion (roughly US$494 million). If it goes ahead, it would be the first known case of a foreign government formally choosing Chinese accelerators over American ones. Between Washington's export controls and Beijing's tech push, Kuala Lumpur is turning "data sovereignty" into a diplomatic puzzle.

## A RM2 billion sovereign-AI wager

The plan, led by the Malaysian Communications and Multimedia Commission, requires government, military and intelligence data to stay onshore. After a public tender, state-linked Telekom Malaysia (TM) was picked as the key operator — a firm already partnered with Huawei on cloud and AI infrastructure, though its current AI compute runs on Nvidia. The chip under review is Huawei's marquee Ascend 910C, not the supply-limited 950 line. If procured, it would replace incumbent Nvidia silicon.

## Ascend 910C: a chip 60–70% cheaper

Huawei's pitch is blunt: the 910C costs about 60–70% less than a comparable Nvidia H100. For a budget-conscious government wanting its own compute, that is a real lure. But cheapness carries a cost — the 910C still trails Nvidia badly in performance, and Huawei's manufacturing has been throttled by years of US restrictions. What Malaysia buys is a "no-strangulation" narrative at a lower price, paid for by a head-on clash with the US export-control system.

## The Cloud Act: the real push factor

What truly nudges Anwar toward Huawei is the extraterritorial reach of America's Cloud Act. Under it, even data stored in Malaysia can be compelled by US courts if a US tech firm is in the chain. Anwar told university students in April: "I do not consider that law reasonable, but how does one argue with President Trump?" For those who treat data sovereignty as a red line, Huawei carries risks too — but at least it sits outside that American statute.

## Why Washington is nervous

The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security, in a May 13, 2025 global guidance, named Ascend 910B/C/D as "presumed unauthorized" — use, resale, repair or financing anywhere could be deemed a violation. The State Department, without naming names, warned that "untrusted suppliers" can be manipulated by authoritarian regimes and threaten critical infrastructure and personal data. For an administration pushing AI hardware across the Global South, Malaysia is both a key market and a test of the rules.

## Anwar's itch to not be a mere consumer

Anwar has repeatedly said Malaysia does not want to be merely a consumer of foreign technology. The framing speaks to a post-colonial anxiety about technological autonomy: who holds the data and sets the standard is a question of dignity. The catch is that over the past three years Malaysia courted tens of billions in US hyperscaler data-centre investment precisely on the strength of its Nvidia-based stack and its "reliable partner" status in the US control regime. Autonomy and dependence are now in open tension.

## Telekom Malaysia and the parallel supply chain

TM was chosen through a public tender and for its existing Huawei ties. If the Huawei plan lands, Malaysia would run a supply chain parallel to the American one: sensitive government loads on Chinese silicon, commercial loads still on Nvidia. That is the balance Kuala Lumpur wants — yet the moment Washington deems it past a red line, "parallel" may be forced into "pick one." Trump's team is reportedly preparing AI hardware-and-software packages for the Global South, possibly with US financing, to keep partners close.

## A probe before the Washington trip

Officials from Malaysia's National Security Council plan to visit Washington in late September to discuss the matter. It is both explanation and probe: Kuala Lumpur wants to gauge how far Washington will tolerate. A similar 3,000-chip Huawei plan in May 2025 was quietly withdrawn after announcement, with Huawei references scrubbed from official material — last time, Washington's red line won. This time, the resolve looks firmer.

## A Malaysian choice, or a Global South template

If Malaysia pulls it off, it hands the Global South a tempting yet dangerous template: preserving choice between US and Chinese tech stacks under the banner of sovereignty. For Vietnam, Thailand and Indonesia — all torn between cost, sovereignty and alliance — Kuala Lumpur's success or failure will be studied closely. The flip side is that great-power pressure rises with the US–China contest; small fruit fears strong wind most.

## Further reading

**Sovereign AI**: A country's drive to build autonomous, controllable AI compute and data infrastructure so that sensitive government data stays domestic and dependence on foreign stacks shrinks.

**Cloud Act**: A US law in force since 2018 letting American courts compel US tech firms to hand over data stored abroad, fuelling data-sovereignty concerns worldwide.

**Ascend**: Huawei's in-house AI chip and compute series; the 910C is the current workhorse, the 950 line more advanced but supply-limited, and a focal point of US export control.

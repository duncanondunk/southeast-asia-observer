# 不在一领域牺牲另一领域：泰国否认以军事合作换取对美贸易让步

泰国政府8月12日驳斥外媒报道，否认计划在美泰贸易谈判中，把包括"金眼镜蛇"（Cobra Gold）军演在内的防务关系作为谈判筹码。发言人拉查达（Ratchada）强调，贸易谈判聚焦泰国经济利益，"不会在一个领域牺牲国家利益，以换取另一个领域的利益"。一场关于关税与军演的拉扯，折射出中等国家在霸权博弈中的平衡术。

## 彭博社的报道与泰方的否认

彭博社日前引述泰国匿名官员报道称，泰国政府计划在本月晚些时候与美国恢复贸易谈判时，把"高额关税可能迫使泰国缩减军事合作"纳入反建议之中。对此，泰国政府发言人拉查达于8月12日作出驳斥，明确表示防务合作不会与商业谈判挂钩。她说，泰美贸易谈判将聚焦泰国的经济利益，并强调政府"不会在一个领域牺牲国家利益，以换取另一个领域的利益"。

## "金眼镜蛇"：东南亚规模最大的多边军演

"金眼镜蛇"（Cobra Gold）是东南亚地区规模最大的多边军事演习，自1982年起每年在泰国举行，由泰国皇家武装部队与美国印太司令部联合主办，日本、韩国、新加坡、马来西亚、印度尼西亚等亚洲多国均派员参与。明年的联演定于3月举行，参演各方预计下月在夏威夷召开中期规划会议。这场演习早已超越双边范畴，成为地区安全架构的一块基石。

## 关税阴影下的同盟关系

美国早前以不公平贸易行为为由，对包括泰国在内的数十个国家的大部分出口商品加征12.5%关税（2026年7月24日）。泰国去年对美贸易顺差达514亿美元（约合3690亿元人民币）；为满足华盛顿的要求，曼谷此前已提出多项贸易让步。泰国副首相兼商务部长素帕吉（Suphakit）上月曾表示，鉴于两国之间的战略联盟、友好条约，以及泰国在美国的投资，泰国理应获得更优惠的待遇。

## 中等国家的平衡术

泰国是美国在东南亚仅有的两个条约盟友之一，另一个是菲律宾（美菲条约盟友）。此次风波显示出一个中等国家如何抵御安全议题与贸易胁迫的捆绑，同时维系自身自主权。把"金眼镜蛇"与关税谈判切割，既保护了军演制度的连续性，也向华盛顿与北京同时传递出审慎而独立的信号——在倚仗美国安全庇护、又无法脱离中国经济引力之间，曼谷选择的，是一条不愿被单方面绑定的中间道路。

# Not Sacrificing One Sphere for Another: Thailand Denies Using Military Ties as US Trade Leverage

On August 12 the Thai government rebutted foreign-media reports that it planned to use defence relations — including the Cobra Gold (金眼镜蛇) exercise — as bargaining chips in trade talks with the United States. Spokesperson Ratchada stressed that the negotiations focus on Thailand's economic interests and that Bangkok "will not sacrifice national interest in one area to gain in another." The tussle over tariffs and military drills reveals the balancing act of a middle power amid great-power competition.

## Bloomberg's report and Bangkok's denial

Bloomberg, citing an unnamed Thai official, reported that when trade talks resume with Washington later this month, Thailand intended to fold a line — that high tariffs "may force Thailand to scale back military cooperation" — into its counter-proposal. Ratchada, on August 12, rejected this, saying defence cooperation will not be linked to commercial negotiations and that Thailand "will not sacrifice national interest in one area to gain in another."

## Cobra Gold: Southeast Asia's largest multilateral drill

Cobra Gold is the region's biggest annual multilateral exercise, held in Thailand since 1982 and co-hosted by the Royal Thai Armed Forces and the US Indo-Pacific Command, with participants including Japan, South Korea, Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia. Next year's iteration is scheduled for March, with a mid-planning meeting in Hawaii next month. The drill long ago outgrew the bilateral frame to become a cornerstone of the regional security architecture.

## The alliance under a tariff cloud

The US has imposed a 12.5% tariff on most exports from dozens of countries including Thailand (July 24, 2026). Thailand ran a US$51.4 billion trade surplus with the US last year and has offered concessions to meet Washington's demands. Deputy Prime Minister and Commerce Minister Suphakit said last month that, given the strategic alliance, the friendship treaty and Thai investment in the US, Thailand deserves better treatment.

## The balancing art of a middle power

Thailand is one of only two US treaty allies in Southeast Asia, the other being the Philippines. The episode shows a middle power resisting the merger of security and trade coercion while preserving autonomy. By keeping Cobra Gold separate from tariff talks, Bangkok protects the exercise's institutional continuity and sends a measured, independent signal to both Washington and Beijing — navigating US security cover against China's economic gravity, it chooses a middle path reluctant to be bound by any single party.

## Further reading
**Cobra Gold**: The largest annual multilateral military exercise in Southeast Asia, held in Thailand since 1982 and co-hosted by the Royal Thai Armed Forces and US Indo-Pacific Command.
**Treaty ally**: A partner bound by a formal defence treaty to mutual military assistance; Thailand and the Philippines are America's only two treaty allies in Southeast Asia.
**Trade surplus**: The excess of a country's exports over imports; Thailand's persistent surplus with the US is both structural leverage and a source of pressure in negotiations.

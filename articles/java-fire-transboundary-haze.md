# 极端干旱下的东爪哇大火与跨境烟霾：气候、治理与东盟的老难题

2026年8月初，印尼（Indonesia）东爪哇省的布罗莫·滕格尔·塞梅鲁国家公园燃起大火。截至8月7日，过火面积超过120公顷——约180个足球场大小，以火山沙海闻名的景区全面关闭。更值得警觉的不是一场山火本身，而是它再次牵动的那条旧链条：极端干旱 → 林火 → 跨境烟霾 → 邻国空气告急。

## 厄尔尼诺下的"旱季拉长"

起火并非偶然。印尼气象部门早前警告，受厄尔尼诺等现象影响，今年旱季比往年更强、更长，山火风险更高。截至7月底，全国逾七成地区已进入旱季，东爪哇部分地方连续数十日无雨。林业部统计，仅上半年全国被烧毁土地就超过10万7000公顷，是2023年全年林火损毁面积的两倍以上。

国家公园管理局与消防部门说，火点多集中在陡峭崖谷，地面人员难以深入，灭火高度依赖空中作业与风向。当局怀疑人为活动不慎在极端干燥下迅速失控——这是印尼林火反复出现的典型剧本。

## 烟霾又越界了

随着火势蔓延，跨境烟霾再现。加里曼丹林火浓烟随风飘入马来西亚（Malaysia），砂拉越多地空气质量急剧恶化：截至8月8日下午，全马七个地区空气达"不健康"水平，砂拉越独占六席，诗巫（Sibu）最高达172点；吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）傍晚空污指数也升至119点。东盟（ASEAN）特别气象中心数据称，加里曼丹侦测到643个火点，苏门答腊17个，马来西亚25个。

这套景象对东南亚（Southeast Asia）人并不陌生。烟霾是区域性的"公地悲剧"：火在一国境内烧，代价由多国分担。马来西亚已启动露天焚烧与烟霾应对行动，加强巡逻执法，但跨境属性决定了单国努力的天花板。

## 东盟机制的"软约束"困局

东盟跨境烟霾问题由来已久，也有《跨境烟霾污染协议》等机制，却长期陷入"签了难执行"的尴尬。根源在于：火点成因复杂（开垦、种植园清地、气候），归责困难；成员国对主权与经济发展的考量优先于区域环境协同；而厄尔尼诺这类气候外力，正在把本已脆弱的治理进一步推高负荷。

从东爪哇到加里曼丹，一场山火提醒我们：在气候变暖与极端天气常态化的背景下，烟霾已不再是"偶发灾害"，而是区域治理的周期性压力测试。真正能降低代价的，不是灾后相互指摘，而是更早的预警、更透明的火点共享，以及把"空气无国界"真正写进各国的国内执行。

## 延伸阅读

**厄尔尼诺（El Niño）**：赤道太平洋海水异常增温的气候现象，常导致印尼等东南亚国家旱季延长、降雨锐减，显著推高林火与烟霾风险。

**布罗莫·滕格尔·塞梅鲁国家公园（Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park）**：东爪哇著名火山国家公园，以布罗莫火山与火山沙海闻名，2026年8月因山火全面关闭。

**东盟特别气象中心（ASMC）**：东盟下属气象机构，负责监测区域热点与火点，是跨境烟霾早期预警的关键信息源。

# Java's Park Fire and the Cross-Border Haze: Climate, Governance, and ASEAN's Recurring Headache

In early August 2026, a fire broke out in East Java's Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park. By 7 August, it had burned more than 120 hectares — about 180 football fields — and the scenic park famed for its volcanic sand sea was fully closed. What deserves attention is not a single wildfire, but the old chain it sets in motion again: extreme drought → forest fire → transboundary haze → a neighbour's air in distress.

## A "longer dry season" under El Niño

The ignition was no accident. Indonesia's meteorological agency had warned that, driven by El Niño and other factors, this year's dry season would be stronger and longer, with higher fire risk. By end-July, over 70 per cent of the country had entered the dry season, and parts of East Java had seen no rain for dozens of days. The Forestry Ministry reported that more than 107,000 hectares were burned nationwide in the first half of the year alone — more than double the total destroyed by forest fires in all of 2023.

Park authorities and firefighters say hotspots cluster on steep cliffs and valleys unreachable on foot, leaving suppression heavily dependent on aerial operations and wind. They suspect careless human activity, fanned by extreme dryness, spun out of control — the familiar script of Indonesia's recurring fires.

## The haze crosses the border again

As the fire spread, transboundary haze returned. Smoke from Kalimantan drifted into Malaysia on the wind; air quality in Sarawak deteriorated sharply. By the afternoon of 8 August, seven Malaysian districts had reached "unhealthy" levels, six of them in Sarawak, with Sibu highest at 172; Kuala Lumpur's index also climbed to 119 in the evening. Data from the ASEAN Special Meteorological Centre put detected hotspots at 643 in Kalimantan, 17 in Sumatra, and 25 in Malaysia.

The scene is familiar to Southeast Asians. Haze is a regional "tragedy of the commons": the fire burns in one country, the cost is shared by many. Malaysia has activated measures against open burning and haze, stepping up patrols and enforcement — but the cross-border nature sets a ceiling on any single country's effort.

## ASEAN's "soft-law" bind

The regional haze problem is long-standing and equipped with instruments like the ASEAN Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution, yet it has long been stuck in "signed but unenforced." The roots: complex ignition causes (clearing, plantation land prep, climate), hard attribution, member states' prioritising sovereignty and development over environmental coordination, and climate forcings like El Niño loading already-fragile governance.

From East Java to Kalimantan, one wildfire reminds us that, as warming and extreme weather normalize, haze is no longer an "occasional disaster" but a periodic stress test of regional governance. What genuinely lowers the cost is not post-disaster blame, but earlier warning, more transparent hotspot sharing, and writing "air knows no borders" into each country's domestic enforcement.

## Further reading
**El Niño** — A climate phenomenon of abnormal warming in the equatorial Pacific that often lengthens Southeast Asia's dry season and slashes rainfall, sharply raising the risk of forest fires and haze.

**Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park** — East Java's famed volcanic park, known for Mount Bromo and its sand sea, fully closed in August 2026 after the wildfire.

**ASEAN Special Meteorological Centre (ASMC)** — An ASEAN body monitoring regional hotspots and fires, the key source for early warning on transboundary haze.

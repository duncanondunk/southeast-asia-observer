# 泰国央行连续三次维持1%：AI热潮下的"两速经济"与降息弹药见底

## 决议：利率连续三次维持1%

8月26日（周三），泰国央行货币政策委员会一致同意，将一日期回购利率维持在1%，这是自2022年9月以来的最低水平，也是今年以来连续第三次按兵不动。回望轨迹：为应对经济放缓，央行从2024年10月到今年2月连续六次降息，累计下调150个基点，此后便一直原地踏步。在外界普遍预期之中，这次"不动作"更像一次有意为之的停顿，而非犹豫。

## 为何按兵不动：AI 热潮与放缓的现实

委员会的理由听来乐观：当前利率有助于稳住物价、支撑增长与金融稳定，推动复苏。央行称，随着出口与投资受惠于人工智能（AI）热潮，加上政府刺激措施，经济正逐步改善，而温和的通胀压力也给了维持利率的空间。泰国投资促进委员会数据显示，第二季度获优惠政策的项目实际投资额同比增长31%，达到2550亿泰铢（约99亿新元，约合540亿元人民币）；其中与AI相关的产业占总额超过一半，吸金逾1270亿泰铢（约合270亿元人民币）。舞台灯打得很亮。

## 数字：增长落后于区域同伴

但聚光灯之外，景象黯淡。委员会承认，泰国经济增长仍疲弱且不均衡；由于高度依赖进口，AI与科技带来的出口、投资红利对整体经济的渗透仍然有限。第二季度泰国经济仅增长1.9%，在东南亚六大主要经济体中增速垫底——同一时段，越南增长8.4%，菲律宾也达2.3%。这一落差勾勒出一幅"两速经济"：前台是光鲜的AI与数据中心投资，底座却是疲软的内需与传统部门。

## 前路风险：干旱、通胀与外部依赖

隐忧还在后头。受厄尔尼诺带来的干旱气候影响，整体通胀率或于明年一季度走高。更关键的是，利率已贴近下限，进一步降息的弹药所剩无几，刺激经济的重担正更多压向财政政策。而泰国经济高度依赖能源进口、又受美联储政策轨迹牵动，外部脆弱性并未因AI的喧嚣而消失。当潮水退去，真正决定增长成色的，仍是那套老问题。

## 延伸阅读

**一日期回购利率（One-day repurchase rate）**：泰国央行用以引导市场短期利率的政策基准利率，相当于其他央行的利率枢纽。

**两速经济（Two-speed economy）**：少数高增长部门（如AI、数据中心）与广大传统部门增长脱节的结构性分化现象。

**货币政策委员会（Monetary Policy Committee, MPC）**：各国央行内负责利率决策的委员会；泰国以一致表决维持利率，凸显共识下的谨慎。

# Thailand Holds Rate at 1% for Third Time: A "Two-Speed Economy" and Dwindling Ammunition

## The decision: rate held at 1% for the third time

On Wednesday 26 August, the Bank of Thailand's Monetary Policy Committee unanimously agreed to keep the one-day repurchase rate at 1% — the lowest level since September 2022, and the third consecutive hold this year. The trajectory tells the story: to counter a slowing economy, the bank cut six times in a row from October 2024 to February 2025, by 150 basis points in total, and has stood still since. Widely expected, this "no move" reads less like hesitation than a deliberate pause.

## Why hold: the AI boom versus a slowing real economy

The committee's reasoning sounds upbeat: the current rate helps stabilise prices, supports growth and financial stability, and aids the recovery. The central bank says exports and investment are benefiting from the artificial-intelligence (AI) boom, alongside government stimulus, and that mild inflation leaves room to hold. Data from the Board of Investment show actual investment under promoted schemes rose 31% year on year in the second quarter to 255 billion baht (about S$9.9 billion, ~US$7.6 billion); AI-related industries took more than half, attracting over 127 billion baht (~US$3.8 billion). The stage lights are bright.

## The numbers: growth lagging regional peers

But offstage, the picture is dim. The committee concedes growth remains weak and uneven; because Thailand is highly import-dependent, the dividends from AI and tech exports and investment have limited reach into the broader economy. In the second quarter the economy grew just 1.9% — the slowest among the six major Southeast Asian economies. Over the same period Vietnam expanded 8.4% and the Philippines 2.3%. The gap sketches a "two-speed economy": a glitzy front end of AI and data-centre investment, a soft base of domestic demand and traditional sectors.

## Risks ahead: drought, inflation and external dependence

Worries lie ahead. Drought from El Niño may push overall inflation higher in the first quarter of next year. More fundamentally, with rates already near the floor, ammunition for further cuts is nearly spent, and the burden of stimulus is shifting onto fiscal policy. Thailand's heavy reliance on energy imports, coupled with sensitivity to the US Federal Reserve's path, means external vulnerability has not vanished amid the AI noise. When the tide recedes, what really decides the quality of growth is still the old list of problems.

## Further reading

**One-day repurchase rate**: The Bank of Thailand's policy benchmark that steers short-term market rates — the equivalent of a policy rate anchor in other central banks.

**Two-speed economy**: A structural split in which a few high-growth sectors (AI, data centres) outrun the broadly sluggish traditional sectors.

**Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**: The rate-setting body within a central bank; Thailand's unanimous hold underscores caution wrapped in consensus.

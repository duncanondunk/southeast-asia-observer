# 南宁而非深圳：马来西亚（Malaysia）华商青年赴广西学什么人工智能

据中国侨网（China Qiao Wang）报道，20名马来西亚青年企业家组成的人工智能考察团11月23日晚抵达南宁，开展4天考察，主题是人工智能应用、短视频运营与数字化转型。通稿离不开「赋能」「链接」，但值得追问：马来西亚并不缺人工智能课程，这些人为何偏来南宁？带队者说得很直白——不是看热闹，是寻方法、结伙伴、促合作。问题是，广西的方法能否在马来西亚跑起来？

## 避开人工智能名城的四天路线

据中国侨网转引中国新闻网报道，考察团由马来西亚桂商总会、马来西亚广西总会青年团组织。秘书长梁芳譿说，马来西亚市面人工智能课程繁多，此行「不是来看热闹，而是来寻方法、结伙伴、促合作」，希望把广西经验带回去。这句话点出了缺口：概念过剩，落地稀缺。

考察团将按「人工智能赋能、企业链接、实地考察」的主题，在南宁学院体验课程和实操训练，走访中国—东盟国家人工智能应用合作中心（简称「南A中心」）以及润建、迈越科技、山海星辰等企业，了解算力设施、场景应用与数字产业集群，并探访平陆运河。

坐标值得注意。报道中国人工智能，习惯路径是深圳的硬件、杭州的模型、北京（Beijing）的研究；谈东南亚（Southeast Asia），则常写新加坡。南宁不在任何一张「首页地图」上。恰恰是这种边缘位置构成卖点：这里兜售的不是前沿突破，而是面向东盟（ASEAN）、成本可控、中小企业照着做的方案。对中型企业主来说，能复制往往比最先进更有价值。

## 广西的卖点：基础设施加应用场景

南宁市委常委、统战部部长、副市长韦江介绍，南宁正依托南A中心加速构建面向东盟的人工智能国际合作高地。拆开来看：中心意味着算力与公共平台，企业参访意味着现成案例，平陆运河意味着货物流动与跨境通道。三者合在一起，才是「经验」二字的完整含义。

深圳卖迭代速度，南宁卖可及性：更低的试错成本、更邻近的东盟市场、更容易对接的侨乡网络。但场景可以参观，能力无法打包带走。马来西亚企业面对不同的语言、监管与成本结构；润建或迈越科技的系统长在广西的产业政策与数据环境里，照搬回吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）或槟城，很可能只剩一个漂亮界面。

据中国侨网报道，韦江期待青年企业家把优质技术、资源与产业需求引进南宁，也把南宁的机遇与诚意带回马来西亚。这是对称表述，但「引进」难得多：技术能否迁移，取决于本地团队、数据合规与后续服务，而非签约仪式的灯光。

## 乡情如何降低交易成本

南宁市新侨联谊会常务副会长赖中文说得更接地气：人工智能是两地青年最容易找到共同语言、碰撞合作火花的领域。该会今年3月成立以来聚焦东盟与青年，正推进人工智能短剧赋能出海，期待在应用、数字内容、青年互访上找到切入点。

韦江则把乡情称为两地合作「最深厚的底色」，表示将持续深化与马来西亚青年社团、新侨群体在人文、产业、人才上的往来。两段话指向一条路径：侨务正从捐资、助学、联谊转向技术对接与产业撮合。过去看侨汇与投资额，如今要看企业有没有用上工具、青年有没有找到岗位。

乡情确实降低信任成本。由桂商总会与青年团带队，比陌生机构的冷启动更容易获得回应；方言、籍贯与家族关系在跨境商业中并非装饰。但乡情不是合同。知识产权归属、数据跨境规则、收益分配、退出机制迟早要摆上桌面。侨务网络擅长开门，却不擅长替企业守门；把「自己人」的默契误当成治理结构，就会在第一笔真金白银的合作里交学费。

## 「方法」叙事里的四个陷阱

第一是演示式样板。考察能看到部署好的系统与流利的解说，却看不到部署中的坑：数据从哪来、旧系统怎么接、员工为什么不用。对中小企业而言，后者才是门槛。第二是平台依赖。短视频运营写进议程，意味着流量分配权仍在境外平台手里；今天学会的投放技巧，明天可能随算法更新失效。能带走的应是内容生产能力，而非某个平台的操作手册。

第三是本地化。马来西亚多种语言与族群共存，华语、马来语、英语乃至方言可能在同一客户群里交替出现；在南宁验证过的中文应用，到了当地要重做语言层、支付层与客服层。第四是评价指标。若半年后拿得出手的仍只是合影、备忘录与新闻稿，这次考察就只完成了一次情感消费。该追问的是：多少家企业把试点跑过3个月？多少本地员工能独立维护系统？有没有可核算的收入？

这并非苛责。考察团成员说的是「寻方法」，这个词本身就承认缺口存在；承认缺口，是合作开始的前提。需要警惕的是把「看到」当成「学会」，把「合作诚意」当成「合作结果」。

## 第四天之后应该留下什么

若要让这4天产生复利，至少三件事要落实。其一，课程变成共同开发的产品：由南宁机构与马来西亚商会、职业院校设计双语教材，按行业拆案例。其二，参观变成试点：挑出有真实需求的中小企业，明确周期、预算与验收标准，让工具先在订单、库存或客服里跑通。其三，乡情变成机制：建立可回访的联系人名册、争议解决模板与数据合规清单，让下一次合作不必从零建立信任。

平陆运河出现在行程里耐人寻味：它代表硬件联通，人工智能代表软件应用，侨乡网络代表人与信任。三者交汇处，才是广西对东盟真正的差异化卖点。若4天结束后只带回照片和一句「收获很大」，这条路线与走马观花的考察无异。若半年后有人能说出：我们和南宁伙伴共同改造了一条生产线，或做成了一门面向东盟客户的课程，「寻方法、结伙伴、促合作」才算落地。

侨务工作的新指标，从来不是来了多少人，而是走了以后还有多少人愿意再来。

## 延伸阅读

**侨乡外交（Qiaoxiang diplomacy）**：以祖籍地联系为纽带开展民间外交与经贸撮合，传统产出是捐资、侨汇与投资；新形态是把侨社网络用于技术转移、人才培养与产业对接。

**中国—东盟国家人工智能应用合作中心（China–ASEAN National AI Application Cooperation Centre）**：南宁依托面向东盟的区位优势建设的合作平台，定位为算力、场景应用与数字产业的对接枢纽，也是本次考察的重点参访对象。

**人工智能短剧（AI short drama）**：用生成式人工智能降低剧本、分镜与译制成本的短视频形态；南宁市新侨联谊会将其列为「赋能千行百业出海」的抓手，效果取决于内容质量与平台分发能力，而非技术本身。

# Learning AI in Nanning: what Malaysian Chinese entrepreneurs are really after

According to China Qiao Wang, a 20-member study tour of young Malaysian entrepreneurs arrived in Nanning on the evening of 23 November for a four-day programme on artificial intelligence (AI) applications, short-video operations and digital transformation. The press language is familiar: empowerment, enterprise links, field study. The revealing line comes from the organiser. "There is no shortage of AI courses in Malaysia," said Liang Fanghui, secretary-general of the Malaysian Guangxi Chamber of Commerce. "We are not here to watch the spectacle. We are here to find methods, partners and cooperation." That is a more interesting proposition than another exchange of memoranda. What can Guangxi actually teach a Malaysian small businessman about AI — and why look south-west to Nanning rather than to Shenzhen or Singapore?

## A four-day route that skips the usual AI capitals

China Qiao Wang, citing China News Service, reports that the tour was organised by the Malaysian Guangxi Chamber of Commerce and the youth league of the Malaysian Guangxi Association. Liang's remark diagnoses the gap honestly: concepts are plentiful in Malaysia, implementation is not. Under the theme of "AI empowerment, enterprise links and field study", the group will take classes and hands-on training at Nanning University, visit the China–ASEAN National AI Application Cooperation Centre and companies including Runjian, Maiyue Technology and Shanhai Xingchen, examine local computing facilities and digital-industry clusters, and inspect the Pinglu Canal.

The coordinates are worth noting. International coverage of Chinese AI follows a familiar map: Shenzhen for hardware, Hangzhou for models, Beijing for research; Singapore for regional headquarters in Southeast Asia. Nanning appears on none of these front pages. That marginality is precisely the pitch. What is on sale here is not frontier research but a manageable, ASEAN-facing package that a small or medium enterprise might plausibly copy. For a Malaysian owner-operator, a replicable solution is often more valuable than the most advanced one.

## Guangxi's pitch: infrastructure plus scenarios

Wei Jiang, a member of Nanning's municipal Party standing committee, head of the United Front Work Department and vice mayor, says Nanning is building itself into a high ground for international AI cooperation with ASEAN, drawing on the China–ASEAN National AI Application Cooperation Centre. The sentence is worth dismantling. The centre stands for computing capacity and shared platforms; company visits supply ready-made cases; the Pinglu Canal supplies the movement of goods and a cross-border corridor. Only together do these amount to "experience".

This is a different comparative advantage from Shenzhen's. Shenzhen sells speed of iteration; Nanning sells accessibility — lower costs of trial and error, proximity to ASEAN markets, and an easier interface with diaspora networks. The caveat follows from the same logic. Scenarios can be toured, but capabilities cannot be packaged. Malaysian firms face different consumer languages, regulatory thresholds and labour costs. The systems on display in Nanning grew inside Guangxi's industrial policy, customer base and data environment. Transplant the interface to Kuala Lumpur or Penang and it may survive only as a handsome screen.

China Qiao Wang reports that Wei expects the visitors to bring technology, resources and industrial demand into Nanning, and to carry Nanning's opportunities and goodwill back to Malaysia. It is a symmetrical formulation, but importing is the harder half. Whether technology travels depends on local teams, data compliance and after-sales service — not on the lighting of a signing ceremony.

## Hometown sentiment as a transaction cost

Lai Zhongwen, executive vice-chairman of the Nanning New Overseas Chinese Friendship Association, puts it more plainly: AI is the field where young people on both sides most easily find a common language and spark cooperation. Founded in March 2026, the association focuses on ASEAN themes and youth work, and is now promoting AI-generated short dramas as a way to help industries go overseas. It hopes to find entry points in AI applications, digital content and youth exchanges.

Wei calls hometown sentiment the deepest shared foundation and promises continued engagement with Malaysian youth associations and new diaspora groups across culture, industry and talent. Read together, the two remarks describe a clear trajectory: overseas Chinese work is moving from donations, scholarships and social visits towards technology matching and industrial brokerage. The old scorecard counted remittances and investment; the new one should ask whether firms actually use the tools and whether young people actually find jobs.

Sentiment does lower transaction costs. A project introduced by a Guangxi chamber of commerce and a youth league starts with warmer receptions than a cold approach by strangers; dialect, native-place ties and family connections have never been decorative in cross-border business. But sentiment is not a contract. Ownership of intellectual property, cross-border data rules, revenue sharing and exit clauses will all reach the table sooner or later. Diaspora networks are good at opening doors and bad at guarding them. Anyone who mistakes "one of our own" for a governance structure will pay tuition fees in the first serious deal.

## Four traps in the "methods" narrative

First, demonstration theatre. A visit shows a deployed system and a fluent guide; it does not show the mess of deployment — where the data came from, how legacy systems were connected, why employees refused to use the tool. For small firms, that mess is the real threshold. Second, platform dependence. Putting short-video operations on the agenda means distribution power remains with platforms headquartered elsewhere; this year's tactics may be obsolete after the next algorithm update. What can be carried home is content capability, not a manual for one app.

Third, localisation. Malaysia is a market of multiple languages and communities, where Mandarin, Malay, English and dialects may alternate within a single customer conversation. An application validated in a Chinese-language setting in Nanning needs a new language layer, payment layer and customer-service layer. Fourth, metrics. If six months later the only outputs are group photographs, memoranda of understanding and press releases, the tour will have consumed sentiment without producing cooperation. The harder questions are these: how many Malaysian firms ran a pilot for more than three months, how many local staff can maintain the system unaided, and was there any revenue that can be audited?

This is not an indictment of the event. The participants themselves spoke of searching for methods, a phrase that concedes the gap — and admitting a gap is where cooperation begins. The risk is confusing seeing with learning, and goodwill with results.

## What the fourth day should produce

Three things would give the four days a second life. One, turn the classes into jointly developed products: bilingual curricula designed with Malaysian chambers and vocational colleges, organised by industry rather than by the order of demonstrations. Two, turn site visits into pilots: pick Malaysian small and medium enterprises with real demand, set explicit timelines, budgets and acceptance criteria, and make an AI tool work first in one firm's orders, inventory or customer service. Three, turn sentiment into institutions: a revisitable contact roster, dispute-resolution templates and data-compliance checklists, so that the next deal does not have to rebuild trust from zero.

The Pinglu Canal on the itinerary is a telling detail. It stands for physical connectivity; AI stands for applications; the diaspora network stands for people and trust. Their intersection is Guangxi's genuine differentiated offer to ASEAN. If the delegation comes home with photographs and a line about having "learned a lot", the route is indistinguishable from countless other token tours. If, six months on, someone can point to a production line jointly redesigned with a Nanning partner or a course built for ASEAN clients, then "methods, partners, cooperation" will have meant something.

The new metric of overseas Chinese work is not how many people arrive, but how many choose to come back.

## Further reading

**Qiaoxiang diplomacy (侨乡外交)**: the practice of using ancestral-home ties for people-to-people diplomacy and trade brokerage. Traditionally measured in donations, remittances and investment, it increasingly channels diaspora networks into technology transfer, talent development and industrial matching.

**China–ASEAN National AI Application Cooperation Centre (南A中心)**: a Nanning-based platform built on the city's ASEAN-facing position, designed as a hub for computing capacity, applied scenarios and digital industry. It was a key stop on the study tour.

**AI short drama (AI短剧)**: a short-video form that uses generative AI to cut the costs of scripting, storyboarding and dubbing. The Nanning New Overseas Chinese Friendship Association treats it as a tool for helping industries go overseas; its value depends on content quality and distribution, not the technology alone.

# 一程三地：柬埔寨、老挝与越南能把一句口号做成次区域品牌吗？

2026年8月26日至27日，柬埔寨、老挝与越南的旅游部长在胡志明市举行首届"柬埔寨—老挝—越南（CLV）旅游部长会议"，并通过以"一次旅程，三个目的地"为主题的三年联合行动计划。据中新社发自金边的报道，柬埔寨旅游部大臣胡哈、老挝新闻文化旅游部部长苏莎婉与越南文化体育与旅游部部长林氏芳清，同意建立常设的 CLV 旅游部长合作机制，并推进六大优先方向：共同产品开发与互联互通、联合营销与品牌建设、能力建设与行业经验分享、可持续与社区型旅游发展，以及强化机制建设与国际合作。第二届会议定于2027年在老挝举行。

## 头条：一个次区域旅游品牌诞生

说白了，这与其说是一个突破，不如说是一次重启。覆盖三国边境省份的柬埔寨—老挝—越南（CLV）发展三角区自2004年起便作为经济合作框架存在，但旅游很少居于其核心。真正新鲜的是，三国明确决定把自己的国家打包成一个单一的旅游命题：邀请游客在一条线路内跨境穿行，而不是把金边、万象、河内（或者吴哥、琅勃拉邦、下龙湾）当成彼此独立的旅程。三位部长实际上是在尝试，为湄公河次区域打造一个对标亚洲其他地方早已用滥的"一次游多国"公式的产品。

这为什么重要？因为在这里，品牌化绝非表面文章。对于东盟几个最不发达的旅游经济体而言，合并营销意味着更低的获客成本，以及对那些原本只会默认选择泰国或单游越南的长途游客更强的吸引力。

## 2026—2028 行动计划到底包含什么

六大优先方向值得认真对待——并非因为它们新奇（任何区域旅游备忘录里都找得到），而是因为它们异常具体。"共同产品开发与互联互通"指向真正的瓶颈：CLV 三角区的魅力恰恰在于其边境地带的特征，然而跨境公路、签证与旅行社的一体化至今仍参差不齐。"联合营销与品牌建设"是把口号变成可操作的动作。"能力建设"则承认，尤其是老挝与柬埔寨，缺乏可持续增长所需的服务业人才厚度。

真正的考验在于资金与协调。部长级机制比临时峰会进了一步，但计划没有公布公共预算，没有秘书处，也没有约束性的落地指标。与早年的 CLV 框架一样，宣言与执行之间的那道鸿沟，正是好意寿终正寝的地方。

## 为何是现在？旅游作为 CLV 三角的发展杠杆

时机的选择绝非偶然。柬埔寨与老挝是该地区最贫困的经济体之二；两者都渴求非资源型、能吸纳就业的增长。旅游业是最显而易见的候选：它劳动密集、地理上分散，而且与制衣厂不同，它能把收入撒进那些几乎没分享到全球化红利的边境农村省份。对越南而言，CLV 框架是深化其西部腹地与他国融合、同时彰显自己作为次区域支柱经济体的方式。

这里还有后疫情的逻辑。区域旅行复苏很不均衡；三国押注的是，捆绑式产品能分到单一目的地分不到的那一块"中国加亚洲内部"的旅游热潮。

## 中国因素：最大客源市场与互联互通红利

任何对 CLV 旅游的分析，少了中国的维度都不完整。中国是同为三国最大出境客源市场的国家，而中国游客恰恰是那种以打包、多站式行程出游的群体——这正是"一次旅程，三个目的地"这套说辞所瞄准的人群。2021年开通的老中铁路，已把老挝旅游从一门小众生意变成主流产品；正是同一种互联互通的逻辑，让 CLV 环线从幻想变得可行。

但中国红利也是一把双刃剑。对单一客源市场的重度依赖是一种集中风险：北京方面的一项政策变动，或一起安全事件，便能让客流量一夜蒸发。明智的做法是，用中国的互联互通为这条环线播下种子，再去分散客源结构。

## 治理盲区：互联互通是一把双刃剑

这是新闻稿刻意回避的尴尬部分。正是那同一种为旅游输送动力的边境互联互通，也在便利非法流动。近年来，CLV 三角区那些偏远的省份，已与诈骗园区和跨境犯罪联系在一起，受害者既包括当地人，也包括外国游客。更顺畅的边境、更自由的流动，对游客和贩子同样是一份礼物。

行动计划里的"可持续与社区型旅游"表述用对了词，却对让边境地带变得安全所需的安保架构只字未提。在三位部长把游客安全与打击犯罪当作旅游落地的议题——而非一份独立的执法卷宗——之前，这个品牌将一直背着一处隐秘的声誉风险。

## 软实力与部长级宣言的局限

在某一层面上，"一次旅程，三个目的地"是廉价的软实力：一个把三国框定为伙伴而非竞争对手的共同叙事。越南部长把它正确地表述为一个面向"包容、可持续"区域发展的长期品牌。这份心意值得欢迎。

局限在于落地。这片区域里，早已堆满了产出了精美 logo 却没带来几个游客的次区域倡议。部长级机制有帮助，但若没有资金到位的秘书处、共享的数据与可衡量的指标，这就可能沦为湄公河合作承诺那一长串清单里的又一行。

CLV 三角区确有真金白银般的资产——吴哥、老挝高原、下龙湾与越南中部海岸。把它们打包成一次旅程，是经得起算账的经济学。三国政府能否把这句口号变成大巴、签证与实实在在的订单，才是唯一要紧的问题。

## 数字目标与落地压力：3500 万游客的算术题

口号要经得起算术。根据越南文化体育与旅游部披露的数据，2025 年三国合计接待国际游客 3130 万人次，同比增长 10.2%；其中三国间互访的区域内游客约 320 万人次，仅占总量的 10%。旅游总收入估计约 456 亿美元。2026 年上半年，三国已接待国际游客 1660 万人次（含区域内 160 万人次），而会议提出的目标，是全年 CLV 区域国际游客达到 3500 万人次，并力争实现入境游客的双位数年增长。

这组数字的关键不在总数，而在结构。区域内跨境流动目前只占一成，说明"一程三地"的买账者还很少。行动计划真正的考验，是把原本只游越南或单访柬埔寨的客人，转化为在同一条线路上跨境穿行的多国游客——否则 3500 万的目标就只是把三国各自盘子相加，而非创造新增量。把口号变成增量，比把口号写进联合声明难得多。

## 基建与便利化：边境环线公路与"东盟签证"的想象

互联互通不只是愿景，也有看得见的工地。柬埔寨在 CLV 发展三角区的边境环线公路网已完工 98.87%，总长 264.40 公里，自 2024 年 12 月借助"边境基础设施建设基金"动工；其中蒙多基里省已达 100%，拉达那基里省为 97.63%。这条环路把三国边境省份真正缝在一起，是"一程三地"得以成行的物理前提。

在节点城市，胡志明市人民委员会主席阮文得明确把本市定位为战略连接中枢：通过航空、陆路与国家级旅游线路，把 CLV 行程与国际重点客源市场接驳，而不仅是一个停留点。更远的想象来自签证——泰国正推动覆盖柬埔寨、老挝、越南等六国的"类申根"东盟联合签证。若落地，它将是"一次旅程"口号最大的一笔便利化红利；障碍则在于各国移民标准不一，协调难度不亚于修路。

# One Journey, Three Destinations: Can Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam Turn a Slogan into a Subregional Brand?

On 26–27 August 2026, tourism ministers from Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam met in Ho Chi Minh City for the inaugural Cambodia–Laos–Vietnam (CLV) Tourism Ministers' Meeting and adopted a three-year joint action plan built around the tagline "one journey, three destinations." According to Xinhua's report from Phnom Penh, Cambodia's Minister of Tourism Huot Hak, Laos' Minister of Information, Culture and Tourism Suksanha Sithivong, and Vietnam's Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism Nguyen Thi Thanh Phuong agreed to establish a standing CLV tourism ministerial mechanism and advance six priority areas: joint product development and connectivity, co-marketing and brand building, capacity building and experience sharing, sustainable and community-based tourism, and institutional strengthening and international cooperation. The second meeting is scheduled for Laos in 2027.

## The headline: a subregional brand is born

Read plainly, this is less a breakthrough than a relaunch. The CLV Development Triangle Area — covering the border provinces of the three countries — has existed since 2004 as a framework for economic cooperation, yet tourism rarely sat at its centre. What is new is the explicit decision to package the three countries as a single travel proposition: visitors are invited to cross borders within one itinerary rather than treat Phnom Penh, Vientiane and Hanoi (or Angkor, Luang Prabang and Ha Long) as separate trips. The ministers are, in effect, trying to manufacture a Mekong counterpart to the well-worn "visit one, see many" formulas used elsewhere in Asia.

Why does this matter? Because branding is not cosmetic here. For three of ASEAN's least-developed tourism economies, pooled marketing means lower customer-acquisition costs and a stronger claim on the attention of long-haul travellers who might otherwise default to Thailand or Vietnam alone.

## What the 2026–2028 plan actually contains

The six priority areas are worth taking seriously, not because they are novel — most appear in any regional tourism memorandum — but because they are unusually concrete. "Joint product development and connectivity" points to the real bottleneck: the CLV triangle's appeal is precisely its borderland character, yet cross-border road, visa and tour-operator integration remains patchy. "Co-marketing and brand building" is the slogan made operational. "Capacity building" acknowledges that Laos and Cambodia, in particular, lack the hospitality workforce depth that sustained growth demands.

The test will be funding and coordination. A ministerial mechanism is a step up from ad-hoc summits, but the plan sets no public budget, no secretariat, and no binding delivery targets. As with earlier CLV frameworks, the gap between declaration and implementation is the place where good intentions go to die.

## Why now? Tourism as the development lever for the CLV triangle

The timing is no accident. Cambodia and Laos are among the region's poorest economies; both are hungry for non-resource, labour-absorbing growth. Tourism is the obvious candidate — it is labour-intensive, geographically diffuse, and, unlike garment factories, spreads income into rural border provinces that have seen little of the spoils of globalisation. For Vietnam, the CLV frame is a way to deepen its western hinterland's integration with its neighbours while burnishing its role as the subregion's anchor economy.

There is also a post-pandemic logic. Regional travel has rebounded unevenly; the three countries are betting that a bundled offer captures a share of the China-plus-intra-Asia boom that single destinations cannot.

## The China factor: the largest source market and the connectivity dividend

No analysis of CLV tourism is complete without China. China is the single largest outbound market for all three countries, and Chinese tourists are exactly the segment that travels in packaged, multi-stop itineraries — the demographic the "one journey, three destinations" pitch is built for. The Laos–China railway, opened in 2021, has already turned Lao tourism from a niche into a mainstream product; the same connectivity logic is what makes a CLV circuit plausible rather than fantasy.

But the China dividend cuts two ways. Heavy reliance on one source market is a concentration risk: a single policy shift in Beijing, or a safety scare, can hollow out arrivals overnight. The wise play is to use Chinese connectivity to seed the circuit, then diversify the customer base.

## The governance blind spot: connectivity cuts both ways

Here is the uncomfortable part the press releases omit. The same borderland connectivity that powers tourism also eases illicit flows. The CLV triangle's remote provinces have, in recent years, become associated with scam compounds and cross-border crime that prey on both locals and foreign visitors. Smoother borders and freer movement are a gift to tourists and traffickers alike.

The action plan's "sustainable and community-based tourism" plank is the right language, but it is silent on the security architecture needed to make the borderlands safe. Until the three ministers treat tourist safety and crime suppression as a tourism-delivery item — not a separate law-enforcement file — the brand will carry a quiet reputational risk.

## Soft power and the limits of ministerial declarations

At one level, "one journey, three destinations" is soft power on the cheap: a shared narrative that frames the three countries as partners rather than competitors. Vietnam's minister framed it correctly as a long-term brand for "inclusive, sustainable" regional development. The sentiment is welcome.

The limit is delivery. The region is littered with subregional initiatives that produced elegant logos and few arrivals. A ministerial mechanism helps, but without a funded secretariat, shared data, and measurable targets, this risks becoming another line in the long inventory of Mekong cooperation pledges.

The CLV triangle has real assets — Angkor, the Lao highlands, Ha Long and the central Vietnamese coast. Packaging them as one journey is sensible economics. Whether the three governments can convert the slogan into buses, visas and bookings is the only question that will matter.

## The arithmetic of targets: the 35-million visitor maths

Slogans must survive arithmetic. According to data from Vietnam's Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, the three countries welcomed 31.3 million international visitors in 2025, up 10.2% year on year; intra-regional travellers moving between the three countries reached 3.2 million, just 10% of the total. Estimated tourism revenue was about US$45.6 billion. In the first half of 2026 alone the three countries received 16.6 million international arrivals, including 1.6 million intra-regional. The meeting's stated aim is 35 million international visitors to the CLV region for the year, alongside double-digit annual growth in arrivals.

The point of these figures is not the headline total but the structure. Cross-border intra-regional flow is still only one-tenth of the total, which means few travellers have yet bought into "one journey, three destinations." The real test of the action plan is whether it can convert visitors who would otherwise tour Vietnam alone or Cambodia alone into multi-country itineraries — otherwise the 35-million target is merely the sum of three national totals rather than new demand. Turning a slogan into incremental arrivals is far harder than writing it into a joint statement.

## Infrastructure and facilitation: border ring roads and the "ASEAN visa" idea

Connectivity is not only vision; there are visible construction sites. Cambodia's border-ring road network in the CLV Development Triangle has reached 98.87% completion, spanning 264.40 kilometres, since work began in December 2024 under the Border Infrastructure Construction Fund; Mondulkiri province is at 100% and Ratanakkiri at 97.63%. This ring literally stitches the three countries' border provinces together and is the physical precondition for "one journey, three destinations" to be practicable.

At the nodal-city level, Ho Chi Minh City's chairman, Nguyen Van Duoc, explicitly positioned the city as a strategic connectivity hub: linking CLV routes to key international source markets via air, land and national tourism corridors, rather than serving merely as a stop. The more distant imagination concerns visas — Thailand is pushing a Schengen-style ASEAN joint visa covering six countries including Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam. If realised, it would be the single largest facilitation dividend for the "one journey" tagline; the obstacle is that member states' migration standards differ, making coordination no easier than building roads.

Still, the triangle's weakest link is governance, not geography. Cross-border tourism thrives on trust between provincial authorities, yet the CLV borderlands have long doubled as zones of informal trade, migration and, at times, trafficking. A joint brand promising safe, seamless travel must be backed by shared standards on visitor protection and crisis response; without them, the "one journey" risks becoming a slogan that outruns the institutions meant to deliver it.

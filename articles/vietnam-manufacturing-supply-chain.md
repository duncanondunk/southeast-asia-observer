# 越南制造：全球供应链重构下的新机遇

> 在中美博弈与产业转移的浪潮中，越南如何承接下一波制造红利。

2026 年的越南，正站在一个微妙的历史节点上。当全球制造业从"中国 +1"走向"中国 +N"的多元布局，这个拥有 9800 万人口、平均年龄仅 32 岁的国家，又一次被推到了聚光灯下。

## 从代工到自主：产业升级的三道坎

越南制造的崛起并非偶然。自 1986 年"革新开放"以来，河内用整整四十年时间，把一个农业国改造为全球电子与纺织的重要节点。三星近一半的手机在这里下线，耐克、阿迪达斯的鞋类产能也大量迁至此地。

但**真正的产业升级，从来不只是产能的堆砌**。越南要跨过三道坎：

1. **供应链纵深不足**——零部件与核心器件仍高度依赖进口，本地配套率长期徘徊在 25% 上下。
2. **技术工人的断层**——尽管劳动力年轻，但高端工程师与研发人才稀缺，本土 R&D 投入占 GDP 不足 0.5%。
3. **基础设施的瓶颈**——港口吞吐、电力供应与物流效率，在订单激增时屡屡成为掣肘。

## 外资结构：繁荣背后的隐忧

越南吸引外资的能力毋庸置疑。2025 年实际到位 FDI 超过 230 亿美元，韩国、日本、新加坡稳居前三。然而细究结构，外资**重镇集中在电子与纺织两个低附加值环节**，本土企业参与度有限。

> 这意味着，越南看似漂亮的出口数字，相当一部分是"过路经济"——产值记在越南，利润与核心技术却留在海外。

| 指标 | 数值 | 区域排名 |
|------|------|----------|
| 制造业占 GDP | 约 24% | 东盟第二 |
| 出口总额 | 超 4000 亿美元 | 东盟第一 |
| 本地配套率 | ~25% | 待提升 |
| R&D / GDP | ~0.5% | 偏低 |

## 地缘红利：能吃多久

越南的地缘价值，在中美战略竞争中被反复放大。华盛顿将河内视为印太供应链重组的关键支点，《全面战略伙伴关系》的升级、对越南转运晶片的有限放行，都是这一逻辑的延伸。

但红利从来是双刃剑。当越南在地缘上更靠近美国时，与最大贸易伙伴中国的关系也变得更敏感。**走钢丝的艺术，在于让别人相信你不会倒向任何一边**——这正是河内外交的核心。

## 结语

越南的故事，是一个发展中国家如何在巨头夹缝中谋取空间的故事。它的机遇真实存在，但瓶颈同样真实。下一波制造红利能否兑现，取决于越南能否在十年窗口期内，把"代工"真正升级为"自主"。

至于地缘红利——能吃多久，从来不由越南说了算。

# Vietnam Manufacturing: New Opportunities Amid Global Supply-Chain Realignment

> Amid US–China rivalry and industrial relocation, how Vietnam captures the next wave of manufacturing dividends.

Vietnam in 2026 stands at a delicate historical juncture. As global manufacturing shifts from "China + 1" toward a diversified "China + N," this nation of 98 million with a median age of just 32 finds itself under the spotlight once again.

## From OEM to Autonomy: Three Hurdles of Industrial Upgrading

Vietnam's manufacturing rise was no accident. Since the *Đổi Mới* reforms of 1986, Hanoi spent four decades transforming an agrarian country into a key node of global electronics and textiles. Nearly half of Samsung's smartphones roll off lines here, and Nike and Adidas have shifted substantial shoe capacity to its factories.

But genuine industrial upgrading is never just a matter of stacking capacity. Vietnam must clear three hurdles:

1. **Shallow supply-chain depth** — components and core devices remain heavily import-dependent, with local content long stuck around 25%.
2. **A skills gap** — the workforce is young, but high-end engineers and R&D talent are scarce; domestic R&D spending sits under 0.5% of GDP.
3. **Infrastructure bottlenecks** — port throughput, power supply, and logistics efficiency repeatedly become constraints when orders surge.

## Foreign Capital: The Unease Beneath the Boom

Vietnam's ability to attract foreign capital is beyond doubt. Realized FDI topped $23 billion in 2025, with South Korea, Japan, and Singapore firmly in the top three. Yet the structure reveals a concentration in electronics and textiles — two low-value-added segments — with limited participation by domestic firms.

> A sizable share of Vietnam's impressive export figures is "transit economy": output is booked in Vietnam, but profits and core technology stay offshore.

| Indicator | Value | Regional Rank |
|------|------|----------|
| Manufacturing / GDP | ~24% | 2nd in ASEAN |
| Total exports | >$400bn | 1st in ASEAN |
| Local content | ~25% | Needs lift |
| R&D / GDP | ~0.5% | Low |

## Geopolitical Dividend: How Long Can It Last

Vietnam's geopolitical value has been amplified repeatedly amid US–China strategic competition. Washington sees Hanoi as a pivot in Indo-Pacific supply-chain realignment; the upgrade to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership and the limited waiver on chip transshipment are extensions of this logic.

But dividends are always a double-edged sword. As Vietnam tilts closer to the United States geopolitically, its relationship with its largest trade partner, China, grows more sensitive. The art of walking the tightrope is making others believe you will not lean definitively to either side — and that is the core of Hanoi's diplomacy.

## Conclusion

Vietnam's story is one of a developing country carving out space between giants. Its opportunities are real, but so are its bottlenecks. Whether the next wave of manufacturing dividends pays off depends on whether Vietnam, within a ten-year window, can genuinely upgrade from "OEM" to "autonomy."

As for the geopolitical dividend — how long it lasts was never Vietnam's call to make.

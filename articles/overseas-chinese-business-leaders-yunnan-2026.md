# 150名华商侨领聚云南：边疆省份如何重写"侨力"剧本

9月21日，"2026华商聚云南"活动在昆明开幕。来自30多个国家和地区的约150名华商、侨领参会，议题聚焦中国（云南）自由贸易试验区建设、文旅产业与绿电资源优势。这并非一场寻常的招商会——它是云南这个中国第五大侨乡省，借助华侨华人网络与自身区位，把"边疆"重新定义为"中国—东盟门户"的一次系统尝试。

## 数字：三十国、百五十人、七成外资

数字本身就说明问题。据主办方披露，云南省投资促进局会前已梳理出207个省级重点招商引资项目供与会华商对接；目前1200余家在滇华商企业覆盖农业、工业、文旅等领域，贡献了云南超过七成的外资。自2023年"华商聚云南"活动连续举办四年来，已累计吸引近50个国家和地区约600名华商侨领走进云南，推动落地投资项目超130个，带动全省利用外资超30亿美元（约216亿元人民币）。

一个不靠海、不靠中心的西部边境省份，凭什么在吸收外资上跑赢许多沿海地市？答案并不玄妙：华侨华人资本对"家乡"网络的信任，往往高于对正式制度的信任。对东南亚华商而言，云南既是地缘上的近邻，又是文化上的"老乡"——这种双重亲近，让云南在争抢侨资时占了沿海省份难以复制的先天便宜。

## 绿电：云南的新比较优势

真正值得玩味的是议程的重心转移。过去这类活动多围绕传统制造与房地产，而今年绿电资源被单列为核心卖点。云南水电资源丰富，长期存在丰水期弃水与"绿电外送"的供需错配；在"双碳"目标下，低廉且清洁的电力正成为新的比较优势。

泰国正大集团资深副董事长谢炳在会上算了一笔账：正大在云南深耕30余年，累计投资22.9亿元人民币、设18家企业；下一步将以云南花卉产业为切入点，探索绿色农业与绿色能源融合发展的路径。把"一朵花"与"一度绿电"绑定，本质上是云南把生态禀赋包装成低碳叙事，向侨资推销"绿色云南"的区域品牌。这比单纯比拼税收优惠高明——它卖的是未来合规成本更低的"绿色溢价"。

## 文旅：把"寻根"做成生意

文旅是另一个被反复强调的引擎。云南拥有独特的多民族文化与跨境地缘景观，对华侨华人而言兼具"乡愁"与"异域"双重吸引力。活动期间，与会华商侨领将赴红河哈尼族彝族自治州、临沧市开展商务考察，深入了解沿边开放与资源富集优势。

这里藏着一条清晰的逻辑：华侨华人既是文旅投资的出资方，又是最先到来的游客与口碑传播者。从"下南洋"到"回云南"，离散群体的"寻根"情感被转化为可变现的消费与资本流。侨务工作从过去的"联谊慰问"，升级为把情感资产直接对接地方产业链的招商工具——这不可谓不精明。

## 门户：从边疆到节点

更宏大的背景是云南战略定位的位移。随着中老铁路通车、区域全面经济伙伴关系协定（RCEP）落地，以及平陆运河等西部陆海新通道项目推进，云南正从改革开放的末梢，变为面向南亚东南亚的辐射中心。

在这一叙事里，华侨华人网络是不可或缺的"软基础设施"。缅甸、泰国、老挝、越南的华商社团，往往比官方渠道更先感知邻国市场的温度。当云南想把自贸试验区、跨境物流与绿色制造串成链条时，散落周边的侨资与侨团，恰好补上了正式制度对接前的"信任缺口"。换言之，侨力不是锦上添花，而是云南撬动东盟的杠杆。

## 冷思考：落地率与依赖度

当然，掌声之外需要一点清醒。其一，207个项目只是"菜单"，真正落地多少要打问号。好在此前四年的转化率尚可——超130个落地项目、超30亿美元（约216亿元人民币）外资，说明"华商聚"并非只开花不结果。

其二，是对少数巨头的依赖。以正大集团为代表的东南亚华商财团，在滇投资体量大、链条长，既是压舱石，也可能让地方经济在议价中处于被动。当一省的开放叙事过度绑定个别家族企业时，风险的集中度也随之升高。

无论如何，150名华商侨领的云南之行，折射的是中国区域发展战略的一次微妙转向：当沿海趋于饱和，边疆正凭借侨力与区位，把自己写进中国—东盟互联互通的新剧本。

## 延伸阅读

**侨乡（Qiaoxiang）**：指历史上向外大规模移民、在海外形成庞大华侨华人族群的中国省份，云南为中国第五大侨乡省。

**绿电（Green Power）**：指风电、水电、光伏等零碳或低碳电力；云南凭丰沛水电成为西部绿电外送与本地消纳的核心省份。

**中国—东盟自由贸易区（China–ASEAN Free Trade Area）**：自贸区3.0版谈判持续推进，云南凭沿边区位与中老铁路成为对接东盟的前沿门户。

# When 150 overseas Chinese business leaders converge on Yunnan

On 21 September, the "2026 Huashang Ju Yunnan" (Overseas Chinese Business Leaders Gather in Yunnan) event opened in Kunming. Around 150 business leaders and community figures from more than 30 countries and regions attended, with the agenda centred on the China (Yunnan) Free Trade Zone, the cultural-tourism industry and Yunnan's green-power resources. This was no ordinary investment fair. It is a systematic attempt by Yunnan — China's fifth-largest qiaoxiang, or emigrant-home, province — to use its overseas Chinese networks and geographic position to redefine a "borderland" as a "China–ASEAN gateway."

## The numbers: thirty countries, 150 people, 70% of FDI

The figures speak for themselves. According to the organisers, the Yunnan Investment Promotion Bureau had pre-screened 207 provincial-level key investment projects for the attendees. More than 1,200 overseas Chinese enterprises now operate in Yunnan across agriculture, industry and tourism, and they account for over 70% of the province's foreign investment. Since the annual gathering began in 2023, it has drawn some 600 business leaders from nearly 50 countries and regions, helped land more than 130 investment projects, and leveraged over US$3 billion (~¥21.6 billion) in foreign capital.

How does a landlocked western border province out-compete many coastal cities in attracting FDI? The answer is not mysterious: overseas Chinese capital tends to trust "home" networks more than formal institutions. For Southeast Asian Chinese businesspeople, Yunnan is both a geographic neighbour and a cultural "laoxiang" — fellow townsfolk. That double affinity gives Yunnan an innate edge that coastal provinces cannot easily replicate.

## Green power: Yunnan's new comparative advantage

What is genuinely interesting is the shift in the agenda's centre of gravity. Past editions leaned on traditional manufacturing and property; this year green power was singled out as a headline offering. Yunnan is rich in hydropower but has long suffered from seasonal curtailment and a mismatch between abundant supply and outbound transmission. Under China's dual-carbon goals, cheap and clean electricity is becoming a new comparative advantage.

Xie Bing, senior vice-chairman of Thailand's Charoen Pokphand (CP) Group, made the calculus explicit at the meeting: CP has operated in Yunnan for over three decades, investing 2.29 billion yuan (about US$320 million) across 18 enterprises. Its next step, he said, is to use Yunnan's flower industry as an entry point to explore the integration of green agriculture and green energy. Binding "a single flower" to "a unit of green electricity" is, in essence, Yunnan repackaging its ecological endowment into a low-carbon narrative and selling a "Green Yunnan" regional brand to diaspora capital. This is smarter than merely competing on tax breaks — it sells a "green premium" with lower future compliance costs.

## Cultural tourism: monetising "roots-seeking"

Cultural tourism is the other repeatedly emphasised engine. Yunnan's distinct multi-ethnic culture and cross-border landscapes hold a dual appeal for overseas Chinese — both "nostalgia" and "exoticism." During the event, attendees would travel to Honghe Hani and Yi Autonomous Prefecture and Lincang city for business inspections of the province's border-opening features and resource advantages.

Here lies a clear logic: overseas Chinese are both the financiers of cultural-tourism investment and its first tourists and word-of-mouth carriers. From "going down to Nanyang" to "returning to Yunnan," the diaspora's "roots-seeking" sentiment is being converted into realisable consumption and capital flows. Diaspora affairs work has upgraded from past "networking and consolation" into a recruitment tool that directly links emotional assets to local industry chains. One has to admit — it is shrewd.

## Gateway: from periphery to node

The larger backdrop is Yunnan's shifting strategic positioning. With the China–Laos railway in operation, the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) implemented, and western land-sea corridors such as the Pinglu Canal advancing, Yunnan is moving from the tail end of reform and opening-up to a radiation centre facing South and Southeast Asia.

In this narrative, overseas Chinese networks are indispensable "soft infrastructure." Chinese business associations in Myanmar, Thailand, Laos and Vietnam often sense neighbouring markets' temperatures earlier than official channels. When Yunnan seeks to string together its free-trade zone, cross-border logistics and green manufacturing, the diaspora capital and associations scattered around the region fill the "trust gap" that precedes formal institutional alignment. In other words, diaspora leverage is not a garnish — it is the lever Yunnan uses to pry open ASEAN.

## A reality check: conversion and dependency

Of course, a little sobriety is in order alongside the applause. First, the 207 projects are only a "menu"; how many actually land remains an open question. To its credit, the conversion rate over the past four years has been decent — more than 130 landed projects and over US$3 billion (~¥21.6 billion) in foreign capital suggest "Huashang Ju Yunnan" is not all bloom and no fruit.

Second is the reliance on a few giants. Southeast Asian Chinese conglomerates represented by CP Group invest at huge scale and across long chains in Yunnan; they are both an anchor and a potential source of passive bargaining positions for local government. When a province's opening-up narrative becomes over-bound to individual family firms, the concentration of risk rises accordingly.

Whatever the caveats, the 150 business leaders' Yunnan trip reflects a subtle turn in China's regional-development strategy: as the coast saturates, the borderlands are using diaspora capital and geographic position to write themselves into the new China–ASEAN connectivity script.

## Further reading

**Qiaoxiang (Emigrant-Home Province):** Historically, Chinese provinces that sent large numbers of emigrants abroad and developed sizeable overseas Chinese communities; Yunnan is China's fifth-largest qiaoxiang province.

**Green Power:** Zero- or low-carbon electricity from wind, hydro and solar; Yunnan's abundant hydropower makes it a core province for both outbound green-power transmission and local consumption.

**China–ASEAN Free Trade Area:** Negotiations on the upgraded FTA 3.0 continue; Yunnan's border location and the China–Laos railway position it as a front-line gateway to ASEAN.

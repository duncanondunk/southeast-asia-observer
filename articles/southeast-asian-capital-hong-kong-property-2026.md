# 东南亚资本"抄底"香港：新加坡领衔，成为商业地产最大非本地买家

据第一太平戴维斯（Savills）的数据，今年截至目前，香港成交额超过5000万港元的商业地产交易中，东南亚投资者以33.7亿港元（约30.7亿元人民币）登顶非本地买家榜首——这是十年来头一遭。更值得注意的是，这批资本以新加坡为枢纽，背后站着华人家族办公室、上市企业与住宿营运商。当中资与西方基金因资产负债表承压而退场，东南亚资本正趁着价格腰斩"捡便宜"。这究竟是价值洼地的理性回归，还是另一场周期性赌博？

## 数字：一个303.6亿港元的信号

把盘子摊开看：2026年年初至今，香港逾5000万港元的商业地产总成交约303.6亿港元（约276亿元人民币），其中香港本地资本占83.2%，达252.5亿港元（约230亿元人民币）。非本地买家中，东南亚以11%的份额、33.7亿港元（约30.7亿元人民币）位列第一；中国内地买家占5.8%，约17.5亿港元（约15.9亿元人民币）。

第一太平戴维斯香港投资部高级副总监杜宏（Nicholas To）点破关键："东南亚的份额是在一个小得多的非本地资金池里涨起来的——今年它约占所有非本地投资的三分之二。"换句话说，不是东南亚突然暴富，而是别人撤了，它成了"矮子里的高个"。

## 谁在买：新加坡资本挑大梁

带头的是新加坡。今年新加坡企业与投资者买下的资产清单相当扎眼：星展银行（香港）以约26.2亿港元（约23.8亿元人民币）拿下中环地标"中心大厦"（The Center）多个楼层的15.2万平方英尺（约1.41万平方米）空间；新加坡上市企业伟合控股（Wee Hur Holdings）以7.488亿港元（约6.8亿元人民币）收购大角咀写字楼"贝德福坊"（One Bedford Place），楼面达18.4万平方英尺（约1.71万平方米）。

此外，南区的赤柱豪宅群落"翡翠滩别墅"（Jadebeach Villa，30个单位）以近11.7亿港元（约10.6亿元人民币）卖给一家新加坡背景财团；新加坡元和控股（Centurion Holdings）还以3.64亿港元（约3.3亿元人民币）洽购北角"仁和楼"（Yan Wo Building）。

## 价格重置：腰斩之后的"捡便宜"

为什么是现在？答案在价格。据高力国际（Colliers）数据，香港写字楼资产价格较峰值已下跌多达五成。多年的下行把估值打到了新加坡资本难以抗拒的折扣位。"定价已经重置到足够低的水平，让没有历史香港敞口的买家，能够承接本地业主被迫放出的资产。"杜宏如此判断。

中环出现企稳绿芽：2026年上半年，中环甲级写字楼租金上涨7.3%，是15年来最强的半年表现；空置率从2025年底的10.9%降至8.8%。核心地段对优质资产的追逐，给了长线资金入场的理由。

## 三类买家：企业、家族与住宿营运商

细看这股资本，可分为三路：其一是企业自用型，如星展银行买楼办公；其二是私人家族资本，借助新加坡作为亚洲财富枢纽的地位跨境配置；其三是专业住宿营运商，瞄准"城市旅馆"（Hostels in the City）政策带来的改造机会——该政策允许业主在保留原有总楼面面积的前提下，把商业物业改作学生宿舍，免逐宗规划审批。

伟合与元和都在新加坡、澳大利亚、英国运营标准化学生公寓平台，它们的入场正是看中这项政策红利。换言之，东南亚资本买的不是"写字楼"，而是"可改造的活的资产"。

## 中资与西方的退场

反转的参照系是去年。2025年，中国内地投资者以160.6亿港元（约146亿元人民币）、近34%的占比稳坐非本地买家头把交椅；美国与加拿大买家以75.8亿港元（约69亿元人民币）占16%；东南亚仅排第三，占3.7%（17.4亿港元，约15.8亿元人民币）；欧洲买家占2%（9.4亿港元，约8.6亿元人民币）。

今年，西方投资者几乎集体缺席；中资也从34%骤降到5.8%。资产负债表承压、债务成本高企，让它们选择观望。零售、酒店营运商与机构房东发现，新加坡资本带来的不只是钱，还有高密度居住空间的管理经验。

## 不止是钱：华人资本圈的"近水楼台"

这股资本流动，绕不开一个事实：新加坡资本高度华人化。无论是家族办公室、上市企业还是私人财团，出资方多为华裔。香港与新加坡同属汉字文化圈与粤语纽带，法律、语言、人际网络高度相通。当其他外资因信息不对称而迟疑，新加坡资本却能"近水楼台先得月"。

这并非新现象。从上世纪的"南洋"资本北望，到今日家族办公室的跨境配置，华人离散群体的资本向来在区域内循环。只不过，这一次的舞台是估值被打到历史低位的香港核心资产。

## 风险：转型执行才是硬仗

别把"抄底"想得太轻松。商业物业改造有结构与消防等硬约束，回报更取决于非本地学生持续流入，而非企业租户扩张。一旦入学与签证政策收紧，住宿改造的逻辑就会松动。

更根本的是：香港写字楼供过于求、远程办公与企业整并的趋势并未逆转。中环的暖意能否外溢到二线地段，仍是大问号。买入容易、退出难，这才是资本真正要算的账。

## 区域镜鉴：亚洲资本的内部再配置

把这事放回更大的图景：过去十年，新加坡崛起为区域财富枢纽，家族办公室与私募资本云集；而香港楼市在多重冲击下估值重塑。当新加坡本地房价高企，资本外溢、跨域配置就成了自然选择。

东南亚资本成为香港最大非本地买家，本质上是亚洲内部一次资本再配置——不是外部热钱涌入，而是区域内"自己人"在周期低点接住了资产。对观察东南亚与华人离散经济的人而言，这比任何宏观叙事都更耐人寻味。

## 延伸阅读

**第一太平戴维斯（Savills）**：全球房地产服务与咨询机构，其香港投资部数据常被媒体引用，用以追踪商业地产的跨境资本流向。

**"城市旅馆"政策（Hostels in the City）**：香港特区政府允许业主在保留总楼面面积前提下，将商业物业改造为学生宿舍、免逐宗规划审批的住宅政策，是东南亚住宿营运商入场的政策支点。

**家族办公室（Family Office）**：为高净值家族管理财富的独立机构，新加坡近年借此吸引大量亚洲资本，是本轮香港地产资本的重要来源之一。

# Singapore-led Southeast Asian capital moves into Hong Kong's repriced property market

According to Savills, in Hong Kong commercial property deals above HK$50 million so far this year, Southeast Asian investors have topped the non-local buyer table with HK$3.37 billion (about US$431 million) — the first time in a decade. What makes it striking is that this capital is channelled through Singapore and backed by ethnic-Chinese family offices, listed firms and accommodation operators. As mainland and Western funds retreat under balance-sheet pressure, Southeast Asian capital is bargain-hunting amid a 50 per cent price wipeout. Is this a rational return to value, or another cyclical wager?

## The numbers: a HK$30.4 billion signal

Lay the pool open: from the start of 2026 to date, total Hong Kong commercial transactions above HK$50 million reached roughly HK$30.36 billion (about US$3.87 billion). Local Hong Kong capital accounted for 83.2 per cent, or HK$25.25 billion (around US$3.23 billion). Among non-local buyers, Southeast Asia led with an 11 per cent share and HK$3.37 billion (about US$431 million); mainland Chinese buyers took 5.8 per cent, around HK$1.75 billion (roughly US$224 million).

Nicholas To, senior associate director for investment at Savills Hong Kong, makes the key point: "Southeast Asia's share has risen against a much smaller non-local pool — it is roughly two-thirds of all non-local investment this year." In other words, Southeast Asia did not suddenly get rich; others left, and it became the tallest dwarf.

## Who is buying: Singapore-led capital takes the lead

Singapore is at the front. The assets Singaporean companies and investors picked up this year are eye-catching: DBS Bank (Hong Kong) paid about HK$2.62 billion (roughly US$335 million) for some 152,000 square feet across several floors of The Center, a Central landmark; Singapore-listed Wee Hur Holdings acquired One Bedford Place, an office tower in Tai Kok Tsui with 184,041 square feet, for HK$748.8 million (about US$96 million).

Beyond that, the 30-unit luxury residential enclave Jadebeach Villa in Chung Hom Kok, Southern district, was sold for nearly HK$1.17 billion (about US$150 million) to a Singapore-based group; Singapore's Centurion Holdings also provisionally agreed to buy Yan Wo Building in North Point for HK$364 million (around US$47 million).

## Price reset: bargain-hunting after a 50% wipeout

Why now? The answer is in the price. According to Colliers, Hong Kong office asset values have fallen as much as 50 per cent from their peak after years of correction, placing them at discounts Singapore capital finds hard to resist. "Pricing has reset far enough for buyers without legacy Hong Kong exposure to underwrite assets that local owners are having to release," To argues.

Central shows green shoots: in the first half of 2026, Grade A office rents in Central rose 7.3 per cent, the strongest half-year growth in 15 years; vacancy fell to 8.8 per cent from 10.9 per cent at the end of 2025. A flight to quality in prime locations gives long-term capital a reason to enter.

## Three buyer profiles: corporates, family capital, accommodation operators

This capital splits into three streams. The first is owner-occupier corporates, such as DBS buying space for its own use. The second is private family capital, deploying cross-border through Singapore's position as Asia's wealth hub. The third is specialised accommodation operators targeting the opportunities created by the "Hostels in the City" policy, which lets landlords convert commercial properties into student housing without individual planning approval while keeping existing gross floor area.

Both Wee Hur and Centurion run standardised student-accommodation platforms across Singapore, Australia and the UK; their entry rides precisely this policy dividend. In other words, Southeast Asian capital is not buying "offices" but "convertible, living assets".

## The retreat of mainland and Western capital

The reversal is best seen against last year. In 2025, mainland investors led non-local buyers with HK$16.06 billion (about US$2.06 billion) and nearly 34 per cent of the total; US and Canadian buyers spent HK$7.58 billion (around US$970 million), or 16 per cent; Southeast Asia ranked third at 3.7 per cent (HK$1.74 billion, about US$223 million); European buyers took 2 per cent (HK$940 million, roughly US$120 million).

This year, Western investors have been largely absent, and mainland capital collapsed from 34 per cent to 5.8 per cent. Balance-sheet stress and high debt costs pushed them to the sidelines. Retail, hospitality operators and institutional landlords have found that Singapore capital brings not just money but expertise in managing high-density living spaces.

## More than money: the ethnic-Chinese capital network's home advantage

This flow cannot ignore one fact: Singapore capital is heavily ethnic-Chinese. Whether family offices, listed firms or private groups, the money largely comes from ethnic Chinese. Hong Kong and Singapore share a Chinese-character cultural sphere and Cantonese ties, with highly compatible law, language and personal networks. While other foreign capital hesitates over information asymmetry, Singapore capital enjoys the "home advantage" of nearness.

This is not new. From last century's "Nanyang" capital looking north, to today's cross-border allocation by family offices, the capital of the Chinese diaspora has always circulated within the region. Only this time the stage is Hong Kong's core assets repriced to historic lows.

## The real test: execution of the conversion play

Do not imagine the "bottom-fishing" is easy. Commercial conversions carry hard constraints — structure and fire safety — and returns hinge on sustained non-local student inflow rather than corporate tenant expansion. Tighten admissions and visa policy and the conversion logic loosens.

More fundamentally, Hong Kong's office oversupply and the shift to remote work and corporate consolidation have not reversed. Whether Central's warmth spills into secondary districts remains a big question. Buying is easy, exiting is hard — that is the calculation capital must truly run.

## A regional reading: Asia's internal capital reallocation

Place this in the bigger picture: over the past decade Singapore rose as a regional wealth hub, crowding in family offices and private equity; Hong Kong's property, hit by multiple shocks, repriced. With Singapore's domestic prices high, capital spillover and cross-jurisdiction allocation became natural choices.

Southeast Asian capital becoming Hong Kong's largest non-local buyer is, at root, an internal reallocation of Asian capital — not external hot money flooding in, but "insiders" within the region catching assets at the cycle's low. For those watching Southeast Asia and the ethnic-Chinese diaspora economy, this is more thought-provoking than any macro narrative.

## Further reading

**Savills:** A global real-estate services and advisory firm whose Hong Kong investment data are frequently cited to track cross-border capital flows in commercial property.

**"Hostels in the City" policy:** A Hong Kong government residential policy allowing landlords to convert commercial properties into student housing without case-by-case planning approval while retaining gross floor area — the policy lever behind Southeast Asian accommodation operators' entry.

**Family office:** An independent vehicle managing wealth for high-net-worth families. Singapore has used it to attract large amounts of Asian capital in recent years and is a key source of this round of Hong Kong property capital.

# 越南用工荒打脸"中国挤压论"：产业链不是零和游戏

9月3日媒体报道，越南外资持续涌入、工厂遍地开花，连零经验的普工都供不应求，懂设备、懂工艺的中国技工更被当地企业高薪争抢。这一画面，直接反证了西方舆论场上所谓"中国挤压论"——即中国堵死了全球南方国家的工业化道路。现实数据是：近年来东盟保持中国第一大贸易伙伴，中间品贸易占双方贸易比重超过六成，"中国供中间品、东盟做组装、卖全球"的分工清晰而稳定。

## 用工荒里的"反向流动"

传统叙事里，是东南亚劳动力流向中国工厂。而今天的越南，出现的是中国技工被本地企业争抢。越南官方数据显示，2026年上半年GDP同比增长8.18%，工业与建筑业增速达9.86%，制造业成为高增长基石。工厂扩张快过人力供给，普工短缺、技工更缺，"中国经验"因此在当地成了稀缺资源。

## "中国挤压论"从何而来

所谓"中国挤压论"，是把全球南方国家的发展困境简单归咎于中国，声称中国以低价商品与产能"挤占"了他国的工业化空间。这一叙事在西方舆论场颇有市场，常被用作地缘政治话术，抹黑中国工业化进程、否定他国正当发展权。但它在实证层面站不住脚。

## 数据说了相反的话

贸易结构揭示真实逻辑。以东盟为例，近年来东盟始终是中国第一大贸易伙伴，双方贸易中中间品比重超过六成。这意味着中国出口的很大一部分是零部件、设备与资本品，而非与东盟直接竞争的成品。越南服装厂的面料来自浙江，柬埔寨的拉链由义乌供货——各国不必从零搭建产业链，对接中国供应链即可快速开工、接单、获利。

## 不是挤压，是赋能

在非洲同样如此。2025年中国对非出口2250亿美元，其中75%为中间品与资本品，涵盖机械设备、零部件、化工原料等工业化"弹药"。以摩洛哥为例，2025年从中国进口针织与化纤纺织材料12.3亿美元，却出口成衣39亿美元，成衣对外顺差33亿美元。中国提供原料、当地完成加工增值——这是赋能，不是挤压。

## 全球南方的工业化窗口

国际货币基金组织研究曾指出，中非贸易每年为非洲经济贡献1至2个百分点增长，非洲本地加工率从15%大幅提升至45%。对东南亚、非洲等全球南方而言，中国在上游提供的资本、技术与中间品，恰恰是其快速跨过工业化初始门槛的关键支撑。没有这道"上游台阶"，许多国家的建厂与就业要艰难得多。

## 谁在搞发展，谁在搞事情

中国主导的合作强调授人以渔、开放共享，与旧有全球化"低端收割、高端垄断"的模式不同。共建"一带一路"十多年间，全球建成30多所鲁班工坊，开设100多个专业，培养学生超16万人；柬埔寨西港特区引入200多家国际企业，创造3.5万个就业岗位。这些是被数据与就业印证的务实合作。

## 叙事之争背后的利益

"中国挤压论"的核心，是把复杂的全球发展危机甩锅给中国，意在维护既有霸权、干扰全球合作。哥伦比亚大学历史学家图兹曾直言，这一说法并非实证经济分析，而是一套服务政治动员的话术。当全球南方不再被动依附，旧模式的叙事自然会失灵。

## 留给观察者的结论

越南用工荒不是中国"抢了饭碗"的证据，恰恰相反，它说明中国产能与东南亚制造之间是相互成就的分工关系。把产业链当作零和游戏，既误读了中国，也低估了全球南方国家自身的活力。真正的命题不是"谁挤占了谁"，而是如何让分工更均衡、更具韧性、更能惠及普通劳动者。

## 延伸阅读

**中间品贸易（Intermediate Goods Trade）**：指零部件、半成品与资本品等用于再生产的商品贸易，是全局价值链分工的核心指标。

**鲁班工坊（Luban Workshop）**：中国境外职业教育合作项目，通过开设专业与培训当地技术人才，支撑合作国的产业能力建设。

**全球南方（Global South）**：泛指亚洲、非洲、拉丁美洲等发展中国家群体，近年成为重塑全球发展叙事的重要概念。

# Vietnam's Labour Shortage Refutes the "China Squeeze": Supply Chains Are Not a Zero-Sum Game

On 3 September, media reported that Vietnam, flooded with foreign investment and dotted with new factories, faces a shortage even of inexperienced general workers, while Chinese technicians who understand equipment and process are courted by local firms at high salaries. This picture directly refutes the Western narrative of a "China squeeze" — the claim that China has blocked the industrialisation path of the Global South. The real data: ASEAN has remained China's largest trading partner in recent years, with intermediate goods exceeding 60% of bilateral trade, and the division "China supplies intermediates, ASEAN assembles, the world buys" is clear and stable.

## Reverse flows in a labour shortage

The traditional story has Southeast Asian labour flowing to Chinese factories. Today's Vietnam shows Chinese technicians being courted by local firms. Vietnamese official data show first-half 2026 GDP up 8.18% year on year, with industry and construction growing 9.86% and manufacturing the bedrock of high growth. Factories expand faster than labour supply; general workers are scarce and technicians scarcer, so "Chinese experience" becomes a scarce resource locally.

## Where the "China squeeze" came from

The "China squeeze" thesis blames the Global South's development troubles on China, claiming cheap Chinese goods and capacity "crowd out" others' industrial space. The narrative sells in Western discourse, often as geopolitical rhetoric to smear China's industrialisation and deny others' legitimate development rights. Empirically, it does not hold.

## The data say the opposite

Trade structure reveals the real logic. Take ASEAN: it has been China's largest trading partner for years, with intermediate goods over 60% of bilateral trade. That means a large share of China's exports are parts, equipment and capital goods, not finished products competing directly with ASEAN. Fabric for Vietnamese garment factories comes from Zhejiang; zippers for Cambodia from Yiwu — countries need not build supply chains from scratch; plugging into China's lets them start, take orders and profit quickly.

## Not squeeze, but enablement

The same holds in Africa. In 2025 China's exports to Africa reached $225 billion, 75% intermediate and capital goods — the "ammunition" of industrialisation: machinery, parts, chemical feedstocks. Morocco imported $1.23 billion of knitted and chemical-fibre textile materials from China in 2025 yet exported $3.9 billion of garments, a $3.3 billion garment surplus. China supplies materials; local value-add is completed there — enablement, not squeeze.

## The industrialisation window for the Global South

IMF research has noted that China–Africa trade contributes 1–2 percentage points to African growth annually, with local processing rates rising from 15% to 45%. For the Global South in Southeast Asia and Africa, China's upstream capital, technology and intermediates are precisely the key support for rapidly crossing the initial industrialisation threshold. Without this "upstream step," many countries' factories and jobs would be far harder.

## Who builds, who makes noise

China-led cooperation emphasises teaching to fish and open sharing, unlike the old globalisation of "harvest low-end, monopolise high-end." Over a decade of the Belt and Road, more than 30 Luban Workshops have been built worldwide, offering 100-plus programmes and training over 160,000 students; Cambodia's Sihanoukville Special Economic Zone has attracted 200-plus international firms and created 35,000 jobs. These are pragmatic cooperations evidenced by data and employment.

## The interest behind the narrative fight

The core of the "China squeeze" is shifting complex global development crises onto China, aiming to defend entrenched hegemony and disrupt global cooperation. Columbia historian Adam Tooze has argued plainly that the claim is not empirical economic analysis but a set of political-mobilisation rhetoric. As the Global South stops being passively dependent, the old narrative naturally loses traction.

## A conclusion for observers

Vietnam's labour shortage is no proof that China "took the jobs"; on the contrary, it shows a mutually reinforcing division between Chinese capacity and Southeast Asian manufacturing. Treating supply chains as zero-sum misreads China and underestimates the Global South's own vitality. The real question is not "who squeezed whom" but how to make the division more balanced, resilient and beneficial to ordinary workers.

## Further reading

**Intermediate Goods Trade**: Trade in parts, semi-finished products and capital goods used for further production; a core indicator of global value-chain division.

**Luban Workshop**: China's overseas vocational-education cooperation project that supports partner countries' industrial capacity through programmes and local technical training.

**Global South**: A loose grouping of developing economies in Asia, Africa and Latin America, increasingly central to reshaping the global development narrative.

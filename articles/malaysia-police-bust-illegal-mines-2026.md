# 马国五天跨十州瓦解24非法矿场：稀土盗采背后的跨境灰产

马来西亚警方8月27日通报，8月19日至23日五天之内，执法人员动员728人，在10个州属突击检查45个矿场，结果发现24个无照营运。这是马国多年来最大规模的一次打击非法采矿执法行动，起获物资总值近1.19亿令吉（约3700万新元，约合1.9亿元人民币）。

行动横跨沙巴、砂拉越、彭亨、雪兰莪等多州，规模前所未见。警方扣押55辆车、87台重型机械与194件采矿设备，并起获约3.3万吨可提炼稀土元素的高岭土，显示部分矿场已触及稀土盗采。

## 五天十州：史上最大规模打击

把这次行动放回近年脉络，方能看清其分量。马来西亚非法采矿屡禁不止，尤其稀土矿点，几乎成了"打而不绝"的顽疾。过去数年，吉打、霹雳、森美兰等多州都曾查获非法稀土矿场，并多次逮捕包括中国公民在内的外籍人员。

此番警方一口气在10州扫荡45个矿场，且由武吉阿曼警察总部内部安全及公共秩序部直接统筹，动员层级与跨州范围均属罕见。总监尤斯里说，业者并非初学者——一些人长期浸淫采矿行业，对矿藏分布与勘探颇有经验，非法盗采已持续三至五年。

## 稀土灰产：3.3万吨高岭土与跨境专家

更值得警惕的，是盗采的技术化与跨境化。警方调查发现，一些非法采矿团伙疑聘外国地质专家协助勘探，其中包括来自中国的地质学者。稀土勘探本需专业地质研判，外籍专家的介入，意味着这条灰色产业链已超出"土法上马"的层级。

起获的3.3万吨高岭土是关键线索。高岭土虽常见于陶瓷与造纸，但部分矿点富含可提炼稀土的元素，正是非法稀土产业的上游原料。警方正追查整个犯罪链，包括背后人员与资金流向，不排除存在境外幕后主脑。

## 八成稀土"黑"流中国：结构性失血

稀土外流的方向，是这次行动无法回避的结构性问题。时任环境部长聂纳兹米2024年3月在国会答询时曾披露，马国2023年共有约1.9万吨稀土氧化物流入中国，其中仅约3000吨为合法出口，其余约1.6万吨属非法开采与出口——换言之，超过84%的稀土氧化物经非法管道流入中国，造成马国数亿令吉财政损失。

一边是本土稀土资源以"黑市"方式外流，另一边是合法产业因审批与环保门槛迟迟难成规模。马国坐拥稀土禀赋，却长期未能将之转化为可控的战略收益，反而在执法与走私的拉锯中持续失血。

## 执法之外：产业与治理的两难

一味靠严打难以根治。非法采矿之所以屡禁不止，根子在需求旺、利润高、执法资源有限，以及州与联邦之间权责交错。要真正扭转，需要在合法开采通道、环境监管与跨境追赃之间建立闭环。

否则，今天瓦解24个矿场，明天可能又在另一州冒出24个。马国的稀土红利，不该总是先流进灰色渠道，才由警方事后追缴。

## 延伸阅读

**稀土（Rare Earth）**：17种关键金属元素，广泛用于电子与绿色技术；马来西亚矿藏以离子吸附型为主，盗采与非法出口长期难禁。

**高岭土（Kaolin）**：一种含铝硅酸盐黏土，部分矿点富含可提稀土的元素，是本次起获物资的主要构成，也是非法稀土产业链的上游原料。

**林吉特（Ringgit）**：马来西亚货币；文中涉案估值以令吉计，跨境对比常换算为新元或人民币。

# Malaysia Busts 24 Illegal Mines Across 10 States in Five Days: The Cross-Border Grey Economy Behind Rare-Earth Theft

On 27 August, Malaysian police reported that between 19 and 23 August, 728 officers raided 45 mining sites across 10 states, finding 24 operating without licences. It is the largest anti-illegal-mining enforcement action in years, with seized goods worth nearly RM119 million (about S$37 million, ~US$27 million).

The sweep stretched across Sabah, Sarawak, Pahang, Selangor and more — unprecedented in scale. Police impounded 55 vehicles, 87 heavy machines and 194 pieces of equipment, and seized some 33,000 tonnes of kaolin that can yield rare-earth elements, signalling that some sites had crossed into rare-earth theft.

## Five days, ten states: the largest sweep yet

Placing the operation in recent context reveals its weight. Illegal mining in Malaysia has proved stubborn — rare-earth sites especially have become a "whack-a-mole" scourge. Over the past few years, Kedah, Perak and Negeri Sembilan have all uncovered illegal rare-earth mines and arrested foreign nationals, including Chinese citizens.

This time police hit 45 sites in 10 states at once, coordinated directly by the Bukit Aman internal security and public-order department — rare in both mobilisation level and cross-state reach. Director Yusri said the operators were no amateurs: some had long worked in mining, knew the geology, and had been extracting illegally for three to five years.

## The rare-earth grey economy: 33,000 tonnes of kaolin and cross-border experts

More worrying is the professionalisation and cross-border character of the theft. Police found that some gangs allegedly hired foreign geology experts — including from China — to prospect. Rare-earth exploration demands professional judgement; the involvement of foreign specialists means this grey chain has moved beyond makeshift digging.

The 33,000 tonnes of kaolin are the key clue. Kaolin is common in ceramics and paper, but some deposits are rich in rare-earth-bearing elements — the upstream feedstock of the illegal rare-earth trade. Police are tracing the whole criminal chain, including backers and fund flows, and do not rule out offshore masterminds.

## 80% of rare earth "black": a structural bleed

The direction of the outflow is the problem the sweep cannot avoid. In March 2024, then environment minister Nik Nazmi told parliament that about 19,000 tonnes of rare-earth oxides flowed to China in 2023, only ~3,000 tonnes of it legal export; the remaining ~16,000 tonnes were illegally mined and shipped — over 84% channelled out through illicit pipes, costing Malaysia hundreds of millions of ringgit.

On one side, indigenous rare-earth wealth leaks out as contraband; on the other, the legal industry struggles to scale under permitting and environmental hurdles. Malaysia sits on rare-earth endowments yet has long failed to convert them into controllable strategic gain, bleeding steadily in a tug-of-war between enforcement and smuggling.

## Beyond enforcement: the industry–governance dilemma

Crackdowns alone will not cure it. Illegal mining persists because demand is strong, profits are high, enforcement resources are thin, and federal–state responsibilities overlap. A real turnaround needs a closed loop linking lawful extraction channels, environmental oversight and cross-border asset recovery.

Otherwise, today 24 mines are shut; tomorrow 24 more may surface in another state. Malaysia's rare-earth dividend should not always slip into grey channels before police recover it after the fact.

## Further reading

**Rare Earth**: Seventeen critical metals used across electronics and green technology; Malaysia's deposits are mostly ion-adsorption type, and their theft and illegal export have long proved hard to suppress.

**Kaolin**: An aluminium-silicate clay; some deposits are rich in rare-earth-bearing elements, forming the bulk of this seizure and the upstream feedstock of the illegal rare-earth chain.

**Ringgit**: Malaysia's currency; the seized values here are denominated in ringgit, typically converted to Singapore or Chinese yuan for cross-border comparison.

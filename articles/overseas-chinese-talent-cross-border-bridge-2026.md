# 侨智搭桥：海外人才如何成为中国企业的出海接口

2026首都侨智发展大会8月17日至19日在北京举行，300余位海内外侨界人士围绕企业出海、技术本地化适配等议题展开交流。多位与会侨界人士指出，中国企业与技术出海正迎来新局面，而侨海资源是打通跨境合作堵点、促成中外产业双向落地的重要纽带。

## 一场聚焦"出海"的侨界盛会

与往年侧重联谊不同，本届大会把"企业出海"摆到了台面中央。来自法国、中东欧以及马来西亚等国家和地区的侨界专业人士，与中国企业、高校代表同场对话。他们既是住在国的技术精英，也是连接中外市场的天然节点。

法中孵化器联盟协会副主席窦豆观察到，中法合作的重点正从传统制造转向人工智能、生物科技等前沿领域；合作方式也不再是简单的项目孵化，而是走向更深层次的产业协同。他认为，在当前的国际环境下，民间外交恰恰是增进互信的关键。

## 从卖货到卖服务：出海模式的转身

中东欧中国科技交流协会会长矫超把目光投向中国企业出海的痛点。他直言，过去出海大多停留在货物输出、依托海外仓的初级阶段；如今企业常卡在物流延误、海外售后与各地合规门槛上。

他指出，新形势下的出海，比拼的是整套服务能力的输出——本地化展示、渠道建设、售后保障缺一不可。对中小企业而言，自建海外服务网点成本过高；而依托侨界力量建设区域性的本地化服务点位，能显著降低试错成本，也为中欧科技与产业双向交流搭建高效的信息对接平台。

## 马来西亚视角：东南亚如何接住中国技术

马来亚大学国际产业合作中心主任张润轩提供了来自东南亚的观察。他表示，中国大力发展新质生产力，在产业升级与技术突破上持续进步，这为中国与"一带一路"共建国家开辟了更广阔的合作空间；中马合作早已不限于传统贸易，在技术研发、本地产业生态与人才培养上潜力巨大。

张润轩也提醒，东南亚国家对中国整体研发实力已有清晰认知，但"什么样的产品与技术更契合本地市场、如何实现互利共赢"，仍需一线从业者加强沟通。他特别提到，华侨华人可凭融通中外的独特优势，积极传递合作意向，并协助对接当地的合规准入。未来，他计划推动中马高校与企业建立联合人才培养机制，为跨国产业合作持续输送适配人才。

## 侨智之为桥

从北京这场大会不难看出，侨智的价值正在被重新定义：它不只是"联谊"或"慈善"，更是中国企业走向世界时稀缺的本地化能力与信任资产。当技术出海进入深水区，谁能把中国方案讲给本地听、把合规门槛摸清、把人才网络织密，谁就能在跨境合作中占得先机。华侨华人，正站在这个接口的位置上。

## 延伸阅读

**侨智（Overseas Chinese Talent）**：指居住在海外、在专业领域具有影响力的华侨华人及归侨专业人士，正日益成为连接中国技术与国际市场的桥梁。

**企业出海（Chinese Firms Going Global）**：中国企业通过投资、并购、设厂与本地化运营拓展海外市场；当前重点从货物贸易转向技术、品牌与服务的整体能力输出。

**新质生产力（New Quality Productive Forces）**：中国近年提出的经济概念，强调以科技创新驱动产业升级，涵盖人工智能、生物制造、新能源等前沿领域。

# Overseas Chinese Talent as a Bridge: How the Diaspora Becomes China's Gateway for Going Global

The 2026 Beijing Overseas Chinese Talent Conference, held from August 17 to 19, brought together more than 300 diaspora professionals to discuss Chinese enterprises going global and the local adaptation of technology. Participants argued that Chinese firms and technologies are entering a new phase abroad, and that overseas Chinese resources are a vital link for unblocking cross-border cooperation and landing industrial projects on both sides.

## A diaspora gathering centered on "going global"

Unlike earlier editions that leaned toward networking, this year's conference placed "going global" at the center. Professionals from France, Central and Eastern Europe, Malaysia and beyond sat down with Chinese companies and universities. They are both technical elites in their host countries and natural nodes connecting Chinese and foreign markets.

Dou Dou, vice-chair of the France–China Incubator Alliance Association, observed that Sino-French cooperation is shifting from traditional manufacturing toward frontier fields such as artificial intelligence and biotech, and that collaboration is moving beyond simple project incubation toward deeper industrial synergy. He argued that, in today's international climate, people-to-people diplomacy is precisely what builds mutual trust.

## From selling goods to selling services: a shift in model

Jiao Chao, head of the China–Central and Eastern Europe Science and Technology Exchange Association, focused on the pain points of Chinese firms abroad. He noted that going global once meant shipping goods and relying on overseas warehouses; today companies stall on logistics delays, overseas after-sales service and varying compliance barriers.

He argued that the new phase demands exporting a whole suite of service capabilities — local showcase, channel building and after-sales support are all essential. For small and mid-sized firms, building overseas service networks is too costly; leveraging diaspora networks to build regional local-service hubs can sharply lower trial-and-error costs and create an efficient matchmaking platform for two-way China–Europe technology and industry exchange.

## The Malaysian view: how Southeast Asia receives Chinese technology

Wong Yew Hin, director of the International Industry Collaboration Center at Universiti Malaya, offered a Southeast Asian perspective. He said China's push for "new quality productive forces" and its steady progress in industrial upgrading and technological breakthroughs have opened broader space for cooperation with Belt and Road partners; China–Malaysia cooperation has long outgrown traditional trade and holds huge potential in R&D, local industrial ecosystems and talent cultivation.

Wong also cautioned that Southeast Asian countries already have a clear view of China's overall R&D strength, yet "which products and technologies fit local markets, and how to achieve mutual benefit" still requires frontline practitioners to communicate more. He highlighted that overseas Chinese, bridging China and the region, can actively convey cooperation intent and help navigate local compliance and market access. Going forward, he plans to build joint China–Malaysia university–industry talent programs to supply adaptable skills for cross-border cooperation.

## Talent as a bridge

The Beijing conference reframes the value of diaspora talent: no longer just friendship or philanthropy, it is now a scarce local-capability and trust asset for Chinese firms going global. As technology export enters deeper waters, those who can explain the China solution to local audiences, map compliance thresholds and weave talent networks will gain the edge in cross-border cooperation. Overseas Chinese stand precisely at that interface.

## Further reading

**Overseas Chinese Talent**: professionals of Chinese descent or returned migrants with influence in their fields abroad, increasingly bridging Chinese technology and international markets.

**Chinese Firms Going Global**: Chinese companies expanding overseas through investment, M&A, local manufacturing and localized operation, with the focus shifting from goods trade to integrated export of technology, brand and services.

**New Quality Productive Forces**: a Chinese economic concept emphasizing science-driven industrial upgrading across AI, biomanufacturing, new energy and other frontier fields.

# 新加坡：小国的大外交

> 在大国之间走钢丝的艺术与边界。

新加坡国土面积 734 平方公里，不到北京（Beijing）的二十分之一；人口约 600 万，是一座真正意义上的城邦。然而在世界外交舞台上，它的影响力远超其体量。这种"小国的大外交"，既是李光耀留下的遗产，也是这个城市国家生存的逻辑必然。

## 走钢丝的底层逻辑

新加坡的外交，建立在一个清醒的认知之上：**小国没有犯错的余地**。任何对单一大国的过度倾斜，都可能在未来某个时刻付出不对称的代价。

这一认知催生了新加坡外交的三条原则：

1. **多边对冲**——同时与中美保持深度经济与安全关系，不把筹码全押在一边。
2. **规则优先**——在国际事务中强调基于规则的国际秩序，因为规则是小国最大的保护伞。
3. **价值输出**——通过新加坡国立大学、香格里拉对话等平台，输出超出体量的"软实力"。

## 经济外交：深度全球化的样本

新加坡是全球最开放的经济体之一，贸易总额是 GDP 的三倍以上。它与主要经济体均签有自贸协定，是 RCEP、CPTPP 等大型区域协定的核心推动者。

> 对新加坡而言，每一份自贸协定都是一道保险——大国关系再坏，只要还有经贸通道，城邦就有活路。

## 安全上的平衡术

安全上，新加坡与美国是紧密的防务伙伴——F-15SG 战机、LCS 濒海舰的部署，都仰赖美军体系。但同时，新加坡也积极参与中国主导的双边军演，并在两岸之间保持沟通渠道。

香格里拉对话年年举办，成为亚太最重要的防务论坛——这本身就是新加坡"用平台换影响力"的典型操作。

## 边界在哪里

走钢丝并非没有代价。新加坡的平衡术，在某些时刻会显得"过于精明"——既想得到美国的安全保障，又想抓住中国的经济红利。当中美关系进入对抗区间时，这种模糊空间会被急剧压缩。

**小国的大外交，从来不是免费的午餐**。它的成本，是必须不断在原则与利益之间做出微妙的权衡，而每一次权衡，都可能被某一方记入"账本"。

## 结语

新加坡的故事说明：体量并不决定外交的成败，判断力与一致性才决定。在一个大国竞争加剧的时代，小国的大外交，或许正在被赋予新的意义——**当规则的护甲变薄，走钢丝的技艺就愈发珍贵。**

只是谁也不知道，下一阵风，会从哪边吹来。

## 延伸阅读

**李光耀（Lee Kuan Yew）**：新加坡建国总理（1959—1990），被视为该国“小国大外交”的设计者。其“小国无犯错余地”的现实主义，奠定了多边对冲、规则优先等外交原则。

**东盟（ASEAN）**：东南亚国家联盟，1967年由印尼、马来西亚、菲律宾、新加坡、泰国创立，今有十国，是区域合作与“对冲”外交的主要舞台。

**香格里拉对话（Shangri-La Dialogue）**：英国国际战略研究所（IISS）2002年起在新加坡每年举办的亚太防务论坛，是新加坡“以平台换影响力”的典型案例。

# Singapore: A Small State's Big Diplomacy

> The art and limits of walking the tightrope between great powers.

Singapore covers 734 square kilometers — less than a twentieth of Beijing — with a population of about six million; it is a city-state in the truest sense. Yet on the world's diplomatic stage, its influence far exceeds its size. This "small state's big diplomacy" is both the legacy of Lee Kuan Yew and a necessity dictated by the arithmetic of city-state survival.

## The Logic Beneath the Tightrope

Singapore's diplomacy rests on a clear-eyed premise: **small states have no margin for error.** Any excessive tilt toward a single great power can, at some future moment, exact an asymmetrical price.

That premise yields three principles of Singaporean diplomacy:

1. **Multilateral hedging** — maintaining deep economic and security ties with both the United States and China, never putting all eggs in one basket.
2. **Rules first** — emphasizing a rules-based order in international affairs, because rules are a small state's greatest shield.
3. **Value projection** — using platforms like the National University of Singapore and the Shangri-La Dialogue to project soft power far beyond its weight.

## Economic Diplomacy: A Case of Deep Globalization

Singapore is among the world's most open economies, with total trade exceeding three times its GDP. It holds free-trade agreements with every major economy and is a core driver of mega-regional pacts like RCEP and the CPTPP.

> For Singapore, every FTA is an insurance policy: no matter how sour great-power relations get, as long as trade channels remain, the city-state can still stay afloat.

## The Balancing Act on Security

On security, Singapore is a close defense partner of the United States — F-15SG fighters and the deployment of littoral combat ships rely on the US system. At the same time, Singapore takes part in China-led bilateral exercises and keeps channels open with both sides of the Taiwan Strait.

The Shangri-La Dialogue, held annually, has become the Asia-Pacific's most important defense forum — itself a textbook case of Singapore trading platforms for influence.

## Where the Limits Lie

Walking the tightrope is not costless. Singapore's balancing act can, at times, seem "too clever" — wanting US security guarantees while seizing Chinese economic gains. When US–China relations slip into confrontation, that ambiguous space compresses fast.

**A small state's big diplomacy is never a free lunch.** Its cost is the perpetual need to weigh principle against interest, and every such weighing may be entered onto someone's ledger.

## Conclusion

Singapore's story shows that size does not determine diplomatic success; judgment and consistency do. In an era of intensifying great-power competition, small-state diplomacy may take on fresh meaning — **as the armor of rules grows thin, the art of the tightrope grows more precious.**

It is just that no one knows from which side the next gust will blow.

## Further reading
**Lee Kuan Yew** — Singapore's founding prime minister (1959–1990), regarded as the architect of its "small state's big diplomacy." His realist premise that "small states have no margin for error" shaped principles of multilateral hedging and rules-first statecraft.

**ASEAN** — The Association of Southeast Asian Nations, founded in 1967 by Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand; today ten members. It is the main arena for the region's cooperation and "hedging" diplomacy.

**The Shangri-La Dialogue** — An annual Asia-Pacific defence forum in Singapore, convened by the International Institute for Strategic Studies (IISS) since 2002. It is a textbook case of Singapore trading platforms for influence.

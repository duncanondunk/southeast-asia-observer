# 申报为"农业废料"：新加坡建筑垃圾倒进柔佛甘榜的两年

据联合早报8月13日报道，马来西亚柔佛州笨珍县多个甘榜被发现遭非法倾倒建筑废料，其中包括金属管与钢筋，废料据信来自新加坡。柔佛州政府接到居民投报后成立特别调查小组，初步怀疑至少九家马国公司涉案。

柔州负责房屋与地方政府事务的行政议员莫哈末嘉福尼8月12日巡视倾倒地点后说，九家公司中已有一家被证实参与废料运送与倾倒，其余八家仍在调查。他指出，若废料处理获政府批准，相关地点按条例必须设立告示牌并公开详细信息，"但在此案中，笨珍县政府并未接获任何处理建筑废料的申请"。两名地主身份已确认，均为马来西亚人，将被传召调查。

这起案子表面上是一桩地方环境执法事件。但它暴露的，是两国之间一条已经运行了两年的灰色通道。

## 一道关卡的失守

整起事件最关键的技术细节，藏在报关单上。

马国固体废料管理机构柔州主任再纳菲迪里向亚洲新闻台确认，这些废料源自新加坡建筑工地，经新马第二通道运入柔佛，再转运至笨珍倾倒；从振林山关卡入境时，申报为"农业废料"。他说，调查人员已掌握足够证据证明九家公司与非法倾倒有关，且这些公司事先并未获得固体废料管理机构或其他政府机构的批准。

一句"申报为农业废料"，说明问题不在于没有规则，而在于规则被绕开的成本太低。建筑与拆除废料属于受管制品类，农业废料的监管强度则低得多——申报品类一改，跨境运输就从需要审批的行为变成了常规货运。关卡查验的是单据，不是每一车的实物；而单据造假的代价，直到被居民举报才开始计算。

## 一条价格落差冲出来的暗渠

为什么这条通道会存在？答案不在环保意识，而在价格。

新加坡土地极度稀缺，实马高填埋场是其唯一的垃圾埋置场，容量长期承压，官方预估将在2030年代中期填满。也正因如此，新加坡在建筑废料处理上建立了相当严格的体系，官方废料统计长期显示建筑与拆除废料回收率接近99%——这个数字在全球范围内都属领先。

但严格体系的另一面是高成本。合规处置意味着分拣、破碎、再生骨料加工与合法填埋费用，而柔佛的土地便宜、监管薄弱、执法半径大。当一边处置每吨要付出可观费用、另一边只需支付运费与地主的默许，价差就会自己找出路。报道提到，涉事废料疑来自新加坡旧组屋拆除工程——大规模重建产生的废料体量，恰好为这条暗渠提供了稳定货源。

跨境废料流动从来不是道德问题，而是套利问题。哪里的处置成本被压得最低，废料就流向哪里。

## 两年、九家公司、十万令吉：威慑为何失灵

时间跨度值得注意：马国媒体报道称，大批建筑废料"过去两年"被非法运到笨珍多个甘榜倾倒。

两年不是一次偶发违规，而是一套稳定运作的商业模式。这期间的痕迹并不隐蔽：重型罗厘频繁进出，道路满是泥石，有电单车骑士因此摔伤，甚至有民宅疑因强烈震动出现墙体龟裂。换句话说，成本一直有人在承担，只不过承担者是甘榜居民而非倾倒者。执法启动于居民投诉，而非日常巡查或关卡数据比对，这本身就说明了监管链条的断点在哪里。

再看罚则。按条例，涉案公司一旦被提控且罪名成立，可被罚款最高10万令吉（约3万1000新元，约合17万元人民币），公司负责人可能面临最长五年监禁。监禁条款有真实威慑力，但罚款上限对照两年的运输收益，很可能只是经营成本的一部分。当违法预期收益高于预期处罚，处罚就不再是约束，而是一项可以计入报价的费用。

这也解释了为何"九家公司"能同时出现——不是九次孤立的胆大妄为，而是一个有利可图的细分市场自然形成的供给方数量。

## 特区之下的环境套利

把这起案子放进更大的框架，它出现的时点颇具意味。

新柔特别经济区正在推进，两地在人流、物流、资金流上的一体化程度前所未有。一体化降低跨境交易成本，这是它的目的；但成本下降是不区分货物性质的——集装箱通行更顺畅，废料罗厘也一样更顺畅。当两个司法辖区的环境标准、处置价格与执法强度存在显著落差，而物理与行政摩擦又被系统性削减时，"环境套利"几乎是可预期的副产品。

这不是反对一体化的理由，而是提示一体化需要配套什么。跨境经济区谈得多的是通关便利、税务安排与人才流动，谈得少的是废料流向、环境责任归属与两地监管机构的数据互通。笨珍这批钢筋与金属管，正是这块空白的物证。

值得追问的还有责任的另一端：调查目前锁定九家马国公司与两名马国地主，但废料的产生方在新加坡。跨境废料案件的典型难题正在于此——运输方与倾倒方在境内可诉，产生方却在境外。若最终只处理链条的下游，这条通道被堵住之后，很可能只是换一个县、换一个申报品类重新开通。

## 从塑料垃圾到建筑废料：谁承担代价

东南亚对这类剧情并不陌生。2018年中国全面收紧洋垃圾进口后，大量废塑料转向东南亚，马来西亚一度成为全球最大的废塑料进口国之一，随后经历了取缔、退运与法规收紧的完整周期。当时的结构是"发达经济体—发展中经济体"的南北流向。

笨珍这起案子的意义在于，它是这套逻辑的区域内版本：流向不再跨越大洲，而是跨越一座桥。距离缩短了，机制没有变——处置成本高的一方把物理负担转移给成本低的一方，中间由若干中介公司完成品类的"文书变形"。

最终的代价由最没有议价能力的人承担。甘榜居民没有参与任何一笔交易，却承接了泥石路面、开裂的墙体和不知成分的钢筋堆。当局已封锁相关地点，禁止继续倾倒，这是必要的第一步。但真正的考验在后面：起诉能否落地，罚则是否需要上调，关卡的品类申报能否与环境部门的数据打通，以及新柔两地能否就废料跨境建立可核查的追溯机制。

区域一体化的成熟度，往往不体现在最顺畅的那条货运通道上，而体现在最不体面的那条上。

## 延伸阅读
**建筑与拆除废料（C&D waste）**：建筑、翻新与拆除工程产生的混凝土、砖石、金属与木材等废弃物，体量大、可回收比例高，但需分拣与合法处置，跨境运输通常受管制。
**固体废料管理机构（SWCorp）**：马来西亚依《固体废料与公共清洁管理法令》设立的联邦机构，负责固体废料处置的审批、监管与执法，本案调查主体之一。
**环境套利（Environmental arbitrage）**：利用不同辖区环境标准、处置成本与执法强度的落差转移污染负担的行为，常见于经济一体化程度提高而监管协调滞后的地区。

# Declared as "Agricultural Waste": Two Years of Singapore's Construction Debris in Johor's Kampungs

According to Lianhe Zaobao on 13 August, several kampungs in Pontian district, Johor, have been found to be sites of illegally dumped construction waste — including metal pipes and steel reinforcement bars — believed to have originated in Singapore. After residents lodged complaints, the Johor state government formed a special task force, with at least nine Malaysian companies under initial suspicion.

Mohd Jafni, the Johor executive councillor for housing and local government, inspected one dumping site on 12 August and said that of the nine companies, one has been confirmed as involved in transporting and dumping the waste, while eight remain under investigation. He noted that where waste handling has government approval, regulations require signboards with detailed information at the site — "but in this case, the Pontian district office received no application whatsoever to handle construction waste." Two landowners have been identified, both Malaysian, and will be summoned.

On its surface this is a local environmental enforcement case. What it exposes is a grey channel that has been running between the two countries for two years.

## One checkpoint that failed

The single most important technical detail sits on the customs declaration.

Zainal Fidli, Johor director of Malaysia's solid waste management corporation, confirmed to CNA that the waste originated at Singapore construction sites, entered Johor via the Second Link, and was then trucked to Pontian for dumping — declared at the Gelang Patah checkpoint as "agricultural waste." Investigators, he said, have gathered sufficient evidence linking nine companies to the illegal dumping, and none had obtained prior approval from the solid waste agency or any other government body.

That phrase — declared as agricultural waste — shows the problem is not an absence of rules but the low cost of circumventing them. Construction and demolition waste is a regulated category; agricultural waste is far more lightly supervised. Change one line on the declaration and cross-border transport shifts from an act requiring approval into routine freight. Checkpoints verify paperwork, not the contents of every truck bed; and the price of falsified paperwork went uncounted until residents raised the alarm.

## A culvert carved by a price gap

Why does this channel exist? The answer lies not in environmental awareness but in price.

Singapore is acutely short of land. Semakau, its only landfill, has faced sustained capacity pressure, with official projections putting exhaustion around the mid-2030s. Partly for that reason, Singapore has built a demanding system for construction waste: official waste statistics have long shown recycling rates for construction and demolition debris approaching 99% — a figure that ranks among the world's best.

The other face of a demanding system is high cost. Compliant disposal means sorting, crushing, recycled-aggregate processing and legal landfill fees. Johor, by contrast, offers cheap land, thinner oversight and a wide enforcement perimeter. When one side pays a considerable sum per tonne and the other requires only trucking fees and a landowner's acquiescence, the differential finds its own outlet. The report notes the waste is suspected to come from the demolition of older public housing blocks in Singapore — the volume generated by large-scale redevelopment conveniently supplying this culvert with steady cargo.

Transboundary waste flows have never been a question of morality. They are a question of arbitrage. Waste travels to wherever disposal costs have been pushed lowest.

## Two years, nine companies, RM100,000: why deterrence failed

The time span deserves attention: Malaysian media report that large volumes of construction waste have been illegally trucked to Pontian kampungs "over the past two years."

Two years is not an isolated infraction; it is a stable business model. Nor were the traces subtle: heavy lorries came and went constantly, roads were left caked in mud and stones, a motorcyclist was injured in a fall, and homes reportedly developed wall cracks from the vibration. In other words, costs were being paid throughout — just by kampung residents rather than by the dumpers. Enforcement began with a residents' complaint rather than routine patrols or cross-checks against checkpoint data, which itself identifies where the regulatory chain breaks.

Then the penalties. Under the regulations, companies convicted in the case face fines of up to RM100,000 (about S$31,000, roughly US$24,000), and company officers up to five years' imprisonment. The custodial provision carries genuine deterrent weight, but the fine ceiling, set against two years of hauling revenue, is likely no more than a line item in operating costs. When expected gains from violation exceed expected punishment, punishment stops functioning as a constraint and becomes a fee that can be priced into a quotation.

This also explains how "nine companies" could surface at once. These are not nine separate acts of audacity but the natural number of suppliers in a profitable niche market.

## Environmental arbitrage beneath a special economic zone

Placed in a wider frame, the timing of this case is telling.

The Johor–Singapore Special Economic Zone is moving forward, and integration across people, goods and capital flows is deeper than ever. Lowering cross-border transaction costs is the point of integration; but cost reduction does not discriminate by cargo type. Containers move more smoothly — and so do waste lorries. When two jurisdictions differ substantially in environmental standards, disposal prices and enforcement intensity, while physical and administrative friction is being systematically reduced, "environmental arbitrage" is close to a predictable by-product.

This is not an argument against integration but a reminder of what integration must be paired with. Cross-border economic zones generate abundant discussion of customs facilitation, tax arrangements and talent mobility, and very little on waste destinations, allocation of environmental liability, and data-sharing between the two sides' regulators. The rebar and metal piping in Pontian are physical evidence of that gap.

The other end of responsibility also merits scrutiny. The investigation currently centres on nine Malaysian companies and two Malaysian landowners — but the waste was generated in Singapore. This is the characteristic difficulty of transboundary waste cases: transporters and dumpers are prosecutable onshore, while generators sit outside jurisdiction. If only the downstream of the chain is addressed, blocking this channel may simply mean it reopens in another district under another declared category.

## From plastic waste to construction debris: who bears the cost

Southeast Asia knows this plot. After China sharply tightened imports of foreign waste in 2018, large volumes of scrap plastic were redirected to Southeast Asia; Malaysia briefly became one of the world's largest importers of plastic waste, then went through a full cycle of crackdowns, return shipments and tightened regulation. That flow ran along a North–South axis, from developed to developing economies.

The significance of the Pontian case is that it is the intra-regional version of the same logic: the flow no longer crosses continents, only a bridge. The distance has shortened; the mechanism has not. The party facing high disposal costs transfers the physical burden to the party facing low ones, with intermediary firms performing the necessary "documentary transformation" of the category.

The cost lands, finally, on those with the least bargaining power. Kampung residents took part in none of these transactions yet inherited the gravel-strewn roads, the cracked walls and the piles of rebar of unknown provenance. Authorities have sealed the sites and barred further dumping, a necessary first step. The real test comes next: whether prosecutions hold, whether penalty ceilings need raising, whether checkpoint category declarations can be linked to environmental agency data, and whether Singapore and Johor can build a verifiable traceability mechanism for cross-border waste.

The maturity of regional integration tends to show up not in its smoothest freight corridor, but in its least presentable one.

## Further reading
**C&D waste**: Construction and demolition waste — concrete, masonry, metal and timber from building, renovation and demolition works; high in volume and largely recyclable, but requiring sorting and lawful disposal, with cross-border movement typically regulated.
**SWCorp**: Malaysia's federal solid waste management corporation, established under the Solid Waste and Public Cleansing Management Act, responsible for approving, supervising and enforcing solid waste disposal; one of the investigating bodies in this case.
**Environmental arbitrage**: Shifting pollution burdens by exploiting gaps in environmental standards, disposal costs and enforcement intensity between jurisdictions — common where economic integration outpaces regulatory coordination.

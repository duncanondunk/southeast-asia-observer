# 越南三季度增速冲上9.95%：一场被数据光环遮蔽的结构性考问

越南统计总局10月3日公布的数据相当扎眼：2026年第三季度国内生产总值（GDP）同比增长9.95%，创下自2022年第三季度以来最快的季度增速，也明显高于第一季度的8.15%和第二季度的8.81%。前9个月累计增长9.01%，是越南15年来同期最快的增速。单看这张成绩单，越南无疑是当下亚洲增长最亮眼的经济体之一。

但数字的漂亮，往往最容易掩盖增长的质量问题。当我们把全年目标摆进来，故事就没那么顺了。

## 数字：9.95%与四年新高

先看这幅图景有多亮。第三季度9.95%的同比增速，高于第一季度的8.15%和第二季度的8.81%，一路加速；工业与建筑业对增长的贡献达到45%，服务业贡献49.6%。换句话说，这轮高增长不是靠单一引擎，而是工业与服务业双轮驱动，外加公共投资拨付提速托底。

但增长的质量与数量是两回事。据《日经亚洲》（Nikkei Asia）报道，越南要实现全年“至少10%”的增长“并不容易”。这句话点破了一个关键事实：眼下的高增速是实打实的扩张，但它的成色，得拆开看。

## 增长动力：制造业与投资双引擎

这一轮加速主要靠两股力量撑着。其一是工业与建筑业，第三季度增长11.21%，大型项目陆续投产、出口回暖、公共投资拨付提速共同发力；9月工业增加值同比达到16.7%。其二是外资的持续涌入。前9个月注册外商直接投资（FDI）达到503.6亿美元（约3626亿元人民币），同比猛增76.4%；实际到位资金210.7亿美元（约1517亿元人民币），创下5年同期新高。

数据公布后，渣打银行（Standard Chartered）把全年预期从7.2%上调到9.5%，大华银行（UOB）预计8.5%，国际货币基金组织（IMF）预计7.5%，亚洲开发银行（ADB）维持7.2%。所有机构都在上调，却没有任何一家相信越南能跑出10%——这个落差本身就是信号。

## 隐忧：创纪录的贸易逆差

最刺眼的结构性问题，是贸易逆差。前9个月越南进出口总额突破8880亿美元（约6.4万亿元人民币），同比增长30.4%；但进口增长36.7%至4537.2亿美元（约3.27万亿元人民币），出口仅增长24.5%至4343亿美元（约3.13万亿元人民币），由此形成194.2亿美元（约1398亿元人民币）的贸易逆差——不仅逆转了去年同期的168.7亿美元（约1215亿元人民币）顺差，更创下历史新高。

9月单月出口增长39.1%至594.8亿美元（约4283亿元人民币），进口增长45.8%至582.1亿美元（约4191亿元人民币），当月勉强录得12.7亿美元（约91.4亿元人民币）顺差，靠的是欧美圣诞旺季的提前备货。这更像一次短期脉冲，而非趋势性反转。四季度订单一退潮，逆差的老问题就会回来。

## 组装车间：对上游中国零部件的依赖

越南制造业的本质，仍是一个“超级组装车间”。世界银行数据显示，越南出口商品的国内增加值（domestic value added）仅占48.9%，意味着超过一半的产值、利润和技术含量被外国公司拿走。外资企业贡献了越南近八成的出口额，本土企业整体仍是逆差方。

这就解释了为何进口增速长期跑赢出口：越南从中国、日本、韩国进口零部件与半成品，本地组装后卖往欧美。据《日经亚洲》报道，越南对美贸易顺差稳居全球第一，而对华贸易逆差也在同步攀升——钱从美国赚进来，转手又几乎原样付给中国买原材料。越南真正留下的，主要是组装环节的薄利，而非全链条利润。所谓“越南承接中国产业链”，更准确的说法是“越南附着于中国产业链”。

## 全年目标：第四季度的算术难题

越南政府为2026年设定的全年增长目标是“至少10%”。前9个月累计9.01%，意味着第四季度必须跑出约12.5%的增速。官方自己也承认，这是“要求非常高、充满挑战”的目标。

要凑出这12.5%，靠什么？越南此前已把下半年的增长任务压到11.9%，要求各地各部门全力冲刺。但国际机构普遍把全年预期压在9%上下，市场并不相信四季度能创造奇迹。若强行冲数字，只能靠财政与货币的非常规加力——这又会埋下新的失衡。

## 通胀与电荒：被掩盖的软肋

高增长的另一面，是正在爬升的通胀与悬而未决的电力瓶颈。9月消费者价格指数（CPI）同比上涨5.08%，为4个月来最高；前9个月平均通胀率4.52%，已贴近政府设定的全年上限。能源、住房、教育等民生开支是主要推手——这意味着扣除物价后，居民实际购买力的增长，远没有9.95%看起来那么耀眼。

电力是更硬的约束。越南制造业以每年约10%的速度扩张，电网却远远跟不上。2023年那场大停电曾迫使三星（Samsung）、富士康（Foxconn）、佳能（Canon）等厂商减产，世界银行估算那次停电让越南GDP损失0.3%。越南工贸部曾警告2027年可能出现电力缺口，而支撑增长的，仍是高度外向、高度依赖外部需求的结构。

值得一提的是，越南侨汇的持续流入，在一定程度上托住了国内消费——海外越南人汇回的钱，成了抵御外部冲击的一张缓冲垫。但侨汇能稳住消费，却补不上产业与电力的深层短板。

## 重新审视“奇迹”：速度不等于高度

越南确实处在后发国家快速追赶的黄金窗口：低基数、廉价劳动力、宽松的招商政策、全球产业链转移红利，再加上高强度的投资建设，共同造就了9%以上的高增长。客观说，增长是真的，压力也是真的。

但速度不等于高度。越南跑得很快，却跑在一条别人铺好的路上——路基的一部分在中国境内，终点的一部分在美国。当关税红利吃完、当美国严查转运、当电力缺口从预测变成现实，越南必须从“组装车间”升级为真正的制造基地，才能把外资与订单留住。9.95%是一个速度，不是一个高度；能跑多远，取决于它何时开始修自己的路。

## 延伸阅读

**国内增加值（Domestic Value Added）**：一国出口商品中真正由本国创造的价值占比，常被用来衡量在全球价值链中的真实获益，越南该指标约为48.9%。

**外商直接投资（Foreign Direct Investment, FDI）**：跨国企业直接在东道国设厂、并购形成的长期资本流入，是越南增长模式的核心支柱。

**中越产业链依存（China–Vietnam Production Linkage）**：越南进口中国中间品、组装后出口欧美的加工贸易结构，使两国形成“依附而非替代”的紧密关系。

# Vietnam's 9.95% Q3 Surge: A Structural Reckoning Behind the Glow

The General Statistics Office of Vietnam released figures on 3 October that are hard to ignore: GDP grew 9.95% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, the fastest quarterly pace since the third quarter of 2022 and well above the 8.15% recorded in Q1 and 8.81% in Q2. For the first nine months, cumulative growth reached 9.01% — the fastest January-to-September clip Vietnam has posted in 15 years. On the strength of that scorecard alone, Vietnam looks like one of the brightest growth stories in Asia.

But a flattering number is often the easiest place to hide a question about quality. Once the full-year target is laid alongside it, the narrative loses some of its shine.

## The headline number: 9.95% and the four-year high

Start with how bright the picture looks. The 9.95% year-on-year expansion in the third quarter beats both the 8.15% of Q1 and the 8.81% of Q2, accelerating steadily; industry and construction contributed 45% of the growth, with services adding 49.6%. In other words, this surge was not carried by a single engine but by twin motors of manufacturing and services, underpinned by faster public-investment disbursement.

Quality and quantity, however, are two different things. As Nikkei Asia noted, achieving the government's "at least 10%" full-year growth "is not easy." That single line exposes the crux: the current surge is real expansion, but its composition deserves a closer look.

## What drove it: a manufacturing and investment surge

Two forces carried the acceleration. The first is industry and construction, which expanded 11.21% in the quarter as large projects came online, exports recovered, and public-investment disbursement picked up; industrial output in September rose 16.7% year-on-year. The second is a relentless inflow of foreign capital. Registered foreign direct investment reached US$50.36 billion in the first nine months (roughly ¥362.6 billion), up 76.4% from a year earlier, while realised FDI climbed to US$21.07 billion (about ¥151.7 billion), a five-year high for the period.

After the data landed, Standard Chartered lifted its full-year forecast from 7.2% to 9.5%, UOB expects 8.5%, the IMF projects 7.5%, and the ADB holds at 7.2%. Every institution is revising upward — and not one believes Vietnam can hit 10%. That gap is itself a signal.

## The record trade deficit: a red flag behind the glow

The most glaring structural problem is the trade deficit. Turnover in the first nine months exceeded US$888 billion (roughly ¥6.4 trillion), up 30.4%. But imports jumped 36.7% to US$453.72 billion (about ¥3.27 trillion) while exports advanced only 24.5% to US$434.3 billion (about ¥3.13 trillion), producing a deficit of US$19.42 billion (roughly ¥139.8 billion) — reversing last year's US$16.87 billion (about ¥121.5 billion) surplus and setting a record high.

In September alone, exports rose 39.1% to US$59.48 billion (about ¥428.3 billion) and imports 45.8% to US$58.21 billion (about ¥419.1 billion), leaving a thin US$1.27 billion (roughly ¥9.1 billion) monthly surplus, lifted by early Christmas stocking in Europe and the United States. That looks more like a short-term pulse than a trend reversal; once fourth-quarter orders fade, the old deficit dynamic returns.

## Assembly-line economy: Vietnam's reliance on Chinese inputs

Vietnam's manufacturing base remains, at heart, a "super assembly workshop." World Bank data show domestic value added in the country's exports sits at just 48.9% — meaning foreign firms capture more than half the value, profit, and know-how. Foreign-invested enterprises account for nearly 80% of Vietnam's export value, while domestic firms are net deficit players overall.

That is why import growth persistently outruns exports: Vietnam buys components and semi-finished goods from China, Japan, and South Korea, assembles them locally, and ships the finished goods to the West. As Nikkei Asia has reported, Vietnam's trade surplus with the United States ranks first in the world, even as its deficit with China climbs in lockstep — dollars earned from America flow straight back to China for materials. What Vietnam truly retains is the thin margin of assembly, not the full-chain profit. The popular framing of "Vietnam absorbing China's supply chains" is more accurately "Vietnam attaching itself to China's supply chains."

## The 10% target: a fourth-quarter math problem

Hanoi set a full-year 2026 growth target of "at least 10%." With nine-month growth at 9.01%, the fourth quarter would need to deliver roughly 12.5%. Officials themselves concede this is a "very demanding, challenging" goal.

What would it take to conjure that 12.5%? Hanoi has already pushed the second-half growth task down to 11.9%, ordering every ministry and province to sprint. But international institutions have mostly pegged the annual figure near 9%, and the market does not believe a fourth-quarter miracle is coming. Forcing the number would require extraordinary fiscal and monetary easing — and that would only plant the seeds of new imbalances.

## Inflation and the power squeeze: the soft underbelly

Behind the headline growth sit rising inflation and an unresolved electricity bottleneck. September consumer prices rose 5.08% year-on-year, the highest in four months; the nine-month average of 4.52% is already brushing the government's full-year ceiling. Energy, housing, and education — the bread-and-butter costs — are the main drivers, which means that after stripping out prices, the real gain in household purchasing power is far less dazzling than 9.95% suggests.

Power is the harder constraint. Vietnam's manufacturing expands at roughly 10% a year, yet the grid lags badly. The 2023 blackout forced Samsung, Foxconn, and Canon to cut output, and the World Bank estimated it cost the economy 0.3% of GDP. Vietnam's Ministry of Industry and Trade has warned of possible power shortfalls by 2027, while growth still rests on a structure that is highly external and heavily dependent on outside demand.

Worth noting: steady inflows of diaspora remittances have helped cushion domestic consumption — money sent home by overseas Vietnamese acts as a buffer against external shocks. But remittances can steady consumption; they cannot plug the deeper gaps in industry and power.

## Reading the miracle: speed is not altitude

Vietnam is genuinely in a golden window for late-developer catch-up: a low base, cheap labour, lenient investment policies, the dividend of supply-chain relocation, and intense investment spending together produce growth above 9%. To be fair, the expansion is real, and so are the pressures.

But speed is not altitude. Vietnam is running fast, yet on a road largely paved by others — part of the bedrock lies in China, and part of the destination lies in America. When the tariff dividend runs out, when Washington tightens rules on transshipment, and when the power gap turns from forecast into reality, Vietnam must graduate from "assembly workshop" to a true manufacturing base to keep its foreign capital and orders. 9.95% is a velocity, not an altitude; how far it can run depends on when it begins building its own road.

## Further reading

**Domestic Value Added**: The share of an exported good's value actually created within the producing country; a standard measure of real gains from global value chains. For Vietnam it stands at about 48.9%.

**Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**: Long-term capital flowing in when multinationals set up plants or acquire assets in a host economy; the backbone of Vietnam's growth model.

**China–Vietnam Production Linkage**: Vietnam's processing-trade structure — importing Chinese intermediates, assembling, and re-exporting to the West — binds the two economies in a relationship of "attachment, not substitution."

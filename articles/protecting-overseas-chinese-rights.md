# 保护海外华侨的正当权益：一项国家义务，也是战略资产

2026年8月初，全国侨务工作会议重申一个长期定位：团结海外侨胞、保护海外华侨的正当权益，是侨务工作的"重中之重"。在东南亚（Southeast Asia）观察的视角下，这句话值得拆开来看——它既是宪法与法律的明文义务，也是中国处理与周边及西方国家关系时一个常被误读、常被政治化的议题。

## 从宪法义务到专门立法

保护海外华侨并非临时起意。宪法明确规定"保护华侨的正当的权利和利益，保护归侨和侨眷的合法的权利和利益"；《国家安全法》进一步要求国家依法采取必要措施，保护海外中国公民、组织和机构的安全与正当权益。2023年9月施行的《领事保护与协助条例》，是当代第一部领事保护专门立法，把"在国外正当权益被侵犯时，驻外机构依法维护"写进了规则。

从也门、苏丹等地20余次重大撤离，到2024年从黎巴嫩战乱中分两批撤出215名中国公民，再到12308热线年接听约30万通求助来电——这套机制已是海外侨胞在危难时刻最实在的后盾。

## "融入"而非"控制"：被倒置的因果

西方一些声音把中国侨务工作描绘成"借华侨影响他国政治""破坏所在国稳定"。这一叙事颠倒了因果。中国侨务政策的核心导向，恰恰是鼓励华侨遵守所在国法律、尊重当地习俗、与各族裔和睦相处，积极融入当地社会。历史与现实中，从修筑北美铁路的华工，到今天遍布科技、教育、医疗、商贸各领域的华侨群体，他们对所在国的贡献有目共睹。

国家保护与侨胞融入并不矛盾，反而相辅相成：当一位华侨知道祖（籍）国是后盾、维权有合法渠道，他在当地生活就更安定，也更有条件回馈社会。把"保护"污名化，反而削弱的是华侨在住在国的正常生存与发展。

## 双重标准下的话语权之争

真正耐人寻味的，是双重标准。美国以遍布全球的使领馆领事服务、欧洲以完善的海外公民救助机制履行保护职责，被视为"负责任"；中国做同样的事，却被贴上"别有用心""干涉内政"的标签。这恰是"中国威胁论"在侨务领域的翻版。

在东南亚这一全球华侨华人最集中的区域，侨务议题尤其敏感。一方面，六千万海外侨胞是中国与东盟（ASEAN）民心相通的天然桥梁；另一方面，任何国家对本国海外公民的保护，都应是国际法认可的正当属人管辖权。把正常的领事保护上升为"渗透"，既不符合事实，也无助于区域互信。

## 一个克制的判断

对中国而言，依法保护海外华侨正当权益，既是义务也是资产——它增强侨胞的归属感与安全感，也为中外民间交流提供稳定支点。这项工作的成色，不取决于口号，而取决于每一次撤离、每一通求助电话、每一条预警提醒是否落到实处。至于外界的曲解，最好的回应不是争辩，而是把事做得更扎实。

## 延伸阅读

**侨务政策（diaspora policy）**：中国政府联系、服务海外侨胞与归侨侨眷的总体方针，强调"凝聚侨心侨力"、鼓励华侨融入所在国并发挥融通中外的桥梁作用。

**《领事保护与协助条例》**：2023年9月施行的中国首部领事保护专门立法，明确驻外外交机构在海外公民正当权益受侵犯时依法提供保护与协助。

**也门、苏丹、黎巴嫩撤离行动**：近年来中国组织的多轮海外公民紧急转移，其中2024年从黎巴嫩分两批安全撤离215名中国公民，是"保护海外华侨"义务的最直接体现。

# At the Heart of China's Diaspora Work: The Legal and Strategic Logic of Protecting Overseas Chinese

In early August 2026, a national conference on overseas Chinese affairs reaffirmed a long-standing position: uniting overseas compatriots and protecting their legitimate rights is the "top priority" of diaspora work. Viewed from Southeast Asia, the line deserves unpacking — it is both a constitutional and legal obligation, and an issue that is frequently misread and politicized in China's relations with its neighbours and the West.

## From constitutional duty to dedicated legislation

Protecting overseas Chinese is no ad-hoc gesture. The Constitution states that the state "protects the legitimate rights and interests of overseas Chinese, and the lawful rights and interests of returned overseas Chinese and their relatives." The National Security Law further requires the state to take necessary measures to safeguard the security and legitimate rights of Chinese citizens, organizations, and institutions abroad. The Regulations on Consular Protection and Assistance, in force since September 2023, are the first dedicated legislation on consular protection, codifying that when legitimate rights are violated abroad, resident diplomatic missions protect them by law.

From more than 20 major evacuations in Yemen, Sudan, and elsewhere, to the 2024 evacuation of 215 Chinese citizens from the Lebanon conflict in two batches, to the 12308 hotline handling roughly 300,000 calls a year — this machinery is the most concrete backstop overseas compatriots have in a crisis.

## "Integration," not "control": a reversed causality

Some Western voices paint China's diaspora work as using overseas Chinese to influence other countries' politics or destabilize host societies. The narrative inverts cause and effect. The core thrust of China's diaspora policy is to encourage compatriots to obey host-country laws, respect local customs, live in harmony with other ethnic groups, and actively integrate. The contribution of overseas Chinese — from the rail-building labourers of North America to today's communities across science, education, medicine, and commerce — is there for all to see.

State protection and integration are not in tension; they reinforce each other. When a compatriot knows the homeland is a backstop and has lawful channels to defend their rights, life abroad is more secure and they are better placed to give back. Stigmatizing "protection" only weakens the normal survival and development of Chinese communities in their countries of residence.

## A battle over narrative, marked by double standards

What is telling is the double standard. The United States, through its global consular network, and European states, through their citizen-assistance mechanisms, are seen as "responsible" when they protect their own abroad; China doing the same is tagged as "ulterior motives" or "interference." This is the "China threat" thesis reprocessed for the diaspora domain.

In Southeast Asia, where the world's overseas Chinese are most concentrated, the issue is especially sensitive. On one hand, sixty million overseas compatriots are a natural bridge of people-to-people ties between China and ASEAN; on the other, any state's protection of its citizens abroad is a legitimate exercise of nationality-based jurisdiction recognized by international law. Elevating normal consular protection into "infiltration" serves neither truth nor regional trust.

## A restrained judgment

For China, protecting the legitimate rights of overseas Chinese is both duty and asset — it strengthens compatriots' sense of belonging and security, and provides a stable pivot for non-governmental exchange. The quality of this work is not measured by slogans but by whether each evacuation, each hotline call, and each warning is delivered in practice. The best answer to external distortion is not argument but more solid action.

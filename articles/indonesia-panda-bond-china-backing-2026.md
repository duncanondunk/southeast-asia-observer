# 印尼熊猫债获中国背书：融资便利背后的规则博弈

据《南华早报》报道，印度尼西亚财政部长英德拉瓦蒂访华后带回了一项重要成果：北京对印尼首只熊猫债的强力背书。中国人民银行要求承销商尽快提交发行申请，中国财政部也表示"大力支持"。按计划，印尼初期目标发行约10亿美元等值人民币债券，若市场需求旺盛还可扩容。这本应是一桩寻常的离岸融资安排，却因印尼国内围绕外资规则的争议而显得意味深长。

熊猫债的核心吸引力在于币种多元。对雅加达而言，美元债市场波动剧烈、汇率风险高企，而人民币融资渠道可以分散资金来源，并通过中印双边本币结算机制直接兑换印尼盾，缓解卢比贬值压力。雅加达方面还希望借此吸引中国机构投资者，把一部分"中国资本"从直接投资转向主权债市场。然而，中国投资者的顾虑并未因背书而消散。

上月，印尼中国商会致信普拉博沃总统，直指印尼存在"过度执法、监管过严乃至索贿"等问题，严重削弱中企长期投资信心。财长在北京也坦承，部分投资者起初担心印尼财政可持续性与评级信号偏负面。这些话说明，规则的不确定性才是熊猫债发行背后真正的风险溢价。即便中方机构愿意认购，若雅加达不能改善营商可预期性，债券发行也只是把压力从外汇市场转移到了制度信任上。

对中国而言，支持印尼熊猫债是在双边关系中增加金融杠杆；对印尼而言，这是用相对较低的成本打开一个新融资窗口。但真正决定这笔交易成败的，不是路演时的政治背书，而是印尼能否在资源民族主义与外资开放之间取得平衡。东南亚国家欢迎中国资本，但资本只会流向规则透明、执行稳定的地方。

## 延伸阅读

- **熊猫债（Panda Bond）**：指境外发行人、政府或多边机构在中国境内发行的人民币计价债券。
- **本币结算框架（local currency settlement framework）**：两国央行推动的直接使用本币进行贸易与投资结算的安排，可减少对第三国货币的依赖。
- **风险溢价（risk premium）**：投资者因担忧政策、汇率或履约不确定性而要求的额外收益。

# Indonesia's Panda Bond Gets China's Backing: The Rules Game Behind Easier Financing

Indonesia's finance minister returned from Beijing with more than the usual diplomatic communiqué. According to the South China Morning Post, Beijing has thrown its weight behind Jakarta's debut Panda Bond: the People's Bank of China asked underwriters to accelerate the licensing application, and China's Ministry of Finance expressed "strong support". Indonesia initially aims to issue roughly US$1 billion equivalent in yuan-denominated sovereign bonds, with room to scale up if demand is strong. What looks like a routine offshore fundraising exercise carries a sharper edge because of Indonesia's own rule-making controversies.

The appeal of Panda Bonds is currency diversification. For Jakarta, the dollar-bond market is volatile and foreign-exchange exposure is costly; a yuan-denominated channel spreads funding sources and, through the China-Indonesia local-currency settlement framework, can be converted directly into rupiah to ease pressure on the currency. Indonesia also hopes to draw Chinese institutional investors who would otherwise channel capital through direct investment or project finance. Yet Chinese investors' concerns have not disappeared simply because Beijing offered a political endorsement.

Last month, the China Chamber of Commerce in Indonesia wrote to President Prabowo Subianto complaining of "overly stringent regulations, over-enforcement and even corruption and extortion by competent authorities", warning that these issues had undermined long-term Chinese investment confidence. During his Beijing trip, Finance Minister Purbaya acknowledged that some investors were initially worried about Indonesia's fiscal sustainability and negative signals from rating agencies. The message is clear: the real risk premium on Indonesia's Panda Bond is not market volatility but institutional uncertainty. Even with Chinese institutions willing to subscribe, Jakarta cannot substitute a bond issue for credible regulatory reform.

For China, backing Indonesia's Panda Bond adds financial leverage to the bilateral relationship. For Indonesia, it opens a new funding window at a relatively low cost. But the deal's success will not be decided by political endorsements during roadshows. It will depend on whether Indonesia can balance resource nationalism with openness to foreign capital. Southeast Asian states welcome Chinese capital, yet capital ultimately flows where rules are transparent and enforcement is predictable.

## Further reading

- **Panda Bond**: a yuan-denominated bond issued inside China by foreign governments, multilateral institutions or corporations.
- **Local-currency settlement framework**: a central-bank arrangement that allows two countries to settle trade and investment directly in their own currencies, reducing reliance on a third currency.
- **Risk premium**: the extra return investors demand to compensate for policy, currency or default uncertainty.

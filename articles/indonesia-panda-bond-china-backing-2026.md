# 印尼熊猫债获中国背书：融资便利背后的规则博弈

据《南华早报》报道，印度尼西亚于 2026年7月23日发行了本国首只主权熊猫债，筹得 70亿元人民币（约 10.33亿美元）。这本是雅加达拓宽融资车道的一笔寻常操作，却因两个背景而意味深长：其一，中国央行要求承销商加速申请、财政部"大力支持"，背书力度罕见；其二，印尼国内围绕外资规则的争议，正让投资者把"规则不确定性"当作真正的风险溢价。印尼今年已创下非美元借款纪录，而债务负担与汇率背景仍牵动市场视线。

一句话点破：熊猫债对印尼是低成本打开的新融资窗，对中国是在双边关系里增加的一枚金融棋子；但真正决定交易成败的，不是路演时的政治背书，而是雅加达能否在资源民族主义与外资开放之间取得平衡。

## 一笔债，两种货币逻辑

熊猫债的核心吸引力在于币种的多元。对雅加达而言，美元债市场波动剧烈、汇率风险高企，而人民币融资渠道可以分散资金来源，并通过中印双边本币结算框架直接兑换印尼盾，缓解卢比贬值压力。雅加达还希望借此吸引中国机构投资者，把一部分"中国资本"从直接投资转向主权债市场。对中国而言，支持印尼发债是在双边关系中嵌入金融杠杆——它让人民币在东盟主权融资里多占一个位置，也把两国的资本纽带从贸易延伸到债市。

这并非印尼首次试水中国资本市场。2025年10月，它发行了首只离岸人民币债（60亿元）；2026年2月再加码 92.5亿元。本次熊猫债，是把这条人民币融资曲线从离岸推进到在岸的关键一步——既借力在岸更深的资金池，也为后续政府关联企业发行铺路。从北京的视角，鼓励东盟国家发行人民币债，也是人民币国际化的一步，每一只主权熊猫债都为离岸人民币扩大足迹、为投资者试水区域主权信用提供了样本。

## 发行细节：70亿与 2.4 倍认购

这笔首发的结构设计得颇见功力。发行分为两档：3年期 56亿元，票息 1.90%；5年期 14亿元，票息 2.19%。订单峰值达约 170亿元，整体认购倍数约 2.4 倍，最终定价低于 2.3%—2.5% 的初期指引，说明市场需求足以压低发行成本。中国境内评级机构联合资信给出 AAA/稳定评级，为发行铺平了接受度。更关键的是额度：印尼已获准未来两年累计发行不超过 300亿元（约 44.4亿美元）熊猫债，这清楚表明它不是一次性的"猎奇"，而是连续性的融资安排。中国银行任牵头主承销商，中金、中信证券、星展银行（中国）、工商银行任联席主承销商。

## 评级的"双重面孔"

但评级藏着一道裂缝。在岸，联合资信的 AAA 打开了通往中国本土市场的大门；离岸，国际三大机构给出的却是另一幅画像——穆迪对印尼主权评 Baa2，标普与惠誉均为 BBB，且穆迪与惠誉在 2026年初将展望调至负面。这种分化意味着，熊猫债并非对国际评级担忧的替代，而是对它们的回应：当发行方希望多元化融资、而非在单一市场证明新故事时，往往选择评级框架与投资者基础最适配的市场。全球投资者仍会透过国际机构的镜头，审视这笔主权信用成色。

## 背后的财政算术

把镜头拉远，才能看清印尼为何需要这扇窗。2026年印尼国家预算的融资目标定为约 689.1万亿印尼盾（约 441.7亿美元，约 3130亿元人民币），广义债务融资目标约 832.2万亿印尼盾（约 533.5亿美元，约 3790亿元人民币）；截至 2026年3月底，政府债务总额达 9,920.4万亿印尼盾（约 6350亿元人民币）。在债务高企、市场对汇率敏感的当下，哪怕只是温和地向非美元借贷倾斜，也能帮助平滑再融资。从区域视角看，印尼并非孤例：在美元利率高位震荡、汇率波动加剧的年份，东盟多国纷纷拓展非美元融资渠道，熊猫债、本币债与离岸人民币债此起彼伏。印尼的尝试之所以受关注，是因为它把主权信用直接接入了全球第二大在岸债市，示范意义超出募资本身。在此之前，印尼今年已筹得约 70亿美元（约 5000亿元人民币）的非美元债务（涵盖欧元、日元与离岸人民币），创下纪录。熊猫债是把这条多元化曲线推进中国在岸市场的下一环。

## 中企信心的"隐性条款"

政治背书没能驱散所有顾虑。据《南华早报》报道，上月印尼中国商会致信普拉博沃总统，直指印尼存在"过度执法、监管过严乃至索贿"等问题，严重削弱中企长期投资信心。财政部长普尔巴亚·尤迪·萨德瓦在北京也坦承，部分投资者起初担心印尼财政可持续性与评级信号偏负面。这些话说明，规则的不确定性才是熊猫债背后真正的风险溢价。即便中方机构愿意认购，若雅加达不能改善营商可预期性，债券发行也只是把压力从外汇市场转移到制度信任上——纸面认购再多，也填不满信心的缺口。这封致总统的信，折射出的是"靠关系进门、靠规则留人"的普遍困境：中企愿意来，却未必愿意留；愿意投实体，却对制度性摩擦敏感。熊猫债的政治背书能打开募资窗口，却填不满长期信任的缺口——而这恰恰是雅加达最该回答的考题。

## 熊猫债不是万能钥匙

还需厘清的是，熊猫债不会取代印尼既有的融资工具。国内政府证券、美元全球债、欧元债与武士债仍是主权债务管理的支柱。本次募资将全额汇出境外用于一般性财政资金，并可转换为其他货币——这说明它提供的是财务灵活性，而非对人民币的单纯营运需求。对雅加达而言，真正值钱的不是一次 10亿美元（约 71亿元人民币）的募资，而是打开了未来银行、公用事业与基建类政府关联企业进入中国在岸市场的通道，让首笔交易成为可复制的模板。

## 真正的信号：融资车道的拓宽

因此，当英文世界容易把熊猫债视作新奇事件时，更值得关注的结构性信号在于：印尼正在利用中国境内市场拓宽自己的融资车道。这一年它已创下非美元借款纪录，而债务负担与汇率背景仍牵动市场视线。北京给出的政治背书降低了准入门槛，却也把"规则可预期"的考题摆到了雅加达面前。对越南、马来西亚等同样寻求融资多元化的东盟主权发行人而言，印尼的定价与认购表现提供了一份可参照的模板；若更多东盟国家跟进，中国在岸债市有望从偶发的融资通道，变为区域主权融资的常态选项。对雅加达而言，真正的考题不在路演，而在日常治理：简化投资许可、稳定执法尺度、保护少数股东权益，远比一次成功的发债更能积累信用。在东盟竞相争夺资本的当下，规则的可预期，才是最稀缺也最值钱的"主权评级"。东南亚国家欢迎中国资本，但资本只会流向规则透明、执行稳定的地方——这扇新开的窗，最终由制度而非路演来定夺开合。

## 延伸阅读

**熊猫债（Panda Bond）**：境外发行人、政府或多边机构在中国境内银行间债券市场发行的人民币计价债券，与在香港发行的"点心债"相对。

**本币结算框架（Local-Currency Settlement Framework）**：两国央行推动的直接使用本币进行贸易与投资结算的安排，可减少对第三国货币的依赖。

**风险溢价（Risk Premium）**：投资者因担忧政策、汇率或履约不确定性而要求的额外收益；在印尼案例中，主要表现为制度性不确定。

# Indonesia's Panda Bond Gets China's Backing: The Rules Game Behind Easier Financing

According to the South China Morning Post, on 23 July 2026 Indonesia issued its debut sovereign Panda Bond, raising 7 billion yuan (about US$1.033 billion). On its face this was a routine move by Jakarta to widen its funding lanes — yet two contexts make it sharper. First, the backing was unusually strong: the People's Bank of China asked underwriters to accelerate the application and the Ministry of Finance expressed "strong support." Second, domestic controversy in Indonesia over foreign-investment rules is leading investors to price "institutional uncertainty" as the real risk premium. Indonesia has already set a non-dollar borrowing record this year, while its debt load and currency backdrop still weigh on investors.

One sentence lays bare the subtext: the Panda Bond is a low-cost new funding window for Indonesia and a financial pawn China adds to the bilateral relationship; but what decides the deal's success is not political endorsement on the roadshow, but whether Jakarta can balance resource nationalism with openness to foreign capital.

## One bond, two currency logics

The core appeal of a Panda Bond is currency diversification. For Jakarta, the dollar-bond market is volatile and foreign-exchange exposure is costly; a yuan-denominated channel spreads funding sources and, through the China–Indonesia local-currency settlement framework, can be converted directly into rupiah to ease pressure on the currency. Indonesia also hopes to draw Chinese institutional investors, channelling some "China capital" from direct investment into the sovereign-debt market. For China, backing the issue embeds financial leverage in the bilateral tie — it gives the renminbi one more seat in ASEAN sovereign financing and extends the capital link from trade into the bond market. For Beijing, encouraging an ASEAN anchor to issue in renminbi is also a step in the currency's internationalisation. Each sovereign Panda Bond enlarges the offshore footprint of the yuan and lets Chinese institutional investors test appetite for regional sovereign credit. It is a quiet but strategic move to weave the renminbi into Asia's financial plumbing — one that complements, rather than rivals, the region's existing dollar-based funding architecture.

This is not Indonesia's first dip into China's capital market. In October 2025 it issued its maiden offshore renminbi bond of 6 billion yuan; in February 2026 it added another 9.25 billion yuan. The Panda Bond is the crucial step that pushes this yuan funding curve from offshore into onshore — tapping a deeper onshore pool while paving the way for later issuance by state-linked firms.

## The deal in detail: 7 billion and 2.4 times covered

The maiden structure was carefully engineered. The offering split into two tranches: a 3-year bond of 5.6 billion yuan (US$826.4 million) at a 1.90 per cent coupon, and a 5-year bond of 1.4 billion yuan (US$206.6 million) at 2.19 per cent. Peak orders reached about 17 billion yuan (roughly US$2.51 billion), an overall bid-to-cover of roughly 2.4 times, and final pricing came below the initial 2.3–2.5 per cent guidance — evidence that demand was strong enough to compress issuance cost. China Lianhe Credit Rating assigned an AAA/stable onshore rating, smoothing market acceptance. The more telling number is the quota: Indonesia has been cleared to issue up to 30 billion yuan (about US$4.4 billion) of Panda Bonds over the next two years — clearly signalling a recurring programme, not a one-off curiosity. Bank of China led as bookrunner, with CICC, CITIC Securities, DBS Bank (China) and ICBC as joint leads.

## The rating's "two faces"

But the rating hides a fissure. Onshore, Lianhe's AAA opened the door to China's domestic market; offshore, the three global agencies paint a different picture — Moody's rates Indonesia Baa2, S&P and Fitch both BBB, and Moody's and Fitch cut their outlooks to negative in early 2026. This split means the Panda Bond is not a substitute for international-rating concerns but a response to them: when an issuer wants diversification rather than to prove a new story in a single market, it tends to choose the market whose rating framework and investor base fit best. Global investors will still read the sovereign credit through the lens of the international agencies.

## The fiscal arithmetic behind it

Step back and the need for the window becomes clear. Indonesia's 2026 state budget sets a financing target of about 689.1 trillion rupiah (roughly US$44.17 billion), with gross debt-financing targeted at about 832.2 trillion rupiah (about US$53.35 billion); by end-March 2026 government debt totalled 9,920.4 trillion rupiah (about US$635 billion). With debt high and markets sensitive to currency swings, even a mild tilt toward non-dollar borrowing helps smooth refinancing. Before this, Indonesia had already raised about US$7 billion in non-dollar debt this year — in euros, yen and offshore yuan — a record. The Panda Bond pushes that diversification curve into China's onshore market.

## The "hidden clause" of Chinese investor confidence

Political backing did not dissolve every concern. Per the SCMP report, last month the China Chamber of Commerce in Indonesia wrote to President Prabowo Subianto, flagging "overly stringent regulations, over-enforcement and even corruption and extortion by competent authorities" that had undermined long-term Chinese investment confidence. Finance Minister Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa, on his Beijing trip, also acknowledged that some investors were initially worried about Indonesia's fiscal sustainability and negative rating signals. The message is clear: the real risk premium on the bond is not market volatility but institutional uncertainty. Even with Chinese institutions willing to subscribe, if Jakarta cannot make its business environment predictable, the bond merely shifts pressure from the FX market onto institutional trust — no amount of paper subscriptions fills a confidence gap.

## A Panda Bond is no master key

It should also be clear that the Panda Bond will not replace Indonesia's established tools. Domestic government securities, dollar global bonds, eurobonds and Samurai bonds remain the backbone of sovereign debt management. Proceeds from this issue will be remitted offshore in full for general budget purposes and may be converted into other currencies — evidence that it offers financial flexibility rather than a pure renminbi operational need. For Jakarta, what is truly valuable is not a single US$1 billion (about 7.1 billion yuan) raise but the channel it opens for Indonesian banks, utilities and infrastructure-related state-linked firms to enter China's onshore market later, turning the debut into a replicable template.

## The real signal: widening the funding lanes

So while the English-language world may treat the Panda Bond as a curiosity, the more important structural signal is that Indonesia is using China's onshore market to widen its own funding lanes. This year it has already set a non-dollar borrowing record, while its debt load and currency backdrop still weigh on investor minds. Beijing's political backing lowers the entry barrier, yet also puts the "predictable rules" exam in front of Jakarta. Southeast Asian states welcome Chinese capital, yet capital ultimately flows where rules are transparent and enforcement is stable — For other ASEAN sovereign issuers such as Vietnam and Malaysia that are also seeking funding diversification, Indonesia's pricing and cover ratios offer a reusable template; if more regional sovereigns follow, China's onshore market could shift from an occasional channel into a routine option for regional sovereign financing. For Jakarta, the real exam is not the roadshow but day-to-day governance: streamlining investment permits, stabilising enforcement, protecting minority shareholders — these build credibility far more than a single successful sale. As ASEAN economies compete for capital, predictable rules are the scarcest and most valuable "sovereign rating" of all. Southeast Asian states welcome Chinese capital, yet capital ultimately flows where rules are transparent and enforcement is stable — this newly opened window is finally opened or closed by institutions, not by roadshows.

## Further reading

**Panda Bond:** A yuan-denominated bond issued inside China's onshore interbank market by foreign governments, multilateral institutions or corporations — distinct from "Dim Sum" bonds issued offshore in Hong Kong.

**Local-currency settlement framework:** A central-bank arrangement letting two countries settle trade and investment directly in their own currencies, reducing reliance on a third currency.

**Risk premium:** The extra return investors demand to compensate for policy, currency or default uncertainty; in Indonesia's case it is expressed mainly as institutional uncertainty.

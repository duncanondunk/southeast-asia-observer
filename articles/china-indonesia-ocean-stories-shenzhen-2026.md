# 从海洋看中国—印尼：一场深圳故事会串起二十年蓝色合作

9月1日，一场名为"海洋发展的蓝色奇迹"的中国—印尼友好故事会在深圳举行。来自两国高校、智库与企业的代表，以海洋调查、防灾减灾、资源开发、生态保护与人才培养为主题，讲述了合作与民间友好的鲜活故事。在两国关系常被大国博弈叙事覆盖的当下，这场聚会提醒人们：中印尼的联结，最早也是最深的一条，是海。

## 一场以海为媒的聚会

故事会由两国高校、智库与企业代表共同参与，特邀十位讲述者围绕海洋调查与防灾减灾、海洋资源开发、海洋生态保护、渔业与产业标准对接、海洋教育与人才培养五个方向分享亲身经历。自然资源部第一海洋研究所、印尼苏迪曼将军大学、印尼国家研究与创新署中印尼中心、中国海油等机构代表先后发言。形式上是"讲故事"，实质上是对二十年合作成果的一次民间盘点。

## 二十年联合科考的底子

中印尼的海洋合作并非临时起意。自2006年"SITE计划"启动以来，双方在卡里马塔海峡等海域开展了十九个联合调查航次，共同监测印尼贯穿流等关键数据，为应对全球气候变化提供科学支撑。印尼苏迪曼将军大学教师穆克蒂表示，两国科学家通过二十年联合观测与深度互信，更深刻地体会到合作的重要性。从一次次出海到长期数据共享，合作的模式已从单一项目拓展为战略协作。

## 从单一项目到长期战略

中国"一带一路"倡议与印尼"全球海洋支点"战略高度契合、深度对接，这是两国海洋合作能够走深走实的政策底座。与会者指出，早期合作多停留在具体项目，如今已转向常态化监测、标准对接与机制化交流。当科研合作沉淀为制度，它便不再依赖个别人物的热情，而能在政府更迭与外部环境波动中保持韧性。

## 红树林里的气候合作

印尼红树林面积占全球23%，保护意义举足轻重。2024年，两国在"中国—印尼海洋气候中心"合作框架下，于印尼芝拉扎市开展实地调研、编撰红树林保护与修复手册，并将红树林科普引入当地学校。从论文到课堂，气候合作被翻译成社区能理解的语言。这种"把科学种进土地"的路径，正是南南合作区别于单向援助的地方。

## 能源与民生：海油的另一面

中国海油不仅是印尼最大的天然气生产商之一，也主动参与修筑码头、种植红树林、职业培训与修建校舍等民生项目，回馈当地社区。印尼国家研究与创新署研究员费萨尔指出，印尼坐拥广袤海洋资源，中国拥有先进海洋技术，双方可以携手为海洋可持续开发探寻可行方案。能源合作若只算商业账，难以持久；把民生计入，才是长期主义的写法。

## 青年与人才：蓝色梦想的接力

正在中国海洋大学攻读博士学位的印尼青年拉菲达，分享了她参与海洋科研项目的经历，认为持续投入联合科考、技术转移与人才培养，是守护共同海洋的基础。青年是合作的未来变量。当印尼青年在中国实验室里成长，当中国叙事被印尼同行用母语复述，民间友好的代际传递才有载体。

## 为什么是"故事"而不是"声明"

与动辄发布联合声明的官方交往不同，故事会刻意选择了"讲述"这种柔软形态。它把宏大的战略语言，还原为一个个具体的人、一次次日夜兼程的科考、一所所建起的校舍。在信息高度极化、国家间信任稀缺的今天，可感知的个体经验，往往比文件更能穿越偏见。这也是公共外交最朴素的逻辑。

## 民间叙事的示范效应

中印尼海洋合作的启示在于：真正的区域合作，未必始于高层协议，也可以始于一群科学家、教师和企业的持续往来。当民间叙事足够厚实，它反过来会为官方关系提供缓冲与底气。对东南亚其他点对点合作而言，深圳这场故事会提供的，不只是一份成果清单，更是一种可复制的叙事方法。

## 延伸阅读

**印尼贯穿流（Indonesian Throughflow）**：赤道太平洋与印度洋之间最重要的深层海流通道，其长期监测对理解全球洋流与气候系统具有关键科学价值。

**全球海洋支点（Global Maritime Axis）**：印尼总统佐科提出的国家战略，强调以海洋强国定位重塑印尼的地缘与经济角色，与中国"一带一路"在海上互联互通上高度对接。

**中国—印尼海洋气候中心（China–Indonesia Ocean Climate Center）**：两国在2024年框架下设立的对口合作平台，聚焦红树林保护、气候适应与科普教育等务实议题。

# Ocean as the Tie: A Shenzhen Storytelling Session Reframes Two Decades of China–Indonesia Blue Cooperation

On 1 September, a China–Indonesia friendship storytelling session titled "The Blue Miracle of Ocean Development" was held in Shenzhen. Representatives from universities, think tanks and enterprises of both countries shared first-hand stories of cooperation and people-to-people friendship across marine survey, disaster prevention, resource development, ecological protection and talent cultivation. At a time when the bilateral relationship is often framed through great-power rivalry, the gathering was a reminder: the earliest and deepest link between China and Indonesia is the sea.

## A gathering anchored in the ocean

The session brought together participants from universities, think tanks and companies, with ten storytellers speaking on five themes: marine survey and disaster reduction, resource development, ecological protection, fisheries and industry-standard alignment, and marine education and talent. Representatives from the First Institute of Oceanography of the Ministry of Natural Resources, Universitas Jenderal Sudirman, the China–Indonesia Center of Indonesia's National Research and Innovation Agency, and CNOOC addressed the audience. The format was storytelling; the substance was a civilian stocktaking of two decades of cooperation.

## Two decades of joint expeditions

China–Indonesia marine cooperation was no impromptu idea. Since the SITE programme began in 2006, the two sides have conducted nineteen joint survey voyages in waters such as the Karimata Strait, jointly monitoring the Indonesian Throughflow and other key data that support global climate research. A lecturer from Universitas Jenderal Sudirman noted that two decades of joint observation and deep mutual trust had made the importance of cooperation concrete. From single cruises to continuous data sharing, cooperation has evolved from isolated projects into strategic collaboration.

## From projects to strategy

China's Belt and Road Initiative and Indonesia's Global Maritime Axis strategy are highly compatible and deeply aligned — the policy bedrock that allows marine cooperation to deepen. Participants observed that early cooperation centred on specific projects, whereas today it has shifted toward regularised monitoring, standards alignment and institutionalised exchange. When scientific cooperation settles into institutions, it no longer depends on individual enthusiasm and can endure political transitions and external volatility.

## Climate cooperation in the mangroves

Indonesia holds about 23% of the world's mangrove area, making its protection globally significant. In 2024, under the China–Indonesia Ocean Climate Center framework, the two countries conducted field research in Cilacap, compiled a mangrove protection and restoration manual, and introduced mangrove science into local schools. From papers to classrooms, climate cooperation is translated into a language communities understand. This "planting science in the soil" approach is precisely where South–South cooperation differs from one-way aid.

## Energy and livelihoods: CNOOC's other face

CNOOC is not only one of Indonesia's largest natural-gas producers; it has also built jetties, planted mangroves, provided vocational training and constructed school buildings to give back to local communities. A researcher from Indonesia's National Research and Innovation Agency pointed out that Indonesia commands vast marine resources while China possesses advanced marine technology, and the two can jointly explore viable solutions for sustainable ocean development. Energy cooperation that counts only commercial ledgers will not last; factoring in livelihoods is the grammar of long-termism.

## Youth and talent: passing the blue baton

Rafida, an Indonesian doctoral candidate at Ocean University of China, shared her experience in marine research projects and argued that sustained investment in joint surveys, technology transfer and talent cultivation is the foundation for protecting the shared ocean. Youth are the future variable of cooperation. When Indonesian youth grow in Chinese laboratories, and when Chinese narratives are retold by Indonesian colleagues in their own language, the intergenerational transmission of people-to-people friendship gains a载体.

## Why stories, not statements

Unlike official exchanges that routinely issue joint statements, the storytelling session deliberately chose the softer form of "telling". It reduces grand strategic language to concrete individuals, night-and-day surveys and schools built one by one. In an era of polarised information and scarce interstate trust, perceptible personal experience often travels further than documents. This is also the most elementary logic of public diplomacy.

## The demonstration effect of civilian narrative

The lesson of China–Indonesia marine cooperation is that genuine regional cooperation need not begin with high-level agreements; it can start with the persistent exchanges of scientists, teachers and enterprises. When civilian narratives grow thick enough, they in turn cushion and undergird official relations. For other point-to-point partnerships across Southeast Asia, this Shenzhen session offers not merely a results list but a replicable narrative method.

## Further reading

**Indonesian Throughflow**: The most important deep-current channel linking the equatorial Pacific and the Indian Ocean; its long-term monitoring is scientifically vital for understanding global circulation and climate systems.

**Global Maritime Axis**: An Indonesian strategy advanced by President Joko Widodo that repositions the country as a maritime power, aligning closely with China's Belt and Road on maritime connectivity.

**China–Indonesia Ocean Climate Center**: A counterpart platform established in 2024 focusing on mangroves, climate adaptation and science education.

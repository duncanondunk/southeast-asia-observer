# 印尼（Indonesia）拟停止向“不尊重”印尼的国家供能

10月22日，印尼下游产业与能源转型统筹部长巴赫里尔（Bahlil Lahadalia）在雅加达（Jakarta）一座天然气生产设施投产仪式上放出重话：政府将重新审查与“不尊重”印尼的国家签署的能源出口协议，并“毫不犹豫地考虑停止出口”。他没有点名。这番话带着普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）时代典型的资源民族主义腔调，但在强硬措辞背后，是东南亚（Southeast Asia）最大经济体对其自然资源运用方式的一次刻意重校。

巴赫里尔同时身兼能源与矿产资源部长，并执掌戈尔卡党（Golkar）。他把这一立场包装为主权诉求：国家间分歧应通过对话与相互尊重解决，而不是像他所说“表现得像个毫无教养的亲戚”。这句生动的话掩盖了一个严肃信号——印尼正告诉合作伙伴，获取其资源如今要看政治表现，这与此前二十年以价格和交易为导向的能源外交截然不同。

## 数字：谁在依赖印尼的天然气

威胁并非空谈。印尼是区域主要的液化天然气（LNG）出口国。据印尼中央统计局数据，2025年印尼向日本出口约380万吨天然气、向中国约379万吨、向新加坡约322万吨、向韩国约227万吨；较小规模则流向泰国（约29.1万吨）与菲律宾（约13.8万吨）。其中新加坡最为脆弱：其电力几乎全部来自进口天然气，相当部分经管道来自印尼，而相关进口合约将在2028年底到期。当一位部长说可能不再续签这些合约，这个城市国家自然会侧耳倾听。

这些数字绝非无足轻重。对新加坡而言，印尼管道天然气是家庭用电的支柱；对日本与韩国而言，印尼液化天然气有助于降低对中东（the Middle East）供应的依赖。依赖方如此之广，恰恰让一句含糊的威胁分量十足——它迫使每个买家自问：自己会不会就是那个“不被尊重”的对象。

## 新加坡为何最紧张

新加坡的脆弱是结构性的。其约95%的电力来自天然气，其中相当大比例经跨境管道从印尼与马来西亚（Malaysia）输入。雅加达与新加坡还在谈判印尼向新加坡出口低碳电力——这是一项旗舰跨境项目，新加坡能源市场管理局已向六个印尼电力进口项目发放有条件执照，合计容量约3吉瓦。巴赫里尔已表示，相关谈判因定价分歧而陷入停滞。把天然气续约与电力定价纳入“尊重”叙事，等于把新加坡原以为属于技术层面的基础设施政治化了。

新加坡其实一直在低调避险——液化天然气如今已占其天然气进口的一半以上，第二座液化天然气码头也在建设中。但管道不可能一夜之间改道，而2028年的到期悬崖正步步逼近。雅加达清楚自己在短期内握有更强筹码，也正在利用这种不对称。

## 从“不削减配额”到“生存模式”的政策急转弯

这一转向来得相当突然。仅仅五个月前，今年5月，巴赫里尔还向油气生产商保证，2026年的出口配额不会削减。如今他却大谈“生存模式”。这一反转紧随总统普拉博沃的定调：10月21日，他将国家自然资源比作血液，警告身体若持续失血便难以存活，并宣称“强者生存，弱者消亡”。这不只是姿态。它标志着政策从出口导向的资源管理，转向以国内优先的分配逻辑，能源被当作战略资产，而非商业商品。

这一反转也暴露了竞选语言与执政现实之间的落差。普拉博沃的“生存”论调，与国内围绕燃油短缺的压力相呼应——中东冲突推高全球能源价格后，巴厘岛以及爪哇、苏门答腊多地都出现过加油站排长队。把出口当作国内短缺的替罪羊，在政治上顺手，即便二者的因果关联其实薄弱。

## 烟霾争端：被点燃的邻国积怨

这番警告并非凭空而来。它出现在印尼与新加坡、马来西亚围绕苏门答腊与加里曼丹林火所引发跨境烟霾关系紧张之际。新加坡已向十家涉嫌助长林火的印尼企业发出法律通知，并质询七家上市种植园公司。将能源威胁与烟霾争端合观，可见同一套逻辑：乐意把相互依存当作筹码，用于处理此前停留在技术渠道的纠纷。巴赫里尔抛向未点名邻国的“毫无教养的亲戚”嘲讽，与其说是外交，不如说是警告。

关键在于，烟霾与能源两条战线正在合流。新加坡对印尼企业发出法律通知、雅加达以天然气相威胁，是彼此的提醒——东盟（ASEAN）内的大小国家彼此缠绕。这究竟会固化成更广泛的裂痕，取决于领导人把这些纠纷留在技术工作组，还是任其外溢为民族主义表演。

## 把资源当武器的代价

历史告诫人们须谨慎。印尼不是第一次走这条路：其镍矿出口禁令重塑了全球供应链，却也招来法律挑战、拖慢了下游投资。把天然气政治武器化，可能把买家——新加坡、日本、韩国——推向多元化（卡塔尔、澳大利亚、美国液化天然气），并侵蚀印尼作为可靠供应国的声誉，而此刻它正为本国能源转型向外资招手。对一个高喊“生存”的政府而言，讽刺意味尖锐：正是这些出口带来的收入，支撑着普拉博沃看重的国内项目。资源民族主义在国内叫好，作为对外谈判手段，却是双刃剑。

对雅加达而言，更聪明的做法是让能源贸易与外交摩擦脱钩，利用2028年续约窗口通过谈判而非威胁争取更好条件。但当下氛围偏爱高调表演。眼下，印尼的邻国最好为一个不再那么慷慨的供应国做准备——并加速推进雅加达正无意间鼓励的多元化。

## 延伸阅读

**液化天然气（LNG）**：在超低温下液化的天然气，便于远洋运输；印尼是对日本、中国、新加坡、韩国的主要供应国。

**资源民族主义（Resource Nationalism）**：主张将自然资源收益优先留在本国、并以资源作为谈判筹码的政策取向，常见于矿产与能源领域。

**跨境烟霾（Transboundary Haze）**：印尼苏门答腊与加里曼丹林火产生的烟尘随风飘至新加坡、马来西亚等国，是东盟长期未解的环境争端。

# Indonesia's Energy Leverage: A Warning to the "Disrespectful"

On October 22, Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Downstreaming and Energy Transition, Bahlil Lahadalia, used the inauguration of a gas facility in Jakarta to deliver a pointed message: Jakarta would review its energy export agreements with countries that "do not respect Indonesia," and would "not hesitate to consider stopping exports" to them. He named no one. The rhetoric carried the hallmark of Prabowo-era resource nationalism, but behind the bluster lies a deliberate recalibration of how Southeast Asia's largest economy deploys its natural wealth.

Bahlil, who also serves as energy and mineral resources minister and chairs the Golkar Party, framed the stance as sovereignty: disputes between nations should be settled through dialogue and mutual respect, not, as he put it, by "behaving like a relative who has no manners." The colourful phrasing obscured a serious point. Indonesia is telling its partners that access to its resources is now conditional on political conduct — a significant departure from the transactional, price-driven energy diplomacy of the past two decades.

## The numbers: who depends on Indonesian gas

The threat is not empty. Indonesia is a major regional liquefied natural gas (LNG) exporter. According to Indonesia's statistics agency, in 2025 it shipped roughly 3.8 million tonnes of gas to Japan, 3.79 million to China, 3.22 million to Singapore and 2.27 million to South Korea. Smaller volumes reached Thailand (about 291,000 tonnes) and the Philippines (about 138,000 tonnes). Singapore is the most exposed: nearly all of its electricity comes from imported natural gas, much of it piped from Indonesia under contracts that expire at the end of 2028. When a minister says he may not renew those contracts, the city-state listens.

These are not trivial volumes. For Singapore, Indonesian piped gas is the backbone of household electricity; for Japan and South Korea, Indonesian LNG helps diversify away from Middle Eastern supply. The breadth of dependents is precisely why a vague threat carries weight: it forces every buyer to wonder whether it is the "disrespected" party.

## Why Singapore is most exposed

Singapore's vulnerability is structural. About 95 percent of its power is generated from natural gas, a large share of it piped from Indonesia and Malaysia through cross-border pipelines. Jakarta and Singapore are also negotiating Indonesia's export of low-carbon electricity — a flagship cross-border project under which Singapore's Energy Market Authority has granted conditional licences to six Indonesian import projects with a combined capacity of about 3 gigawatts. Bahlil has already said those talks are stalled over pricing. Tying gas renewal and power pricing to a narrative of "respect" injects politics into infrastructure that Singapore had assumed was technocratic.

Singapore has been quietly hedging — LNG now accounts for more than half of its gas imports, and a second LNG terminal is under construction. But pipelines cannot be rerouted overnight, and the 2028 cliff is approaching. Jakarta knows it holds the stronger hand in the short term, and is using that asymmetry.

## From "no quota cuts" to "survival mode"

The shift is strikingly recent. Just five months ago, in May, Bahlil assured oil and gas producers that export quotas for 2026 would not be reduced. Now he speaks of "survival mode." The about-face follows President Prabowo Subianto's own framing: on October 21 he compared the nation's natural resources to blood, warning that a body cannot survive if it keeps losing blood, and declared that "the strong survive; the weak perish." This is not merely optics. It signals a pivot from export-led resource management toward domestic-first allocation, with energy treated as a strategic asset rather than a commercial commodity.

The reversal also exposes the gap between campaign rhetoric and governing reality. Prabowo's "survival" language dovetails with domestic pressure over fuel shortages — after the Middle East conflict lifted global energy prices, Bali and parts of Java and Sumatra saw queues at petrol stations. Scapegoating exports for domestic shortfalls is politically convenient, even if the causal link is thin.

## The haze dispute: lingering neighbourly grievances

The warning did not land in a vacuum. It comes amid escalating tension with Singapore and Malaysia over transboundary haze from fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan. Singapore has issued legal notices to ten Indonesian companies suspected of contributing to the fires and queried seven listed plantation firms. Read together, Jakarta's energy threat and the haze row form a single pattern: a willingness to use interdependence as leverage in disputes that previously stayed in technical channels. The "ill-mannered relative" jibe Bahlil aimed at unnamed neighbours reads less like diplomacy than a warning.

Crucially, the haze and energy fronts are converging. Singapore's legal notices to Indonesian firms and Jakarta's gas warnings are reciprocal reminders that large and small ASEAN states are entangled. Whether this hardens into a broader rift depends on whether leaders keep these disputes in technical working groups or let them spill into nationalist theatrics.

## The cost of weaponising resources

History counsels caution. Indonesia has walked this road before: its nickel ore export ban reshaped global supply chains but also invited legal challenges and slowed downstream investment. Turning gas into a political weapon risks pushing buyers — Singapore, Japan, South Korea — toward diversification (Qatar, Australia, US LNG) and erodes Indonesia's reputation as a reliable supplier, just as it courts foreign capital for its own energy transition. For a government invoking "survival," the irony is sharp: the revenue from those very exports helps fund the domestic programmes Prabowo prizes. Resource nationalism plays well at home; as a negotiating tactic abroad, it is a double-edged sword.

The smarter play for Jakarta would be to keep energy commerce separate from diplomatic spats, using the 2028 renewal window to extract better terms through negotiation rather than threat. But the current mood favours spectacle. For now, Indonesia's neighbours would be wise to plan for a less generous supplier — and to accelerate the diversification Jakarta is inadvertently encouraging.

## Further reading

**Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG)**: Natural gas cooled to a liquid state for ocean transport; Indonesia is a major supplier to Japan, China, Singapore and South Korea.

**Resource Nationalism**: The policy impulse to keep natural-resource rents at home and wield resources as leverage, common in mining and energy.

**Transboundary Haze**: Smoke from fires in Sumatra and Kalimantan that drifts to Singapore and Malaysia, a long-running ASEAN environmental dispute.

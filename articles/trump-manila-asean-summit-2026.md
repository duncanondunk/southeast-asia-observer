## 马尼拉的邀请函：一次精心安排的"在场"

菲律宾官方10月14日证实，美国总统唐纳德·特朗普将于11月16日前往马尼拉出席东盟（ASEAN）峰会，随后转赴中国深圳参加11月17日至19日的亚太经合组织（APEC）领导人非正式会议。白宫称，此行旨在展现美国对东南亚盟友的承诺，并推进美国的经济与安全目标。

对小马科斯（Ferdinand Marcos Jr.）政府而言，这是一次期待已久的"确认"——在美国国会对东南亚的关注起伏不定之际，国家元首层面的出席是最直观的战略背书。东盟轮值主席国菲律宾也得以借主场之利，把南海、安全合作与经济议题嵌入地区议程。

## 从吉隆坡到马尼拉：连续出席意味着什么

特朗普去年曾赴吉隆坡出席东盟峰会，本任期内的连续出席打破了"美国领导人习惯性缺席"的刻板印象。与第一任期时仅2017年赴马尼拉参会相比，这一节奏转变颇为明显。

对美国而言，此行也是在南海局势持续紧张背景下的一次姿态宣示。菲律宾海岸警卫队与中国海警近期在争议水域的摩擦仍在继续，华盛顿希望借助峰会平台重申对盟友的防务承诺，而不必单独发表对华强硬言论。白宫官员更直白地表示，峰会之行要"展现美国对东南亚盟友的承诺"。

## 承诺与现实的落差：东南亚的"在场"焦虑

不过，仅凭一次峰会出席难以消弭东南亚国家对美承诺稳定性的深层疑虑。过去数年，美国高官缺席东盟部长级会议、贸易议程推进迟缓、地区经济倡议资金落实不足，都让"美国回来了"的说法显得空洞。

东盟国家真正关心的是可预期的制度性接触：自贸协定、基础设施融资、供应链安全、气候与公共卫生合作。特朗普若能从马尼拉带回具体成果，比任何外交辞令都更有说服力；否则，"在场"很容易退化为一次性的拍照外交。

## 峰会之后：APEC与深圳之行

东盟峰会结束后，特朗普将前往深圳与中国国家主席习近平会面，这将是两人年内第三次面对面会谈。从马尼拉到深圳的紧凑行程，勾勒出美国在亚洲同时操演"安抚盟友"与"管控对手"的双重剧本。

分析人士指出，东南亚国家并不愿在中美之间选边站队。它们希望美国"在场"，也希望中美竞争不要演变为区域冲突。特朗普能否在两者之间取得平衡，将决定马尼拉之行的真实分量。

## Manila's invitation: a carefully choreographed 'presence'

Philippine officials confirmed on 14 October that US President Donald Trump will travel to Manila on 16 November to attend the ASEAN summit, before heading to Shenzhen in China for the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting from 17 to 19 November. The White House said the trip is intended to demonstrate Washington's commitment to Southeast Asian allies and to advance US economic and national-security goals.

For the Marcos administration, the confirmation is long-awaited reassurance. With US congressional attention toward Southeast Asia fluctuating, a head-of-state visit is the most visible strategic endorsement. As ASEAN chair for 2026, the Philippines can also use the host's advantage to insert maritime, security and economic issues into the regional agenda.

## From Kuala Lumpur to Manila: what consecutive attendance signals

Trump attended last year's ASEAN summit in Kuala Lumpur, and his back-to-back presence this term breaks the stereotype of an American leader habitually skipping the annual gathering. The contrast with his first term, when he only travelled to Manila for the 2017 summit, is notable.

For Washington, the visit is also a gesture at a moment of continued tension in the South China Sea. Recent run-ins between the Philippine Coast Guard and China's maritime forces are still fresh, and the White House wants to use the summit platform to reaffirm its defence commitments to an ally without having to deliver a standalone harsh message on China. A White House official put it bluntly: the trip is meant to "demonstrate US commitment to Southeast Asian allies."

## The gap between promise and reality: Southeast Asia's presence anxiety

Yet a single summit appearance cannot erase the deeper worry in Southeast Asia about the consistency of US commitments. In recent years, the absence of senior American officials from ASEAN ministerial meetings, slow progress on trade initiatives and thin funding for regional economic programmes have made the slogan "America is back" sound hollow.

What ASEAN members really want is predictable institutional engagement: free-trade agreements, infrastructure finance, supply-chain security, climate and public-health cooperation. If Trump returns from Manila with concrete deliverables, it will matter far more than any diplomatic phrase; otherwise, "presence" risks becoming a one-off photo opportunity.

## After the summit: APEC and Shenzhen

After ASEAN, Trump will fly to Shenzhen for a meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping, their third face-to-face conversation this year. The tightly scheduled itinerary from Manila to Shenzhen sketches out Washington's dual script in Asia: reassuring allies while managing a rival.

Analysts note that Southeast Asian states do not want to choose sides. They want the United States to be present, but they also want the US-China rivalry to stop short of regional conflict. Whether Trump can balance the two will determine the real weight of the Manila stop.

# 省级侨务会议密集召开：当「侨」变成地方发展的施工图

据《中国新闻》报报道，8月下旬至11月中旬，从广东、浙江到青海、内蒙古，至少13个省级地区召开侨务工作会议。时间如此集中，绝非偶然。全国层面的侨务方针已经定调，眼下的景象是它被翻译成各省的实施蓝图——各自有品牌、有指标、有重点人群。这件事的影响远不止于中国境内：华侨华人大多生活在东南亚（Southeast Asia），对当地而言，值得追问的已不是北京（Beijing）怎么说侨务，而是省级政府究竟怎么做。

## 一场接一场：从方针到省级施工图

报道提到的会议包括：8月26日河南、8月27日海南和重庆、9月10日河北、9月29日江西、9月30日山西、10月13日吉林、上海和浙江、10月16日内蒙古、10月20日广东、11月4日青海、11月10日山东。广东那场会议同时挂着「侨助经济高质量发展工作推进会」的名头；11月4日广州举办侨商侨企投资专场，7组穗企侨团签下合作意向，方向涵盖出海服务与产业协作。

会议本身不产出政策，但如此密集的日程说明，侨务已从过去那种几年一次全国大会的象征性议程，变成地方年度工作里的常规动作。全国定方向、省级出抓手，这套传导机制一旦运转起来，其实际影响往往比顶层文件更直接。也因此，真正值得读的不是会议通稿的套话，而是各省写进自己那张施工图里的东西。

## 品牌与赛道：同一个词，不同的地方考题

广东持续打造「侨兴南粤」，做深做实以侨促贸、以侨引资、以侨引智；浙江立足共同富裕示范区，擦亮「侨兴浙江」，推进「侨创未来」「侨助共富」；海南围绕自贸港「45432」发展架构对接侨商侨企需求。河南要为侨胞留住「乡愁」，山西要把根祖文化、关公文化、晋商文化转化为侨务资源，河北强调「根、魂、梦」主线，山东着眼文明互鉴与对外开放，青海则办座谈会、读书会、音乐会，创作带有地方特色的「侨字号」节目。

暨南大学（Jinan University）国际关系学院／华侨华人研究院教授陈奕平接受采访时说，应完善「大侨务」体系和机制，让侨胞分享中国式现代化的机遇与红利；地方政府尤其是重点侨乡虽成绩亮眼，却面临大国关系、地缘政治和复杂舆论环境的挑战，需要精准掌握侨情、建好人才引进与产业项目对接平台。这番话点出了要害：沿海地区要的是资本与人才，文化大省要的是情感认同，西部省份要的是可见度。同一个「侨务」，装进的是各自的发展议程。

这当然是合理的分权，但也埋着一个委托与代理的难题。当成绩被换算成签约金额、落地项目与招商数字，「为侨服务」很容易悄悄滑向「向侨取材」。口号一致，激励不同，结果就会不同——这是观察省级侨务时最不能省掉的一层。

## 司法划线：同日发布的典型案例说明了什么

就在报道刊出的11月23日，最高人民法院与中国侨联联合发布侨益司法保护典型案例。这份名单不是花边新闻，而是给省级品牌设定了底线。

厦门一案中，位于思明区的侨房由侨胞继承，但因历史原因，吴某一家在此居住70余年，不仅装修维护，还把外墙租给公司悬挂大型广告牌；原租户与房管所的租赁合同早在2009年终止，继承人2025年才提起诉讼。一审判决限期腾还并支付占用费、拆除广告牌；二审期间，法院启用「五侨」协同机制，依托侨益保护工作站与海丝中心，会同侨联、社区和综治中心，通过搬迁帮扶、房屋安置、合理腾退期限与激励补偿，一揽子化解了延续十余年的多个纠纷。

另两案同样具体：黄山一家侨企因东莞公司拖欠装饰纸货款38万余元诉至安徽歙县法院，法院以「以保促调」促成限期支付、暂不解保的调解协议；广西某国营华侨农场身兼市场经营主体与归侨侨眷安置保障双重属性，执行法院协调帮助其增强清偿能力，最终提前清偿债务，同时减少对侨胞职工基本生计的影响。

这些案子提醒地方：产权与合同环境本身就是招商的一部分。若一位侨胞继承人拿不回房子、一家侨企收不回货款，再漂亮的「服务侨胞」也只是口号。值得注意的是方法——协同而非命令，调解而非强推。它给「大侨务」提供了一个不太起眼却很实在的注脚。

## 新生代是真正的增量：从慰问到平台

吉林提出持续关注华裔新生代、少数民族侨胞；江西提出用好红色文化、客家文化、陶瓷文化资源，开展海外华裔青少年江西之旅；重庆强调深化对外民间交往。侨海（中国）创新发展研究院执行院长邢菁华的建议更直接：一方面活化江门赤坎古镇这类侨文化遗产，拉近情感联结；另一方面跳出传统慰问帮扶，面向海外侨商与青年人才搭建常态化对接平台，把侨务资源和地方产业、科创需求精准匹配，让侨胞愿意回来、留得住、发展好。

为什么这对东南亚尤其重要？因为所谓新生代，大多在当地出生、在当地受教育，中文未必流利，他们与中国的关系不靠记忆，而靠算账：签证、市场、职业机会。单靠情感联络，未必经得起一位马来西亚（Malaysia）或印尼（Indonesia）青年专业人士的现实考量。搭建平台的方向是对的，但平台必须双向——如果它只是为某个省抽取资本与人才，在吉隆坡（Kuala Lumpur）或雅加达（Jakarta）就会被读成有条件的示好，甚至被当成培养亲己群体的动作。

## 东南亚的镜子：地方热情在海外如何被阅读

广东、福建、海南是通往东南亚的主要出发地，它们的侨务动作最先被区域社会感知。省际竞争能带来活力，也会带来重复：相似的侨商对接活动在多省轮番上演，争夺同一批企业家。而在部分东南亚国家，有组织的侨社动员容易被安全化解读，省级官员高调「争侨」，可能被重新叙述成一种跨越国界的延伸。

反过来看，同日发布的司法案例与「大侨务」协同，讲的恰恰是服务与维权——这两件事很难被指责。如果省级侨务停留在保护产权、执行合同、维持教育与文化交流、让回国成为可选项而非任务，它反而能降低疑虑；一旦变成忠诚与投资指标的竞赛，就会印证此前分析过的那种「第五纵队」叙事，给本来中性的人员往来加上不必要的问号。

衡量这一轮省级侨务的成败，别数开了多少会、创了多少品牌、签了多少意向书。看厦门那位继承人能否拿回房子，看黄山那家侨企能否收到货款，看一位华裔马来西亚青年能否找到一个不必在两种归属之间二选一的参与理由。做到了，侨务才是服务；做不到，施工图再漂亮也只是纸面上的政绩。

## 延伸阅读

**大侨务（Greater Qiaowu Framework）**：跨部门、跨层级协同的侨务治理机制，强调法院、侨联、社区与综治力量联动，而非由单一部门包办。

**侨益司法保护（Judicial Protection of Diaspora Rights）**：通过典型案例统一裁判尺度，处理涉侨房归属、侨企合同与历史遗留纠纷，为地方招商引资提供可预期的法治环境。

**华裔新生代（New Generation of Chinese Descent）**：在海外出生和成长的华裔青年，是侨务从慰问帮扶转向双向平台对接的主要对象，其参与意愿取决于现实机会而非血缘记忆。

# Provincial diaspora-work conferences multiply: when "diaspora" becomes a local development blueprint

According to *China News*, at least 13 provincial-level regions — from Guangdong and Zhejiang to Qinghai and Inner Mongolia — held diaspora-work conferences between late August and mid-November 2026. The clustering is too tight to be coincidental. National qiaowu doctrine has been set; what we are watching is its translation into provincial implementation plans, each with its own brand, indicators and target constituency. That matters well beyond China's borders. Most overseas Chinese live in Southeast Asia, and for the region the useful question is no longer what Beijing says about diaspora work, but what provincial governments actually do.

## Meeting after meeting: from doctrine to provincial blueprints

The reported conferences include Henan on 26 August; Hainan and Chongqing on 27 August; Hebei on 10 September; Jiangxi on 29 September; Shanxi on 30 September; Jilin, Shanghai and Zhejiang on 13 October; Inner Mongolia on 16 October; Guangdong on 20 October; Qinghai on 4 November; and Shandong on 10 November. Guangdong paired its meeting with a work conference on "overseas Chinese helping high-quality economic growth". On 4 November, a Guangzhou investment event for diaspora firms saw seven groups of local enterprises and diaspora associations sign cooperation intentions covering outbound services and industrial collaboration.

Meetings do not themselves produce policy. But a calendar this dense suggests qiaowu has moved from a symbolic item on the agenda of an occasional national congress to a routine part of provincial annual work. When the centre sets direction and provinces supply instruments, the transmission mechanism often matters more than the top-level document. The thing to read, then, is not the boilerplate in the communiqués but what each province has written into its own blueprint.

## Brands and lanes: the same word, different local exams

Guangdong is building the "Qiaoxing Nanyue" brand around trade, investment and talent driven by overseas Chinese. Zhejiang, a common-prosperity demonstration zone, promotes "Qiaoxing Zhejiang" alongside "Qiaochuang Weilai" and "Qiaozhu Gongfu". Hainan is matching the needs of diaspora businesses to its free-trade-port "45432" development framework. Henan wants to preserve *xiangchou*, or hometown nostalgia; Shanxi wants to convert ancestral, Guan Gong and Jin-merchant culture into diaspora-work resources; Hebei speaks of "root, soul and dream"; Shandong emphasises civilisational exchange and opening-up; Qinghai stages symposia, reading clubs and concerts to produce locally distinctive "Qiaohao" programmes.

Chen Yiping, a professor at Jinan University's School of International Relations and Institute of Overseas Chinese Studies, told the outlet that the "greater qiaowu" system and mechanisms should be improved so overseas Chinese can share in the opportunities and dividends of Chinese modernisation. He noted that local governments, especially in major qiaoxiang home districts, have performed well but face challenges from great-power rivalry, geopolitics and a complex information environment, requiring precise knowledge of diaspora conditions and better platforms for talent and industrial projects. That is the crux: coastal provinces want capital and talent, cultural-heartland provinces want emotional recognition, western provinces want visibility. One word, many local agendas.

This is reasonable decentralisation. It also creates a principal–agent problem. Once success is counted in signed contracts, landed projects and investment totals, "serving overseas Chinese" can quietly slide into "harvesting overseas Chinese". Slogans converge; incentives diverge; outcomes follow the incentives. That layer cannot be skipped when reading provincial qiaowu.

## A judicial floor: what the same-day typical cases say

On 23 November, the day the provincial round-up appeared, the Supreme People's Court and the All-China Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese released typical cases on the judicial protection of diaspora rights. This list is not a footnote; it sets the floor beneath the provincial branding.

In Xiamen, a historic qiaofang in Siming district was inherited by overseas Chinese heirs but had been occupied for more than 70 years for historical reasons by a family that renovated it and rented its outer wall to an investment company for a large billboard. The original lease with the housing office had ended in 2009; the heirs sued only in 2025. The first-instance court ordered vacant possession within three months, occupancy fees and removal of the billboard. On appeal, the court activated the "five qiaowu" coordination mechanism, working through diaspora-rights protection stations and a Maritime Silk Road centre with the federation, street community and mediation body. Relocation assistance, housing progress, a realistic vacating timetable and incentive compensation resolved several disputes that had dragged on for more than a decade.

Two other cases are equally concrete. A Huangshan qiao enterprise sued a Dongguan wood-products firm in She County, Anhui, over more than 380,000 yuan (roughly US$53,000) owed for decorative paper; the court used asset preservation to promote a mediated agreement on a payment deadline with preservation left in place. A state-run overseas Chinese farm in Guangxi, both a market actor and a resettlement provider for returned overseas Chinese and their relatives, received coordinated help to rebuild repayment capacity and settled its debt early without wrecking employees' livelihoods.

These cases remind local officials that the rule-of-law environment is itself part of the investment proposition. If a diaspora heir cannot recover a house and a qiao firm cannot collect a debt, "serving overseas Chinese" remains a slogan. Equally notable is the method: coordination rather than fiat, mediated timelines rather than forced eviction. It is an understated but practical gloss on "greater qiaowu".

## The new generation is the real increment

Jilin has pledged continued attention to the new generation of Chinese descent and to minority overseas Chinese. Jiangxi plans to draw on its revolutionary, Hakka and ceramic culture through summer tours for overseas Chinese youth. Chongqing stresses deeper people-to-people exchange. Xing Jinghua, executive director of the Qiaohai (China) Innovation and Development Research Institute, is blunter: revive qiaoxiang heritage such as Chikan old town in Jiangmen to shorten the emotional distance, but also move beyond condolence visits and welfare aid, building standing matchmaking platforms for overseas merchants and young professionals so that diaspora resources meet local industrial and research needs — giving overseas Chinese reason to come back, ability to stay, and room to develop.

Why does this matter particularly for Southeast Asia? Because this "new generation" is largely born and schooled locally, often not fluent in Chinese. Its relationship with China rests less on memory than on calculation: visas, markets, careers. Emotional outreach alone may not survive contact with a young Malaysian or Indonesian professional's actual incentives. Building platforms is the right direction, but they must be reciprocal. If a platform merely extracts capital and talent for one province, Kuala Lumpur or Jakarta will read it as conditional courtship, or worse, as cultivation of a pliable constituency.

## The Southeast Asian mirror: how provincial enthusiasm reads abroad

Guangdong, Fujian and Hainan are the main launch pads into Southeast Asia, so their diaspora work is felt there first. Inter-provincial competition generates energy and duplication: similar diaspora-investment events rotate through several provinces, competing for the same small pool of entrepreneurs. In parts of Southeast Asia, organised diaspora mobilisation is readily securitised; highly visible provincial courtship can be recast as an extension of state reach across borders.

The counter-reading is that the same-day judicial cases and the "greater qiaowu" coordination are about service and rights — difficult things to object to. If provincial work stays at protecting property, enforcing contracts, keeping educational and cultural channels open, and making return an option rather than an obligation, it can lower suspicion rather than raise it. If it becomes a contest over loyalty and investment targets, it will confirm the "fifth column" narrative flagged in earlier analysis, adding needless question marks to otherwise ordinary people-to-people ties.

Judge this round of provincial qiaowu not by the number of conferences, brands or signed intentions, but by three tests: whether the Xiamen heir gets the house back, whether the Huangshan firm gets paid, and whether a young Chinese Malaysian can find a reason to engage with Guangdong without choosing between two homes. Where those hold, qiaowu is service. Where they do not, the blueprint is just paper.

## Further reading

**Greater qiaowu framework**: Cross-departmental, cross-level coordination in diaspora governance, joining courts, overseas-Chinese federations, communities and mediation bodies rather than leaving the work to one agency.

**Judicial protection of diaspora rights**: The use of typical cases to unify standards in disputes over inherited qiaofang, diaspora-enterprise contracts and historical claims, giving local investment promotion a predictable legal environment.

**New generation of Chinese descent**: Locally born and educated descendants of Chinese migrants, now the main target of a shift from welfare visits to reciprocal platforms; their willingness to engage depends on real opportunities more than ancestral memory.

# 从"听故事"到"存历史"：汕大在新加坡为潮籍侨领留下口述档案

近日，汕头大学区域国别与华侨华人研究院（Shantou University's Institute of Area Studies and Overseas Chinese）在新加坡启动第五个国别口述历史采集工程。调研团队由院长郑慕强（Zheng Muqiang）带队，对新加坡潮州八邑会馆（Teochew Poit Ip Huay Kuan）会长吴木兴、新加坡潮州总会会长黄海庆、醉花林俱乐部（Chui Huay Lim Club）总理林雍杰等20位潮籍杰出侨领展开深度访谈，记录他们跨越重洋、艰苦创业的历程，以及在家族传承、在地奉献与回馈桑梓中的动人篇章。

这次采集看似一次寻常的"师生下乡"，实则触及华侨华人研究的一个根本命题：当一个族群的历史从口耳相传转向系统存档，记忆才真正获得对抗遗忘的韧性。

## 第五个国别工程：把田野搬到南洋

汕头大学的国别口述史计划已走到第五站，新加坡是其中最具象征意义的一站。与东南亚（Southeast Asia）许多族群一样，新加坡潮籍社群的历史长期散落在会馆簿册、族谱私藏与老人口述之中，缺少统一的学术梳理。此次调研以"杰出侨领"为切口，并非只为表彰个人成就，而是试图通过个体生命史，拼出一幅社群百年迁徙与在地化的全景图。

郑慕强在访谈中反复强调一点：新加坡潮侨的历史，是由无数个体经历交织而成的珍贵记忆，它为侨史研究注入了"鲜活的生命力"。这句话点出了口述史的方法论价值——统计数据告诉我们"有多少"，而口述史告诉我们"为什么"和"怎样活下来"。

## 二十位侨领：一部社群的微观史

20位受访者的名单本身就是一份社会学的样本。从会馆会长到俱乐部总理，他们既是商界与宗乡组织的骨干，也是连接新加坡与潮汕原乡的"活的桥梁"。调研团队记录的，不止是发家史，更包括他们如何在异乡维系方言、祭祀与互助传统，又如何在第二代、第三代逐渐本地化之后，重新定义"潮籍"的身份边界。

学生吴炜林、陈思彤在分享中提到，与侨领的深入交流，让他们对"爱国、爱乡、爱家人"的潮侨精神有了"更立体、更真切的体会"。这恰是口述史教育的隐性功能：它把教科书上的"华侨精神"还原为有温度的人。

## 地标里的社群记忆：从醉花林到同济医院

除访谈之外，团队还走访了承载潮侨历史的三处地标。拥有180余年历史的醉花林俱乐部，见证了乡亲守望相助的传统；同济医院体现了跨籍贯、跨族群的慈善情怀；潮州八邑会馆则展示了侨团在凝聚乡情、服务社群上的当代实践。

这些地标不是孤立的建筑，而是一套"记忆基础设施"。当学者把田野调研与侨社现实相联结，理论才不至于悬空。换句话说，侨史研究若只停留在文献，便永远缺了那一声来自会馆厅堂的乡音。

## 口述史作为侨史研究的"活档案"

从更长远的视角看，此次采集的意义超出一次调研本身。随着二战前出生的侨领逐年凋零，许多亲历记忆正以惊人速度流失。系统化的口述档案，是对抗这种"记忆断层"最朴素的办法。

对东南亚观察者而言，潮籍社群的案例具有普遍启示：海外华人的在地化从来不是"同化"的单向过程，而是在保持文化与融入社会之间反复协商的结果。汕头大学的这场"听故事"，本质上是在为一段仍在生长的历史，提前留下可被检索的注脚。

## 延伸阅读
**口述史（Oral history）**：通过录音访谈系统记录亲历者记忆的研究方法，弥补文献对个体经验与情感维度的缺失。
**宗乡会馆（Clan and locality association）**：东南亚华人以方言群或原乡地缘为纽带的自助组织，兼具互助、文化传承与政治代表功能。
**潮籍社群（Teochew diaspora）**：源自中国广东潮汕地区的海外移民群体，在新加坡、泰国（Thailand）、马来西亚（Malaysia）等地形成密集而具影响力的商贸网络。

# From "Hearing Stories" to "Keeping History": Shantou University Archives Teochew Leaders' Oral Histories in Singapore

Recently, Shantou University's Institute of Area Studies and Overseas Chinese launched its fifth country-specific oral history project in Singapore. Led by dean Zheng Muqiang, the research team conducted in-depth interviews with twenty prominent Teochew leaders — including Goh Bok Heng, president of the Teochew Poit Ip Huay Kuan; Ng Hoe Kheng, president of the Singapore Teochew Federation; and Lim Yong Kiat, chairman of the Chui Huay Lim Club — recording their migration, entrepreneurship, and their roles in family continuity, local contribution, and hometown philanthropy.

The exercise may look like a routine student field trip, yet it touches a fundamental question in overseas-Chinese studies: only when a community's memory shifts from word-of-mouth to systematic archiving does it gain resilience against forgetting.

## The fifth country project: taking the field to Nanyang

Shantou University's country oral-history programme has now reached its fifth station, and Singapore is among the most symbolically charged. Like many Southeast Asian groups, the Singapore Teochew community's past has long been scattered across clan-association ledgers, private genealogies, and elderly recall, with little unified scholarly mapping. By focusing on "prominent leaders," the project does not merely celebrate individuals; it uses life histories to assemble a panorama of a century of migration and localisation.

Zheng stressed that Singapore's Teochew history is precious memory woven from countless individual experiences, injecting "living vitality" into diaspora scholarship. The remark captures oral history's methodological value: statistics tell us "how many," while oral testimony tells us "why" and "how they survived."

## Twenty leaders: a community's micro-history

The list of twenty interviewees is itself a sociological sample. From association presidents to club chairmen, they are both business and kinship-organisation backbone and "living bridges" linking Singapore with the Teochew heartland in China's Guangdong. The team recorded not only rags-to-riches tales but how these figures sustained dialect, ritual, and mutual-aid traditions abroad, and how they redefined the boundaries of "Teochew" identity as the second and third generations localised.

Students Wu Weilin and Chen Sitong noted that deep exchanges with the leaders gave them a "more three-dimensional, more genuine" grasp of the Teochew spirit of "love the nation, love the hometown, love the family." This is the hidden pedagogical function of oral history: it restores the textbook "overseas-Chinese spirit" to warm, specific human beings.

## Memory embedded in landmarks: from Chui Huay Lim to Thung Wai

Beyond interviews, the team visited three sites carrying Teochew memory. The 180-year-old Chui Huay Lim Club embodies the tradition of neighbours watching over one another; Thung Wai (Tongji) Hospital reflects cross-dialect, cross-ethnic charity; the Teochew Poit Ip Huay Kuan shows the contemporary practice of bonding fellow villagers and serving the community.

These landmarks are not isolated buildings but a set of "memory infrastructure." When scholars link field research to the lived reality of diaspora associations, theory stops floating. In other words, diaspora history that stays in documents forever lacks that native accent echoing through an association hall.

## Oral history as a "living archive" of diaspora studies

Seen from a longer horizon, the project's value exceeds a single survey. As leaders born before the war pass away year by year, many firsthand memories are vanishing at an alarming rate. Systematic oral archives are the plainest defence against this "memory fault line."

For Southeast Asia watchers, the Teochew case carries a general lesson: the localisation of overseas Chinese was never a one-way "assimilation," but a repeated negotiation between preserving culture and embedding in society. Shantou University's "story-listening" is, in essence, leaving retrievable footnotes for a history still being written.

## Further reading
**Oral history**: A research method that systematically records interviewees' memories via recorded conversation, filling the gap left by documents on individual experience and emotion.
**Clan and locality association**: Southeast Asian Chinese self-help organisations bonded by dialect group or ancestral homeland, combining mutual aid, cultural transmission, and political representation.
**Teochew diaspora**: The overseas migrant group originating from China's Teochew region in Guangdong, forming dense, influential business networks in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia and beyond.

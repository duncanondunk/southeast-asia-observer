# 普拉博沃的「二十六大员」：富豪、将军与宗教领袖同堂问政

10月2日，印度尼西亚总统普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）在雅加达总统府主持宣誓仪式，正式成立新一届总统顾问理事会，委任26名来自政商界与宗教界的领袖出任顾问。这是普拉博沃就任总统近两年来的首次顾问委任；上一届理事会于2024年十月任期届满后一直悬空，此番26人的规模近乎此前三倍。表面看是一场常规的人事安排，细读名单却透出耐人寻味的权力逻辑：资本、枪杆与信仰，被同时请进了同一张顾问桌。

## 26人理事会：规模近三倍于前届

总统顾问理事会（Wantimpres）的法定职责，是就国家行政权力的行使向总统提供战略建议。这个机构并非普拉博沃首创——它是印尼宪政框架内长期存在的咨询机制，却在空缺近两年后才被重新填满，且人数大幅扩张。从约九人增至26人，几乎三倍于佐科（Jokowi）时代末期的规模。一个值得玩味的问题是：当行政部门本身已有完整的内阁与官僚体系，为何要在一旁增设一个规模如此庞大的「影子智囊」？

## 富豪、将军与宗教领袖同堂

翻开名单，结构一目了然。理事会由前总统佐科时期的人类发展与文化统筹部长、伊斯兰复兴党（PKB）主席穆海明（普拉迪诺）出任主席。商界方面，国信集团（Mayapada Group）创始人翁俊民在列——他是印尼第六大富豪，业务横跨银行、医院、房地产与媒体；金融与传媒大亨凯鲁（查伊鲁·丹绒）亦榜上有名，曾在前总统尤多约诺时期短暂担任经济事务统筹部长，目前的富豪榜排名第十五。军方一侧，退役将领阿古姆在列；司法一侧，宪法法院前首席大法官安瓦尔入席。宗教界则囊括了伊斯兰教士联合会（Nahdlatul Ulama，NU）的两名前主席，以及基督教、佛教与孔教会（Konghucu）的代表。

## 资本、枪杆与信仰的三合一

把上述名单并排来看，最突出的不是某个人的分量，而是三类力量的「同框」：掌控国民经济的华商巨贾、身居军职的退役将领、以及掌握道德话语权的宗教领袖，被统一纳入一个直接向总统负责的咨询机构。这让人想起苏哈托（Suharto）时代专业集团党（Golkar）式的社会统合术——以制度化渠道把商界、军方与宗教势力一并「收编」，换取稳定。普拉博沃本就出身军方、带有鲜明的威权气质，如今用顾问理事会的形式把这种统合做得更为柔和、却也更为集中。

## 华人巨贾入席：从排华到问政

名单中最具象征意义的，莫过于华人巨贾翁俊民的入列，以及孔教会代表的获委。印尼的华裔群体曾在长期同化政策与1998年排华骚乱中备受挤压，华人资本与宗教长期被排除在国家的核心咨询架构之外。后改革时代（Reformasi）以来，华人逐步重返经济与政治主流，但进入总统级顾问机构仍属罕见。此番把华商与孔教会一并请入，既说明华人资本已是体制内不可或缺的一环，也折射出普拉博沃试图以「全民代表性」包装权力集中的叙事需要。

## 非选举的权力：顾问理事会的隐忧

然而，光环之下藏着隐忧。总统顾问理事会是未经选举、不对议会负责的机构，其建议不公开、不问责。当26名由总统亲自挑选的富豪与将领环绕左右，内阁各部与国会的政策制定是否会被「前置消化」？更现实的冲突在于利益：身兼大集团掌门人的顾问，所提战略建议会不会顺带利好自家产业？佐科执政时期虽然也倚重亲信，却少见如此大规模地把商界与军方并置于同一顾问台面。顾问越多，看似共识越广，实则权力越向总统个人收敛。

## 结语：共识外壳下的权力集中

说到底，26人理事会是普拉博沃执政风格的一个注脚：他以「广泛包容」的姿态，把商界、军方与宗教精英一并纳入麾下，既换取统治的稳固，也把非正式的影响力收拢到总统府之内。对印尼的华人社会而言，这或许意味着更深的主流融入；对印尼的民主体制而言，这未必是一道轻松的考题。问政的桌子大了，监督的窗口却未必随之打开。

## 延伸阅读

**总统顾问理事会（Wantimpres）**：印尼宪法框架内的总统咨询机构，成员由总统任命、任期与总统一致，就国家战略提供非约束性建议，不对国会负责。
**伊斯兰教士联合会（Nahdlatul Ulama，NU）**：印尼最大的伊斯兰组织，根基在东部农村与基层穆斯林，长期是历届政府争取的宗教政治力量。
**翁俊民（Tahir）**：印尼华裔企业家，国信集团创始人，业务横跨金融、医疗、地产与媒体，长期以慈善与政治捐助活跃于印尼与华人社会。

# Prabowo's "Council of 26": Tycoons, Generals and Clerics at One Table

On 2 October 2026, Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto presided over a swearing-in ceremony at the Jakarta presidential palace and formally constituted a new Presidential Advisory Council, appointing 26 leaders from business, the military and religious circles as his advisers. It was Prabowo's first such appointment in nearly two years in office. The previous council had stood vacant since its term expired in October 2024; the new 26-strong body is almost three times the size of its predecessor. On the surface this looks like routine personnel management. Read the roster closely, however, and a distinct logic of power emerges: capital, the gun and faith have all been invited to the same advisory table.

## A 26-member council: nearly triple the previous size

The statutory task of the Presidential Advisory Council (Wantimpres) is to give the president strategic advice on the exercise of state administrative power. The institution is not Prabowo's invention — it has long existed within Indonesia's constitutional architecture. What is striking is that it was left empty for almost two years before being refilled, and refilled at nearly triple the size. The body grew from roughly nine members to 26, almost three times the scale seen at the end of the Jokowi era. The question worth asking is simple: when the executive branch already commands a full cabinet and bureaucracy, why erect a "shadow brain trust" of this magnitude alongside it?

## Tycoons, generals and clerics at one table

The composition is easy to read. The council is chaired by Muhaimin Iskandar — former Coordinating Minister for Human Development and Culture under Jokowi and chairman of the National Awakening Party (PKB). On the business side, Tahir (Ang Tjoen Ming), founder of the Mayapada Group, is included; he is Indonesia's sixth-richest man, with interests spanning banking, hospitals, property and media. Also on the list is Chairul Tanjung, founder of CT Corp, who briefly served as Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs under Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono and currently ranks fifteenth on the wealth list. From the military, retired general Agum Gunandjar Sumantri appears; from the judiciary, former Constitutional Court chief justice Anwar Usman. The religious wing brings in two former chairmen of Nahdlatul Ulama (NU), Indonesia's largest Islamic organisation, together with representatives of the Christian, Buddhist and Confucian (Konghucu) communities.

## Capital, the gun and faith fused into one

Lined up together, the most conspicuous feature of the roster is not any single heavyweight but the coexistence of three kinds of power: ethnic-Chinese tycoons who command the national economy, retired officers who embody coercive capacity, and religious leaders who hold moral authority — all folded into one body that reports directly to the president. It recalls the social-corporatist co-optation of the Suharto era, when the Golkar machine institutionalised channels to absorb business, the military and religious forces in exchange for stability. Prabowo, a former general with an unmistakably authoritarian temperament, has now performed that absorption more softly — and more centrally — through the device of an advisory council.

## Ethnic-Chinese tycoons at the table: from exclusion to inclusion

The most symbolically charged inclusion is that of the ethnic-Chinese magnate Tahir, alongside a representative of the Confucian community. Indonesia's Chinese diaspora long endured assimilationist pressure and the anti-Chinese violence of 1998, and both Chinese capital and Chinese religion were kept outside the core structures of state consultation. Since the Reformasi of 1998, ethnic Chinese Indonesians have gradually returned to the economic and political mainstream, yet entry into a president-level advisory body remains rare. Bringing Chinese business and the Confucian community into the council at once signals that ethnic-Chinese capital is now an indispensable part of the establishment, and serves Prabowo's narrative need to wrap centralisation in the language of "inclusive representation".

## Power without election: the risks of an advisory council

Beneath the glow, however, lie real concerns. The Presidential Advisory Council is an unelected body that answers to no parliament; its advice is neither published nor accountable. When 26 hand-picked tycoons and generals gather around the president, do the policymaking processes of the cabinet and the legislature get "pre-digested" in their presence? The more immediate conflict is one of interest: an adviser who also chairs a conglomerate may well advance strategic advice that quietly benefits his own empire. Jokowi relied on trusted lieutenants too, but rarely placed business and military elites on the same advisory stage at this scale. The more advisers, the broader the apparent consensus — and the more power converges on the president himself.

## Conclusion: centralisation behind a consensus façade

In the end, the Council of 26 is a footnote to Prabowo's governing style: with a posture of "broad inclusion", he draws business, military and religious elites under his wing, securing his rule while pulling informal influence back inside the palace. For Indonesia's Chinese community, this may mean deeper integration into the mainstream. For Indonesia's democratic institutions, it is far from an easy test. The table where policy is discussed has grown larger — but the window of oversight has not necessarily opened with it.

## Further reading

**Presidential Advisory Council (Wantimpres):** Indonesia's constitutional advisory body to the president; members are appointed by and serve at the pleasure of the president, offering non-binding strategic counsel and answering to no legislature.
**Nahdlatul Ulama (NU):** Indonesia's largest Islamic organisation, rooted in the countryside and among grassroots Muslims, and a religious-political force every administration has courted.
**Tahir (Ang Tjoen Ming):** An ethnic-Chinese Indonesian entrepreneur and founder of the Mayapada Group, with interests across finance, healthcare, property and media, long active in charity and political donation within Indonesia and the Chinese community.

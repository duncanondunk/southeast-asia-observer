# Grab 15亿美元控股 Atome：东南亚超级App的金融赌注

## 一笔"买时间"的收购

2026年9月15日，东南亚网约车与外卖巨头 Grab（纳斯达克代码：GRAB）宣布，将以15亿美元现金收购先买后付平台 Atome Financial 六成股权，卖方为 Atome 母公司 Advance Intelligence Group。消息一出，Grab 股价当日走高，市场读出的信号很明确：这家以"出行+送餐"起家的超级应用，正在把消费信贷当作下一根增长支柱。与其花数年自建信贷模型、承受试错亏损，不如直接买下一个已经跑通的平台。

## 从接送乘客到借出资金

Grab 的金融版图并非一日建成。它已在新加坡、马来西亚、印尼持有数字银行与放贷牌照，业务涵盖支付、保险与消费信贷，最初服务司机与商户伙伴。2025年，Grab 平台上68%的司机伙伴借款人是第一次获得正规信贷，其中一半表示，这样做是为了避开高利贷。这个数字揭示了 Grab 的算盘：它掌握着数百万司机与商户的行为数据，放贷的获客成本远低于传统银行。收购 Atome，是把这套能力从"生态内"推向"全市场"。

## Atome 是谁

Atome 意为"Available to me"，是新加坡金融科技公司 Advance Intelligence Group 旗下的数字金融服务平台，业务覆盖新加坡、马来西亚、菲律宾、印尼与泰国五国，累计交易用户达2500万，合作品牌超过3万家，场景横跨时尚、美妆、生活方式、旅游、健身与家居。它提供的不只是先买后付，还包括消费现金贷款、BNPL 联名卡与数字借贷。2025年，Atome 营收同比增长80%至4.7亿美元，并实现连续第二年税前盈利——对一个消费信贷平台而言，这是难得的成绩单。

## 用现金而非股票下注

交易结构经过精心设计。Grab 首期支付15亿美元现金取得60%股权，其中2.6亿美元作为增资注入 Atome 的资产负债表，用于扩张贷款组合；其余约12.3亿美元支付给卖方。Grab 同意在交割约两年后收购剩余40%，最终对价与 Atome 的营收与 EBITDA 挂钩，估值区间设定在20亿至45亿美元之间。整笔交易全部以自有现金支付，动用其74亿美元流动性，不增发股票、不新增债务——在利率高企、融资成本上升的环境里，这是财务实力的直接展示。

## 七十亿"无银行账户"人口的生意

这笔收购的叙事底色，是金融普惠。东南亚逾70%的成年人没有银行账户或处于"银行服务不足"状态，传统征信体系覆盖不到他们。Atome 的看点在于用人工智能做信贷审批：每一笔交易都让它的风控模型更聪明，从而在"服务无征信人群"与"控制坏账"之间找到平衡。Grab 拥有近5400万月度交易用户，Atome 拥有庞大的商户网络，两者结合，既能为 Grab 生态导入分期支付，也能把 Grab 的流量导向 Atome 的品牌合作方。

## 股价承压下的第二曲线

这笔交易的时机，透着紧迫感。Grab 股价在2026年下跌近40%，市值约123亿美元，早年三位数的增长早已失速。为寻找新故事，它今年已先后收购美国 Stash Financial（企业价值4.25亿美元）与外送平台 Foodpanda 的台湾业务（6亿美元）。它把金融服务的2028年目标上调至经调整 EBITDA 5亿美元、贷款组合超60亿美元，并宣称2025至2028年集团营收年复合增速超30%——数字很漂亮，但资本市场要看到兑现路径，而非愿景。

## 整合与监管的双重考验

前景之下，风险同样清晰。交易预计2027年第三季度完成，仍需通过多地监管审批；跨境整合五个市场的牌照、风控与团队，难度不小。更关键的是监管环境：先买后付在东南亚快速渗透的同时，也因诱导过度负债、信息披露不足而受到各国央行与消费者保护机构的审视。Grab 管理层强调会"负责任地"放贷，并希望用旗下三家数字银行降低 Atome 的资金成本，但信贷周期的下行风险，终究要由资产负债表承担。

## 东南亚金融科技的下半场

从区域视角看，这桩收购是东南亚金融科技进入"下半场"的标志。上半场的主题是支付与钱包的圈地，下半场则是信贷的深水区：谁能掌握场景与数据，谁就能把授信嵌入日常消费。新加坡咨询机构 Momentum Works 首席执行官李江玕的评价点破要害——Grab 买的其实是"时间与规模"，当风控与催收日益同质化，分发渠道才是壁垒。对消费者而言，更便捷的信贷意味着更低的门槛，也意味着更需要警惕的负债陷阱。东南亚的信贷故事，才刚刚写到第二章。

## 延伸阅读

**先买后付（BNPL, Buy Now Pay Later）**：一种消费金融模式，允许消费者在结账时选择分期免息或低息付款，近年随电商与超级应用在东南亚迅速普及，也因可能诱导过度负债而受到监管关注。

**数字银行（Digital Bank）**：指持牌经营的虚拟银行，不设实体网点，依托手机应用提供存贷与支付服务。Grab 已在新加坡、马来西亚与印尼持有数字银行或数字借贷牌照。

# Grab's US$1.49bn Atome Bet: Southeast Asia's Super-App Goes All-In on Lending

## A deal that buys time

On 15 September 2026, Southeast Asian ride-hailing and delivery giant Grab (NASDAQ: GRAB) announced it would pay US$1.49 billion in cash for a 60 per cent stake in buy-now-pay-later platform Atome Financial, buying from parent Advance Intelligence Group. Grab's shares rose on the news, and the signal was clear: the super-app that built its name on rides and food is betting on consumer credit as its next growth pillar. Rather than spend years building its own credit models and absorbing the losses of trial and error, Grab would simply acquire a platform that already works.

## From ferrying passengers to lending money

Grab's financial footprint was not built overnight. It already holds digital-bank and lending licences in Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia, spanning payments, insurance and consumer credit, initially for drivers and merchant partners. In 2025, 68 per cent of driver-partner borrowers on Grab accessed formal credit for the first time, half of them to avoid predatory lenders. That statistic reveals the logic: Grab holds behavioural data on millions of drivers and merchants, so the cost of acquiring a borrower is far below a traditional bank's. Buying Atome pushes that capability beyond its own ecosystem into the open market.

## Who Atome is

Atome — short for "Available to me" — is the digital financial services arm of Singapore's Advance Intelligence Group, operating in Singapore, Malaysia, the Philippines, Indonesia and Thailand, with 25 million cumulative transacted users and more than 30,000 partner brands across fashion, beauty, lifestyle, travel, fitness and homeware. Beyond BNPL it offers consumer cash loans, BNPL cards and digital lending. In 2025 its revenue jumped 80 per cent to US$470 million, delivering a second straight year of pre-tax profit — a rare scorecard for a consumer-lending platform.

## Betting cash, not shares

The deal is carefully engineered. Grab pays US$1.49 billion in cash for an initial 60 per cent, of which US$260 million is primary capital injected into Atome's balance sheet to expand the loan book, with the remaining US$1.23 billion going to sellers. Grab has agreed to buy the remaining 40 per cent roughly two years after closing, with final consideration tied to Atome's revenue and EBITDA and a valuation collar of US$2 billion to US$4.5 billion. The whole transaction is funded from internal cash — its US$7.4 billion in liquidity — with no new shares and no new debt: a direct demonstration of financial strength when borrowing costs are high.

## The business of seven billion "unbanked"

The narrative underneath is financial inclusion. More than 70 per cent of adults in Southeast Asia are unbanked or underbanked, beyond the reach of traditional credit bureaus. Atome's draw is AI-driven underwriting: every transaction makes its risk models smarter, balancing service to the credit-invisible against bad-debt control. Grab brings nearly 54 million monthly transacting users; Atome brings a large merchant network. Combined, they can push instalment payments into Grab's ecosystem and channel Grab's traffic to Atome's brand partners.

## A second curve under a falling share price

The timing betrays urgency. Grab's shares have fallen almost 40 per cent in 2026, giving it a market value of about US$12.3 billion, and its once triple-digit growth has long stalled. Hunting for a new story, it has already bought America's Stash Financial (enterprise value US$425 million) and Foodpanda's Taiwan operations (US$600 million) this year. It raised its 2028 targets for financial services to US$500 million in adjusted EBITDA and a loan book above US$6 billion, and touted a group revenue CAGR above 30 per cent from 2025 to 2028 — attractive numbers, but capital markets want a path to delivery, not a vision.

## The twin tests of integration and regulation

Beneath the promise, the risks are plain. The deal is expected to close in the third quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals across several markets, and integrating licences, risk models and teams across five countries is no small feat. More important is the regulatory climate: even as BNPL spreads rapidly across Southeast Asia, it faces scrutiny from central banks and consumer-protection bodies over induced over-indebtedness and weak disclosure. Grab's management stresses it will lend "responsibly" and hopes its three digital banks will lower Atome's funding costs — but the downside of a credit cycle ultimately lands on the balance sheet.

## The second half of Southeast Asian fintech

Regionally, the acquisition marks fintech's entry into a "second half." The first half was about grabbing territory in payments and wallets; the second is the deep water of credit, where whoever owns the scenario and the data can embed lending into everyday spending. Jianggan Li, chief executive of Singapore consultancy Momentum Works, put it sharply: what Grab is really buying is time and scale, because as underwriting and collections commoditise, distribution becomes the moat. For consumers, easier credit means a lower threshold — and a sharper need to guard against debt traps. Southeast Asia's credit story is only in its second chapter.

## Further reading

**BNPL (Buy Now, Pay Later):** A consumer-finance model that lets shoppers split payments interest-free or at low cost at checkout, spreading fast across Southeast Asia with e-commerce and super-apps, and drawing regulatory attention over induced over-indebtedness.

**Digital bank:** A licensed virtual bank with no physical branches, offering deposits, loans and payments through a mobile app. Grab holds digital-bank or digital-lending licences in Singapore, Malaysia and Indonesia.

# 平陆运河通航在即，中国西南贸易捷径将改写东盟货运

据《南华早报》（South China Morning Post）9月2日报道，耗资约108亿美元（约767亿元人民币）的平陆运河，将于第23届中国—东盟博览会召开前夕通航。这条纵贯广西的人工运河，被外界视为中国西南地区"通江达海"的关键一跃，也将为东盟货物开辟一条更短、更便宜的北上通道。

## 一条运河的"十年磨一剑"

平陆运河是新中国成立以来建设规模最大、里程最长的水运工程之一，连通西江航运干线与北部湾，全长约134公里。项目自2022年动工，计划于2026年9月中国—东盟博览会前夕实现通航。

108亿美元的投资规模，放在全球运河史上亦属醒目。对地处西南内陆的广西而言，这不仅是基础设施，更是一次经济地理的"换向"——从面向内陆，转为面向海洋与东盟。

## 从"背向大海"到"通江达海"

历史上，广西虽近海，却因地形与水系分隔，大宗货物长期"望海兴叹"：西江沿线的物资要出海，需绕行珠江口，路途远、成本高。平陆运河相当于为西江"截弯取直"，直接打通至北部湾。

通航后，西南地区货物无需再绕行珠三角，可由贵港、南宁一线直下钦州湾。对重庆、成都等长江上游城市而言，这也意味着多了一条不依赖长江出海口、更靠近东盟的南向通道。

## 截弯取直的物流革命

物流的核心变量是距离与成本。据测算，平陆运河有望显著缩短西江中上游至北部湾的运距，单吨运输成本随之下降。对煤炭、矿石、建材等低值大宗货物，运费每降一分，竞争力便增一分。

更深远的是，它把"水运廉价、铁路快捷、公路灵活"的组合重新排布。当水运瓶颈被打通，西南制造与东盟原料之间的对流将更顺畅，区域分工也随之细化。

## 东盟货物的"北上"新通道

对东盟而言，平陆运河的价值在于"反向通道"。过去，东盟货物入华多走东部沿海港口再内灌；今后可经北部湾的钦州、防城港上岸，沿运河与水系直抵西南腹地。

越南的农产品、泰国的热带商品、印尼的矿产加工品，得以更快触达中国增长最快的内陆消费市场。一套"东盟—北部湾—运河—西南"的陆海新通道，正从图纸走向日常。

## 与RCEP和自贸区3.0的共振

时间点上，平陆运河通航与区域自贸进程高度契合。2025年中国与东盟双边贸易额首次突破1万亿美元，中国连续17年居东盟第一大贸易伙伴，东盟连续6年为中国第一大贸易伙伴。

《区域全面经济伙伴关系协定》（RCEP）与中国—东盟自贸区3.0版升级议定书加快落地，使通关、标准与规则衔接更顺。运河提供的是"硬通道"，协定提供的是"软通道"，二者共振，贸易便利化被进一步放大。

## 广西与平陆运河的经济地理重塑

运河改变的首先是广西自身的角色。作为面向东盟的门户，广西长期受限于"通道而不枢纽"。运河与北部湾港联动后，钦州、北海、防城港有望从"过路港"升级为"枢纽港"。

这也将带动临港产业、冷链物流与跨境加工集群。对长期面临区域发展不平衡的西南而言，一条河可能撬动一片产业带——这正是"通江达海"四个字背后的真实红利。

## 隐忧：生态、运力与水资源的考验

超级工程从来伴随权衡。运河涉及跨流域调水与航运用水，生态影响、航运安全与水资源调度都是长期课题。如何在通航效益与流域保护之间拿捏，考验治理智慧。

此外，运力能否如期释放、临港产业能否真正集聚，也取决于配套与需求。基础设施只是前提，让货物"愿意走、走得划算"，才是运河价值的终极检验。

## 结语：捷径背后的区域一体化

平陆运河不只是广西的事。它折射的是中国—东盟互联互通从"倡议"走向"设施"的进程，也是"一带一路"陆海联动的又一注脚。

当一条河把西南内陆与北部湾、把中国与东盟更紧地系在一起，捷径的意义便超越了运费本身——它让区域一体化从概念，落成了可以通航的航道。

## 延伸阅读

**北部湾（Beibu Gulf）**：中国南海西北部海域，环抱广西、广东、海南，是面向东盟的重要出海口。

# Pinglu Canal Nears Opening, Rewriting ASEAN Cargo via China's Southwest Shortcut

According to the South China Morning Post on 2 September, the Pinglu Canal — costing about US$10.8 billion — is set to open just before the 23rd China–ASEAN Expo. The artificial canal running through Guangxi is seen as a pivotal leap for China's southwest to "reach the ocean," and will open a shorter, cheaper northbound channel for ASEAN cargo.

## A Decade in the Making

The Pinglu Canal is among the largest and longest waterway projects built since the founding of the PRC, linking the Xijiang waterway to the Beibu Gulf over roughly 134 km. Construction began in 2022, with opening planned for September 2026, ahead of the China–ASEAN Expo.

A US$10.8 billion price tag is striking even by global canal standards. For landlocked-in-feeling Guangxi, this is not merely infrastructure but an economic-geographic "reorientation" — from facing inland to facing the sea and ASEAN.

## From "Turning Its Back to the Sea" to "Reaching the Ocean"

Historically, though coastal, Guangxi's bulk cargo long "sighed at the sea" because of terrain and river-system separation: goods on the Xijiang had to detour via the Pearl River estuary, long and costly. The Pinglu Canal "straightens the bend," directly connecting to the Beibu Gulf.

Once open, southwest goods need not bypass the Pearl River Delta, descending from Guigang and Nanning straight to Qinzhou Bay. For upper-Yangtze cities like Chongqing and Chengdu, it means an additional southbound corridor closer to ASEAN, independent of the Yangtze estuary.

## A Logistics Revolution by Straightening the Route

The core variables in logistics are distance and cost. Estimates suggest the canal will markedly shorten the route from the mid-upper Xijiang to the Beibu Gulf, lowering per-tonne transport cost. For low-value bulk goods like coal, ore and building materials, every saving lifts competitiveness.

More profoundly, it re-sequences the mix of "cheap water, fast rail, flexible road." With the waterway bottleneck opened, the two-way flow between southwest manufacturing and ASEAN raw materials becomes smoother, and regional division of labour fine-tunes accordingly.

## A New "Northbound" Channel for ASEAN Goods

For ASEAN, the canal's value lies in the reverse channel. Previously, ASEAN goods entered China mostly via eastern coastal ports then inland; now they can land at Qinzhou or Fangchenggang on the Beibu Gulf and travel via canal and waterways straight to the southwest hinterland.

Vietnamese farm produce, Thai tropical goods and Indonesian processed minerals can reach China's fastest-growing inland consumer markets faster. A new "ASEAN–Beibu Gulf–canal–southwest" land-sea corridor is moving from blueprint to routine.

## Resonance with RCEP and FTA 3.0

In timing, the canal's opening aligns tightly with regional free-trade progress. In 2025, China–ASEAN bilateral trade surpassed US$1 trillion for the first time; China has been ASEAN's top trading partner for 17 years, and ASEAN China's for six.

The RCEP and the upgraded China–ASEAN FTA 3.0 protocol are landing faster, smoothing customs, standards and rules. The canal provides the "hard channel"; the agreements the "soft channel." Their resonance amplifies trade facilitation.

## Reshaping Guangxi's Economic Geography

What the canal changes first is Guangxi's own role. As the gateway to ASEAN, Guangxi long suffered from being "a passage, not a hub." Linked with the Beibu Gulf port, Qinzhou, Beihai and Fangchenggang may upgrade from "transit ports" to "hub ports."

This will also spur port-adjacent industries, cold-chain logistics and cross-border processing clusters. For a southwest long marked by uneven regional development, one river may lever an entire industrial belt — the real dividend behind "reaching the ocean."

## Concerns: Ecology, Capacity and Water

Mega-projects always carry trade-offs. The canal involves cross-basin water transfer and navigational water use; ecological impact, navigational safety and water-resource scheduling are long-term subjects. Balancing navigational benefit with basin protection tests governance wisdom.

Moreover, whether capacity releases on schedule and whether port-adjacent industry truly clusters depends on supporting facilities and demand. Infrastructure is only a prerequisite; making cargo "willing to use it and at a good price" is the ultimate test of the canal's value.

## Regional Integration Behind the Shortcut

The Pinglu Canal is not just Guangxi's affair. It reflects the process of China–ASEAN connectivity moving from "initiative" to "facility," and is another footnote to the land-sea linkage of the Belt and Road.

When one river ties the southwest hinterland to the Beibu Gulf, and China more tightly to ASEAN, the meaning of a shortcut goes beyond freight rates — it turns regional integration from concept into a navigable waterway.

**Beibu Gulf**: A northwestern South China Sea bay bordering Guangxi, Guangdong and Hainan, a key outlet to ASEAN.

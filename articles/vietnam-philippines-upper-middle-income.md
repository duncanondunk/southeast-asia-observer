# 越南与菲律宾迈入中高收入门槛：半导体出口驱动的增长跃迁

## 数字：越过了那道门槛

在半导体与其他电子产品出口增长的带动下，越南与菲律宾的经济格局悄然升级。世界银行今年7月发布的年度国家收入分类显示，两国去年的人均国民总收入分别升至4970美元（约3.57万元人民币）和4850美元（约3.48万元人民币），双双越过今年4636美元（约3.33万元人民币）的中高收入门槛，首次跻身这一行列。

换句话说，以人均国民总收入计，这两个长期被视作"低成本制造基地"的经济体，正把自己的名字写进更具分量的那一栏。

## 引擎：半导体与电子品的出口浪潮

推力来自全球人工智能投资的勃兴。《日经亚洲》8月18日报道，随着AI投资浪潮涌动，东南亚正经历由半导体及其他电子产品出口增长驱动的增长。站在浪尖的是越南：韩国LG Innotek、三星电子与美国英特尔等巨头，都计划在越南新建或扩建半导体工厂。越南政府更宣布，计划2030年前培养5万名半导体专业人才，目标在2027年前具备半导体设计、制造与测试能力。

在菲律宾，半导体及其他电子产品已占商品出口总值的一半以上。马尼拉也在推动以"后端工艺"为主导的产业改革，并于今年加入美国推出的供应链安全倡议"硅和平"（Pax Silica）。

## 对照：谁掉进了中等收入陷阱

然而，《日经亚洲》同时指出，要跻身高收入国家，东南亚须摆脱对廉价劳动力的依赖，培育本土企业，向高附加值产业转型。

泰国与印度尼西亚是镜鉴。两国虽早于越南、菲律宾跨入中高收入行列，却因工业化进程滞后，增长陷入停滞，双双跌入"中等收入陷阱"。泰国因尖端产业滞后、人口老龄化快于邻国，已成为地区经济增速最慢者；作为东盟最大经济体的印尼，增长同样放缓。

马来西亚则是另一番图景：自1996年跻身中高收入以来，马国也曾长期增长乏力，但借发展半导体、推进先进晶片封装与设计研发，经济重回轨道，正朝2030年前成为高收入国家的目标迈进。

## 下一程：从廉价劳力到高附加值

越南能否在2037年前后超过泰国、2040年左右跻身高收入，取决于它能否把"组装"升级为"创造"。菲律宾加入"硅和平"（Pax Silica），是借势，也是警示：供应链的安全叙事，终要落到本土能力上。

一个普遍命题浮现于此：中等收入不是终点，而是一道筛子。它奖励那些把外部红利转化为内生能力的国家，也冷落那些停在舒适区里的玩家。越南与菲律宾刚刚跨过第一道门槛，真正的比赛，才刚刚鸣枪。

# Vietnam and the Philippines Cross the Upper-Middle-Income Threshold

## The numbers: crossing the threshold

Buoyed by growth in semiconductor and electronics exports, the economic profile of Vietnam and the Philippines has quietly upgraded. In the World Bank's July annual income classification, the two countries' gross national income per capita last year rose to US$4,970 (about S$6,350, ≈RMB 35,700) and US$4,850 respectively, both clearing this year's upper-middle-income threshold of US$4,636. They entered the bracket for the first time.

In other words, by per-capita GNI, two economies long cast as "low-cost manufacturing bases" have written their names into the more consequential column.

## The engine: a semiconductor export wave

The push comes from the global AI investment boom. Nikkei Asia reported on August 18 that as AI capital pours in, Southeast Asia is experiencing growth driven by semiconductor and electronics exports. At the crest stands Vietnam: South Korea's LG Innotek, Samsung Electronics and the US-based Intel all plan new or expanded semiconductor plants there. Hanoi has announced a goal of training 50,000 semiconductor professionals by 2030 and aims to acquire design, manufacturing and testing capability by 2027.

In the Philippines, semiconductors and electronics already make up more than half of the value of goods exports. Manila is also pushing a reform centred on "back-end" processes and this year joined the US-led supply-chain security initiative "Pax Silica."

## The contrast: who fell into the trap

Yet Nikkei Asia also notes that to reach high-income status, ASEAN must shed its reliance on cheap labour, nurture local firms and move up the value chain.

Thailand and Indonesia are cautionary mirrors. Both entered the upper-middle-income bracket before Vietnam and the Philippines, yet stalled as industrialisation lagged, and both slipped into the "middle-income trap." Held back by lagging frontier industries and faster ageing than its neighbours, Thailand is now the slowest-growing economy in the region; Indonesia, the bloc's largest economy, is also slowing.

Malaysia tells a different story: after entering the upper-middle-income group in 1996 and stagnating for years, it leveraged semiconductors — advanced chip packaging and domestic design and R&D — to get back on track, and is now aiming to become high-income before 2030.

## The next climb: from cheap labour to value

Whether Vietnam overtakes Thailand around 2037 and reaches high-income near 2040 depends on whether it can upgrade "assembly" into "creation." The Philippines' Pax Silica bet is both an opportunity and a warning: the security narrative of supply chains ultimately lands on indigenous capability.

A broader proposition emerges: middle income is not a destination but a sieve. It rewards those who convert external dividends into endogenous capacity, and sidelines those who settle in the comfort zone. Vietnam and the Philippines have just cleared the first threshold; the real race is only starting.

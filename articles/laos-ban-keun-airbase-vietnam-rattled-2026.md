# 班根上空的八架战机：卫星影像坐实中国在老挝的“第三海外据点”，越南为何坐不住

9月29日，中国国防部宣布，位于老挝万象以北班根（Ban Keun）机场的“中老联合保障与训练中心”与“中老友谊航空学院”已正式运行。一篇10月8日发自《南华早报》的分析进一步指出：最新卫星影像显示，班根设施内已出现约八架K-8型教练兼轻型攻击机（K-8）与二十余辆支援车辆。这意味着，外界长期揣测的中国“第三海外军事据点”，已从概念落到了跑道上。

## 现场：卫星影像里的八架K-8

据路透社援引商业卫星公司万托尔（Vantor）9月30日的影像，班根跑道上停着被安全分析人士判断为八架K-8型教练机的机体，以及二十多辆支援车辆。K-8是中巴联合研制的双座喷气式教练机，主要用来培养军事飞行员，必要时也能执行部分轻型作战任务，是许多发展中国家空军的标配。伦敦国际战略研究所的数据显示，老挝空军目前仅有四架K-8。

换句话说，卫星图像呈现的，不是几间教室或模拟器，而是已经具备初始作战与训练能力的航空节点。跑道仍在修筑，但“中心”已经开张——这种“边建边用”的节奏，本身就很说明问题。商业卫星公司通常不会随意公开敏感设施的内部部署，影像流出本身，也折射出各方对这座设施的紧盯程度。

## 数字：2000米跑道与“第三据点”

班根设施目前拥有一条约2000米长的跑道、一条滑行道、数万平方米停机坪和一座空中交通管制塔。大部分支援基础设施已经完工。

放在坐标上看，它的分量更清楚。中国此前的两个海外军事设施，一是2017年在非洲之角吉布提设立的保障基地，二是2025年4月在柬埔寨云壤（Ream）海军基地挂牌的“中柬联合保障与训练中心”。班根因此被广泛称为北京的“第三个海外据点”。把这三处串起来：吉布提扼守印度洋西口，云壤面向泰国湾与南海南部，班根则深入中南半岛腹地。一海一空，南北呼应。

## 措辞的玄机：中心，而非基地

请注意北京的表述。班根不叫“基地”，而叫“联合保障与训练中心”，由双方共同维护运营，并“不针对任何第三方”。同样的措辞，也用在云壤与吉布提。

这种语言是刻意的。一旦叫“基地”，就会在整个高度珍视“不结盟”叙事、强调东道国主权规范的地区拉响警报；叫“中心”，则保留了合理的推诿空间，也让东盟的外交机器不至于卡死。批评者指出，真正的关键不在于今晚是否有中国士兵睡在班根，而在于：老挝对本国空军训练的“主权”，在事实上是否已经外包给一个既修了铁路、又建了水坝、还兜着预算的伙伴。

## 越南为何坐不住：被“南北海空呼应”夹住

越南的焦虑最直接。阿联酋拉卜丹安全与防务研究所的研究员拉赫曼（Abdul Rahman Yaacob）认为，河内很可能把班根看作北京“侵蚀其在老挝影响力”的一步。

他的判断基于一组地理事实：向北，中国本就与越南接壤；向西，班根设施可能让北京在老挝建立常驻军事存在；向南，云壤海军基地已在柬埔寨落成。换言之，一旦与中国陷入危机，越南将面临不少观察人士口中的“四面受敌”局面。正因如此，越南总理黎明兴在9月中旬的国防学院讲话中明确表示，越南必须为“前所未有的军事与国防情况”做好准备。

更有意味的是时机。10月8日，黎明兴以政府首脑身份首次对老挝进行双边访问，成为老挝新任总理沙伦赛（Saleumxay Kommasith）接待的首位外国领导人。越南最高层的这次“破冰”式到访，与班根设施投入运作几乎同步——很难说只是巧合。

## 老挝的平衡术：在中俄越之间走钢丝

班根也不只是中国的棋子。拉赫曼指出，这表明老挝正试图在中国、俄罗斯与越南之间平衡自身的防务关系。越南长期与俄罗斯保持紧密的防务合作，老挝则可能把中国当作对冲的一方。

从万象的角度看，中国确实是理想的伙伴：老挝空军规模小、能力受限，长期把飞行员送往中国受训；如今有了班根，本地飞行员可以在本国完成整套训练大纲。阿布扎比拉卜丹学院的助理教授苏尔萨（Olli Pekka Suorsa）直言，对中国而言，“中国已成为老挝空军现代化的首选伙伴”，未来还会有中国飞行教官进驻，老挝也可能采购性价比高的中国战机，如JF-17与FTC-2000两型（JF-17/FTC-2000）。

## 美国的焦虑与中国的“黏性”打法

班根设施还释放出一个信号：中国在大陆东南亚与美国的影响力竞争正在加深。华盛顿照例对北京的海外军事存在扩张表示担忧，从云壤到班根无一例外。

但美国的剧本——制裁加说教——在老挝施展不开。中国用的是另一套：先基础设施、后安全合作，主权话术始终在场。中老铁路2021年12月通车，耗资约60亿美元（约432亿元人民币）；铁路之外，还有水坝、电网与各类贷款。中国持有老挝近一半的主权外债。这套打法更慢，却更黏——它不要求结盟，只培育依赖。这套逻辑在老挝尤其奏效，因为万象几乎没有更廉价的现代化选项，只能把筹码不断押在同一个伙伴身上。

## 镜鉴：中南半岛的天平在倾斜

把班根放回更大的图景：它不只是送几台飞行模拟器，而是北京在中南半岛腹地悄然铺开的安全架构里，最新也最耐人寻味的一块砖。云壤管海，班根管天，两者合起来把泰国夹在中间、框住越南的西侧翼。

对东盟而言，这是“战略自主”这副门面又一道细小的裂缝。老挝与柬埔寨作为区域内最贫困、对外援依赖最深的成员，最愿意接纳更深的中国安全合作；它们这样做，正把区域重心进一步拉向北京。其余成员不愿在中美之间选边，却只能面对一个内部天平正在悄然倾斜的东盟。班根上空的八架K-8，也许是这种倾斜最具体、也最安静的注脚。它并不喧哗，却比任何外交辞令都更诚实。

## 延伸阅读

**中老铁路（China–Laos Railway）**：2021年12月通车、全长1035公里、连接昆明与万象的旗舰工程，是北京对老挝经济押注的招牌，也被普遍视为班根安全合作的经济与后勤前提。

**云壤海军基地（Ream Naval Base）**：位于柬埔寨泰国湾岸，2025年4月挂牌“中柬联合保障与训练中心”，与班根形成“一海一空”的南北呼应，是观察中国东南亚军事布局的关键节点。

**债务陷阱外交（Debt-trap Diplomacy）**：指通过大额贷款使借款国陷入依赖、进而换取战略让步的批评性框架；老挝因中国持有其近一半主权外债，常被援引为该叙事的典型案例。

# Eight jets over Ban Keun: satellite images confirm China's 'third overseas base' in Laos, and why Hanoi is rattled

On 29 September, China's Ministry of National Defence announced that the "China–Laos Joint Support and Training Centre" and the "China–Laos Friendship Aviation Academy" at Ban Keun Airport, north of Vientiane, had formally begun operations. An analysis published by the South China Morning Post on 8 October went further: the latest satellite imagery shows roughly eight K-8 light attack jets and more than two dozen support vehicles already on the ground at Ban Keun. What outside observers had long suspected — China's "third overseas military base" — has, in effect, moved from concept to runway.

## The scene: eight K-8s in the satellite imagery

According to satellite imagery from Vantor dated 30 September, cited by Reuters, what appear to be eight K-8 trainer aircraft and over twenty support vehicles sit on the Ban Keun runway. The K-8 is a Chinese–Pakistani twin-seat jet trainer, primarily used to train military pilots and, when needed, to carry out light attack missions; it is standard equipment for many air forces in the developing world. Data from the International Institute for Strategic Studies in London put Laos's existing K-8 inventory at just four.

In other words, the image does not show a few classrooms or simulators. It shows an aviation node with initial operational and training capacity. The runway is still being built, yet the "centre" is already open — and that "build-while-operating" rhythm is itself telling.

## The numbers: a 2,000-metre runway and the 'third base'

Ban Keun now has a runway of about 2,000 metres, a taxiway, tens of thousands of square metres of apron, and an air-traffic-control tower. Most of the supporting infrastructure is complete.

Plotted on a map, its weight becomes clearer. China's two prior overseas military facilities are the 2017 support base at Djibouti, at the western mouth of the Indian Ocean, and the "China–Cambodia Joint Support and Training Centre" inaugurated at the Ream Naval Base in April 2025. Ban Keun is thus widely described as Beijing's "third overseas base." String them together and a pattern emerges: Djibouti guards the western Indian Ocean, Ream faces the Gulf of Thailand and the southern South China Sea, and Ban Keun reaches deep into the heart of the Indochinese peninsula. Sea and air, north and south, in concert.

## The wording: a 'centre', not a 'base'

Note Beijing's language. Ban Keun is not called a "base" but a "joint support and training centre," co-maintained and co-operated by both sides, and "not directed against any third party." The same wording was used at Ream and at Djibouti.

This is deliberate. Call it a "base" and alarms sound across a region that prizes its "non-aligned" narrative and its norms of host-state sovereignty; call it a "centre" and you preserve a measure of plausible deniability while keeping ASEAN's diplomatic machinery from seizing up. The real question, critics say, is not whether a Chinese soldier sleeps at Ban Keun tonight, but whether Laos's "sovereignty" over its own air-force training has, in practice, been outsourced to a partner that built the railway, the dams and now underwrites the budget.

## Why Hanoi is rattled: squeezed by the sea–air pincer

Vietnam's anxiety is the most immediate. Abdul Rahman Yaacob, a research fellow at the Rabdan Security and Defence Institute in the United Arab Emirates, argues that Hanoi is likely to view Ban Keun as Beijing's move to "encroach into its area of influence" in Vientiane.

His reading rests on geography. To the north, China already shares a border with Vietnam. To the west, the Ban Keun facility could let Beijing establish a persistent military presence in Laos. To the south, the Ream naval base is already operational in Cambodia. In a crisis with China, Vietnam could face what many observers call an "encirclement" — flanked from the north, west and south. It is no accident that Prime Minister Pham Minh Chinh, speaking at the National Defence Academy in mid-September, warned that Vietnam must prepare for "unprecedented" military and defence circumstances.

The timing is telling, too. On 8 October, Pham Minh Chinh paid his first bilateral visit as head of government to Laos, becoming the first foreign leader received by newly appointed Lao Prime Minister Saleumxay Kommasith. That highest-level "ice-breaking" trip and the activation of Ban Keun landed almost in the same week — hard to dismiss as coincidence.

## Laos's balancing act: walking the tightrope between China, Russia and Vietnam

Ban Keun is not merely a Chinese pawn. Rahman Yaacob notes that it signals Laos's attempt to balance its defence relationships among China, Russia and Vietnam. Vietnam's own close defence partnership with Russia gives Laos a reason to court China as a counterweight.

From Vientiane's perspective, China is indeed an ideal partner: the Lao air force is small and capacity-constrained, and has long sent pilots to train in China; with Ban Keun, local pilots can now complete their full syllabus at home. Olli Pekka Suorsa, assistant professor of security at Rabdan Academy in Abu Dhabi, puts it bluntly — for modernising the Lao air force, "China has become the chosen partner." Chinese flight instructors are expected to be posted to the facility, and Vientiane may acquire budget-friendly Chinese combat aircraft such as the JF-17 and the FTC-2000.

## Washington's anxiety and Beijing's 'sticky' playbook

The facility also sends a signal: China's competition with the United States for influence across mainland Southeast Asia is deepening. Washington, as usual, has voiced concern over the expansion of Beijing's overseas military footprint, from Ream to Ban Keun.

But the American script — sanctions and sermons — does not play in Vientiane. China runs a different playbook: infrastructure first, security cooperation after, with the sovereignty rhetoric never far away. The China–Laos Railway opened in December 2021 at a cost of about US$6 billion; beyond the railway sit dams, power grids and a dense web of loans. China holds nearly half of Laos's sovereign external debt. This approach is slower, but stickier — it demands no alliance, only dependence.

## The takeaway: the Indochina balance is tilting

Set Ban Keun back into the larger picture and it is more than a few flight simulators. It is the newest and most intriguing brick in the security architecture Beijing is quietly laying across the Indochinese heartland. Ream commands the sea; Ban Keun commands the air. Together they sandwich Thailand and box in Vietnam's western flank.

For ASEAN, this is one more fine crack in the façade of "strategic autonomy." Laos and Cambodia, the bloc's poorest and most aid-dependent members, are the most willing to deepen security ties with China; in doing so they pull the regional centre of gravity further toward Beijing. The rest of the membership wants neither to choose sides between Washington and Beijing nor to watch the internal balance tip — yet tip it slowly does. The eight K-8s over Ban Keun may be the quietest, most concrete footnote to that tilt.

## Further reading

**China–Laos Railway**: The 1,035-kilometre flagship line linking Kunming and Vientiane, opened in December 2021, is Beijing's signature economic bet on Laos and is widely seen as the economic and logistical precondition for security cooperation such as Ban Keun.

**Ream Naval Base**: On Cambodia's Gulf of Thailand coast, the "China–Cambodia Joint Support and Training Centre" inaugurated in April 2025 forms the "sea-and-air" north–south pairing with Ban Keun and is a key node for reading China's Southeast Asian military layout.

**Debt-trap Diplomacy**: A critical framework arguing that large loans are used to ensnare borrowers into dependence and extract strategic concessions; Laos, with China holding nearly half its sovereign external debt, is frequently cited as its textbook case.

# 柬埔寨扫荡三万涉诈嫌犯：雷霆行动背后，诈骗经济只是转入地下

过去一年，柬埔寨以一场全国性的专项整治，向盘踞多年的电信诈骗产业开刀。据联合早报报道，柬埔寨国家打击网络诈骗委员会（National Committee to Combat Online Scams，简称"打击网诈委"）在过去一年的清剿中，扫荡了七百多处诈骗窝点，拘留近三万名嫌犯，并逮捕了十五名涉嫌包庇的高级官员与执法人员。数字触目惊心，但它真正暴露的，是诈骗经济如何深度嵌入地方权力与资本。

## 数字：700 处窝点、近三万嫌犯

这场行动的规模本身就是一份诊断书。在一年的时间里，柬埔寨方面救助并遣返了五万八千二百六十六名外国人，其中既有被骗入园区、被迫从事诈骗的劳工，也有来自中国、越南、法国、日本、韩国、新加坡、泰国等二十九个国家和地区的外籍嫌犯。换言之，诈骗园区早已不是本地治安问题，而是一张横跨数十国、以全球网民为猎物的跨国网络。

更值得玩味的是赌场这条线。执法部门重点打击了二十七家涉案赌场，永久吊销十八家执照，勒令九家停业整顿。赌场与电诈长期共生：前者提供资金洗白的壳，后者提供诱骗的话术与流量。把赌场一并端掉，说明柬方终于承认，诈骗产业的命脉就在这些灰色牌照之中。

## 为何是现在：北京施压，华盛顿注视

为什么是这一年，而不是更早？答案写在压力的来源上。联合早报引述法新社与新华社的报道指出，此次清剿是在中国与美国等国的"强大压力"下展开的。说白了，诈骗园区之所以能长期存在，是因为它在相当一段时间里被一些地方势力当作"招商引资"和"外汇创收"的灰色引擎；而它之所以被定性为必须铲除的毒瘤，是因为它已经严重外溢——既伤害了中国公民，也损害了柬埔寨自身的国际形象。

北京的关切不难理解：数以万计的中国公民在园区里既是受害者也是被胁迫的施害者，保护海外同胞的安危，是侨务与外交无法回避的硬任务。华盛顿的注视，则更多出于反诈与地区安全的考量。两大国的共同施压，给了洪玛内政府一个难得的"内外一致"的清剿理由。

## 15 名官员落网：腐败本就是商业模式

十五名高级官员与执法人员落网，是这份声明里最不该被轻轻带过的细节。被查的包括一名副检察官、一名移民总局副局长、金边市警察局一名副局长，以及外交部前总司长。这说明什么？说明在不少园区里，腐败不是偶然的副产品，而是这门生意得以运转的底层架构。没有保护伞，窝点根本开不下去；没有里应外合，近三万名嫌犯也关不进、跑不掉。

所以，把十五名官员一并拿下，恰恰说明此轮行动与以往"运动式"整治的不同——它试图从权力末端切断保护链。但也要清醒：反腐局目前只针对四起具体案件出手，这更像是"杀猴儆鸡"的开端，而非整张腐败网的覆灭。

## 从园区到咖啡馆：诈骗像九头蛇般重生

真正棘手的问题，藏在声明末尾一句轻描淡写的提醒里：犯罪团伙已经改变策略，转向零散和隐蔽化作案，潜伏在宾馆、公寓、出租屋、汽车乃至咖啡馆中。换句话说，当大型园区被端，这门生意并没有消失，只是化整为零。

这正是观察者最该警惕的地方。诈骗产业之所以屡打不绝，根本在于供需两端都还在：一端是全球范围内被虚假恋爱、加密货币投资诱惑的庞大受害群体；另一端，是被高薪招聘骗来、失去人身自由后被强迫行骗的劳工。只要这两端不除，窝点从工业园搬进咖啡馆，不过是换了个门牌。柬埔寨九月将主办以"共同切断链条，终结网络诈骗"为主题的国际会议，方向是对的，但若只靠一国扫荡，终究是按下葫芦浮起瓢。

## 一场会议，一次合法性自救

九月二十三日到二十四日，柬埔寨将主办打击网络诈骗国际会议，邀请各国政府、国际组织、执法机构、金融机构与科技公司参会。这既是务实的合作平台，也是一场精心计算的形象工程。

过去一年，诈骗园区让柬埔寨背上了"犯罪天堂"的标签，外资犹豫、游客却步、外交承压。把一场国际会议办起来，等于向外界递出一份"我在认真治理"的答卷。问题是，会议能凝聚多少实质性的联合执法机制，而非仅仅停留在宣言与合影，才是检验诚意的关键。

## 对华侨华人的意味

这桩事，与海外华侨华人并非毫无瓜葛。一方面，被困园区的中国籍劳工，是不折不扣的同胞受害群体，他们的安危直接牵动侨务与领事保护神经；另一方面，诈骗产业在外泛滥，往往以"华人圈""中文社群"为掩护与入口，久而久之，损害的正是整个华人社群在当地的声誉与信任基础。

更深层看，诈骗园区已成为中国—柬埔寨关系里一个挥之不去的摩擦点。中方持续施压要求清剿，柬方既要回应国内灰色利益的惯性，又要维系对华关系的稳定。如何在主权与协作之间找到平衡，既考验金边的治理勇气，也考验两国关系的成熟度。

## 遣返与区域流动：三十万外籍人员离境，犯罪向邻国外溢

清剿的直接后果，是数十万外籍涉诈人员被迫离场。柬埔寨副总理兼内政大臣萨尔索卡表示，自去年加大打击以来，累计约三十万名涉嫌或从事电诈的外籍人员被捕、被驱逐或自行离开。这一数字远超被拘留的近三万嫌犯，说明大量低端"水军"选择了主动撤离而非正面硬扛。

但人走，生意未必走。随着柬埔寨持续施压，部分犯罪活动向缅甸、老挝、菲律宾等地转移，诈骗网络跨境流动、就地重生。柬官方也承认，单靠一国难以根除，必须依靠区域协作。金边的扫荡在切除本地病灶的同时，也可能把风险"挤出"到监管薄弱的邻境——这正是东南亚反诈的"打地鼠"困局。

对中国而言，跨境遣返是合作最直接的成果。据中国驻柬大使汪文斌撰文，今年以来柬方已向中方遣返逾两千七百名电诈嫌疑人，数量创历史新高；中柬警方更在蒙多基里、金边、柴桢等地联合行动，抓获两千一百余名嫌疑人并全部移交中方。被解救的同胞，正一批批经包机押解回国。

## 六国机制与法律框架：从"运动式"走向常态协作

决定清剿能否真正落地的，是区域协作与制度。二〇二五年十一月十四日，中、柬、老、缅、泰、越六国打击电诈部级会议在昆明召开，六国签署成果文件，并就建立国际反诈联盟磋商；国际刑警组织与联合国毒罪办以观察员列席，反诈协作由此从双边个案升级为多边常态化机制。

配套的是法律框架搭建。柬方称本轮行动系统建立了网络安全法律体系，并对涉案人员、网络与涉事官员采取严格法律行动。执法思路也从追查基层"键盘手"转向瓦解全链条、追捕主谋。柬国家通讯社披露，太子控股创始人陈志、金边集团创始人刘任、汇旺集团前董事长李雄等高级嫌疑人已被捕并引渡中国受审——把"老板"而非仅"马仔"送上法庭，才是切断命脉的关键。

## 延伸阅读

**网络诈骗委员会（National Committee to Combat Online Scams）**：柬埔寨首相洪玛内设立的高级别专责机构，统筹全国打击电诈与人口贩运的执法行动。

**跨境治理（Cross-Border Governance）**：指单一国家无力单独应对、必须依靠多国执法与司法协作的犯罪形态，电诈与贩运是其典型代表。

**人口贩运（Human Trafficking）**：诈骗园区常以高薪招聘为饵，诱骗他国公民入境后限制人身自由，强迫其从事诈骗，属典型贩运犯罪。

# Cambodia's Scam Crackdown Arrests 30,000 — But the Industry Is Simply Going Underground

Over the past year, Cambodia has waged a nationwide campaign against the telecom-fraud industry that had taken root for years. According to Lianhe Zaobao, the National Committee to Combat Online Scams (the country's lead task force) raided more than 700 scam compounds in a year-long sweep, detained nearly 30,000 suspects, and arrested 15 senior officials and law-enforcement officers suspected of shielding the networks. The numbers are staggering — but what they really expose is how deeply the scam economy has fused with local power and capital.

## The numbers: 700 dens, 30,000 arrests

The sheer scale of the operation is itself a diagnosis. In a single year, Cambodian authorities rescued and repatriated 58,266 foreigners — a mix of deceived laborers forced to defraud others and foreign suspects drawn from 29 countries and territories, including China, Vietnam, France, Japan, South Korea, Singapore and Thailand. In other words, the scam compounds were never a local law-and-order nuisance; they were a transnational web spanning dozens of states, with internet users worldwide as prey.

The casino thread is the most telling. Enforcement targeted 27 implicated casinos, permanently revoked the licences of 18, and ordered nine others to suspend for rectification. Casinos and cyber-scam rings have long been symbiotic: the former launder the money, the latter supply the scripts and the traffic. Shutting down the casinos signals that Phnom Penh has finally admitted the scam industry's lifeblood runs through these grey licences.

## Why now: Beijing's pressure, Washington's watching

Why this year, and not earlier? The answer lies in where the pressure comes from. Citing Agence France-Presse and Xinhua, Lianhe Zaobao notes the crackdown unfolded under "strong pressure" from China, the United States and others. Plainly put, the compounds persisted for so long because some local forces treated them as a grey engine of "investment promotion" and foreign-exchange earnings. They were reclassified as a tumour to be excised only when the damage spilled over — harming Chinese citizens and tarnishing Cambodia's own international standing.

Beijing's concern is straightforward: tens of thousands of Chinese citizens have been trapped in these compounds, at once victims and coerced perpetrators. Protecting compatriots abroad is a hard task that diaspora affairs and diplomacy cannot回避. Washington's gaze is shaped more by counter-fraud and regional-security calculations. The convergence of the two great powers handed the Hun Manet government a rare "both domestic and external" justification for a cleanup.

## The 15 officials: corruption is the business model

The 15 senior officials and officers swept up are the detail that should not be glossed over. Those implicated include a deputy prosecutor, a deputy director-general of the immigration department, a deputy chief of the Phnom Penh police, and a former director-general of the foreign ministry. What does this tell us? In many compounds, corruption was never an incidental byproduct — it was the underlying architecture that made the business run. Without protective umbrellas, the dens could not open; without inside collusion, nearly 30,000 suspects could neither be contained nor escape.

Arresting the 15 therefore marks a break from past "campaign-style" cleanups: it tries to sever the protective chain at the level of state power. But stay sober — the anti-corruption bureau has so far acted on only four specific cases. This looks more like the opening move of "killing the chicken to scare the monkeys" than the dismantling of the entire corrupt web.

## From compounds to cafés: the hydra adapts

The genuinely stubborn problem hides in a lightly worded caveat at the end of the statement: the criminal groups have changed tactics, shifting to scattered and concealed operations, lurking in hotels, apartments, rented rooms, cars and even cafés. In other words, as the large compounds are raided, the business does not vanish — it simply atomizes.

This is precisely where observers should be most alert. The reason scam syndicates keep reviving is that both ends of the supply chain survive: on one side, a vast global pool of victims lured by fake romance and crypto-investment scams; on the other, laborers deceived by lucrative job offers, stripped of freedom, and forced to defraud. So long as those two ends persist, moving the den from an industrial park to a café is merely a change of signboard. Cambodia will host an international anti-scam conference themed "Cut the Chain Together, End Online Scams" in September — the direction is right, but a single country's sweeps will always be a case of pressing the gourd only to see the calabash bob up elsewhere.

## A conference, and a bid for legitimacy

On 23–24 September, Cambodia will host an international anti-online-scam conference, convening government representatives, international organizations, law-enforcement agencies, financial institutions and tech firms. It is both a practical platform for cooperation and a carefully calculated act of image repair.

For a year now, the scam compounds have branded Cambodia as a "criminal haven" — spooking investors, deterring tourists, and straining diplomacy. Staging an international conference amounts to handing the world a report card reading "we are seriously governing." The real test of sincerity, however, is how many substantive joint-enforcement mechanisms the meeting actually forges, rather than how many declarations and group photos it produces.

## What it means for the overseas Chinese

This affair is not unrelated to the overseas Chinese. On one hand, the Chinese nationals trapped in the compounds are compatriots who are unambiguously victims; their safety bears directly on diaspora affairs and consular protection. On the other hand, the scam industry's outward spread often uses "Chinese circles" and "Chinese-language communities" as cover and entry points — and over time, what suffers is the reputation and the trust base of ethnic-Chinese communities throughout the region.

At a deeper level, the scam compounds have become a persistent friction point in China–Cambodia relations. Beijing keeps pressing for a cleanup; Phnom Penh must both answer the inertia of domestic grey interests and keep its ties with China steady. How to balance sovereignty and cooperation will test not only Phnom Penh's governing courage but also the maturity of the bilateral relationship.

## Deportations and regional spillover

The crackdown's immediate effect is the exodus of hundreds of thousands of foreign scam workers. Cambodia's Deputy Prime Minister and Interior Minister Sar Sokha said that since the intensified drive began last year, a cumulative total of roughly 300,000 foreign nationals suspected of or engaged in online fraud have been arrested, deported, or left the country on their own. That figure dwarfs the nearly 30,000 detained suspects, indicating that a large rank-and-file of low-end "keyboard workers" chose to flee rather than confront the sweep.

But people leave, the business may not. As Phnom Penh keeps up the pressure, some criminal activity is migrating to Myanmar, Laos and the Philippines, where scam networks re-establish themselves across borders. Cambodian officials themselves concede that no single country can root out the crime and that regional cooperation is essential. The sweep thus excises the local tumour while possibly squeezing risk into less-regulated neighbours — the classic Southeast Asian "whack-a-mole" dilemma.

For China, cross-border repatriation is the most tangible fruit of the cooperation. In an article, Chinese Ambassador to Cambodia Wang Wenbin noted that this year alone Phnom Penh has handed over more than 2,700 scam suspects to Beijing — a record high — while Chinese and Cambodian police, in joint operations across Mondulkiri, Phnom Penh and Svay Rieng, detained over 2,100 suspects and transferred all of them to China. Rescued compatriots are being flown home in batches.

## The six-nation framework and the legal architecture

What determines whether the cleanup sticks is regional coordination and institutions. On 14 November 2025, the anti-online-scam ministerial meeting of China, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Thailand and Vietnam convened in Kunming, where the six signed an outcome document and discussed building an international anti-scam alliance. INTERPOL and the UN Office on Drugs and Crime attended as observers, upgrading cooperation from bilateral casework to a standing multilateral mechanism.

Alongside it comes a legal framework. Phnom Penh says the campaign systematically built a cybersecurity legal system and pursued strict legal action against perpetrators, networks and complicit officials. Enforcement thinking has also shifted — from rounding up front-line "keyboard workers" to dismantling the whole chain and chasing kingpins. Cambodia's state news agency reports that senior suspects including Chen Zhi of Prince Holding Group, Liu Ren of Phnom Penh Group and former Huione chairman Li Xiong have been arrested and extradited to China — putting the "bosses," not just the foot soldiers, on trial is what severs the industry's lifeline.

## Further reading

**National Committee to Combat Online Scams**: A high-level body established under Prime Minister Hun Manet to coordinate Cambodia's nationwide crackdown on online fraud and human trafficking.

**Cross-Border Governance**: The class of crimes — online scams, trafficking — that no single state can contain alone and that require multinational law-enforcement and judicial cooperation.

**Human Trafficking**: Scam compounds routinely lure foreign nationals with fake job offers, then detain them and force them to defraud others — a textbook trafficking offence.

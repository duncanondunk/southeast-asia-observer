# 印尼迎来"加查·马达"号：东南亚第二艘航母的梦想与账单

一艘服役近四十年的意大利老舰，正穿越苏伊士运河与印度洋，驶向印尼苏门答腊南端的楠榜海军基地。它就是意大利海军退役轻型航母"加里波第"号，抵达后将更名"加查·马达"号——以满者伯夷王朝名相命名，加入印尼海军。这意味着印尼将成为继泰国之后，东南亚第二个拥有航母的国家。一艘一九八五年服役、二〇二四年退役的"空壳"老舰，为何让千岛之国如此郑重？答案藏在印尼的海权梦想与地区海上态势的新变量里。

## 来龙去脉：从塔兰托到楠榜

"加里波第"号（舷号 C-551）是意大利战后建造的第一艘航母，一九八五年正式服役，长期担任意大利海军旗舰，二〇二四年退役时已在役三十九年。意大利议会于二〇二六年四月二十八日批准将其无偿捐赠印尼。八月二十四日，该舰在护卫舰簇拥下离开塔兰托港，预计耗时二十余天，经苏伊士运河、红海进入印度洋，九月上中旬抵印尼，并计划于九月二十五日举行入役与换旗仪式，总统普拉博沃拟亲自登舰见证。

对印尼而言，这并非一次普通的装备引进，而是一次象征意义浓厚的"入座"——时隔近三十年，东南亚的航母俱乐部终于迎来第二位成员。上一位是泰国的"差克里·纳吕贝特"号，自一九九〇年代服役以来，如今已很少出海。

## 性能与现实：一艘"空壳"航母能做什么

"加里波第"号满载排水量约一点三八万吨，全长一百八十点二米，舰艏设有滑跃甲板，机库面积约一千六百五十平方米，最多可搭载十六至十八架舰载机。它原本的配置是 AV-8B"鹞II"垂直起降战斗机与 SH-3D"海王"、EH-101"灰背隼"直升机。

但现实是，意大利在交付前已拆除阿斯派德防空导弹、奥托马特反舰导弹与三联装鱼雷管等核心武备，交给印尼的是一艘"空壳"。加之 AV-8B 机队已全部退役，印尼短期内拿不到替代的固定翼舰载机。因此，雅加达的打算是把它改造为"直升机母舰加无人机母舰"：搭载 AS565"黑豹"、H225M、米-17 等多型直升机，并引入土耳其拜卡公司的 TB-3 察打一体无人机。TB-3 起飞重量约一点四五吨、载荷可达二百八十公斤、具备二十一到二十四小时超长航时，此前已在土耳其"阿纳多卢"号轻型航母上完成短距起飞测试。

## 海上雄心：万岛之国的战略逻辑

印尼号称"万岛之国"，国土散布于超过一万七千个岛屿，扼守马六甲、巽他与龙目等全球关键水道。对雅加达而言，一艘具备三十节高航速（四台 LM2500 燃气轮机驱动）的直通甲板大舰，价值不在远洋决战，而在对核心海峡的快速机动管控、对分散岛屿的巡逻与后勤支援，以及地震海啸等灾害中的海上救援指挥。

这股雄心有其历史底色。苏加诺时期，印尼在苏联援助下曾建立起东亚地区规模领先的海军，主力"伊里安"号轻巡洋舰满载排水量超过一点六万吨；一九六五年政变后印尼倒向西方，苏联断供，这支"无敌舰队"在热带盐雾中迅速锈蚀瘫痪，至一九七二年"伊里安"号被变卖拆解。今日迎接"加查·马达"号，某种程度上是那段"大国梦"的隔代回响。

## 地区涟漪：东南亚海上军备的再平衡

一艘万吨级直通甲板舰的出现，必然在本以二三千吨级护卫舰、巡逻舰为主的水域投下新的参照物。它客观上为周边国家提供了一个"标准航母物理特征"的活样本——全钢舰体、高耸舰岛、三十节机动轨迹，恰好填补了该海域在反舰体系演练中对大型水面目标的识别空白。

更值得关注的是心理层面。长期以来，东南亚海军装备偏小、分散，缺乏"旗舰"符号。印尼此举可能刺激区域内其他国家对"蓝水象征"的重新想象，即便多数国家并不具备持续的航母运营能力。海上态势的微妙变化，往往始于一艘船的象征，而非其实战效能。

## 意大利的算盘：送船背后的防务生意

罗马方面绝非"做好事"。意大利公开材料显示，继续封存这艘退役老舰，每年至少耗资约五百万欧元（约三千九百万元人民币），若拆解则需近一千八百七十万欧元（约一点四六亿元人民币）。无偿转让，等于把维护费和拆解费一次性归零，还顺手换得与东南亚重要国家的防务合作窗口。

更深的盘算是后续军售。分析普遍指出，意大利可借此向印尼推销鲉鱼级潜艇、教练机与巡逻机等装备，以"赠舰"为敲门砖，打开长期军贸通道。对预算并不宽裕的印尼海军而言，这"零元购"的账单，真正的大头在交付之后的改装、人员培训、母港建设与航空器整合。

## 前车之鉴：泰国"差克里·纳吕贝特"的警示

泰国那艘"差克里·纳吕贝特"号，是最好的镜子。它自一九九〇年代服役后，因维护不济、舰载机缺失，很快从"国之重器"沦为"港口景观"，多数时间泊于港内。印尼显然吸取了教训：朱安达海军航空基地已在停机坪上画出巨型航母甲板，供飞行员与甲板组练手；楠榜新母港也在赶工，力求与舰到港同步启用。

但母港与涂装解决不了根本问题。一艘老龄燃气轮机舰的燃油消耗惊人，长期运作对印尼海军的财政是沉重负担；本土 PTPAL 船厂能否承接维护改造，亦是对其防务工业的一次大考。

## 冷思考：梦想与账单之间

客观地说，"加查·马达"号的到来，既是印尼推行海上远洋战略、强化核心水道管控的关键一步，也在无形中成为周边海空精确打击体系研判万吨大舰的实体样本。它既是海权梦想的旗舰载体，也是一道长期待解的财政与工业考题。

对中国与地区而言，这艘老舰改变的不是力量平衡，而是心理预期。东南亚的海上叙事，正从"近海守成"悄悄转向对"蓝水符号"的重新想象——而这，恰恰是更值得持续观察的变量。

## 延伸阅读

**加查·马达（Gajah Mada）**：十四世纪满者伯夷王朝的名相，以统一群岛的"帕拉帕誓言"闻名，在印尼民族叙事中象征统一与海权抱负，故被用来命名这艘航母。

**TB-3 无人机（Baykar TB-3）**：土耳其拜卡公司研制的舰载察打一体无人机，已在"阿纳多卢"号轻型航母完成短距起飞测试，被视为中小航母的低成本航空战力方案。

**差克里·纳吕贝特号（HTMS Chakri Naruebet）**：泰国海军航母，一九九〇年代服役，东南亚首艘航母，因维护与舰载机短缺长期出海甚少，常被引为"航母变港口景观"的前车之鉴。

# Indonesia Welcomes the "Gajah Mada": The Dream and the Bill of Southeast Asia's Second Aircraft Carrier

An Italian warship nearly four decades old is steaming across the Suez Canal and the Indian Ocean toward the Lampung naval base at the southern tip of Sumatra. It is the decommissioned Italian light carrier Giuseppe Garibaldi, which upon arrival will be renamed Gajah Mada—after the chief minister of the Majapahit empire—and join the Indonesian navy. This makes Indonesia the second Southeast Asian nation, after Thailand, to operate an aircraft carrier. Why does an "empty-shell" veteran, commissioned in 1985 and retired in 2024, command such ceremony from the archipelago? The answer lies in Indonesia's maritime ambitions and a new variable in the region's naval equation.

## How it came to be: from Taranto to Lampung

The Giuseppe Garibaldi (hull C-551) was Italy's first post-war carrier, entering service in 1985 and serving as the fleet flagship for much of its 39 years before retirement in 2024. Italy's parliament approved its gratis transfer to Indonesia on 28 April 2026. On 24 August the ship left Taranto under escort, a voyage of more than 20 days through the Suez Canal and the Red Sea into the Indian Ocean, reaching Indonesia in early-to-mid September, with a commissioning and flag-change ceremony planned for 25 September—President Prabowo is expected to board for the occasion.

For Indonesia this is no ordinary hardware acquisition but a symbolic "taking of the seat": nearly three decades on, Southeast Asia's carrier club finally gains a second member. The first was Thailand's Chakri Naruebet, which since the 1990s has rarely left port.

## Capability and reality: what an "empty-shell" carrier can do

The Garibaldi displaces about 13,850 tonnes at full load, is 180.2 metres long, carries a ski-jump ramp and a hangar of roughly 1,650 square metres, and could in principle embark 16–18 aircraft—originally the AV-8B Harrier II V/STOL fighter and SH-3D Sea King or EH-101 Merlin helicopters.

The catch is that Italy stripped the Aspide air-defence missiles, Otomat anti-ship missiles and triple torpedo tubes before handover, delivering an empty hull. With the AV-8B fleet long retired, Indonesia has no short-term fixed-wing replacement. Jakarta's plan is therefore to convert it into a "helicopter carrier plus drone carrier": embarking AS565 Panther, H225M and Mi-17 helicopters and introducing Turkey's Baykar TB-3 armed drone. The TB-3, with a take-off weight of about 1.45 tonnes, a 280 kg payload and 21–24 hours of endurance, has already completed short-takeoff trials aboard Turkey's light carrier Anadolu.

## Maritime ambition: the strategic logic of an archipelagic state

Indonesia, an "archipelago of a thousand islands" spread across more than 17,000 islands, commands the Malacca, Sunda and Lombok straits—global chokepoints. For Jakarta, a straight-deck ship capable of 30 knots (powered by four LM2500 gas turbines) matters less for blue-water combat than for rapid control of core straits, patrol and logistic support to scattered islands, and seaborne command in quake- and tsunami-relief operations.

This ambition has historical roots. Under Sukarno, aided by the Soviet Union, Indonesia fielded the region's largest navy, headlined by the light cruiser Irian (over 16,000 tonnes at full load). After the 1965 coup and the westward reorientation, Soviet supply ceased; the "invincible fleet" rusted away in the tropical salt air and the Irian was sold for scrap in 1972. Receiving the Gajah Mada is, in a sense, an echo across generations of that earlier "great-power dream."

## Regional ripples: rebalancing Southeast Asia's naval kit

A 10,000-tonne straight-deck vessel inevitably casts a new reference point in waters otherwise dominated by 2,000–3,000-tonne frigates and patrol craft. Objectively it provides neighbouring countries a living sample of "standard carrier physical signatures"—an all-steel hull, tall island, 30-knot manoeuvre profile—filling a gap in the region's anti-ship training for large surface targets.

The psychological layer matters more. Southeast Asian fleets have long been small and dispersed, lacking a "flagship" symbol. Indonesia's move may prompt others to reimagine "blue-water emblems" even if few can sustain carrier operations. Subtle shifts in the maritime picture often begin with one ship's symbolism rather than its combat value.

## Rome's calculus: the defence business behind the gift

Rome is hardly being charitable. Italian disclosures show that keeping the retired hull in storage cost at least €5 million (about US$5.5 million) a year, while scrapping it would run nearly €18.7 million (about US$20.6 million). A gratis transfer zeroes out both the upkeep and scrap bills and buys a defence-cooperation opening with a major regional state.

The deeper play is follow-on sales. Analysts widely note Italy can use the carrier as a door-opener to pitch Scorpène-class submarines, trainers and patrol aircraft to Indonesia, turning a "free ship" into a long-term arms-trade conduit. For a navy of modest budget, the real bulk of this "zero-cost" bill arrives after delivery—refits, crew training, base construction and aircraft integration.

## A cautionary tale: Thailand's Chakri Naruebet

Thailand's Chakri Naruebet is the clearest mirror. Since the 1990s, poor maintenance and the absence of carrier-capable aircraft turned it from a "national treasure" into a "harbour landmark," mostly moored in port. Indonesia appears to have learned: the Juanda naval air base has painted a giant carrier deck for pilots and deck crews to rehearse on, and the Lampung base is being rushed to coincide with the ship's arrival.

But a home port and fresh paint solve nothing fundamental. An ageing gas-turbine hull drinks fuel voraciously, and sustaining it is a heavy fiscal burden; whether the domestic PTPAL shipyard can handle upkeep and modification is itself a test of Indonesia's defence industry.

## A cool note: between dream and bill

Objectively, the Gajah Mada's arrival is both a step in Indonesia's maritime strategy and an accessible physical sample for neighbouring precision-strike systems to study large surface targets. It is at once the flagship of a maritime dream and a long-running fiscal and industrial exam paper.

For China and the region, this old ship changes not the balance of power but the balance of expectations. Southeast Asia's naval narrative is quietly shifting from "coastal holding" toward a renewed imagination of "blue-water symbols"—and that, precisely, is the variable worth watching.

## Further reading

**Gajah Mada**: The famed 14th-century chief minister of the Majapahit empire, known for the "Sumpah Palapa" oath to unify the archipelago; a symbol of unity and maritime ambition in Indonesian national narrative, hence the carrier's name.

**TB-3 (Baykar TB-3)**: A Turkish shipborne armed drone that has completed short-takeoff trials aboard the light carrier Anadolu, viewed as a low-cost aviation solution for small carriers.

**HTMS Chakri Naruebet**: The Royal Thai Navy carrier commissioned in the 1990s, Southeast Asia's first; long underused owing to maintenance and aircraft shortfalls, often cited as the cautionary tale of a carrier becoming a "harbour landmark."

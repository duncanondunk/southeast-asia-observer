# 越南外长将访缅甸，政变以来首次

据路透社报道，越南外交部周二（8月25日）宣布，外长黎怀忠将于周三（8月26日）正式访问缅甸。这是自2021年缅甸政变以来，越南外长首次对缅甸进行正式访问。据联合早报报道，此行他预计将与缅甸外长丁貌瑞共同主持双边合作会议。

## 河内主动出手的时机

在东盟与缅甸军政府关系持续疏远的大背景下，河内选择此时派出外长，绝非偶然。去年以来，东盟内部对缅政策出现明显分化：部分成员国主张继续施压，另一些则倾向于恢复接触。越南素来以"静默外交"著称，此次高规格访问，既是对缅甸作为东盟不可或缺成员的再次重申，也是在区域外交版图上提前落子。在湄公河次区域合作与东盟一体化双重议程下，河内更不愿见到缅甸议题被域外力量主导。

## 五点共识下的接触逻辑

越南的立场表述相当清晰：缅甸是东盟不可缺少的成员，而五点共识是东盟持续接触并支持缅甸的关键框架。这一表述与东盟官方口径保持一致，却也透露出务实倾向——在和平进程长期停滞的情况下，越南更倾向于通过双边渠道维持对话，而非将接触完全悬置。据联合早报报道，上月东盟外长与缅甸外长举行五年来首次面对面会议，越缅双方也互派了新任大使，可见接触正在多层次铺开。

## 泰国与老挝的先例

越南并非孤例。近几个月，泰国与老挝已主动推动与缅甸的重新接触。据联合早报报道，缅甸军方领导人敏昂莱先后于7月访问老挝、8月访问泰国。东盟的"静默外交"正悄然转向"务实接触"，越南此访可视为这一趋势的延续与确认。

## 缅甸重返区域外交的信号

黎怀忠的访问，象征着缅甸正以缓慢却可见的步伐，重新嵌入区域多边外交网络。对越南而言，保持与缅甸的接触，既是维护东盟中心地位，也是在湄公河次区域影响力竞争中不落人后。对东盟整体而言，若能在五点共识框架下重启有效对话，缅甸的逐步回归将有利于区域稳定。当然，一次访问难以改变缅甸国内僵局，但其象征意义不容低估——它表明东盟的接触路径正在从冻结走向务实。

## 延伸阅读

**五点共识（Five-Point Consensus）**：东盟于2021年4月就缅甸局势达成的五项共识，包括停止暴力、各方对话、人道主义援助等，是东盟对缅政策的核心框架。

**东盟中心地位（ASEAN Centrality）**：指东盟在区域合作机制中保持主导与协调角色的原则，是越南等成员国维护区域秩序的重要依托。

**静默外交（Quiet Diplomacy）**：以低调、非公开方式开展外交磋商的做法，越南常用此策略处理敏感议题。

# Vietnam's Foreign Minister to Visit Myanmar, First Since the Coup

Reuters reported on Tuesday that Vietnam's Ministry of Foreign Affairs announced Foreign Minister Bui Thanh Son will travel to Myanmar on Wednesday, August 26. The visit marks the first formal trip by a Vietnamese foreign minister since Myanmar's 2021 coup. According to Lianhe Zaobao, he is expected to co-chair a bilateral cooperation meeting with Myanmar's Foreign Minister Than Swe during the trip.

## Hanoi's timing in stepping forward

Against the backdrop of distant relations between ASEAN and Myanmar's junta, Hanoi's decision to dispatch its foreign minister now is hardly incidental. Since last year, ASEAN's internal policy toward Myanmar has visibly fractured: some member states favor continued pressure, while others lean toward renewed engagement. Vietnam, long known for its "quiet diplomacy," is with this high-level visit both reaffirming Myanmar as an indispensable ASEAN member and staking out its position on the regional diplomatic map. Within the dual agenda of Mekong subregional cooperation and ASEAN integration, Hanoi is equally unwilling to see the Myanmar question dominated by extra-regional powers. The calculus is therefore as much about regional order as about bilateral ties.

## The engagement logic under the Five-Point Consensus

Vietnam's stance is clear: Myanmar is an indispensable member of ASEAN, and the Five-Point Consensus remains the key framework through which the bloc sustains engagement and support. This aligns with official ASEAN language, yet reveals a pragmatic tilt — with the peace process stalled for years, Vietnam prefers keeping dialogue alive through bilateral channels rather than suspending contact entirely. As Lianhe Zaobao noted, last month ASEAN foreign ministers held their first face-to-face meeting with Myanmar's counterpart in five years, and Vietnam and Myanmar also exchanged new ambassadors, showing engagement unfolding across multiple levels. The co-chairing of a bilateral cooperation meeting further institutionalizes this contact.

## The Thailand and Laos precedents

Vietnam is not alone. In recent months, Thailand and Laos have actively pushed to strengthen ties with Myanmar. According to Lianhe Zaobao, junta chief Min Aung Hlaing visited Laos in July and Thailand in August. ASEAN's "quiet diplomacy" is quietly shifting toward "pragmatic engagement," and Hanoi's visit can be read as a continuation and confirmation of that trend. The willingness of multiple mainland Southeast Asian neighbors to re-open channels suggests a broader recalibration within the bloc. Such precedents lower the political cost for Hanoi to follow suit.

## Signals of Myanmar's re-entry into regional diplomacy

Bui Thanh Son's visit symbolizes Myanmar's slow but visible steps back into the web of regional multilateral diplomacy. For Vietnam, maintaining contact safeguards ASEAN centrality and ensures it does not fall behind in the contest for influence across the Mekong subregion. For ASEAN as a whole, a gradual Myanmar return — if effective dialogue can resume under the Five-Point Consensus — would bolster regional stability. A single visit cannot unravel Myanmar's domestic stalemate, yet its symbolic weight should not be underestimated: it signals that ASEAN's engagement path is moving from frozen to pragmatic.

## Further reading

**Five-Point Consensus**: The five-point agreement ASEAN reached in April 2021 on the Myanmar crisis, covering an end to violence, inclusive dialogue, and humanitarian access; it remains the bloc's core framework for Myanmar policy.

**ASEAN Centrality**: The principle that ASEAN retains a leading and coordinating role in regional cooperative mechanisms, a cornerstone for members like Vietnam in upholding regional order.

**Quiet Diplomacy**: The practice of conducting diplomatic consultations discreetly and away from the public spotlight, a strategy Vietnam frequently employs on sensitive issues.

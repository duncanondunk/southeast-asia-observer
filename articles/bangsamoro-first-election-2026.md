在棉兰老岛（Mindanao）南部的丘陵与海岸之间，一项迟到了多年的政治实验正走向投票站。菲律宾南部邦萨摩洛（Bangsamoro）自治区将迎来其历史上首次议会选举，十三支政党角逐八十个席位。对这片饱经战火的土地而言，选票取代枪声，本身就是和平最朴素的定义。

## 迟到的选票

邦萨摩洛自治区于二〇一九年正式取代此前的自治区域，由菲律宾政府与摩洛（Moro）伊斯兰解放阵线达成和平协议后设立，起初由过渡机构执政。此次选举是这一安排从"临时"走向"常态"的关节点：八十个议席连同地方首长的产生，将把和平协议的文字变为可问责的政治结构。十三支参选力量中，既有前武装派系的转型政党，也有世俗与地方宗族力量，竞争本身即是多元利益进入体制的信号。

## 从枪到票

这场选举的根，扎在长达数十年的分离主义冲突里。摩洛（Moro）穆斯林群体与马尼拉中央政府的张力，曾催生多支武装与反复的血火。二〇一四年框架协议与随后的立法，开启了以"政治自治"替代"军事解决"的路径。把步枪上缴、把诉求写进选票，是这一转向最可见的刻度——尽管去武装（Decommissioning）与安置的完成度，仍被外界审慎看待。

## 未竟的答卷

和平的红利远未落袋。自治区内贫困率仍高于全国平均，宗族政治与选举暴力阴影未散，与极端组织有关联的武装曾在南部制造袭击，提醒人们和平进程的外缘依然脆弱。前战斗人员的安置、经济造血能力的培育，构成新议会无法回避的硬任务。选票能改变权力归属，却不能直接抹平世代的积贫。

## 东盟的镜子

邦萨摩洛的尝试，为东盟内部其他族群—宗教张力提供了可对照的样本。从泰国南隅到缅甸边缘，分离主义与自治诉求并不鲜见。以制度容纳差异、以发展稀释仇恨，比以武力压制更能换来长久安稳。菲律宾这步棋的成色，将被区域内外静默地记取。

## 一场未完成的成年礼

邦萨摩洛的首投，是和平进程的一次成年礼，却非终点。真正考验在于：当欢呼退去，新议会能否把自治的承诺转化为街巷里的灯与学堂。选票打开了门，门后的路仍要一步步走。

## 延伸阅读

**邦萨摩洛（Bangsamoro）**：菲律宾南部以穆斯林人口为主的自治区，二〇一九年正式设立，意在以政治自治终结长期分离主义冲突。

**摩洛（Moro）**：菲律宾南部信仰伊斯兰的本土族群统称，历史上与中央政权存在长期的自治与认同张力。

**去武装化（Decommissioning）**：和平协议中前武装人员解除武装、脱离战斗序列并转入平民生活的安置过程，是冲突后治理的关键环节。

In the hills and coasts of southern Mindanao, a political experiment deferred for decades is finally walking to the ballot box. The Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM) is holding its first-ever parliamentary election, with 13 parties contesting 80 seats. For a land long scarred by war, ballots replacing gunfire is itself the plainest definition of peace.

## The Long-Overdue Ballot

BARMM was formally established in 2019, replacing the earlier autonomous region after the Philippine government and the Moro Islamic Liberation Front (MILF) sealed a peace accord; it was initially run by an interim body. This election is the hinge that moves the arrangement from "interim" to "institutional": the 80 parliamentary seats plus regional leadership will turn the words of the peace deal into an accountable political structure. Among the 13 contenders are transitional parties born from former armed factions alongside secular and clan-based forces. The contest itself signals that diverse interests are entering the system rather than the battlefield.

## From Guns to Ballots

The roots of this vote lie in decades of separatist conflict. Tension between the Moro Muslim population and the Manila central government spawned multiple armed groups and repeated bloodshed. The 2014 framework agreement and subsequent legislation opened a path of "political autonomy" as an alternative to a "military solution." Surrendering rifles and inscribing demands on ballots is the most visible gauge of that turn—though the completeness of demobilisation and reintegration remains, rightly, under outside scrutiny.

## Unfinished Business

The dividend of peace is far from banked. Poverty rates in the region still exceed the national average; the shadows of clan politics and electoral violence linger; and IS-linked armed groups have struck in the south, a reminder that the fringe of the peace process stays fragile. The resettlement of former combatants and the cultivation of an economic engine are hard tasks the new parliament cannot dodge. Ballots can shift who holds power, but they do not by themselves erase generations of deprivation.

## A Mirror for ASEAN

Bangsamoro's attempt offers a comparable template for other ethnic-religious tensions inside ASEAN. From southern Thailand to the edges of Myanmar, separatism and autonomy claims are no rarity. Accommodating difference through institutions and diluting hatred through development buys more durable stability than repression by force. How the Philippines plays this move will be quietly noted within and beyond the region.

## An Unfinished Coming-of-Age

Bangsamoro's first vote is a coming-of-age for the peace process—but not its finish line. The real test comes after the cheers fade: can the new parliament convert the promise of autonomy into lights on the street and schools in the neighbourhood? The ballot opens the door; the road beyond still has to be walked, step by step.

## Further reading

**Bangsamoro**: The Philippines' predominantly Muslim autonomous region in the south, formally established in 2019 to end a long separatist conflict through political autonomy.

**Moro**: A collective term for the indigenous Muslim populations of the southern Philippines, with a long history of autonomy and identity tensions with the central state.

**Decommissioning**: The process under a peace accord by which former combatants lay down arms, leave the fighting order, and reintegrate into civilian life—a linchpin of post-conflict governance.

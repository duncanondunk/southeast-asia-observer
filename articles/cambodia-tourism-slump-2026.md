# 国际游客锐减，柬埔寨旅游业路在何方？

柬埔寨旅游部10月8日宣布，将选派一名官员自2027年1月1日起赴驻华使馆担任旅游专员，任期至2029年底，专责推动柬中旅游合作、招徕中国游客。这看似一记不起眼的“小动作”，实则是金边面对国际游客断崖式下滑的无奈自救。今年前8个月，这个东南亚国家接待国际游客逾225万人次，同比下降44.4%。当支柱产业的引擎骤然熄火，柬方的焦虑，完全在情理之中。

## 数字：前8月国际游客骤降44.4%

先摆数字。今年1至8月，柬埔寨共接待国际游客225万余人次，比去年同期少了将近一半。最大客源国仍是中国，58.8万人次，却也同比下降25%；越南以58.57万人次紧随其后，下降27.6%；美国12.98万人次，降幅7.3%，位列第三。表面看，中国、越南、美国“三足”尚在，可总量塌了将近一半，说明症结不在某个单一市场，而在整体需求与区域环境的同步恶化。

## 泰国客源断崖：边境局势的代价

最刺眼的是泰国。去年同期泰国赴柬游客还有96.24万人次，今年骤降至4.78万人次，同比锐减95%。一条边境线，几乎把泰国客源“清零”。这背后是柬泰边境局势持续紧张——政治摩擦外溢为出行警示与民间抵触，游客用脚投票。值得一提的是，本站此前已关注过柬埔寨消费者抵制泰货的民间情绪；如今边境对峙反过来吞噬柬埔寨自身的旅游业，恰恰印证了经济民族主义那把回旋镖，迟早会飞回来。

## 中国仍是最大客源，但“独木难支”

中国游客虽降25%，仍是柬埔寨的第一大客源，且降幅远小于整体。这也解释了为何金边把“旅游专员”派往北京而非别处：在区域客源普遍疲软时，稳住并做大中国这一基本盘，是成本最低的解法。但观察者要追问一句：把希望押在单一市场上，真的安全吗？2019年前后，中国游客曾撑起柬埔寨旅游业的半边天，疫情期间归零的教训还热乎着。过度依赖单一客源，本质上是把国运绑在别人的出行意愿上。

## 驻华“旅游专员”与组合拳

除派驻专员，柬旅游部10月7日已启动综合干预计划：涵盖暹粒旅游业复苏专项方案、一系列配套措施与税收优惠，并联合业界开展“绿色季节游柬埔寨”推广活动，意在提振国内游、推动国际客源多元化。这些举措方向没错——用政策与营销托底，为复苏争取时间。东盟与中日韩宏观经济研究办公室首席经济学家何东也指出，柬埔寨面临多重压力，应继续改善投资与旅游环境、推动服务业复苏。

但措施再多，也难掩一个尴尬：柬埔寨的旅游资产高度集中在吴哥窟等少数地标，产品同质化严重。柬旅游协会主席蔡秀玲坦言，必须开发更丰富的体验项目、提升服务质量，才能满足不同兴趣与背景的游客。说白了，光靠免签与打折，留不住见多识广的游客。

## 结构性短板：脆弱的服务业主导型经济

更深层的问题，是柬埔寨经济对旅游及相关服务业的依赖过重。世界银行、亚洲开发银行、国际货币基金组织近期相继下调其2026年增长预期，并点名旅游业及相关服务业疲软是拖累增长的主因之一。当一个国家的增长高度绑定“外国人来不来”这种外部变量，抗风险能力天然薄弱：边境一紧张、邻国一收紧，国内生产总值就跟着抖三抖。

这也正是单一“拉中国游客”策略治标不治本的原因。旅游复苏需要的是区域整体稳定、签证便利与产品升级的合力，而不是一纸专员任命。

## 路在何方：从数量到质量

柬埔寨旅游业要翻身，先得想清楚“要多少人、要什么人”。继续追求人次的粗放增长，只会重蹈过度依赖与生态承载的老路；转向高质量、高停留、高复购的体验型旅游，才是可持续的解法。柬老越“一程三地”旅游合作已做出样板——把次区域串成线路，用组合拳而非单点对抗波动。

一位名叫法拉的网友在柬旅游部社交账号下留言：要倾听一线导游、司机、酒店、餐厅与旅游运营者的声音，他们最了解真实情况，经验应进入决策过程。这句大白话点破了关键：复苏的答案不在会议室，而在街头巷尾的真实体验里。柬埔寨若真能把“绿色季节”做出口碑，把吴哥之外的故事讲给世界听，这场44.4%的滑坡，未必不是一次被迫的转型契机。

## 延伸阅读

**中柬关系（China–Cambodia Relations）**：两国于1960年建交，近年升级为全面战略合作伙伴，中国长期是柬埔寨最大的投资与援助来源国，旅游合作是双边人文往来的重要纽带。

**暹粒（Siem Reap）**：柬埔寨西北部省份，世界文化遗产吴哥窟所在地，长期贡献全国旅游业约六成收入，其复苏进度常被视为全国旅游业的风向标。

**绿色季节（Green Season）**：指东南亚雨季（约5月至10月）的旅游营销概念，意在以更低价格与更少人流吸引错峰游客，缓解旱季的过度拥挤。

# Cambodia's Tourism Freefall: Where Does It Go From Here?

On October 8, Cambodia's Ministry of Tourism announced it will post an official to its embassy in Beijing as a dedicated tourism commissioner, serving from January 1, 2027, through the end of 2029, tasked with deepening Cambodia–China tourism cooperation and drawing more Chinese visitors. It looks like a minor bureaucratic move — but in truth it is a telling symptom of how sharply the kingdom's international arrivals have collapsed. In the first eight months of this year the Southeast Asian country welcomed just over 2.25 million foreign visitors, down 44.4% year on year. When the engine of a pillar industry suddenly stalls, Phnom Penh's anxiety is entirely understandable.

## The numbers: a 44.4% slide in eight months

Let the figures speak first. From January to August, Cambodia received more than 2.25 million international arrivals — nearly half of what it drew a year earlier. China remains the largest single source market at 588,000, yet even that fell 25%; Vietnam followed at 585,700, down 27.6%; the United States, at 129,800, slipped 7.3% to take third place. On the surface, the "three legs" of China, Vietnam and America still stand — but with the total cut almost in half, the problem is clearly not one weak market. It is a synchronized deterioration of overall demand and the regional environment.

## The Thai collapse: the price of the border standoff

The most striking casualty is Thailand. A year ago, Thai arrivals numbered 962,400; this year they plummeted to just 47,800 — a 95% collapse. A single border line has almost erased the Thai market entirely. Behind this lies the protracted Cambodia–Thailand border tension: political friction has spilled over into travel advisories and popular resentment, and tourists have voted with their feet. It is worth recalling that this site has already covered the Cambodian consumer boycott of Thai goods; now the border standoff is devouring Cambodia's own tourism in return — proof that the boomerang of economic nationalism always comes back around.

## China is still the top source — but one pillar is not enough

Chinese visitors, though down 25%, remain Cambodia's number-one source, and their decline is far milder than the overall drop. That explains why Phnom Penh is sending its commissioner to Beijing rather than anywhere else: when regional demand is broadly soft, stabilizing and enlarging the Chinese base is the cheapest fix available. Yet a fair observer must ask — is betting on a single market truly safe? In the years around 2019, Chinese tourists propped up half of Cambodia's tourism; the memory of that base vanishing to zero during the pandemic is still warm. Over-reliance on one source market is, in the end, staking national fortune on the travel whims of others.

## A Beijing commissioner and a relief package

Beyond the envoy, the Ministry of Tourism on October 7 launched a comprehensive intervention plan: a Siem Reap tourism-recovery programme, a set of supporting measures and tax incentives, and a "Green Season Go Cambodia" campaign co-run with the industry — all aimed at lifting domestic travel and diversifying international source markets. The direction is right: using policy and marketing to buy time for a recovery. Ho Dong, chief economist of the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office, has likewise noted that Cambodia faces multiple pressures and should keep improving its investment and tourism environment and revive the services sector.

Yet no amount of measures can hide an awkward truth: Cambodia's tourism assets are heavily concentrated in a handful of landmarks such as Angkor Wat, and its products are badly homogenized. Cai Xiuling, chair of the Cambodia Tourism Association, concedes that the country must develop more diverse experiences and raise service quality to satisfy visitors of different interests and backgrounds. In plain terms, visa waivers and discounts alone will not retain seasoned travellers.

## Structural fragility: an over-reliant services economy

The deeper problem is how heavily Cambodia's economy leans on tourism and related services. The World Bank, the Asian Development Bank and the IMF have all recently trimmed their 2026 growth forecasts for the country, naming weak tourism and related services as a chief drag. When a nation's growth is bound to an external variable as fickle as "whether foreigners show up," its shock absorption is inherently thin: let the border tense up or a neighbour tighten the tap, and GDP trembles in sympathy.

This is precisely why the single-track "pull more Chinese tourists" strategy treats the symptom, not the disease. A real recovery demands the combined force of regional stability, visa facilitation and product upgrading — not a commissioner's appointment alone.

## Where to go: from volume to value

For Cambodia's tourism to rise again, it must first decide whom it wants and how many. Relentlessly chasing raw arrival counts only repeats the old path of over-dependence and ecological strain; shifting toward high-quality, high-stay, high-repeat experience-based travel is the sustainable answer. The Cambodia–Laos–Vietnam "one journey, three destinations" scheme already offers a template — stringing the sub-region together and fighting volatility with itineraries rather than single sites.

As one netizen, Farah, commented on the ministry's social-media page: listen to the frontline — the guides, drivers, hotels, restaurants and tour operators who know the reality best, and let their experience feed into decision-making. Those plain words capture the crux: the answer to recovery lies not in the boardroom but in the real experience on the street. If Cambodia can truly build a reputation around its "green season" and tell the world stories beyond Angkor, this 44.4% slump may yet prove a forced turning point.

## Further reading

**China–Cambodia Relations**: The two countries established diplomatic ties in 1960 and have since upgraded to a comprehensive strategic partnership. China is consistently Cambodia's largest source of investment and aid, with tourism cooperation a key thread of bilateral people-to-people exchange.

**Siem Reap**: A northwestern province that hosts the Angkor Wat UNESCO site and has long generated roughly 60% of the nation's tourism revenue; its recovery is widely treated as a barometer for the sector nationwide.

**Green Season**: A marketing term for the Southeast Asian rainy season (roughly May to October), used to attract off-peak visitors with lower prices and thinner crowds, easing dry-season overcrowding.

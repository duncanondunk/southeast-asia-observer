# 普拉博沃换将外交官：印尼外交部的一次重置

10月1日，印尼总统普拉博沃（Prabowo Subianto）改组内阁，任命外交部前常驻联合国代表阿尔马纳塔·纳西尔接替苏吉奥诺出任外交部长。这是普拉博沃不到三周内第二次调换内阁要职。表面看是一次寻常的人事更迭，实则折射出这位将军总统正把外交大权更紧地攥在自己手里，同时用"专业外交官"的牌面来安抚市场与邻国。

## 换将：从党工到职业外交官

被换下的苏吉奥诺并非职业外交官。他来自普拉博沃所属的大印尼运动党，行事低调、极少公开露面，印尼官方通讯社安塔拉（Antara）曾称其为普拉博沃的"意识形态之子"、最信任的助手之一。换句话说，过去这一年印尼外交部更像总统府的"内廷"，而非独立的外交中枢。

接任的纳西尔是另一条路数。他生于1971年，是外交部科班出身的职业外交官，曾任发言人、驻法国大使，2021年起出任印尼常驻联合国代表，2024年10月起担任外交部副部长。据路透社报道，普拉博沃9月30日向国会提交换将信函，次日纳西尔即在雅加达国家宫宣誓就职。

这一进一退，耐人寻味。把"自己人"挪到人类发展统筹部长，把外交部交还给职业外交官——这究竟是权力的收，还是形象的放？

## 被边缘化的外交部

分析人士普遍认为，苏吉奥诺主政期间，外交部已被边缘化。普拉博沃本人在外交上扮演了更主导的角色。印尼长期奉行"自由而积极"的不结盟传统，但过去一年，重大外交动作往往由总统府直接拍板。

最典型的一例，是2024年印尼与中国签署的一份联合声明，承诺在"两国重叠水域"开发项目。这份声明被普遍解读为变相承认了中国在南海广泛的领土主张，在国内引发哗然。印尼观察家德维·福尔图娜·安瓦尔就此点明：外交部此后的每笔外交交易，都需要更审慎地审视。

苏吉奥诺任内并非没有建树。印尼加入金砖国家机制、加入美国主导的加沙"和平委员会"倡议、并承诺派兵参与拟议中的国际维和部队，都是他的手笔。但问题在于，这些动作更像是总统意志的延伸，而非外交部的系统谋划。

## 三周内的第二次"内部接棒"

这次换将并非孤例。9月14日，普拉博沃解除财政部长普尔巴亚的职务，由副手接任。投资者对此表示欢迎，认为在数月政策不确定之后，这一举动有助于恢复对这个规模达1.5万亿美元（约10.8万亿元人民币）的二十国集团经济体的信心。

一个清晰的图案浮现：普拉博沃偏好"内部接棒"——撤掉一把手，提拔其副手。财长是如此，外长也是如此。这既保证了权力不外流，又用"政策延续性"的话术稳住外界。说白了，这是一种把关键岗位"自己人化"同时又"专业化包装"的统治术。

## 新外长的牌面

纳西尔的专业成色不低。他早年在多边合作司经手经济与发展议题，在联合国大会第二委员会代表"七十七国集团加中国"谈判多项决议；2023年出任联合国经社理事会副主席。观察家安瓦尔评价他"沉稳"，并直言他可以成为普拉博沃的"制衡者"。

纳西尔宣誓后表示，优先事项包括维持地区稳定、推动多边主义，并重申印尼仍在观望加沙"和平委员会"与国际维和部队的进展。对东盟而言，这或许是个好消息：一位懂多边规则的外交官，更可能把印尼"拉回地图"——尤其是在缅甸问题上，印尼作为东盟轮值主席国曾力推"五点共识"，但收效甚微。

## 区域含义：东盟、南海与大国平衡

换将之后，印尼外交会转向吗？短期看，大概率不会。普拉博沃的外交基调味浓：既不愿选边站队，又想在南海保持"自由而积极"的弹性。但职业外交官回归，意味着执行层面会更专业、措辞会更谨慎。

对中国而言，纳西尔是熟面孔——他任内处理过印尼与中国在南海的若干摩擦，也参与过多边协调。换将不等于转向，但意味着北京未来在雅加达谈的事，要多过一道外交部的专业筛子。对东盟而言，一个更有能力的印尼外交部，既可能强化区域议程，也可能放大雅加达与邻国在南海油气开发上的分歧。

说到底，这场换将的本质，是普拉博沃用"专业"为"集权"镶了一道边。外交部看起来被扶正了，但方向盘，仍在总统手里。

## 延伸阅读

**职业外交官（Career Diplomat）**：指从外交系统基层做起、经驻外与多边岗位历练的专业官员，与"政治任命"的外长相对；南亚与东南亚多国近年出现"军人总统加职业外交官"的搭配。

**自由而积极（Free and Active）**：印尼自1948年确立的不结盟外交原则，强调不参与军事同盟、自主决策；是理解印尼对华、对美、对东盟立场的钥匙。

**金砖国家机制（BRICS）**：由巴西、俄罗斯、印度、中国、南非等新兴经济体组成的外交与经济协调机制；印尼于苏吉奥诺任内正式加入。

# Prabowo Taps a Career Diplomat: A Reset for Indonesia's Foreign Ministry

On October 1, President Prabowo Subianto reshuffled his cabinet, naming Arrmanatha Nasir — Indonesia's former permanent representative to the United Nations — as foreign minister, replacing Sugiono. It was Prabowo's second change to a top cabinet post in under three weeks. On the surface a routine rotation; in substance, a signal that the general-turned-president is tightening his grip on foreign policy while using a "career diplomat" facade to reassure markets and neighbours.

## The change: from party man to career diplomat

The man out, Sugiono, was not a diplomat. He came from Prabowo's Gerindra party, kept a low profile, and rarely appeared in public. State news agency Antara once called him Prabowo's "ideological child" and one of his most trusted aides. In other words, for the past year Indonesia's foreign ministry looked more like an inner court of the presidency than an independent diplomatic engine.

The man in, Nasir, is a different breed. Born in 1971, he is a career foreign-service officer — spokesman, ambassador to France, and from 2021 Indonesia's permanent representative to the UN, before becoming deputy foreign minister in October 2024. Reuters reported that Prabowo submitted the change to parliament on September 30; Nasir was sworn in at the State Palace in Jakarta the next day.

One out, one in — and the choreography is telling. Moving the loyalist to coordinating minister for human development while handing the ministry back to a professional: is this a concentration of power, or a makeover of its image?

## A sidelined ministry

Analysts broadly agree that under Sugiono the foreign ministry was sidelined, with Prabowo playing the dominant hand in diplomacy. Indonesia's long-standing "free and active" non-alignment tradition did not prevent major calls from being made directly from the palace.

The clearest case was a 2024 joint statement with China pledging projects "in the overlapping waters claimed by the two countries" — widely read as a tacit nod to Beijing's sweeping South China Sea claims and a domestic firestorm. Indonesianist Dewi Fortuna Anwar pointed precisely to this: every diplomatic deal since then would need far closer scrutiny.

Sugiono was not without wins. Indonesia's entry into BRICS, its joining of the US-led Gaza "Board of Peace" initiative, and its pledge of troops to a proposed international stabilisation force were all his. But the pattern is the point: these read as extensions of presidential will rather than the ministry's own design.

## The second 'in-house succession' in three weeks

This was no isolated move. On September 14 Prabowo sacked finance minister Purbaya Yudhi Sadewa and installed his deputy — a change investors welcomed as restoring confidence in the $1.5 trillion G20 economy after months of uncertain policymaking.

A clear pattern emerges: Prabowo prefers the "in-house succession" — remove the chief, promote the deputy. Finance, then foreign affairs. It keeps power from leaking out while selling "continuity" to the outside world. In plain terms, it is a ruling technique that makes key posts both "his own" and "professionally packaged."

## What the new minister brings

Nasir's credentials are solid. He cut his teeth in the directorate-general for multilateral cooperation, handled economic and development issues in the UN General Assembly's Second Committee, and negotiated for the Group of 77 and China; in 2023 he became vice-president of ECOSOC. Anwar calls him "even-keeled" and flatly says he can be "a foil to Prabowo."

After the swearing-in, Nasir said his priorities include regional stability and multilateralism, and that Indonesia is still monitoring the Gaza Board of Peace and the stabilisation force. For ASEAN this may be good news: a diplomat fluent in multilateral rules is more likely to put Indonesia "back on the map" — not least on Myanmar, where, as a former ASEAN chair, Jakarta pushed the "Five-Point Consensus" to little effect.

## Regional implications: ASEAN, the South China Sea, and great-power balancing

Will policy shift after the change? In the short run, probably not. Prabowo's geopolitical instinct runs deep: no bandwagoning, but room to keep "free and active" flexibility in the South China Sea. A career diplomat's return means more professional execution and more careful wording.

For China, Nasir is a familiar face — he handled several frictions with Beijing in the South China Sea and took part in multilateral coordination. A change of face is not a change of course, but it means future talks in Jakarta will pass through the ministry's professional sieve. For ASEAN, a more capable Indonesian foreign ministry could strengthen the regional agenda — or amplify Jakarta's disputes with neighbours over offshore gas development.

In the end, this reshuffle is Prabowo edging "professionalism" onto "centralisation." The ministry looks restored; the steering wheel, however, stays with the president.

## Further reading

**Career Diplomat**: A professional officer who rises through the foreign service from the ground up via postings and multilateral roles, as opposed to a politically appointed minister — a pairing of "soldier-president plus career diplomat" seen across several Asian states.

**Free and Active**: Indonesia's non-alignment principle, adopted in 1948, stressing no military alliances and autonomous decision-making; the key to reading its stance toward China, the United States, and ASEAN.

**BRICS**: A diplomatic and economic coordination forum of major emerging economies — Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa and others — which Indonesia formally joined under Sugiono.

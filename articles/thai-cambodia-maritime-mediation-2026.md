# 泰柬启动海上争端国际调解：陆地硝烟未散，海界谈判开场

## 从陆地边境到海上划界的延伸

泰柬关系在2026年夏被两道伤口同时撕开：一道在陆地，围绕边境争议区的对峙与偶发冲突；一道在海上，围绕重叠声索海域的划界僵局。9月中旬，在陆地摩擦仍时有反复的当口，两国宣布启动海上争端的国际调解，试图把最棘手的海域议题拉回谈判桌。这一动作耐人寻味——当陆地上的民族情绪尚未降温，先把更复杂的海洋议题摆上桌面，既是危机管控的尝试，也暗含"以海牵陆"的策略算计：用一条线的进展，为另一条线的紧张降压。

## 争议海域在哪里

泰柬海上争端的主轴，是泰国湾重叠的大陆架与专属经济区（EEZ）声索。两国海岸线相邻，却在油气资源富集的海域存在大面积的划界空白，数十年未能就"从哪里画线"达成共识。矛盾焦点不止于主权宣示，更在于潜在的碳氢化合物收益——谁控制海域，谁就握住未来能源红利的门票。过去虽有过联合开发的非正式讨论，却始终缺乏具约束力的框架。此次调解，正是要把"各说各话"升级为"第三方见证下的规则博弈"。

## 为何此时启动国际调解

时机的选择绝非偶然。陆地边境冲突已让两国关系降至近年低点，若海上再起摩擦，双边将无暇他顾、风险叠加。启动国际调解，首先是把"失控概率"压下去：引入中立第三方，等于给争端套上程序性缰绳，让任何单边行动都面临更高的外交成本。其次，对泰国而言，新政府需要在民族主义压力与务实外交之间找平衡，主动提议调解可塑造成"负责任一方"；对柬埔寨而言，借多边机制制衡邻国、避免单边受压，同样符合其利益。双方各取所需的"体面退路"，构成了此刻谈判的底层动力。

## 泰柬各自的算盘

泰国的诉求，是在不放弃主权立场的前提下，争取一份能稳定油气开发预期的法律安排；其底气来自更成熟的海事执法体系与区域盟友网络。柬埔寨则更强调历史与条约依据，倾向把争端框定为"殖民时期边界遗产"问题，并借国际机制稀释泰国的相对优势。两国都明白，全面划界短期无望，因此更现实的期望是"冻结争议、共商开发"——先就一小块海域达成联合勘探谅解，再以点带面。算盘不同，但都不想真打。

## 国际调解机制的局限

第三方调解并非万能钥匙。东南亚的海洋争端历来难解，部分因声索方对"国际法优先"与"双边优先"的排序不一；部分因域内大国与各自主权叙事深度绑定。调解能提供的，是程序、声势与临时降温，却难以替代各方在核心利益上的妥协。更何况，泰柬此前的边境冲突已损害互信，海上谈判很难脱离陆地氛围独立运行。若陆地再起波澜，海上的善意很可能被瞬间对冲。机制到位，政治意愿才是变量。

## 东盟"东盟方式"的再检验

这场调解也是对"东盟方式"（ASEAN Way）的一次再检验。东盟长期偏好不干涉、协商一致、双边优先，反对外部势力主导；但面对成员国内反复燃起的冲突，这一温和路线常显迟滞。泰柬若能在东盟框架或东盟背书下完成调解，将是对"东盟中心性"的正面佐证；若仍须借域外机制才能完成，则暴露出区域组织调处硬争端的力有不逮。无论哪种结果，都在重塑外界对东盟危机管理能力的观感。

## 对地区海洋秩序的意味

泰柬海界的走向，牵动的不只是两国。泰国湾是南海西南缘的敏感扇区，周边越南、马来西亚亦各有重叠声索；任何一份新的划界或共开发模式，都可能被邻近国家援引为 precedent。更复杂的是，域外大国在南海的军事与外交存在持续加码，使"小型双边争端"动辄被放进"大国竞逐"的显微镜。泰柬能否把争端"去安全化"、当作普通海洋治理问题处理，将影响整片海域的去风险化节奏。

## 前路：低烈度僵持或是常态

短期内，最可能出现的并非划界突破，而是"低烈度僵持+程序性推进"：成立联合技术委员会、交换海图数据、就个别区块试谈共开发。这种"慢火炖"模式虽不酣畅，却是避免冲突升级的现实选择。真正的风险在于国内民族主义被煽动——一旦任一方的媒体或反对派把海洋议题武器化，谈判空间会被迅速压缩。对两国决策者而言，最大的考验不是谈成什么，而是能否在国内压力下守住"谈下去"的耐心。海界之难，终究是政治的延伸。

## 延伸阅读

**专属经济区（Exclusive Economic Zone, EEZ）**：沿海国在领海以外、从基线量起不超过200海里的海域，享有自然资源勘探与开发的主权权利；重叠 EEZ 声索是东南亚海洋争端的主要来源。

**东盟方式（ASEAN Way）**：东盟处理内部关系的原则，强调不干涉内政、协商一致与双边优先，常被批评在调处硬性安全争端时缺乏强制力。

# Thailand and Cambodia Launch International Mediation on Maritime Dispute: Land Tension Uncooled, Sea Talks Open

## From land border to maritime delimitation

Thai–Cambodian relations were torn by two wounds in summer 2026: one on land, over standoffs and sporadic clashes around disputed border zones; one at sea, over the deadlock of overlapping maritime claims. In mid-September, while land friction still flared intermittently, the two states announced international mediation on the maritime dispute, pulling the thorniest sea issue back to the table. The timing is telling — with nationalist heat on land not yet cooled, putting the more complex maritime agenda on the desktop is both a crisis-management attempt and a tacit "use the sea to leash the land" calculus: progress on one line to lower pressure on the other.

## Where the disputed waters lie

The Thai–Cambodian maritime dispute runs along overlapping continental-shelf and Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) claims in the Gulf of Thailand. With adjacent coastlines but a vast delimitation gap in hydrocarbon-rich waters, the two have failed for decades to agree "where to draw the line." The friction is not only about sovereign proclamation but about potential hydrocarbon gains — who controls the sea holds the ticket to future energy dividends. Past informal talks on joint development never yielded a binding framework. This mediation aims to upgrade "talking past each other" into "rule-bound contestation under third-party witness."

## Why launch mediation now

The timing is no accident. Land border clashes have dragged bilateral ties to a recent low; a maritime flare-up would leave neither side room and stack risks. Launching international mediation first presses down the "probability of losing control": a neutral third party puts procedural reins on the dispute, raising the diplomatic cost of any unilateral move. For Thailand, the new government must balance nationalist pressure with pragmatic diplomacy; proposing mediation proactively casts it as "the responsible party." For Cambodia, using multilateral mechanisms to check the neighbour and avoid unilateral pressure also serves its interest. Each side's "face-saving exit" constitutes the underlying motive now.

## The respective calculations of Bangkok and Phnom Penh

Thailand's ask is a legal arrangement stabilising oil-and-gas expectations without surrendering its sovereignty stance; its confidence rests on a more mature maritime enforcement system and regional ally network. Cambodia leans harder on historical and treaty grounds, framing the dispute as "colonial-era border heritage" and using international mechanisms to dilute Thailand's relative advantage. Both know a full delimitation is impossible short-term, so the realistic hope is "freeze the dispute, co-manage development" — first reach a joint-exploration understanding on a small block, then expand from point to surface. Different abacuses, but neither wants a real fight.

## The limits of international mediation

Third-party mediation is no master key. Southeast Asian maritime disputes are chronically hard because claimants rank "international law first" versus "bilateral first" differently, and because regional powers are deeply bound to each side's sovereignty narrative. What mediation offers is procedure, momentum and temporary cooling — not a substitute for compromise on core interests. Worse, the prior border clashes have eroded mutual trust, so sea talks can hardly run independent of the land atmosphere. If the land flares again, maritime goodwill may be offset instantly. Mechanism in place, political will remains the variable.

## A re-test of the "ASEAN Way"

This mediation re-tests the "ASEAN Way." ASEAN long prefers non-interference, consensus and bilateral priority, opposing external domination; yet facing recurrent intra-member conflicts, this gentle route often looks sluggish. If Thailand and Cambodia complete mediation within or under ASEAN auspices, it positively corroborates "ASEAN centrality"; if they still need extra-regional mechanisms, it exposes the organisation's limited prowess in hard-security disputes. Either outcome reshapes outside perception of ASEAN's crisis-management capacity.

## What it means for the regional maritime order

The trajectory of the Thai–Cambodian sea line concerns more than the two states. The Gulf of Thailand is a sensitive sector on the southwest rim of the South China Sea, where Vietnam and Malaysia also hold overlapping claims; any new delimitation or joint-development model may be cited as precedent by neighbours. Compounding this, extra-regional military and diplomatic presence in the South China Sea keeps intensifying, placing "small bilateral disputes" under the microscope of great-power competition. Whether the two can "de-securitise" the dispute into an ordinary ocean-governance matter will shape the de-risking rhythm of the entire sea.

## The road ahead: low-intensity stalemate as the norm

In the near term, the most likely outcome is not a delimitation breakthrough but "low-intensity stalemate plus procedural advance": a joint technical committee, exchange of nautical charts, trial talks on joint development of specific blocks. This "slow simmer" mode is unsatisfying yet the realistic choice to avoid escalation. The real risk is domestic nationalism being stoked — once either side's media or opposition weaponises the maritime issue,谈判 space shrinks fast. For both decision-makers the hardest test is not what is agreed but whether they can preserve the patience to "keep talking" under domestic pressure. The difficulty of the sea line is, in the end, an extension of politics.

## Further reading

**Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ)**: a sea zone beyond the territorial sea, up to 200 nautical miles from the baseline, where the coastal state holds sovereign rights over natural-resource exploration and development; overlapping EEZ claims are the main source of Southeast Asian maritime disputes.

**ASEAN Way**: the principles guiding ASEAN's internal relations — emphasis on non-interference, consensus and bilateral priority — often criticised for lacking enforcement when mediating hard security disputes.

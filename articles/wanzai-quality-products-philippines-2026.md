# 从江西万载到马尼拉货架：侨，如何成为县域出海的通道

近日，一场名为「侨见万载 优品出海」的活动跟随「江西第二届经贸合作推介会」走进菲律宾马尼拉。组织方是万载县委统战部、县工商联、县侨办侨联，现场由万载籍乡友、菲律宾江西总商会副会长兼秘书长涂乔兴与南昌市万载商会会长黄宇带队，集中推介万载的历史文旅资源以及花炮、龙牙百合粉、罗城扎粉等三十余种特色产品。报道里有个细节：一位菲律宾来宾在百合粉展台前好奇发问，涂乔兴递上一杯冲泡好的百合粉，笑着说「希望通过这一味百合粉，认识我的家乡万载」。一杯地方特产，成了最自然的开场白。

这则看似寻常的县区招商花絮，其实折射出中国地方治理中一个正在成形的清晰逻辑：县域经济要出海，最先被激活的往往不是外贸公司，而是海外乡亲网络。

## 机制：以侨为桥，把乡愁变成渠道

万载的打法并不新鲜，却极其典型。县委统战部、侨办侨联这类原本负责「凝聚侨心侨力」的部门，如今被赋予了拉动出口的硬任务。「以侨为桥，以情聚力」——这句话的潜台词是：海外华侨华人不只是情感上的牵挂，更是现成的、带信任背书的海外分销节点。

涂乔兴们扮演的正是这种双重角色：既是同乡会里的乡贤，又是家乡产品的「代言人」。他们向来宾讲万载的开口傩、古城风貌，推介花炮与百合产业，把「家乡记忆」摆上展台。对海外侨胞，这是一解乡愁的慰藉；对菲律宾客商，这是一扇认识中国县城的窗口。乡情与商机，在同一张展台前交汇。

## 背景：县域出口焦虑下的「侨力量」

为什么是现在？中国县域经济普遍面临内需不足与产能外寻的压力，而东盟被视为最具确定性的增量市场之一。菲律宾人口过亿、消费活跃，但本地渠道门槛高、信任成本大。对万载这样的内陆农业县而言，自己组队闯市场既不经济也缺人脉；而菲律宾江西总商会这类侨团，恰好提供了低成本的「信任中介」——同乡关系降低了信息不对称，也替县城分担了部分市场进入风险。

这也是一篇更大叙事的碎片。全国范围内，各县市正密集推出「优品出海」「县域品牌出海」等行动，意图把工业与农业产能向外疏导。万载版本的特殊之处在于，它不依赖国有外贸公司，而是把市场进入外包给本来就双语、熟稔监管、且被双方信任的侨商。统战部门做情感动员，商会做渠道落地，特色农产品借由乡谊完成第一次出海。报道中「万品出海」「画好最大同心圆」「注入侨力量」等表述，正是这套逻辑的官方话术。

## 局限：温情叙事之外的真问题

话术归话术，效果要打问号。这类县级经贸推介，体量小、频次低，更多是象征性的亮相而非可持续的贸易通道。一杯百合粉能换来一句「真不错」，却未必能换来稳定的海外订单。真正决定「优品」能否留下来的，是冷链、标准认证、售后与本地零售网络——这些恰是县域单打独斗最薄弱的环节。

两道硬约束尤其醒目。花炮作为万载的招牌产品，在海外面临严格的进口管制与安全认证；百合粉等食品则需逐国打通卫生与标签标准。一场县里的展销可以秀出产品，却无法独自爬完监管的阶梯。更值得警惕的，是温情叙事可能遮蔽的结构性张力：此前本栏目曾关注东南亚华人小商贩被中国工厂直销挤压的现象——当「优品出海」以价格与规模优势涌入，本地中小零售商未必是受益者。侨团搭桥固然高效，但若只服务于县城的出口指标，而忽视东道国本地工商业的承受力，所谓「双向奔赴」就可能流于单方面叙事。

## 结论：值得盯紧的县域实验

万载的尝试规模不大，却是一个有代表性的样本：它显示中国侨务工作正从「联谊慰问」转向「经贸赋能」，把离散族群网络变成实实在在的出口基础设施。这套打法成本低、可信度高，若被更多县市复制，或会重塑中国对东盟的小额贸易地貌。

但通道是否可持续、红利是否外溢到东道国，仍要看后续。一杯百合粉的故事很好听，真正的考验，是它能不能变成马尼拉货架上的长期生意。

## 延伸阅读

**侨乡（Qiaoxiang）**：指历史上大批居民出国谋生、在海外形成稳定华侨华人社区的沿海侨乡（如广东、福建、江西部分县市），是当代中国动员海外乡亲网络的经济与情感基础。

**侨务（Diaspora Affairs）**：国家面向海外华侨华人的工作体系，传统以联谊、权益保护为主，近年与经贸、引资、出海挂钩。

**江西总商会（Jiangxi Chambers Abroad）**：以地缘为纽带的海外赣籍商人组织，在东盟多国设点，既是同乡互助平台，也日益成为县域产品出海的落地渠道。

# From a Jiangxi County to Manila's Shelves: How the Diaspora Became an Export Channel

Recently, an event branded "Qiao Jian Wanzai, Quality Products Going Global" travelled to Manila as part of the Second Jiangxi Economic and Trade Cooperation Promotion Conference in the Philippines. The organisers were the Wanzai County United Front Work Department, the Federation of Industry and Commerce, and the Overseas Chinese Affairs Office and Federation. On the ground, the mission was led by Tu Qiaoxing, a Wanzai native who serves as vice-president and secretary-general of the Philippine Jiangxi General Chamber of Commerce, and Huang Yu, president of the Nanchang Wanzai Chamber of Commerce. They showcased more than thirty local specialities — fireworks, Longya lily powder, Luocheng rice noodles, aged Wanzai wine, Laoshan rice — alongside the county's cultural and tourism offerings.

One detail stands out. A Filipino visitor stopped at the lily-powder booth and asked, curiously, what it was. Tu handed over a cup of the prepared drink and smiled: "I hope that through this one taste of lily powder, you come to know my hometown, Wanzai." A humble local product became the most natural ice-breaker imaginable.

Seen at face value, this is a routine county-level trade fair. Seen closely, it reveals a logic now taking shape in Chinese local governance: when a county economy wants to go global, the first network it activates is often not a foreign-trade corporation but its overseas kin.

## The mechanism: the diaspora as a bridge, nostalgia as a channel

Wanzai's playbook is unoriginal yet archetypal. Departments once tasked mainly with "uniting the hearts and strength of overseas Chinese" — the united-front and overseas-Chinese affairs offices — are now handed a hard mandate to pull exports. The slogan "use the overseas Chinese as a bridge, unite through sentiment" carries a subtext: the diaspora is not merely an emotional attachment but a ready-made, trust-endorsed overseas distribution node.

Figures like Tu play exactly this dual role: a respected elder in the hometown association, and simultaneously the "spokesperson" for local products. They talk up Wanzai's ritual masks, its old-town scenery, its fireworks and lily industries, placing "hometown memory" on the booth. For overseas Chinese, it is solace for homesickness; for Filipino buyers, a window onto a Chinese county they had never heard of. Sentiment and commerce meet at the same small table.

## The backdrop: "overseas-Chinese strength" amid county export anxiety

Why now? China's county economies broadly face weak domestic demand and a pressing need to find outlets for capacity, while ASEAN is treated as one of the most predictable growth markets. The Philippines, with its population above one hundred million and lively consumption, is attractive — yet local distribution is gated by high barriers and steep trust costs. For an inland agricultural county like Wanzai, building a market-entry team from scratch is neither economical nor well connected. A diaspora chamber such as the Philippine Jiangxi General Chamber happens to supply a low-cost "trust intermediary": shared hometown ties cut information asymmetry and partly absorb the risk of entering a foreign market.

The Wanzai mission is also a fragment of a larger national script. Across China, county governments are rolling out "quality products going global" and "county brands going overseas" campaigns to offload industrial and agricultural overcapacity onto foreign markets. What makes Wanzai's version distinctive is that it does not rely on state trading companies; it outsources market entry to diaspora merchants who already speak the language, know the regulators, and carry the trust of both sides. The united-front apparatus supplies emotional mobilisation, the chamber supplies channel execution, and specialty farm products complete their first voyage abroad through kinship. The official phrases in the report — "Wanzai products going global," "draw the largest concentric circle," "inject overseas-Chinese strength" — are precisely the official vocabulary of this logic.

## The limits: the real questions behind the warm narrative

Rhetoric aside, the effect deserves a question mark. County-level trade missions of this kind are small in scale and infrequent; they read more as symbolic appearances than as durable trade corridors. A cup of lily powder may earn a polite "not bad," but it will not by itself secure a stable overseas order. What actually decides whether a "quality product" stays is cold-chain logistics, standards certification, after-sales service and local retail networks — exactly the links where a county acting alone is weakest.

Two hard constraints loom. Fireworks — Wanzai's signature product — face tight import controls and safety certification abroad, while food items like lily powder must clear sanitary and labelling standards that differ country by country. A county trade fair can showcase the product; it cannot, by itself, climb the regulatory ladder. More worth flagging is the structural tension the warm narrative may obscure. This column has earlier examined how ethnic-Chinese small traders in Southeast Asia are squeezed by direct factory sales from China: when "quality products going global" arrive on price and scale, local small retailers are not necessarily the beneficiaries. A diaspora chamber builds a bridge efficiently, but if it serves only a county's export targets while ignoring the absorptive capacity of the host country's own businesses, the proclaimed two-way embrace risks becoming a one-sided story.

## The verdict: a county experiment worth watching

Wanzai's attempt is small, yet it is a representative specimen. It shows China's diaspora work shifting from "fellowship and condolences" toward "economic empowerment," turning diaspora networks into tangible export infrastructure. The approach is cheap and credibly trusted; if replicated by more counties, it could reshape the micro-trade landscape between China and ASEAN.

But whether the channel is sustainable, and whether the dividend spills over to the host country, remains to be seen. The story of one cup of lily powder is charming. The real test is whether it can become a lasting business on Manila's shelves.

## Further reading

**Qiaoxiang (侨乡)**：Coastal counties and cities with a history of mass emigration that produced stable overseas Chinese communities abroad — the economic and emotional bedrock on which contemporary China mobilises its diaspora networks.

**Diaspora Affairs (侨务)**：The state system serving overseas Chinese, traditionally centred on fellowship and rights protection, and in recent years increasingly tied to trade, investment attraction and export facilitation.

**Overseas Jiangxi Chambers (江西总商会)**：Geographically based associations of Jiangxi-born merchants abroad, with branches across ASEAN; both mutual-aid platforms and, increasingly, landing channels for county products going global.

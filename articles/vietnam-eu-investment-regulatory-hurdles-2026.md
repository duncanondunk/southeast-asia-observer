# 越南招揽欧洲投资：关系升级之后，规则才是硬通货

## 关系升级之后，项目在哪里

据《日经亚洲》11月19日报道，越南在2026年1月把与欧盟的关系提升为全面战略伙伴关系，随后密集加码招揽欧洲投资。这是一条听起来很熟悉的叙事：关系升级、企业论坛、高级别代表团、双方欢迎词。点评一句，外交等级提高不等于项目自动落地。《欧盟—越南自由贸易协定》生效已逾6年，关税减让的红利早就摆在桌面上；真正的问题从来不是要不要降税，而是降税之后企业能不能算清一笔账。河内眼下遇到的，正是从外交文本到投资清单的转换题。

## 数字很漂亮，意愿不是资本

欧盟—东盟商业理事会副主席诺埃尔·克莱恩（Noel Clehane）给出的数字相当亮眼：理事会最新调查中，64%的成员计划在未来五年扩大在越南的业务；尚未进入越南的企业里，34%正考虑入场。欧洲商会越南分会的商业信心指数为78.3分，90%的受访企业愿意把越南推荐为投资目的地，23%的企业投资视野超过20年。点评：这些是意向投票，不是资本开支。从董事会的扩产动议到工厂真正开工，中间还隔着土地、审批、电价和人才；把情绪指标当成投资预测，是招商报道里最常见的偷懒。

## 110位高管来到河内

11月4日至6日，近40家欧洲企业的约110名高管组成的代表团访问河内，与越南副总理以及财政、工贸、科技、卫生等部门负责人对话，这是欧盟—东盟商业理事会第六次组织此类访问。11月17日，欧盟委员会又在布鲁塞尔举行与东南亚的贸易对话。该理事会执行主任克里斯·汉弗莱（Chris Humphrey）说得很直白：谈成自贸协定只是开始。点评：一年前大家问的是有没有协定，现在问的是协定能不能用。这个变化本身是进展，也意味着剩下的都是硬骨头。

## 卡点在税制、数据与许可

克莱恩点名的瓶颈不是关税，而是税收政策、跨境数据传输、个人数据保护，以及企业需要更清晰的许可规则。他呼吁越南在制定政策与法律时更多咨询外国企业。点评：这类诉求听上去像技术细节，实际上是投资回报率的分母。规则不可预测，企业就会提高折现率，把风险折进报价。对数字服务、医疗数据和金融服务而言，数据规则的重要性已经超过传统关税；谁把规则写清楚，谁就先拿到下一轮投资。

## 八个挑战与二十七个门槛

欧洲商会的调查显示，企业在越南经营平均遇到8项挑战；受监管变动影响较大的企业，面临多达27项行政壁垒。商会给出的优先事项也很具体：行政程序数字化、培养可再生能源与高科技产业所需技能人才、改善绿色电力获取。点评：27这个数字比78.3更值得盯住。信心是温度，壁垒是摩擦力，温度高不等于机器转得动。一个市场可以同时被企业看好、又让人疲于应付，越南正处在这种并不罕见的状态里。

## 绿色电力与原产地规则

欧洲企业列出的机会清单包括医疗、生物科技、可持续制造、航空、金融服务与教育；可再生能源被反复提及，因为欧洲在风电、太阳能、储能和电网方面有技术积累。与此同时，欧洲消费者对可持续与可追溯的要求，正沿着供应链传导到越南本地厂商，环境、社会与治理（ESG）标准不再是可选项。欧盟全球门户倡议也被视为基础设施资金的潜在来源。点评：欧洲资本带来的不是单纯的资金，而是一整套标准。标准能提高附加值，也会把配套能力不足的企业挡在门外。

## 华商的位置：桥梁不是豁免

越南华商长期在制造、贸易和物流中扮演连接角色，语言能力、家族网络与跨境经验，既可以帮助欧洲企业找到本地配套，也可以把欧洲的合规要求翻译给中小供应商。点评：桥梁不等于豁免。侨资同样要面对税制、数据、环保许可和劳工规则；把华商当成制度替代物，既高估了侨网的功能，也低估了越南本土治理的演化速度。真正有价值的，是把关系网络转化为合规能力，而不是绕开规则。

## 中欧之间，不是二选一

越南既是中国—东盟产业链的重要节点，也是欧洲资本寻找供应链多元化的目的地。中国—东盟自贸协定3.0已经签署，货物贸易与数字合作继续升级。点评：欧洲资本进场不等于中国资本退场。原产地规则会重新计算谁能享受关税优惠，但供应链的现实是多层次叠加，不是非此即彼的站队。河内需要做的不是挑选投资者，而是把规则做清楚，让所有投资者在同一张表上竞争。把招商写成地缘选边，反而会误判越南的真正诉求。

## 三个问题决定成色

第一，税收、数据与个人保护规则何时从征求意见变成可预期的条文；第二，64%的扩产意向有多少能转化为实际资本开支；第三，争端解决与知识产权保护能否跟上服务业投资的扩张。点评：如果到2027年东盟成立60周年时，这些答案仍停留在论坛与联合声明层面，升级后的关系就只升了名分。对东南亚其他经济体也是提醒：吸引投资的下半场，拼的不是优惠力度，而是确定性；而确定性恰恰是最难用口号生产的东西。

## 延伸阅读

**全面战略伙伴关系（Comprehensive Strategic Partnership）**：国家间外交关系的高级别定位，通常意味着更频繁的制度化对话与更广的合作领域，但并不自动带来具体投资项目。

**跨境数据流动（Cross-border Data Flows）**：数据跨越国境传输与处理的规则安排，涉及隐私保护、数据本地化和安全评估，是数字服务与医疗科技投资的关键变量。

**环境、社会与治理标准（ESG Standards）**：衡量企业环境绩效、社会责任与治理结构的规范体系，正通过欧洲供应链传导至东南亚供应商，影响订单资格与融资成本。

# Vietnam courts European investment: after the upgrade, rules are the hard currency

## From political signalling to an investment checklist

Nikkei Asia reported on 19 November that Vietnam is stepping up its effort to attract European investment after upgrading relations with the EU to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in January 2026. The story has a familiar shape: a diplomatic promotion, a business forum, a senior delegation, and warm words on both sides. The blunt observation is that a higher diplomatic label does not automatically turn into projects. The EU–Vietnam Free Trade Agreement has been in force for more than six years, so the tariff cuts have long been on the table. The real question was never whether to reduce duties; it is whether, after the reduction, a company can still make the numbers work. Hanoi is facing that conversion problem — from diplomatic text to an investment pipeline.

## The numbers look good, but intention is not capital

Noel Clehane, vice chairman of the EU-ASEAN Business Council, offered a striking set of figures. The council's latest survey finds that 64% of members intend to expand their operations in Vietnam over the next five years, while 34% of companies not yet present are considering entry. EuroCham Vietnam's Business Confidence Index stood at 78.3 points, with 90% of respondents willing to recommend Vietnam as an investment destination and 23% saying their investment horizon exceeds 20 years. These are votes of confidence, not capital expenditure. Between a board's expansion motion and a factory opening stand land, permits, power prices and skills. Treating sentiment indicators as investment forecasts is the laziest habit in coverage of investment promotion.

## 110 executives arrive in Hanoi

Between 4 and 6 November, about 110 senior executives from nearly 40 European companies visited Hanoi, meeting Vietnam's deputy prime minister and officials from finance, industry and trade, science and technology, and health. It was the EU-ASEAN Business Council's sixth such delegation. On 17 November the European Commission hosted a dialogue in Brussels on trade with Southeast Asia. Chris Humphrey, the council's executive director, put it plainly: finalising a free trade agreement is only the beginning. A year ago the question was whether an agreement existed; now it is whether the agreement can actually be used. That shift is progress, but it also means the remaining problems are the hard ones.

## The friction: tax, data and licensing

The bottlenecks Clehane names are not tariffs. They are tax policy, cross-border data transfers, personal data protection and the need for clearer licensing rules. He also calls for greater consultation with foreign businesses when policies and laws are drafted. These demands sound technical, but they sit in the denominator of any investment return. When rules are unpredictable, companies raise their discount rate and price the risk into the quote. For digital services, healthcare data and financial services, data rules now matter more than conventional tariffs. Whoever writes those rules clearly will be first in line for the next round of investment.

## Eight challenges, twenty-seven hurdles

EuroCham's survey shows companies facing an average of eight operational challenges in Vietnam; those significantly affected by regulatory change report as many as 27 administrative barriers. Its priorities are equally concrete: digitise and streamline administrative procedures, develop skilled workers for renewable energy and high-tech industries, and improve access to green power. The figure of 27 deserves more attention than the confidence score of 78.3. Confidence is temperature; barriers are friction. A high temperature does not make the machine run. A market can be strongly favoured by investors and still exhausting to operate in, and Vietnam currently sits in that not-uncommon condition.

## Green power and rules of origin

European companies list healthcare, biotechnology, sustainable manufacturing, aviation, financial services and education as areas of opportunity, with renewable energy repeatedly highlighted because Europe has accumulated technology in wind, solar, storage and grids. At the same time, European consumers' demands for sustainability and traceability travel down the supply chain to Vietnamese suppliers, making environmental, social and governance standards rather than optional extras. The EU's Global Gateway initiative is also mentioned as a potential source of infrastructure finance. European capital is not merely money; it arrives with a standards package. Standards can raise value added, but they can also exclude local suppliers whose capabilities have not caught up.

## Where ethnic Chinese businesses fit: bridge, not immunity

Vietnamese ethnic Chinese businesses have long connected manufacturing, trade and logistics. Their language skills, family networks and cross-border experience can help European firms find local suppliers and translate European compliance requirements for smaller vendors. A bridge is not an exemption, however. Overseas Chinese capital faces the same tax rules, data obligations, environmental permits and labour regulations. Treating the diaspora network as a substitute for institutions both overstates what networks can do and underestimates how fast local governance evolves. The valuable move is to convert relational capital into compliance capability, not to route around rules.

## Between China and Vietnam, not an either-or choice

Vietnam is both a key node in China–ASEAN production networks and a destination for European capital seeking supply-chain diversification. The China–ASEAN Free Trade Agreement 3.0 has been signed, extending cooperation in goods trade and digital issues. European entry does not mean Chinese capital exits. Rules of origin will recalculate who qualifies for tariff preferences, but supply chains are layered rather than neatly aligned with geopolitical camps. What Hanoi needs is not to pick investors but to make the rules legible so that all of them compete on the same schedule. Framing investment promotion as geopolitical alignment misreads what Vietnam actually wants.

## Three questions that determine the outcome

First, when will tax, data and personal-protection rules move from consultation drafts to predictable statutes? Second, how much of that 64% expansion intention will become actual capital expenditure? Third, can dispute resolution and intellectual-property protection keep pace with services investment? If these answers remain at the level of forums and joint statements by 2027, when ASEAN turns 60, the upgraded relationship will have upgraded only its title. The lesson for other Southeast Asian economies is simple: the second half of the competition for investment is not about incentives but about predictability, and predictability is the hardest thing to manufacture with slogans.

## Further reading

**Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**: A high-level designation of bilateral relations that usually implies more frequent institutional dialogue and a wider agenda, but does not by itself deliver specific investment projects.

**Cross-border Data Flows**: The rules governing the transfer and processing of data across borders, covering privacy, localisation and security reviews; a decisive variable for digital services and health-tech investment.

**ESG Standards**: Norms measuring environmental performance, social responsibility and governance, now transmitted through European supply chains to Southeast Asian suppliers and affecting both order eligibility and financing costs.

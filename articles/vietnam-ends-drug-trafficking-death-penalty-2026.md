据《南华早报（South China Morning Post）》报道，越南正酝酿在刑法修订草案中取消"毒品贩运"的死刑适用，将之列出现行死刑罪名。若获通过，越南死刑将仅保留于叛国、谋杀、恐怖主义等极少数重罪。在禁毒以严刑著称的东南亚，这一松动引发地区争论。

## 一份草案搅动的地区争议

草案本身尚未表决，却已触动敏感神经。支持者视其为司法文明的一步；批评者担忧毒贩会因此更有恃无恐。越南长期以重刑震慑毒品犯罪，任何松动都被放在"松绑"与"进步"的两极解读之间。

## 越南禁毒的严刑传统

越南是全球禁毒最严的国家之一，每年执行涉毒死刑的数量居前。2013年越南曾被指处决多名外籍毒贩引发外交风波；法律将贩运少量毒品亦列为极刑，形成强大威慑，却也招致人权组织长期批评。

## 改革为何此时提上桌面

时机并非偶然。越南正谋求从低成本制造向高附加值产业跃迁，改善营商环境与"现代法治"形象是其叙事需要。此外，越共十三大后司法改革被反复提及；在签署更多国际经贸协定的背景下，刑法与人权标准的对接成为隐性压力。

## 支持者的理由

改革派主张：死刑对毒贩威慑有限，却可能阻断"线人"与从犯认罪；将资源投向康复与边境治理，比处决更有效。他们也指出，保留死刑并未能根除越南的跨境毒品通道——源头在金三角，不在刑场。

## 反对者的担忧

反对声音则强调：越南地处金三角外缘，毒情严峻；一旦取消极刑，跨境集团可能将越南当作"安全走廊"。基层执法者也担心，量刑降低会削弱一线查缉的正当性与威慑力，尤其在民众安全感本就脆弱的边境省份。

## 东南亚的"死刑地图"差异

东南亚对毒品死刑态度参差：新加坡坚持严打，印尼对外籍毒贩亦有执行；老挝、缅甸相对模糊；泰国已大幅限缩。越南若松绑，将与该地区"严—宽"光谱中的宽端靠拢，也可能影响邻国的立法气压。

## 人权叙事与主权逻辑

西方人权组织多将此举视为进步；河内则强调这是自主司法改革，非外部施压结果。两种叙事并存：一边是普遍人权标准，一边是主权国家依国情立法的逻辑。越南的措辞，恰是在二者之间寻得表述空间。

## 一纸修法背后的治理转向

取消毒贩死刑，看似只动一条罪名，却折射越南治理思路的变化：从"以刑吓止"转向"以治代刑"，辅以康复、边境合作与国际协定对接。成败不在条文本身，而在配套能否落地——毕竟，真正决定毒情的，是金三角的供给与区域的协作，而非刑场的有无。

## 延伸阅读

**金三角（Golden Triangle）**：缅泰老交界的毒品产地，长期为东南亚毒品通道源头，越南毒情与之相连。

**司法改革（Judicial Reform）**：越南在融入国际经贸体系过程中持续推进法治与刑法现代化，死刑议题是其中敏感一环。

**社会政策（Social Policy）**：禁毒从惩罚转向康复与预防，反映社会治理由"严刑"向"治理"的范式迁移。

South China Morning Post reports Vietnam may drop the death penalty for drug trafficking in a penal-code revision, leaving capital punishment only for treason, murder and a few grave crimes. In a region known for harsh anti-drug sentencing, the easing stirs debate.

## A Draft That Stirs Regional Debate

The draft is not yet voted, yet it has touched a nerve. Supporters see a step toward judicial civilisation; critics fear traffickers emboldened. Vietnam has long deterred drugs with harsh sentences, and any easing is read at the pole of "leniency" versus "progress."

## Vietnam's Tough-Sentence Tradition on Drugs

Vietnam is among the toughest on drugs, executing among the most drug convictions yearly. In 2013 it faced diplomatic rows after executing foreign traffickers; the law sets capital punishment even for small trafficking, a strong deterrent yet long criticised by rights groups.

## Why Reform Surfaces Now

The timing is no accident. Vietnam seeks to leap from low-cost manufacturing to higher value-add, and a "modern rule-of-law" image serves that story. After the 13th Party Congress, judicial reform recurred in speeches; signing more trade pacts quietly pressures alignment of penal and rights standards.

## The Case for Reform

Reformers argue the death penalty weakly deters traffickers yet may block informants and accomplices from confessing; funds spent on rehabilitation and border control work better. They note capital punishment has not ended Vietnam's cross-border drug routes—the source is the Golden Triangle, not the execution ground.

## The Worries of Opponents

Opponents stress Vietnam sits on the Golden Triangle's edge with severe drug pressure; dropping the ultimate penalty may let transit groups treat it as a "safe corridor." Frontline police worry lower sentences weaken the mandate and deterrence, especially in border provinces where public security is already fragile.

## Southeast Asia's Divergent "Death-Penalty Map"

The region splits on drug execution: Singapore holds the hard line, Indonesia too executes foreign traffickers; Laos and Myanmar are ambiguous; Thailand has sharply narrowed it. If Vietnam eases, it moves toward the lenient end of the spectrum and may shift neighbours' legislative pressure.

## Human-Rights Narrative vs Sovereign Logic

Western rights groups hail the move as progress; Hanoi says it is autonomous judicial reform, not external pressure. Two narratives coexist: universal rights standards on one side, a sovereign state legislating by context on the other. Vietnam's wording seeks room between them.

## A Governance Turn Behind One Amendment

Scrapping the trafficker death penalty touches one charge yet signals a shift: from "deter by sentence" to "govern by cure," with rehabilitation, border cooperation and pact alignment. Success lies not in the text but in matching delivery—what truly decides the drug scene is Golden Triangle supply and regional coordination, not the presence of an execution ground.

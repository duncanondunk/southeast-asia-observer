# 议席从七席砍到两席：马华公会在甲州选举中的「离席抗议」说明了什么

马来西亚（Malaysia）国民阵线（国阵）成员党马华公会，在来临的马六甲州选举中参选议席从上届的七席骤降至两席。10月24日晚，国阵在马六甲举行竞选机制推介礼，临时增设公布候选人环节，并将腾出的五个议席直接让给国民联盟（国盟）候选人。马华总会长魏家祥当场率全体代表离席抗议，并撂下「你不要玩我」的重话，暗示马华可能退出国阵。一场看似地方性的席位纠纷，折射出马来西亚华人政党在马来政治重组中的结构性窘境。

## 风波始末：一场「先斩后奏」的推介礼

据《联合早报（Lianhe Zaobao）》报道，马华星期天（10月25日）发表的声明披露，国阵执行秘书在10月23日晚曾书面确认，24日的推介礼「纯属竞选机制启动，绝不会安排公布候选人名单」。然而到了现场，国阵领导层临时增设公布候选人名单的环节，宣布马华参选的甲州议席由上届七席骤降至两席，并将五个席位直接让给国盟。

用魏家祥的话说，这是「先斩后奏」——马华在完全未获征询、双方也未讨论的情况下，原本属于自己的竞选议席被「打包」让给了别人。面对突如其来的变故，魏家祥当场率领全体马华代表集体离席，令在场人士侧目。国阵甲州主席阿都拉勿夫起初态度强硬，称「谈判已敲定、名单无需更动」，到了星期天却改口称尚未收到完整报告，「再作定夺」。

## 魏家祥的怒火：「你不要玩我」

离席之后，魏家祥在场外对媒体直言：「你不要玩我。我跟你讲，我是敢做的。我出于尊重而来，但如果你们玩我，我会走的。」这番话被视为对国阵、尤其是巫统的公开警告：若不满未获解决，马华「可能做出重大决定」，外界普遍解读为暗指退出国阵。

但明眼人都看得出来，这更像是一场边缘博弈（brinkmanship）——以退为威胁，逼巫统在提名日（10月31日）前让步。国阵及巫统主席阿末扎希随即降温，称将与马华协商，赶在提名日前化解危机，并强调自己「尚未签署候选人委任状，名单仍有机会调整」。说白了，扎希需要马华这张华人牌，至少在表面上维持国阵「多元」的门面。

## 巫统的独断与国阵的裂痕

政治评论人默里汉特撰文抨击巫统的做法「践踏了马华的政治尊严」。他指出，事件暴露出巫统领导层长期以来目中无人、漠视既定协商制度的作风已变本加厉；这种独断专行的态度若不加约束，将成为动摇国阵根基乃至导致马国政局动荡的乱源。

这并非孤例。马国时评员蓝志峰就指出，马华在森美兰州选举时已让出议席，在马六甲再让议席「也并非不可能」。换言之，把原本华人政党竞争、华裔选民占多数的选区让渡给以马来选民为基本盘的国盟，已逐渐成为巫统整合马来选票的一种惯例。民主行动党（行动党）的陆兆福更直言，巫统「卖掉」了马华的议席，把原本属于马华的选区让给伊斯兰党及国盟。

## 华人政党的结构性困局

把镜头拉远，这场风波的本质是马来西亚华人政党在马来政治重组中的集体困境。自2020年「喜来登政变」以来，巫统、伊斯兰党与土团党（国盟核心）在马来选票上加速合流，形成一股以马来民族主义为基调的政治合力。在这一格局下，代表华社的政党——无论是执政联盟内的马华，还是在野的民主行动党——都面临被「边缘化」的压力。

马华的尴尬在于：它既依赖国阵的平台获取政治资源，又在与巫统的议席谈判中屡屡退让。此次从七席砍到两席，既是对马华实力的「如实反映」（马华在近几届大选民票颓靡），也是巫统借机向国盟示好、巩固马来阵营的算计。魏家祥的「离席」，更像是一记无奈的姿态政治，而非真正准备好「出走」。

## 官委议员：一个民主赤字的缓冲垫

值得注意的是，评论普遍认为，甲州已修宪通过「官委议员制度」，允许州政府委任最多七名官委议员。这或许能作为巫统日后在行政层面补偿马华的政治筹码——「但前提是马华与巫统的关系不能彻底闹僵」。

这恰恰点出了问题的核心：当选举席位的分配可以靠「行政委任」来补偿，政党竞争的公平性便被悄悄侵蚀。官委议员制度本身，就是马来西亚选举政治中一个长期备受争议的民主赤字设计。用「幕后补偿」来安抚盟党，看似化解了眼前的危机，却也坐实了：在当下的马来西亚，华人政党的「尊严」，更多取决于盟友的施舍，而非选票的实力。

## 延伸阅读

**国民阵线（Barisan Nasional）**：马来西亚长期执政的政党联盟，由巫统、马华公会、印度国大党等族群政党组成；2020年后影响力下降，但仍是重要政治平台。

**官委议员（Appointed Assemblymen）**：部分马来西亚州属允许州政府直接委任议员、无须选举；甲州修宪后最多可委任七名，常被视为弥补盟友或稀释选举结果的机制。

**马来选票整合（Malay Vote Consolidation）**：巫统、伊斯兰党与土团党近年来在马来裔选民中加速合流，挤压代表少数族裔政党的议席空间。

# From Seven Seats to Two: What MCA's Malacca Walkout Reveals About Chinese Politics in Malaysia

The Malaysian Chinese Association (MCA), a component party of the Barisan Nasional (BN) coalition, has seen its contested seats for the upcoming Malacca state election slashed from seven in the previous election to just two. On the evening of 24 October, BN staged its election machinery launch in Malacca and abruptly added a candidate-announcement segment, handing the five freed seats directly to candidates from Perikatan Nasional (PN). MCA president Wee Ka Siong led his entire delegation out of the hall in protest and issued a blunt warning — "don't play me" — hinting that MCA might quit BN altogether. What looks like a local seat dispute in fact lays bare the structural predicament of ethnic-Chinese parties amid Malaysia's realignment of Malay politics.

## How it unfolded: a launch event that blindsided MCA

According to Lianhe Zaobao, MCA's statement on Sunday (25 October) disclosed that BN's executive secretary had confirmed in writing on the evening of 23 October that Saturday's launch would be "purely about activating the election machinery, with absolutely no candidate list to be announced." Yet on the ground, BN leaders added the candidate-announcement segment at the last minute, declaring MCA's contested state seats cut from seven to two and handing the five vacated seats to PN.

In Wee's words, this was a done deal presented as a fait accompli — without any consultation, MCA's own constituencies were "packaged" and given away. Faced with the sudden reversal, Wee led all MCA representatives out of the hall, drawing stares from those present. BN's Malacca chair Abdul Rauf was initially defiant, saying "negotiations were settled, the list need not change," before softening on Sunday to say he had not yet received a full report and would "decide later."

## Wee Ka Siong's fury: "Don't play me"

Outside the venue, Wee told the press: "Don't play me. I'm telling you, I am capable of acting. I came out of respect, but if you play me, I will walk. Don't think this president dares not act." The remarks were widely read as a public warning to BN — and especially to UMNO — that if the grievance went unresolved, MCA "might make a major decision," interpreted as a veiled threat to leave BN.

But any close observer can see this is a classic exercise in brinkmanship: using the threat of exit to force UMNO into concessions before nomination day on 31 October. BN and UMNO chairman Ahmad Zahid Hamidi moved quickly to cool tempers, promising talks to resolve the crisis before nomination and stressing he had "not yet signed the appointment letters, so the list can still be adjusted." In plain terms, Zahid needs MCA's Chinese card, at least to preserve BN's superficial image of "multi-ethnic" inclusiveness.

## UMNO's high-handedness and BN's fracture

Political commentator Murray Hunter wrote that UMNO's conduct "trampled on MCA's political dignity." He argued the episode exposed a long-standing UMNO leadership habit of arrogance and disregard for established consultation mechanisms, now escalating; left unchecked, such high-handedness could become the very source of instability shaking BN's foundations and Malaysia's politics at large.

This is no isolated case. Malaysian commentator Lan Chip Feng noted that MCA had already given up seats in the Negeri Sembilan election, so yielding again in Malacca was "not impossible." In other words, handing constituencies that ethnic-Chinese parties used to contest — and where ethnic-Chinese voters form a majority — to the Malay-based PN has quietly become a routine tool for UMNO to consolidate the Malay vote. The DAP's Loke Siew Fook went further, accusing UMNO of "selling out" MCA's seats to PAS and PN.

## The structural predicament of ethnic-Chinese parties

Zooming out, the essence of this row is the collective predicament of Malaysia's ethnic-Chinese parties amid the realignment of Malay politics. Since the "Sheraton Move" political crisis of 2020, UMNO, PAS and BERSATU (the core of PN) have accelerated their convergence on the Malay vote, forming a political force anchored in Malay nationalism. In this configuration, parties representing the Chinese community — whether MCA within the governing coalition or the opposition DAP — face mounting pressure to be marginalized.

MCA's awkward position is that it depends on BN's platform for political resources while repeatedly conceding in seat negotiations with UMNO. The cut from seven seats to two both reflects MCA's real standing (its dismal electoral performance in recent general elections) and serves UMNO's calculus of courting PN and consolidating the Malay bloc. Wee's walkout looks less like a real readiness to "leave" than a gesture of powerless posture politics.

## Appointed assemblymen: a cushion for democratic deficit

Notably, commentators broadly agree that Malacca has amended its constitution to adopt an "appointed assemblymen" system, allowing the state government to appoint up to seven nominated members. This could serve as a political chip for UMNO to compensate MCA at the administrative level later — "provided MCA and UMNO do not fall out completely."

This strikes at the core of the problem: when the allocation of elected seats can be offset by "administrative appointment," the fairness of party competition is quietly eroded. The appointed-assemblymen system itself is a long-contested design that embodies a democratic deficit in Malaysia's electoral politics. Using backroom compensation to soothe a coalition partner may defuse the immediate crisis, but it also confirms a sobering reality: in today's Malaysia, the "dignity" of ethnic-Chinese parties hinges more on the indulgence of their allies than on the strength of the ballot.

## Further reading

**Barisan Nasional (BN)**: Malaysia's long-governing coalition of communal parties including UMNO, MCA and the Malaysian Indian Congress; its influence has waned since 2020 but it remains a key political platform.

**Appointed Assemblymen**: Some Malaysian states let the state government name legislators without an election; after Malacca's constitutional amendment, up to seven may be appointed, often seen as a mechanism to compensate allies or dilute election outcomes.

**Malay Vote Consolidation**: UMNO, PAS and BERSATU have in recent years accelerated their convergence among Malay voters, squeezing the seat space of parties representing ethnic minorities.

# 燃油飙升货币贬值双重夹击，东南亚廉航第二季普遍亏损

## 双重夹击下的季度业绩

据联合早报报道，受中东局势推高的油价与区域货币贬值双重打击，东南亚主要廉价航空2026年第二季业绩普遍走弱。亚航（AirAsia）、酷航（Scoot）与宿务太平洋（Cebu Pacific）最新财报显示，票价上调几乎无法覆盖燃油成本。亚航与宿务太平洋均录得第二季净亏损；酷航经营亏损同比几近翻倍，由1,700万新元（约9,350万元人民币）扩大至3,200万新元（约1.76亿元人民币）。

## 成本结构与定价困境

廉价航空的致命弱点是其成本结构。燃油在总成本中占比极高，而目标客群对价格高度敏感，提价空间极为有限。即便上调票价，也仅能部分抵消成本上行，难以真正恢复利润率。这意味着廉航无法通过简单的涨价把压力转嫁给乘客，盈利弹性远低于全服务航司。

## 货币贬值的放大效应

据路透社报道，令吉、泰铢、印尼盾与菲律宾比索兑美元集体走弱，使以美元计价的燃油与飞机租赁成本雪上加霜。宿务太平洋第二季燃油开支同比翻倍以上，比索贬值8%更令其处境恶化。为对冲短期风险，该公司已锁定约三成第三季燃油需求，价格控制在每桶120美元（约852元人民币）以内——即约152新元（约836元人民币）每桶。

## 航司的止损动作

美银环球研究亚太运输主管内森·吉指出，全服务航司凭借疫后强劲的高端需求更具缓冲，而廉价航空产品基础薄弱、常旅客计划规模有限，受益明显较小。亚航预计第三季持续疲软，计划在最弱的第三季同比削减20%至25%的座位运力，年内向租赁方归还25架老旧飞机，并于10月起暂停悉尼—吉隆坡航线。多家廉航转向收缩，以应对高成本与家庭预算收紧的双重压力。

## 消费韧性的疑问

尽管酷航增加运力并提价，其第二季每可用座位公里成本仍上升21.7%，盈亏平衡载客率升至100%——意味着必须满载才能覆盖客运运营成本，而实际载客率仅为90.6%。独立航空分析师索比认为，受家庭预算收紧制约，东南亚中产旅行需求在年内余下时间及关键旺季或持续受限，短期前景黯淡。尽管业界寄望第四季反弹，眼下仍言之过早。

## 延伸阅读

**盈亏平衡载客率（Break-even load factor）**：航司覆盖运营成本的必要上座率，数值越高代表票价或满载压力越大。

**可用座位公里（Available seat-km, ASK）**：衡量运力供给的指标，等于座位数乘以飞行公里数。

**燃油对冲（Fuel hedging）**：航司通过锁定部分燃油价格，规避油价波动风险的金融工具。

# Fuel Spike and Currency Slide Hit Southeast Asia Budget Carriers in Q2

## Quarterly results under a double bind

Southeast Asia's low-cost carriers reported broadly weaker results for the second quarter of 2026, caught between a surge in fuel prices—driven by the Middle East situation—and the depreciation of regional currencies. According to Reuters, the latest quarterly figures from AirAsia, Scoot and Cebu Pacific show fare increases barely offsetting fuel costs. Both AirAsia and Cebu Pacific posted Q2 net losses, while Scoot's operating loss nearly doubled year on year, widening from S$17 million (about US$13 million) to S$32 million (about US$25 million).

## Cost structure and the pricing dilemma

The structural vulnerability of the budget model is now laid bare. Fuel accounts for a large share of a low-cost carrier's total costs, yet its target customers are intensely price-sensitive, leaving very little room to raise fares without suppressing demand. Even where ticket prices were lifted, the gains only partly absorbed higher input costs, leaving margins under pressure rather than restored. Unlike full-service rivals, budget airlines cannot simply pass the pain through to passengers.

## Currency depreciation amplifies the pain

The ringgit, baht, rupiah and Philippine peso all weakened against the US dollar, inflating the cost of dollar-denominated fuel and aircraft leases. Cebu Pacific's Q2 fuel spending more than doubled year on year, and an 8% peso depreciation made a bad situation worse. To hedge short-term risk, the airline locked in roughly 30% of its Q3 fuel needs below US$120 per barrel (about S$152), capping its exposure to further oil-price swings.

## Carriers move to cut losses

Nathan Gass, BofA Global Research's head of APAC transport, notes that full-service carriers are better shielded by strong post-pandemic premium demand, whereas budget airlines benefit far less because their products are basic and their frequent-flyer programmes are smaller. AirAsia expects Q3 to remain soft and plans to cut seat capacity by 20–25% year on year in the weakest quarter, return 25 older aircraft to lessors this year, and suspend the Sydney–Kuala Lumpur route from October. Several budget carriers are pivoting to retrenchment.

## A question mark over consumption resilience

Even as Scoot added capacity and raised fares, its Q2 cost per available seat-km climbed 21.7%, pushing its break-even load factor to 100%—it must fly full simply to cover passenger operating costs—while its actual load factor stood at 90.6%. Independent aviation analyst Sobie argues that, constrained by tightening household budgets, Southeast Asia's middle-class travel demand may stay limited for the rest of the year and through key peak seasons, leaving the near-term outlook bleak. Players are hoping for a Q4 rebound, but it is far too early to tell.

## Further reading

**Break-even load factor**: The load factor a carrier needs to cover its operating costs; the higher it is, the greater the pressure on fares or occupancy.

**Available seat-km (ASK)**: A measure of capacity supply, equal to the number of seats multiplied by the kilometres flown.

**Fuel hedging**: A financial tool by which airlines lock in part of their fuel price to guard against oil-price volatility.

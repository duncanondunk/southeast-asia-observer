# 「中国+1」掉头：惠普华硕宏碁把个人电脑产能搬回中国，东南亚「组装车间」现原形

据《日经亚洲》10月28日报道，美国惠普、台湾华硕与宏碁等全球主要个人电脑厂商，正把一部分整机生产从泰国、越南迁回中国大陆，并转用一批新兴的中国代工厂。戴尔也在评估类似方案。动作低调，没有发布会，从今年第二季度悄然开始。一位为多家个人电脑品牌供货的芯片行业高管对《日经亚洲》说得直白：「这波转移真的是成本压力驱动的。」这不是情怀，也不是政治，是账本。

## 新闻背后：一次低调的「回流」

当初喊着要「把产能搬出中国」的是他们，如今悄悄回头的也是他们。《日经亚洲》援引多名知情人士称，随着物料成本攀升、人工智能需求导致内存与存储部件严重短缺，个人电脑厂商正寻求更高的制造效率，而中国仍是最佳选项——这里有经验丰富的劳动力、更低的价格，以及能提供全面零部件的完整供应链生态。宏碁嘴上说着「动态调整供应链」，身体却很诚实地把部分采购权交给了代工厂。没有一家厂商高调确认，但方向已被数字印证。

## 导火索：人工智能抢走了内存，成本表被掀翻

真正的推手是人工智能。全球科技公司疯狂建设数据中心，人工智能服务器吞掉了大量存储与逻辑芯片产能。结果就是：个人电脑最关键的几样零件——内存、固态硬盘——价格一年涨了数倍。据《日经亚洲》引述，仅动态随机存取存储器（DRAM）价格一年内就涨了至少八倍；市场机构测算，一台售价900美元（约6480元人民币）的主流笔记本，中央处理器、内存与固态硬盘三项占物料成本的比重，已从2025年初的约45%升到2026年三季度的约68%。要保住原有毛利率，整机售价得涨近八成——可市场不接受。于是品牌方只能回到组装环节抠成本。

## 东南亚的账：人工省了，物料赔了

当初搬出去，算的是东南亚更低的工资。越南工厂工人月均工资约250美元（约1800元人民币），泰国约447美元（约3218元人民币），确实低于中国。可一台电脑的成本大头不在组装人力，而在成百上千个零部件。面板、机构件、连接器、线材，供应链主体仍留在中国。供应链研究估算，在东南亚组装一台笔记本，综合成本反而比中国贵约9美元（约65元人民币）；更保守的口径是整体贵一到两成。物流时间拉长、库存压厚、关税一波动，整条线跟着打摆子。名义工资低，单位产出的相对成本却并不占优。

## 配套率之谜：搬得走产线，搬不走生态

症结在配套率。越南电子业的本土配套率不足三成，七成以上电子元件靠进口，主要来自中国；一家美国贸易机构报告指出，越南电子产业进口投入品中，中国和韩国提供了约95%。工厂可以平地起，产业集群搬不走。珠三角的零件送到组装厂只要三五天，东南亚的链路普遍在十天以上。当存储芯片价格剧烈波动，一个本土配套率不足三成的生产节点，既无法本地多源采购平抑价格，也无法用集群内的高频物流对冲交期风险。这正应了那句话：搬走一条组装线，和搬走一整套产业生态，是两回事。

## 关税理由先撤了：外迁的逻辑松动

当年往外搬，搬的主要是关税风险。但笔记本这个品类，美国对华附加关税始终没有真正落地：301清单里属于暂停征收的一档，2025年的对等关税把消费电子排除在外，今年2月美国最高法院又推翻了对等关税的法律依据。到现在，中国、越南、泰国产的笔记本进入美国，附加关税实际都是零。关税差没了，成本差摆上了台面。美国普查局的进口数据已现端倪：按金额计，中国在美国笔记本进口中的份额，从2018年的约94%跌至2026年前8个月的约8%，但6至8月已回升到10%至12%。

## 代工桌上换了人：台系退、中系进

代工端的牌桌也换了人。广达今年二季度营收中，服务器占比已超过八成，个人电脑是低毛利生意；传统台系代工厂把资源系统性转向利润更肥的人工智能服务器订单。空出来的档口，被立讯精密、华勤技术、龙旗科技这批中国大陆厂商接住。立讯与六联智能合资的立联讯达，过去一年已拿到惠普、戴尔、华硕的订单；华勤预计2026年全年笔记本出货达2000万台。老牌台系代工厂和中国新军的出货量，已经贴到了一起。中国代工厂手里还有一张东南亚没有的牌：国产内存的采购通道。

## 约束边界：不是「脱钩失败」，也不是「全面回潮」

必须说清楚边界。这轮回流没有发布会，动作也不整齐。销往美国的商务机型、涉及政府采购的产品仍留在泰国和越南生产，没有哪家关掉东南亚的厂。多位供应链高管对台湾《经济日报》说，东南亚仍是长期的制造韧性基地。路透社也观察到，美国零售商把部分订单转回中国供应商、某跨境快时尚平台缩减越南业务，但印度、印尼、越南仍在吸纳电子与汽车投资。个人电脑这一例的特殊之处，在于有占比曲线垫底，方向能被数字证实。变的只是算账的权重：从政策风险，转向生产成本与供应链完整度。

## 给东南亚的提醒：工厂神话需要重估

对东南亚而言，这是一记清醒的提醒。过去几年，「中国+1」叙事把越南、泰国包装成「下一个世界工厂」，资本、媒体、智库一拥而上。可这一轮回流暴露的，是东南亚在电子制造上「离岸打包车间」的底色：它能接住组装订单，却接不住上下游生态。这不是说东南亚的出海故事结束了——汽车、储能、关键矿产仍在南下——但「搬厂即升级」的神话，需要被重新评估。真正的产业转移，从来不是一纸订单加几座厂房，而是几十年垒起来的配套、工人与协作网络。

## 结论：算盘比口号算得清

惠普们的回头，说到底是一场成本核算的胜利，也是一堂关于产业规律的公开课。那些嚷嚷着「摆脱依赖」的声音，在真金白银的账本面前，终究得低下头来。下一个中国，还真不一定有。但东南亚也不必妄自菲薄：它承接的是全球制造版图里「韧性备份」的那一块，而不是全部。算盘永远比口号算得清——而这一次的算盘，把「中国+1」推到了需要重估的十字路口。

## 延伸阅读

**中国+1（China+1）**：跨国企业为分散地缘与关税风险，在中国之外另设备份生产基地的策略；本轮回流正是对其成本前提的一次修正。

**动态随机存取存储器（DRAM）**：电脑与手机的核心存储芯片，人工智能数据中心扩产挤占其产能，价格一年内数倍上涨，成为本轮个人电脑成本危机的导火索。

**原始设计制造商（ODM）**：承接品牌商整机设计与代工的企业；台系广达、仁宝长期主导，近年立讯、华勤、龙旗等中国大陆厂商崛起并接走回流订单。

# When 'China+1' reverses: HP, Asus and Acer move PC production back to China — and Southeast Asia's assembly illusion is exposed

On 28 October, Nikkei Asia reported that leading PC makers including America's HP and Taiwan's Asus and Acer are shifting some final-assembly production from Thailand and Vietnam back to mainland China, while turning to a new generation of Chinese contract manufacturers. Dell is weighing a similar move. There was no press conference; the shift began quietly in the second quarter. A chip-industry executive who supplies several major PC brands told Nikkei Asia bluntly: "This move is really driven by cost pressure." It is not sentiment, and it is not politics — it is the ledger.

## Behind the headline: a quiet reverse migration

The same companies that once vowed to move capacity out of China are now quietly coming back. Citing multiple sources, Nikkei Asia said that as material costs climbed and AI demand triggered severe shortages of memory and storage components, PC vendors are seeking higher manufacturing efficiency — and China remains the best option, with experienced labour, lower prices and a complete supply-chain ecosystem that delivers components end to end. Acer talks about "dynamically adjusting the supply chain," yet its body language hands more procurement to contract manufacturers. No vendor confirmed it loudly, but the direction is confirmed by the numbers.

## The trigger: AI devoured memory, and upended the cost sheet

The real catalyst is artificial intelligence. As tech giants race to build data centres, AI servers are swallowing capacity for memory and logic chips. The result: the components that matter most for PCs — DRAM and solid-state drives — have multiplied in price several-fold over a year. Citing industry sources, Nikkei Asia noted that DRAM prices alone rose at least eight-fold in a year. According to market researchers, in a mainstream US$900 laptop the combined bill-of-materials share of CPU, DRAM and SSD climbed from around 45% in early 2025 to roughly 68% by the third quarter of 2026. Holding the old margin would have required an ~80% price hike — which the market will not accept. So brands are squeezing cost out of the assembly step instead.

## Southeast Asia's arithmetic: cheaper labour, dearer total cost

The original move out was justified by cheaper wages. A Vietnamese factory worker earns about US$250 a month, a Thai about US$447 — both well below China's. But labour is not where a laptop's cost sits; it is in the hundreds of components. Panels, enclosures, connectors, cabling — the supply base still lives in China. Supply-chain studies estimate that assembling a laptop in Southeast Asia actually costs about US$9 more per unit than in China; more conservative estimates put the gap at 10–20%. Longer logistics, thicker inventory and tariff swings erase the wage saving. The nominal wage is low; the relative cost per unit of output is not.

## The local-content puzzle: lines can move, ecosystems cannot

The crux is local content. Vietnam's electronics localisation rate sits below 30%, with over 70% of components imported — mostly from China. A US trade body report notes that China and South Korea supply about 95% of Vietnam's imported electronics inputs. A factory can be built on greenfield; an industrial cluster cannot be moved by a purchase order. Parts from the Pearl River Delta reach an assembler in days; Southeast Asian lead times routinely exceed ten days. When memory prices swing violently, a node with under 30% local content can neither source locally to damp prices nor lean on high-frequency cluster logistics to hedge delivery risk. Moving an assembly line and moving an entire ecosystem are two different things.

## The tariff rationale retreated first

The original exit was chiefly about tariff risk. But for laptops, the US additional tariff on China never really landed: the product sits in a suspended category under Section 301, the 2025 reciprocal tariffs carved out consumer electronics, and in February the US Supreme Court struck down the legal basis for the reciprocal tariffs. Today, laptops from China, Vietnam and Thailand all enter the US with effectively zero additional tariff. With the tariff gap gone, the cost gap moves to centre stage. US Census import data hint at the turn: by value, China's share of US laptop imports fell from ~94% in 2018 to ~8% in the first eight months of 2026 — but rebounded to 10–12% in June–August.

## A new cast on the contract-manufacturing table

The contract-manufacturing table has also changed hands. Quanta's server business already exceeds 80% of its revenue, and the traditional Taiwanese ODMs are steering resources toward higher-margin AI servers. The slack is being picked up by China's Luxshare, Huaqin and Longcheer. Luxshare's joint venture with Liu-Lian has won orders from HP, Dell and Asus over the past year; Huaqin expects ~20 million laptop units in 2026. The old Taiwanese incumbents and the new Chinese challengers are now neck and neck on shipment volume. Chinese ODMs also hold a card Southeast Asia lacks: access to domestically produced memory.

## The boundaries: not 'decoupling failed', nor 'full return'

The boundaries must be stated clearly. This reversal had no press conference and is far from uniform. Business laptops bound for the US and products tied to government procurement remain in Thailand and Vietnam; no one is shutting Southeast Asian plants. Multiple supply-chain executives told Taiwan's Economic Daily that Southeast Asia remains a long-term resilience base. Reuters has observed similar back-to-China moves by a US retailer and a scaling-back by a cross-border fashion platform in Vietnam — yet India, Indonesia and Vietnam keep absorbing electronics and automotive investment. What makes the PC case special is that a share curve confirms the direction numerically. What changed is merely the weight in the calculation: from policy risk toward production cost and supply-chain completeness.

## A wake-up call for Southeast Asia

For Southeast Asia, this is a sobering reminder. In recent years the "China+1" narrative dressed Vietnam and Thailand as "the next factory of the world," and capital, media and think-tanks piled in. This reversal exposes the region's true colour as an "offshore packaging workshop" in electronics: it can take assembly orders but not the upstream-downstream ecosystem. This is not the end of Southeast Asia's outward story — autos, storage and critical minerals are still heading south — but the myth that "moving a plant equals upgrading" needs revaluation. Real relocation is never a purchase order plus a few buildings; it is the supporting network, the workforce and the collaboration built up over decades.

## Conclusion: the abacus beats the slogan

HP's about-face is, at bottom, a victory of cost accounting and a lesson in industrial economics. Those who shouted about "ending dependence" must bow before the hard ledger. The next China may not exist. But Southeast Asia need not sell itself short: it absorbed the "resilience backup" slice of global manufacturing, not the whole. The abacus always beats the slogan — and this time the abacus has pushed "China+1" to a crossroads that demands revaluation.

## Further reading

**China+1**: The strategy of keeping a backup production base outside China to hedge geopolitical and tariff risk; this reversal corrects its cost premise.

**DRAM (Dynamic Random-Access Memory)**: The core memory chip for PCs and phones; AI data-centre build-out crowded out its capacity, sending prices up several-fold in a year.

**ODM (Original Design Manufacturer)**: A firm that designs and builds finished products for brands; Taiwan's Quanta and Compal long dominated, while China's Luxshare, Huaqin and Longcheer now absorb the returning orders.

# 侨批：维系一个离散群体的汇款家书

> 一张薄薄的信纸，承载着百年海外华侨的家国与生计。

"侨批"，是闽粤方言里"信"与"汇款"的合体——一封寄回家乡的信，同时也是一笔汇款。从十九世纪到二十世纪中叶，数以千万计的侨批，从东南亚寄回福建、广东的乡村，维系着无数家庭的生计，也维系着一个离散群体的情感纽带。

## 信与钱的合一

侨批的特殊性，在于它把"家书"与"汇款"合二为一。在那个没有电子转账的年代，海外华侨靠水客、批信局、侨批馆等民间网络，把辛苦积攒的银钱连同只言片语，辗转送回故乡。

> 一封侨批，既是经济行为，也是情感行为——它让汇款这件事，装下了远超数字本身的重量。

## 跨越山海的家国情书

侨批的动人之处，在于它的私人性。它们记录的不是宏大叙事，而是柴米油盐：母亲问儿子何时归家，妻子嘱咐丈夫添衣，族亲交代修缮祠堂。正是这些琐碎而具体的牵挂，让一个离散群体在地理与文化上保持了延续。

2013 年，"侨批档案"入选联合国教科文组织《世界记忆名录》，被认定为人类共同的文化遗产。

## 离散的金融基础设施

侨批不只是温情，它是一整套民间金融基础设施。批信局、银信局构建起跨国的汇兑网络，其信用机制、清算规则，堪称早期侨民金融的样本。它的衰落，伴随的是现代银行体系与电子汇兑的兴起——但它的逻辑，至今仍在全球侨汇中回响。

## 结语

侨批提醒我们：一个离散群体的维系，既靠血缘与文化，也靠一套能把钱与信送到家的基础设施。今天的西联汇款、移动钱包，不过是侨批逻辑的当代回响。

# Qiaopi: The Remittance Letters That Sustained a Diaspora

> A thin sheet of paper carrying a century of overseas Chinese livelihood and longing.

*Qiaopi* — the term fuses, in the Hokkien and Teochew dialects, "letter" and "remittance." A single envelope sent home was at once a note and a payment. From the nineteenth century through the mid-twentieth, tens of millions of *qiaopi* traveled from Southeast Asia back to the villages of Fujian and Guangdong, sustaining countless households — and the emotional sinews of a scattered people.

## Letter and Money, Fused

What makes *qiaopi* distinctive is that it merges the family letter with the remittance. In an age before electronic transfer, overseas Chinese relied on a folk network of couriers (*shuike*), letter agencies (*pixinju*), and remittance houses (*qiaopiguan*) to carry hard-saved silver, with a few lines of script, all the way home.

> A *qiaopi* was an economic act and an emotional act at once — so that a remittance carried far more than its face value.

## Love Letters Across the Sea

What is moving about the *qiaopi* is its intimacy. They record no grand narrative, but the texture of daily life: a mother asking when her son will return, a wife urging her husband to dress warmly, a clan noting the repair of an ancestral hall. It is exactly this small, concrete care that held a scattered people together across distance and culture.

In 2013, the *Qiaopi Archives* were inscribed into UNESCO's Memory of the World Register, recognized as a shared heritage of humanity.

## The Financial Infrastructure of a Diaspora

Yet *qiaopi* was not only sentiment. It was an entire folk-financial infrastructure. The letter agencies and silver-letter houses built a transnational remittance network whose credit mechanisms and clearing rules amount to an early specimen of diaspora finance. Its decline tracked the rise of modern banking and electronic transfer — but its logic still echoes through global remittance flows today.

The scale was vast. At its peak, the *qiaopi* network moved sums equivalent to a meaningful share of coastal China's foreign-exchange income, financing schools, clinics, and the very fabric of village life across the southeast. A letter could take weeks and cross three jurisdictions; the trust that held the system together was the diaspora's own.

## Why It Still Matters

The *qiaopi* speaks to a truth easily forgotten in the age of instant transfer: a diaspora endures not only on blood and culture, but on infrastructure that can get money and word home. Western Union, mobile wallets, the remittance corridors of today — each is, in its way, the *qiaopi*'s modern echo. The medium has changed; the human problem it solved has not.

## Conclusion

A century later, the *qiaopi* reminds us that what binds a scattered people is not only memory, but the mundane, stubborn mechanics of staying connected — a courier, a receipt, a promise that the money, and the love folded inside it, will arrive.

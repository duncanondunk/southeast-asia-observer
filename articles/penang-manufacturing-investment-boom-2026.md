# 槟城半年吸金173亿：AI 超级周期下的“东南亚台湾”梦

2026年9月3日，马来西亚投资发展局（MIDA）公布数据：槟城2026年上半年获批制造业投资达173亿林吉特（约36亿美元，约280亿元人民币），同比激增38%，占全马制造业投资的34%。在美中科技博弈与全球AI资本开支爆发的双重背景下，这个面积仅居马来西亚倒数第二的“小州”，正试图接住从台湾与大陆溢出的半导体产能，把自己塑造成“下一个台湾”。但光环之下，土地、人才与执行力的短板，才是决定这场“造岛”能否落地的真正变量。

## 半年173亿：一条陡峭的投资曲线

槟城并非一夜成名。MIDA数据显示，2026年1—6月该州获批制造业投资173亿林吉特，较去年同期增长38%，并创下全马34%的占比。其中外资128亿林吉特（占74%），国内直接投资45亿林吉特（同比飙升125%）；177个获批项目预计创造19,337个就业岗位。扩张项目吸走119亿（占69%），新项目54亿。首席部长曹观友（Chow Kon Yeow）表示，照此势头有望超越2025全年224亿林吉特的纪录。InvestPenang首席执行官拿督斯里卢丽莲（Loo Lee Lian）补充，仍有若干项目尚未计入当期数据，对破纪录“谨慎乐观”。

## 外资占七成：钱从哪来

128亿林吉特外资中，香港、新加坡、中国、美国四地合计占77%。这四类资本各有逻辑：美国与日本的半导体设备厂、IDM（集成器件制造）企业寻求“中国+1”避险；中国企业（含港资）借道东南亚绕开关税与出口管制；新加坡资本则扮演区域总部与基金通道。更值得玩味的是，69%的投资流向既有工厂的扩建，而非绿地新厂——这意味着已落户企业用脚投票“加注”，而非试探性进入。资本在不确定时代选择了“深耕”，而非“下注”。

## AI 超级周期：电子与设备的双引擎

曹观友将增长明确归因于“AI超级周期”。电气电子（E&E）与机械设备两大领域吸走了88%的获批资本。槟城自1970年代起积累的封装测试（AT&P）集群，正精准卡位AI算力供应链——高带宽内存、先进封装、服务器组件皆在其射程之内。据区域机构估算，全球约七成AI相关贸易流经东盟+3，槟城作为成熟节点吃到了外溢订单。但AI资本开支的周期性风险也在累积：一旦全球AI投资增速回落，高度集中的电子出口将首当其冲。

## 不是绿地，是深耕：扩张占69%

69%的资本投向扩建而非新建，对槟城是双刃剑。一方面，这说明产业生态成熟、供应商网络完备，企业无需从零搭建产能；另一方面，也折射出“路径依赖”——新增产能多为现有产线的复制与放大，颠覆性增量有限。相较柔佛以绿地数据中心吸引 hyperscaler（超大规模云商），槟城的增量更“重”、更依赖存量客户，扩张的边际红利或随时间递减。

## 从1970年代到今天：半个世纪的电子生态

槟城的电子根基始于1972年槟威自由港（Bayan Lepas）首个自贸区，英特尔、惠普相继设厂，带出精密工程、模具、测试组装的本地供应链。到1980年，制造业占州经济比重从1970年的12.7%升至40%；2010年前后约聚集1400家制造企业、20万从业者。半个世纪的沉淀，正是今日资本愿意“加注”的底气——新进入者面对的不是荒地，而是一整套现成生态。

## “东南亚的台湾”：地缘博弈中的甜蜜点

日经亚洲等机构将槟城描述为美中科技战下的投资“甜蜜点”。台湾地缘风险上升，使部分封测与先进封装订单外移；美国出口管制倒逼供应链多元化。槟城凭借英语人才、法治环境与华人商业网络，成为承接首选之一。但“下一个台湾”终究是宏大叙事：台湾拥有从IC设计到制造的完整自主链条，槟城目前仍偏重后段封测，上游设计与设备依旧依赖外部，“甜蜜点”不等于“制高点”。

## 隐忧：执行与人才短板

批准投资是先行指标，落地才是硬仗。槟城面临三重约束：其一，土地稀缺——“槟城硅谷岛”（Silicon Island）填海造地进度正与产能扩张赛跑；其二，人才——虽英语普及，但高端IC设计与AI软件人才仍短缺，须与吉隆坡、柔佛争抢；其三，基础设施——槟城国际机场扩建（2028年）与_mutira LRT（2031年）能否跟上产业节奏存疑。曹观友也承认，当前获批投资的影响将在18—24个月后才逐步显现。

## 区域竞赛：槟城、柔佛与越南的三角

槟城并非孤例。柔佛凭借土地（约为槟城450倍）、低价电力，以及微软、谷歌、英伟达的数据中心投资，抢的是“算力基础设施”；越南则以劳动力成本与自贸协定吸走组装产能。三者渐成分工：槟城守高端封测、柔佛接 hyperscale 基建、越南做规模制造。槟城要维持“甜蜜点”，关键在于能否从后段向前段爬升，而非仅仅停留在承接溢出。

## 延伸阅读

**获批制造业投资（Approved Manufacturing Investment）**：马来西亚投资发展局（MIDA）的统计口径，指获批（非实际到位）的制造业资本承诺，含新项目与扩建，是观察投资趋势的先行指标。

**先进封装与测试（AT&P）**：半导体制造后段环节，将晶圆切割、封装并测试为可用芯片；槟城自1970年代起聚集大量AT&P企业，是全球封测重镇。

**槟城硅谷岛（Silicon Island）**：槟城州政府推动的填海高新产业园区，旨在扩展半导体与电子制造空间，缓解土地瓶颈。

# Penang's RM17.3bn Half-Year Haul: The "Southeast Asia's Taiwan" Ambition in the AI Supercycle

On 3 September 2026, Malaysia's MIDA reported that Penang secured RM17.3 billion (about US$3.7 billion) in approved manufacturing investments in the first half of 2026—up 38 per cent year-on-year and 34 per cent of the national total. Against the twin backdrop of the US–China tech rivalry and the global AI capital-expenditure boom, this small state—second-smallest by land area in Malaysia—is trying to catch semiconductor capacity spilling from Taiwan and the Chinese mainland, moulding itself as "the next Taiwan." Yet beneath the halo, the real variables deciding whether this "island-building" actually lands are land, talent and execution capacity.

## RM17.3bn in half a year: a steep investment curve

Penang's rise was not overnight. MIDA data show the state secured RM17.3bn in approved manufacturing investments in 1H2026, a 38 per cent jump from a year earlier and 34 per cent of Malaysia's national total. Foreign direct investment reached RM12.8bn (74 per cent); domestic direct investment RM4.5bn, up 125 per cent year-on-year. The 177 approved projects are expected to create some 19,337 jobs. Expansion activity accounted for RM11.9bn (69 per cent) and new projects RM5.4bn. Chief Minister Chow Kon Yeow said the state is on track to beat 2025's full-year record of RM22.4bn. InvestPenang CEO Dato' Seri Loo Lee Lian added that several projects were not yet reflected in the figures, striking a note of cautious optimism about surpassing the record.

## Foreign capital at 74%: where the money comes from

Of the RM12.8bn in foreign capital, Hong Kong, Singapore, China and the United States together accounted for 77 per cent. Each cohort has its logic: US and Japanese semiconductor-equipment makers and IDMs seek "China+1" hedging; Chinese firms (including Hong Kong capital) route through Southeast Asia to bypass tariffs and export controls; Singaporean capital acts as a regional headquarters and fund channel. More telling, 69 per cent of investment flowed to expansion of existing plants rather than greenfield builds—incumbents are doubling down, not testing the waters. In an age of uncertainty, capital chose to deepen, not to gamble.

## The AI supercycle: electronics and equipment twin engines

Chow attributed the surge explicitly to the "AI supercycle." Electrical and electronics (E&E) and machinery and equipment absorbed 88 per cent of approved capital. Penang's assembly, test and packaging (AT&P) cluster, built since the 1970s, is now precisely positioned in the AI compute chain—high-bandwidth memory, advanced packaging and server components all fall within its reach. By regional estimates, roughly 70 per cent of global AI-related trade flows through ASEAN+3; Penang, a mature node, captures the spillover orders. But the cyclical risk of AI capex is also accumulating: should global AI investment growth cool, the heavily concentrated electronics exports would feel it first.

## Not greenfield, but deepening: expansions at 69%

That 69 per cent of capital went to expansion rather than new builds is a double-edged sword for Penang. On one hand, it signals ecosystem maturity and complete supplier networks—firms need not build from zero. On the other, it reflects "path dependence": most new capacity is replication and scaling of existing lines, with limited disruptive upside. Compared with Johor, which lures hyperscalers with greenfield data centres, Penang's increment is "heavier" and more dependent on incumbent customers; the marginal dividend of expansion may fade over time.

## From the 1970s to today: half a century of electronics ecosystem

Penang's electronics roots trace to 1972, when the Bayan Lepas free trade zone opened and Intel and Hewlett-Packard set up, spawning local supply chains in precision engineering, tooling and test-assembly. By 1980, manufacturing's share of the state economy had climbed from 12.7 per cent (1970) to 40 per cent; by around 2010 some 1,400 manufacturers employed 200,000 people. Half a century of accumulation is precisely the confidence behind today's "doubling down"—new entrants face not barren land but a complete, functioning ecosystem.

## "Southeast Asia's Taiwan": a sweet spot in geopolitical rivalry

Nikkei Asia and others describe Penang as an investment "sweet spot" amid the US–China tech war. Rising geopolitical risk around Taiwan is pushing some AT&P and advanced-packaging orders outward; US export controls force supply-chain diversification. Penang, with its English-speaking talent, rule of law and Chinese business networks, is a top recipient. But "the next Taiwan" remains a grand narrative: Taiwan owns a complete chain from IC design to fabrication, whereas Penang still leans on back-end AT&P, dependent on external upstream design and equipment. A sweet spot is not the same as a commanding height.

## Hidden worries: execution and talent gaps

Approved investment is a leading indicator; landing it is the real battle. Penang faces three constraints. First, land scarcity—the "Silicon Island" reclamation is racing against capacity expansion. Second, talent—English is widespread, but high-end IC design and AI-software skills remain short, competing with Kuala Lumpur and Johor. Third, infrastructure—the Penang airport expansion (2028) and Mutiara LRT (2031) may lag the industry's pace. Chow himself conceded that the impact of current approved investments will only materialise over the next 18–24 months.

## The regional race: Penang, Johor and Vietnam's triangle

Penang is not alone. Johor, with land (roughly 450× Penang's), cheap power and Microsoft–Google–Nvidia data-centre investment, grabs "compute infrastructure"; Vietnam draws assembly with labour cost and free-trade agreements. A division of labour is emerging: Penang holds high-end AT&P, Johor takes hyperscale infrastructure, Vietnam does scale manufacturing. Penang's "sweet spot" hinges on whether it can climb from back-end to front-end—rather than merely absorbing spillovers.

## Further reading

**Approved Manufacturing Investment**: MIDA's statistical measure of committed (not yet realised) manufacturing capital, covering both new and expansion projects—a leading indicator of investment trends.

**Assembly, Test & Packaging (AT&P)**: The back-end of semiconductor manufacturing—dicing wafers, packaging and testing them into usable chips. Penang has clustered AT&P firms since the 1970s and is a global test-and-packaging hub.

**Silicon Island**: A Penang state-government land-reclamation hi-tech park aimed at expanding semiconductor and electronics manufacturing space to ease the land bottleneck.

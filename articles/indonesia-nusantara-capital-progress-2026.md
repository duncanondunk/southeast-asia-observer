# 印尼新首都努山塔拉：迁都两年后的进度与质疑

把首都从爪哇的雅加达搬到加里曼丹的努山塔拉（Nusantara），是佐科时代最雄心勃勃的工程，也被普拉博沃政府接过后继续推进。2026 年，迁都已满两年，核心区初具轮廓，但关于成本、节奏与"谁真正搬过去"的质疑，从未远离。

## 为何要迁：雅加达的"下沉"危机

迁都的逻辑根植于雅加达的不可持续：地面年均下沉 15–25 厘米，北部已低于海平面，地下水超采与海平面上升叠加；同时爪哇岛承载全国过半人口与经济，发展极度失衡。努山塔拉被选址于加里曼丹腹地，意在平衡国土、疏解爪哇、并打造一座"森林中的智慧城市"样板。

## 进度：核心区先行，配套滞后

两年过去，总统府、部委办公楼与公务员住宅等核心区已封顶或投用，部分公务员与驻军先行入驻。但机场、高速、医院、学校的全域配套仍显滞后，私营与外资主导的"新城"板块招商不及预期。换言之，政府主导的政治中枢先落地，市场主导的城市生态却慢半拍——一座首都，正在"先有壳、后有城"的路径上摸索。

## 成本之问：钱从哪来，值不值

努山塔拉总预算估算高达数百兆亿印尼盾（约数百亿美元），政府明确不靠财政全包，而寄望于主权基金、PPP 与外资。但全球利率高企、印尼财政空间收窄，使得融资高度依赖国企与战略投资者。质疑随之而来：在基建、教育、医保等民生缺口仍大的当下，把巨额资源投向一座新城，优先级是否成立？

## 治理象征与均衡叙事

对印尼而言，迁都从来不只是工程，更是国家叙事：把权力从爪哇"核心"推向加里曼丹"腹地"，是对外岛均衡发展的政治宣示，也呼应"全球海洋支点"的国族想象。新首都的绿色、智能定位，亦是印尼对外展示治理现代化的窗口。

## 值得观察的几点

其一，公务员迁册规模能否按计划扩大，决定着城市的真实人气。其二，外资与 PPP 项目落地率，是融资可持续的试金石。其三，政治连续性——努山塔拉是跨总统任期的超级工程，继任者的优先级摇摆将直接改写时间表。

归根结底，努山塔拉是印尼用混凝土写下的国族赌注。两年里它证明了"能建起来"，却还未证明"能活下去、活得好"。进度与质疑并存的当下，真正的考验不在奠基，而在搬迁之后漫长的运营与认同。

## 延伸阅读

**努山塔拉（Nusantara）**：印尼规划中的新首都，位于加里曼丹岛东加里曼丹省，2022 年立法确立，意在替代雅加达的政治功能。

**地面沉降（Land Subsidence）**：因地下水超采等导致地表缓慢下沉的现象，雅加达是全球沉降最快的大都市之一。

**PPP（Public-Private Partnership）**：政府与社会资本合作模式，东道国常以之撬动基建投资、分担财政压力。

# Indonesia's New Capital Nusantara: Progress and Doubts Two Years In

Moving the capital from Jakarta on Java to Nusantara in Kalimantan was Jokowi's most ambitious project and has been carried forward by the Prabowo government. In 2026, two years into the relocation, the core zone has taken shape, yet doubts over cost, pace and 'who actually moves' have never gone away.

## Why move: Jakarta's sinking crisis

The logic rests on Jakarta's unsustainability: the ground sinks 15–25 cm a year, the north is already below sea level, and over-extraction of groundwater meets sea-level rise; meanwhile Java holds over half the population and economy, a severely unbalanced development. Nusantara, sited in Kalimantan's interior, aims to balance the territory, relieve Java and model a 'smart city in the forest'.

## Progress: core first, infrastructure lagging

Two years on, the presidential palace, ministry offices and civil-service housing have topped out or opened, with some officials and troops relocated first. But airport, expressway, hospital and school networks lag, and the privately and foreign-led 'new city' blocks have underperformed on investment promotion. In short, the government-led political core lands first while the market-led urban ecosystem lags — a capital groping along the path of 'shell first, city later'.

## The cost question: where the money comes from, and whether it's worth it

Nusantara's budget is estimated at hundreds of trillions of rupiah (tens of billions of dollars). The government is explicit it won't fully fund it from the budget, betting on a sovereign fund, PPP and foreign capital. But high global rates and narrowing fiscal space make financing hinge on state firms and strategic investors. The doubt follows: with gaps in infrastructure, education and healthcare still large, is pouring huge resources into a new city the right priority?

## A governance symbol and a balancing narrative

For Indonesia, the move is never just engineering but national narrative: pushing power from Java's 'core' to Kalimantan's 'hinterland' is a political statement of outer-island balance and echoes the 'global maritime axis' imagination. Its green, smart positioning is also a window to showcase modern governance abroad.

## A few things to watch

First, whether the scale of civil-service relocation expands as planned, deciding the city's real vibrancy. Second, the landing rate of foreign and PPP projects, the touchstone of financing sustainability. Third, political continuity — Nusantara is a mega-project spanning presidencies; a successor's priority shift would rewrite the timetable directly.

In the end, Nusantara is Indonesia's national wager written in concrete. In two years it proved it 'can be built' but not yet that it 'can survive and thrive'. With progress and doubts coexisting, the real test is not the groundbreaking but the long operation and identity after the move.

## Further reading

**Nusantara**: Indonesia's planned new capital in East Kalimantan, legislated in 2022 to replace Jakarta's political function.

**Land Subsidence**: The slow sinking of ground surface from over-extraction of groundwater and other causes; Jakarta is among the world's fastest-sinking metropolises.

**PPP (Public-Private Partnership)**: A model of government-social capital cooperation, often used by host countries to leverage infrastructure investment and share fiscal pressure.

# 马来西亚"喜鹊行动"扫荡112个诈骗窝点 932人落网含538名中国公民

11月12日，马来西亚全国总警长署商业罪案调查部在记者会上公布一项代号为"喜鹊行动（Magpie 01/2026）"的全国扫荡成果：10月19日至31日的13天里，警方发动153次突击，取缔112个诈骗呼叫中心，逮捕932人，起获手机、电脑等作案工具逾2800件。在这串数字中，最刺眼的是落网者的国籍构成——538名中国公民，占826名外籍人员逾六成。一场针对诈骗的治安行动，就这样把东南亚诈骗产业对中国籍劳动力的深度依赖，赤裸裸地摊在了阳光下。

## 数字：13天932人落网

"喜鹊行动"的规模在马来西亚历次反诈行动中居前。932名落网者中，106人为马来西亚公民，其余826人均为外籍；按性别计，796名男性、136名女性；按角色计，809人是一线"客服"或操作员，70人担任主管或小组负责人。警方检查了650个商业及住宅单位，近七成突击发生在公寓——诈骗集团把民居当成了犯罪工厂。起获的作案工具同样惊人：2058台手机、433台手提电脑或平板、161台电脑或显示器，以及40本护照或签证。现金虽不多，充公的10万2635令吉（约3万2300新元，约合17.4万元人民币）仍揭示出资金链的盘根错节。

## 中国公民为何占了大半

538名中国公民，是落网外籍人员中数量最多的一群，远超越南（93人）、台湾（40人）、印尼（36人）、日本（21人）等地。这一现象并不偶然。过去数年，东南亚诈骗园区反复曝光"高薪招工"陷阱：求职者被以境外客服、翻译、技术岗为名诱骗出境，抵达后被没收证件、限制自由，被迫在园区内从事电信诈骗。换言之，许多落网的中国公民本身既是加害链条的一环，也可能是人口贩运的受害者。马来西亚此次大规模落网，正是这条灰黑产业链外溢的缩影。

## 窝点藏在公寓里

警方153次突击中，106次、近七成在公寓展开，另有34次在住宅，8次在度假村或酒店。把呼叫中心藏进普通民居，既降低租金与曝光，也便于在突击时销毁证据。鲁斯迪总监披露，团伙的诈骗手法多达136种，其中虚假投资诈骗与"爱情骗局"各27起居首，电话诈骗25起、网络赌博16起、虚假招聘11起次之。手法之杂、分工之细，说明这已不是散兵游勇，而是高度组织化的跨国犯罪网络。

## 诈骗链条的跨境属性

更值得警惕的是诈骗的"出口导向"。在团伙144项诈骗记录中，仅14项针对马来西亚民众，其余130项都瞄准海外——中国以62项居首，美国12项，越南、印尼各7项，日本与香港各6项。这意味着，设在马来西亚的窝点，黑手伸向的是全球受害者。诈骗集团借助中文母语优势精准锁定同胞与华语社群，同时也把美、日等国民众纳入猎物范围。这种"在第三国作案、向多国行骗"的模式，决定了任何单一国家都难以独力铲除，必须靠区域执法协作。

## 从鞭刑法案到大规模扫荡

"喜鹊行动"并非孤立事件。就在两个月前，马来西亚政府提出对网络诈骗犯施以鞭刑的法案，引发社会热议；如今再以全国同步扫荡落地，显示吉隆坡对诈骗产业的容忍度正在快速耗尽。一方面，诈骗损害马来西亚作为投资与旅游目的地的形象；另一方面，外籍（尤其中国籍）涉案人员的集中出现，也让执法部门承受着"治外"与"主权"的双重压力。从立法威慑到雷霆行动，马来西亚的强硬转向已经清晰。

## 华人社会的阴影与领事保护

538名中国公民落网，给马来西亚当地华人社会投下了一道复杂的阴影。个别不法分子的所作所为，容易被误读为整个华群的标签，进而加剧本就敏感的族群关系。与此同时，中国驻马使领馆的领事保护工作也面临新课题：如何在依法配合马方执法的同时，甄别并救助那些被诱骗、被胁迫的受害同胞。打击跨境诈骗与保护合法权益，从来是一枚硬币的两面——这正是"华侨华人"议题下最不该回避的现实。

## 延伸阅读

**人口贩运（Human Trafficking）**：指以招工、婚介等名义诱骗并控制他人从事强迫劳动或犯罪，东南亚诈骗园区中不少"客服"实为被囚禁的受害者。

**跨境治理（Cross-Border Governance）**：针对电信诈骗、网络赌博等跨国犯罪，需依托中国—东盟执法合作机制开展联合打击与情报共享。

**领事保护（Consular Protection）**：驻外使领馆依法协助本国公民应对拘押、诱骗等处境，是海外华侨华人权益保障的第一道防线。

# Malaysia's "Magpie 01/2026" Sweep Dismantles 112 Scam Call Centres, 932 Arrested Including 538 Chinese Nationals

On 12 November, Malaysia's Commercial Crime Investigation Department unveiled the results of "Operation Magpie 01/2026," a nationwide crackdown: between 19 and 31 October, in just 13 days, police carried out 153 raids, dismantled 112 scam call centres, arrested 932 people, and seized more than 2,800 devices. The most striking figure was the nationality breakdown — 538 Chinese nationals, over 65% of the 826 foreigners detained. A routine public-order operation has, in effect, laid bare how deeply Southeast Asia's scam economy depends on Chinese-speaking labour.

## The numbers: 932 detained in 13 days

Operation Magpie ranks among Malaysia's largest anti-scam sweeps. Of the 932 arrested, 106 were Malaysians and 826 were foreigners; 796 men and 136 women; 809 were front-line "customer service" operators and 70 were supervisors or team leaders. Police inspected 650 commercial and residential premises, and nearly 70% of the raids struck apartments — the gangs had turned ordinary flats into crime factories. The haul was staggering: 2,058 mobile phones, 433 laptops or tablets, 161 computers or monitors, and 40 passports or visas. The cash seized was modest — RM102,635 (about S$32,300, ~US$24,000) — yet it hints at tangled money trails.

## Why Chinese nationals dominate the arrests

The 538 Chinese nationals were by far the largest foreign group, well ahead of Vietnam (93), Taiwan (40), Indonesia (36) and Japan (21). This is no accident. For years, Southeast Asian scam compounds have recycled the "high-paying job abroad" trap: recruits lured with offers of customer-service, translation or tech posts are stripped of their documents on arrival, confined, and forced to run telecom fraud. In other words, many of the detained Chinese nationals are at once cogs in the offending chain and potential victims of human trafficking. Malaysia's mass arrests are a symptom of that grey-black industry spilling across borders.

## Hideouts inside condominiums

Of the 153 raids, 106 — nearly 70% — hit apartments, with another 34 at houses and eight at resorts or hotels. Tucking call centres into ordinary homes cuts rent and visibility, and lets gangs destroy evidence at the first knock. Director Rusdi disclosed 136 distinct scam typologies; fake-investment and romance scams led with 27 each, followed by phone fraud (25), online gambling (16) and fake-job scams (11). The variety of methods and the precision of the division of labour show this is no ragtag operation but a highly organised transnational network.

## The cross-border anatomy of the fraud chain

More alarming is the scams' export orientation. Of the gangs' 144 fraud records, only 14 targeted Malaysians; the other 130 aimed abroad — China led with 62, the United States with 12, Vietnam and Indonesia with seven each, and Japan and Hong Kong with six each. Compounds based in Malaysia thus reach victims worldwide. Exploiting Chinese-language fluency, the gangs prey on compatriots and diaspora communities while also casting nets over American and Japanese targets. This "operate in a third country, defraud many" model means no single state can dismantle it alone; regional law-enforcement cooperation is indispensable.

## From the caning bill to mass raids

Operation Magpie did not appear from nowhere. Two months earlier, the Malaysian government proposed caning online scammers, a bill that stirred intense debate; the nationwide sweep now translates that hardening stance into action. On one front, scams tarnish Malaysia's image as an investment and tourism destination; on another, the concentration of foreign — especially Chinese — suspects piles "extra-territorial" and sovereign pressures onto the police. From legislative deterrence to lightning raids, Kuala Lumpur's tough turn is now unmistakable.

## A shadow over the ethnic-Chinese community and consular protection

The detention of 538 Chinese nationals casts a complicated shadow over Malaysia's local ethnic-Chinese community. The misdeeds of a few can be misread as a label for the whole group, aggravating already sensitive ethnic relations. At the same time, Chinese missions in Malaysia face a new consular test: how to cooperate with Malaysian enforcement while identifying and rescuing compatriots who were lured and coerced. Cracking down on cross-border fraud and protecting lawful rights are two sides of the same coin — the uncomfortable reality that the "overseas Chinese" agenda cannot ignore.

## Further reading

**Human Trafficking**: The use of fake job or matchmaking offers to lure and confine people into forced labour or crime; many "operators" in Southeast Asian scam compounds are in fact imprisoned victims.

**Cross-Border Governance**: Tackling transnational crimes such as telecom fraud and online gambling requires joint China–ASEAN enforcement and intelligence sharing.

**Consular Protection**: Embassies and consulates assist nationals facing detention or deception abroad, the first line of defence for overseas Chinese rights.

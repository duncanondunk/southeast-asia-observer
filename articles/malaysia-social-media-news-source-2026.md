# 社交媒体成七成马国民众主要新闻源：当传统媒体退潮

2026 年，马来西亚的新闻消费越过一道临界点。路透社新闻研究所《2026 年数码新闻报告》显示，近七成马来西亚受访者通过社交媒体获取新闻，使用率（约 70%）已超越新闻网站（42%）与电视（36%）。从电视独大到社媒登顶，马来西亚用十年走完了一场媒介权力的转移。当“发现新闻”与“消费新闻”都栖身同一块屏幕，政治共同体的信息基础正在被静默重写。

## 七成阈值：信息生态的临界点

七成不是一个普通数字。在报告涵盖的 48 个市场中，社媒平均使用率为 54%，电视 52%，新闻网站 51%；而马来西亚的 70% 明显高出全球均值。更关键的是趋势：2017 年电视以 73% 居首，新闻网站 69%，社媒 54%。十年间社媒并非暴涨，而是电视与新闻网站分别下滑 18 与 24 个百分点，印刷媒体更跌 31 点。社媒登顶，更多反映的是传统渠道的退潮，而非社媒覆盖的狂飙。临界点一旦越过，回流便极为困难。值得注意的是，社媒使用率自 2017 年起便稳定在六成以上，说明此番登顶并非突发，而是长期缓慢累积的结果——传统媒体的份额是被一点点蚕食的，而非被一夜夺走。这也意味着，逆转趋势所需的不只是几家媒体的自救，而是整个信息生态的重建。

## 信任与商业的双重瓦解：传统媒体的退潮

传统媒体的萎缩，既是商业的也是信任的。广告随受众流向算法平台，新闻网站与电视的收入基础被掏空；与此同时，受众对机构媒体的依赖减弱，直接浏览新闻网站或应用的比例仅 27%。当“发现新闻”的主入口变成社媒（63%）与搜索引擎（44%），传统媒体从“把关人”退化为“被转发的来源”。商业模型与公信力的双重松动，使重建举步维艰。一旦受众习惯了免费、即时、个性化的推送，付费与守时的新闻反成例外。

## 四语之国：媒介碎片化中的马来西亚

马来西亚的复杂在于语言。马来语、英语、华语、淡米尔语四大语群各有媒介习惯，社媒算法进一步把用户切进平行的信息泡泡。WhatsApp 以 50% 使用率略超脸书（49%），成为最广泛使用的新闻平台；TikTok 从 2019 年的 2% 飙至 45%，YouTube 升至 41%，Threads 也从 2024 年的 2% 升至 11%，而 X 则从 2022 年峰值 22% 回落至 9%。多语并存本是社会事实，但当每种语言社群都在各自的平台生态中闭环，跨族群的共同议程反而更难形成。一个马来语用户、一个华语用户与一个英语用户，可能在同一时刻“看新闻”，却活在互不相交的信息宇宙里。公共讨论的前提是共享的事实，而碎片化正悄悄抽走这块基石。

## 算法与同温层：族群政治的放大效应

算法推荐天然偏好高互动内容，而族群议题恰恰最易激发互动。在马来西亚这样族群政治敏感的社会，同温层会放大情绪化、标签化的叙事，削弱温和与跨族群的声音。社媒既是新闻入口，也是政治动员场。当“发现新闻”依赖平台推送，哪些议题被看见、哪些被埋没，已不完全由新闻价值决定，而由算法的注意力经济决定。平台不为公共性负责，却握有公共议程的隐形开关。近年马来西亚已出现多起 AI 生成内容干预舆论、甚至被指操纵选民的讨论，算法与合成内容叠加，使本就脆弱的选举信息环境更添变数。技术中立的神话，在族群政治的高压锅里尤其容易破灭。

## 假信息与 AI 内容：叠加的风险

风险在叠加。马来西亚通讯及多媒体委员会（MCMC）《2024 年互联网用户调查》显示，61.5% 的网民会分享新闻，但核实真实性与来源可靠性的比例从 79.2% 降至 70.7%。与此同时，AI 聊天机器人用于获取新闻的比例升至 11%（高于去年的 9%），年轻群体尤其突出。AI 生成内容降低了造假门槛，政治与健康领域的误导信息更易泛滥。当自动化事实查核成为依赖，民众的主动辨别力反而弱化，虚假信息的“半衰期”被拉长。

## 监管的冲动：MCMC 与言论自由之间

政府的本能是收紧。MCMC 的内容许可与监管框架，体现了对虚假信息与外部影响的焦虑；但许可制与平台问责，又常与言论自由、新闻多元形成张力。在多元族群社会，监管尺度极易被质疑为针对特定社群或立场。如何在抑制假信息与保全开放之间拿捏，是马来西亚治理的长期难题，也映照出全球民主体的共同困境。监管若失之过宽，假信息横行；失之过严，则压缩本就脆弱的公共空间。

## 青年世代：新闻消费方式的代际迁移

代际差异清晰。各年龄段社媒使用率介于 65% 至 71%，看似均衡，但年轻人明显更拥抱新兴方式：18 至 24 岁受访者中 21% 用 AI 聊天机器人获取新闻，45 岁以上仅 6%；播客亦呈年轻化。女性与男性社媒使用几乎持平（69% 对 68%），但新闻网站性别差距明显——男性 48%、女性 36%，AI 聊天机器人则为男性 12%、女性 11%。青年把新闻“刷”进日常，而非“读”进议程，这重塑了政治参与的入口与节奏。

## 去中心化之后：政治共同体如何重塑

社媒登顶不只是一个媒介故事，更是政治共同体的重构。当新闻来源去中心化，统一的公共议程被拆成无数算法气泡，跨族群、跨代际的“我们”如何维系，成为新问题。这份调查由民调机构 YouGov 于今年 1 月中旬至 2 月底进行，2076 名马来西亚受访者参与，其结论值得区域邻国对照。马来西亚的 70% 是一面镜子：技术赋权个体的同时，也稀释了共享的事实基础。去中心化之后，民主需要新的黏合机制——否则，自由越多，共识越少。

**马来西亚通讯及多媒体委员会（MCMC）**：马来西亚监管通讯与多媒体内容的法定机构，负责网络治理与内容合规框架。

# Social Media Overtakes Traditional News for 70% of Malaysians: When Legacy Media Recedes

In 2026, Malaysia's news consumption crossed a threshold. The Reuters Institute for the Study of Journalism's Digital News Report 2026 finds that nearly seven in ten Malaysian respondents get news via social media—about 70 per cent, surpassing news websites (42 per cent) and television (36 per cent). From television's dominance to social media's crown, Malaysia completed a transfer of media power in a decade. When "discovering" and "consuming" news both lodge on one screen, the information basis of the political community is being silently rewritten.

## The 70% threshold: a tipping point in the information ecosystem

Seventy per cent is not an ordinary number. Across the 48 markets the report covers, social media averages 54 per cent, television 52, news websites 51; Malaysia's 70 stands clearly above the global mean. The trend matters more: in 2017 television led at 73 per cent, news websites 69, social 54. Over ten years social media did not explode—television and news websites fell 18 and 24 points, print by 31. Social media's crowning reflects the ebb of traditional channels more than any surge in its own reach. Once a tipping point is crossed, the path back grows steep. Notably, social-media use has held above 60 per cent since 2017, so the crowning was no sudden leap but a slow accumulation—traditional media's share was nibbled away, not seized overnight. Reversing it demands not merely a few outlets'自救 but a rebuild of the whole information ecosystem.

## The double unraveling of trust and business: legacy media's retreat

Legacy media's shrink is both commercial and of trust. Advertising followed audiences to algorithmic platforms, hollowing the revenue base of news sites and television; meanwhile dependence on institutions weakened, with only 27 per cent directly browsing news sites or apps. As the main entry to "discover news" became social media (63 per cent) and search engines (44 per cent), legacy media slid from "gatekeeper" to "a source being forwarded." With business model and credibility loosening together, rebuilding is hard. Once audiences grow used to free, instant, personalised feeds, paid and scheduled news becomes the exception.

## A four-language nation: Malaysia inside media fragmentation

Malaysia's complexity is linguistic. Malay, English, Chinese and Tamil speech communities each have media habits, and social algorithms further slice users into parallel information bubbles. WhatsApp leads at 50 per cent, just above Facebook's 49, as the most-used news platform; TikTok leapt from 2 per cent in 2019 to 45, YouTube to 41, Threads from 2 per cent in 2024 to 11, while X fell from a 2022 peak of 22 to 9. Multilingual coexistence is a social fact, but when each language community closes inside its own platform ecosystem, a cross-ethnic common agenda grows harder to form. A Malay-language user, a Chinese-language user and an English-language user may "read news" at the same moment yet live in non-intersecting information universes. Public debate presupposes shared facts, and fragmentation quietly withdraws that foundation.

## Algorithms and echo chambers: amplifying ethnic politics

Algorithmic recommendation naturally favours high-engagement content, and ethnic topics stir engagement most. In a society as sensitive as Malaysia's, echo chambers amplify emotive, label-driven narratives and weaken moderate, cross-ethnic voices. Social media is both a news gateway and a political mobiliser. When "discovering news" relies on platform pushes, which issues are seen and which buried is no longer decided solely by news value but by the attention economy of algorithms. Platforms bear no responsibility for the public good, yet hold its invisible switch. In recent years Malaysia has seen repeated debate over AI-generated content meddling in public opinion, even alleged voter manipulation; algorithms and synthetic media stacked together add volatility to an already fragile electoral information environment. The myth of technological neutrality breaks easiest inside the pressure cooker of ethnic politics.

## Disinformation and AI content: stacked risks

Risks stack. MCMC's 2024 Internet Users Survey shows 61.5 per cent of netizens share news, yet the share verifying authenticity and source reliability fell from 79.2 to 70.7 per cent. Meanwhile the share using AI chatbots for news rose to 11 per cent (up from 9), especially among the young. AI-generated content lowers the bar for fabrication, making political and health misinformation easier to spread. As automated fact-checking becomes a crutch, citizens' active discernment weakens and the half-life of falsehoods lengthens.

## The regulatory impulse: between MCMC and free speech

The state's instinct is to tighten. MCMC's content-licensing and regulatory framework expresses anxiety over false information and external influence; yet licensing and platform accountability sit in tension with free speech and pluralism. In a multi-ethnic society, regulatory calibration is easily questioned as targeting particular communities or stances. Balancing suppression of fakery against openness is Malaysia's long-term governance puzzle—and a mirror of democracies everywhere. Too loose, and disinformation runs wild; too tight, and already-fragile public space is compressed.

## The youth cohort: a generational shift in news habits

Generational lines are clear. Social-media use ranges 65 to 71 per cent across ages, seemingly even, yet the young embrace newer modes: among 18–24-year-olds, 21 per cent use AI chatbots for news versus 6 per cent for those over 45; podcasts skew young too. Women and men use social media almost equally (69 vs 68 per cent), but the news-website gender gap is stark—men 48, women 36, while AI chatbots sit at men 12, women 11. Youth "scroll" news into daily life rather than "read" it into an agenda, reshaping the entry and rhythm of political participation.

## After decentralisation: how the political community is remade

Social media's crown is not merely a media story but a remaking of the political community. As news sources decentralise, a unified public agenda fragments into countless algorithmic bubbles; how a cross-ethnic, cross-generational "we" is sustained becomes the new question. The survey, conducted by YouGov from mid-January to late February this year with 2,076 Malaysian respondents, invites comparison across the region. Malaysia's 70 per cent is a mirror: technology empowers individuals while diluting a shared factual base. After decentralisation, democracy needs new glue—or the more freedom, the less consensus.
